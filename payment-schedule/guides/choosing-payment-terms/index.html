Choosing payment terms

On a $6,000 project at 50/25/25 starting January 4, 2027, the final $1,500.00 is due March 1, 2027 if due on receipt, March 16, 2027 on net 15 and March 31, 2027 on net 30.

What the terms mean

  • Due on receipt: payable when the invoice arrives.
  • Net 15 or net 30: payable within 15 or 30 days of the invoice date.
  • Here, a due date on a weekend or a US federal holiday moves to the next business day.

Which to choose

  • Deposits: due on receipt, or a short net (7 days), because work starts when it arrives.
  • Businesses with an accounts-payable cycle often pay on net 30; agree it up front and plan the cash gap.
  • Individuals and small clients: due on receipt or net 7 to 15.
  • Industry norms differ: the AICP post-production guidelines use 30 days from the invoice date.

Late-fee wording

A clear, proportionate clause names the rate, when it starts and that it is stated on every invoice, for example "Invoices unpaid 30 days after the due date bear interest at 1% a month on the unpaid balance." Whether a late fee is allowed, and how much, depends on where your client is and whether they are a business or a consumer: check the rules there before you rely on it.
