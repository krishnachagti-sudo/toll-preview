How much deposit to ask for

On a $4,000 project the three common shapes are paid in full upfront ($4,000.00 first), half upfront, half on delivery ($2,000.00 first) and a smaller deposit plus milestones ($1,000.00 first); how much is custom, not law, except where a home-improvement rule caps it (California: $400.00 on this job).

What practitioners say

  • One freelancer on Hacker News never starts with a new client without at least 25% upfront, and asks 50% on contracts under $20k (thread).
  • Another finds it "pretty reasonable to ask for 50% down up front" for small amounts (thread).
  • These are individual practices, not a standard. A tiered approach (all upfront on small jobs, half on mid-size, a smaller deposit plus milestones on large ones) follows from the same reasoning.

What drives the percentage

  • Cash flow: how much of your cost (time, subcontractors, materials) comes before the client sees anything.
  • Client risk: a new client, a client abroad, or a project that can stall on their approvals.
  • Project length: the longer it runs, the more stages make sense, so neither side carries too much.
  • The law where it applies: in California the down payment on a home improvement job is at most $1,000 or 10% of the price, whichever is less; in Maryland and Maine one-third. Freelance services have no such cap in these states' rules.

Sources

  • CSLB: Learn About Home Improvement Contracts (read November 8, 2026)
  • Maryland Code, Business Regulation 8-617 (read November 8, 2026)
  • Maine Revised Statutes, Title 10, 1487 (read November 8, 2026)

Not legal or tax advice. The schedule is arithmetic and calendar dates; whether a deposit is refundable, and when tax is due on it, depend on your contract and where you and your client are.
