Stage payments for contractors

On a $40,000 California remodel the down payment can be at most $1,000.00 (the lesser of $1,000 or 10%); a stage schedule then bills $15,000.00, $10,000.00, $10,000.00, $2,000.00 and $2,000.00 as the work passes each stage.

Deposit rules we cover

StateApplies toRuleSource
CaliforniaHome improvement and swimming pool contracts$1,000 or 10% of the contract price, whichever is less (finance charges excluded)Contractors State License Board (CSLB), Learn About Home Improvement Contracts (Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code 7159.5)
MarylandHome improvement contractsone-third of the contract price, before or when the contract is signed; no payment at all before it is signedMd. Code, Business Regulation 8-617(b)

What a 50% deposit on $40,000 ($20,000.00) meets in each: The deposit of $20,000.00 is over California's limit of $1,000.00 ($1,000 or 10% of the contract price, whichever is less (finance charges excluded)). The deposit of $20,000.00 is over Maryland's limit of $13,333.33 (one-third of the contract price, before or when the contract is signed; no payment at all before it is signed). The deposit of $20,000.00 is over Maine's limit of $13,333.33 (one-third of the total contract price, on contracts over $3,000). The deposit of $20,000.00 is more than 10% of the price ($4,000.00): Florida then requires the contractor to apply for permits within 30 days of payment (by February 10, 2027 if it is paid on its due date) and start work within 90 days after the permits are issued.

Only these four states are covered: each was read on the state's own statute site or its contractors' board on November 8, 2026. Other states are not covered here, which does not mean they have no rule.

CSLB's page adds that payments cannot exceed the value of the work performed, the down payment excepted: time each stage to work that is done, not to dates alone.

Inspections, retention and changes

  • Inspections: tie a stage to a passed inspection, so the payment follows a fact both sides can see.
  • Retention: a percentage held back until the job is complete, then released (5% above, 30 days after the walkthrough). Whether and how much retention is allowed is a matter of contract and local law.
  • Variations (change orders): price and approve every change in writing before the work, and add it as its own stage or line.
  • Lien and waiver rules vary by state: check yours before the first stage payment.
