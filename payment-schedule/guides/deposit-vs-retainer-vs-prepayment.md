# Deposit, retainer or prepayment?

On a $9,000 fixed-price project, a 30% deposit is $2,700.00 toward the price; a retainer of, say, $2,000 a month buys time each month whatever is delivered; a prepayment would be the whole $9,000.00 before the work.

## The three side by side

|  | Deposit | Retainer | Prepayment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What it pays for | Part of a fixed price, before the work | Your availability, or a block of hours, for a period | The whole price, before delivery |
| Invoice wording | "Deposit: 30% of the $9,000.00 project" | "Monthly retainer, January 2027" | "Payment in full for (project), in advance" |
| Refundable? | Only as the contract says | Usually not for time already reserved, if the contract says so | Usually for work not delivered, if the contract says so |
| In accrual books, until the work is done | Usually a liability (unearned revenue) | Depends on what it buys: ask your accountant | Usually a liability (unearned revenue) |

| 2. Midpoint milestone | 30% | $2,700.00 | February 15, 2027 | Net 7 | February 22, 2027 | $3,600.00 |

## Say which one it is

Use one word in the contract and on every invoice, and say what happens to the money if the project stops. The same payment called a "deposit" in the proposal and a "retainer" in the contract is how disputes start.

> Not legal, tax or accounting advice: how money received in advance is recorded and taxed depends on your accounting method and where you are.
