# Texas Prompt Payment Act interest calculator

Under Texas Government Code 2251.025, an overdue payment from a Texas governmental entity accrues 7.75% a year (fiscal 2027, fixed on September 1, 2026); on $10,000 due November 5, 2026 and paid December 21 that is $97.67.

- Fiscal 2027 rate: 7.75%
- $10,000, 46 days: $97.67

## The rules

- When it is overdue (2251.021(a)): "a payment by a governmental entity under a contract executed on or after September 1, 1987, is overdue on the 31st day after the later of: (1) the date the governmental entity receives the goods under the contract; (2) the date the performance of the service under the contract is completed; or (3) the date the governmental entity receives an invoice for the goods or service."
- The rate (2251.025(b)): "The rate of interest that accrues on an overdue payment is the rate in effect on September 1 of the fiscal year in which the payment becomes overdue. The rate in effect on September 1 is equal to the sum of: (1) one percent; and (2) the prime rate as published in the Wall Street Journal on the first day of July of the preceding fiscal year that does not fall on a Saturday or Sunday."
- One rate (Comptroller): "When a payment is late during two different fiscal years, only one rate will be used in the interest calculation. The rate used is based on the fiscal year in which the payment becomes late."

The Comptroller's formula: `(# of days late ÷ 365) x (applicable prompt payment interest rate) x (amount of payment) = (interest due).` Its own example, $500 due November 5 and paid December 9 (34 days) at the fiscal 2027 rate, gives $3.61.

> Disputes: chapter 2251 does not apply where a bona fide dispute causes the payment to be late (2251.002). Political subdivisions whose governing body meets once a month or less have 46 days, not 31.

### What is the Texas Prompt Payment Act interest rate?
