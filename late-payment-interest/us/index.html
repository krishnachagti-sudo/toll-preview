Late fees and prompt payment laws

Charging interest on a late invoice in the US

Between US businesses there is no statutory late-payment interest: you charge what your agreed terms say, such as 1.5% a month, which on a $2,500 invoice 39 days late is $48.75. Federal agencies owe Prompt Payment Act interest (4.75% for July to December 2026: $15.09 on $1,500 paid 76 days late), and Texas and Florida public bodies owe interest under state law.

Putting a late fee on the invoice

State the rate before the work starts (in the contract or accepted terms), print it on every invoice, and charge only what it says. Example wording is on the clause page.

State usury and late-fee limits for business invoices were not read for this page; check your state before charging more than a modest rate.

Please read

  • Not legal advice. The figures follow the official rules and rates quoted, read on the date shown; a court decides any dispute.
  • Business-to-business debts only: the UK Act, the Irish regulations, § 288(2) BGB and the EU directive do not cover money owed by consumers.
  • Statutory interest does not apply where your contract sets its own late-payment rate (UK: a "substantial remedy"); use your own terms instead.
  • No interest or compensation is worked out for an invoice paid late because of a genuine dispute.
  • US public-body rules (federal, Texas, Florida local government) apply only to payments by those bodies; between businesses in the US, only the rate in your agreed terms applies, within your state's limits.
