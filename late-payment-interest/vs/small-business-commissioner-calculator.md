# Endwise vs the Small Business Commissioner calculator

Both give the same kind of figure for one UK invoice: £4,800 due August 31, 2026 and paid November 2 comes to £97.35 interest and £70 compensation here. The Commissioner's calculator is free and official; Endwise also shows the reference date and Bank Rate used, writes the claim letter and invoice, and covers Ireland, Germany, the EU minimum and US public bodies.

- Interest, £4,800: £97.35

## Side by side

The Commissioner's pages read November 8, 2026.

|  | Small Business Commissioner | Endwise |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Interest and fixed compensation | Yes | Yes |
| Inputs | Contract days or the statutory 30, invoice date, payment date, amount with VAT | The same, or a due date; or a date to work out to |
| Reference rate shown on the result | Not shown on the result (the guidance explains the rule) | Shown, with the Bank Rate and the date it was set |
| Letter or interest invoice | No; the guidance says to send a new invoice | Yes, a PDF (paid); figure free |
| Ireland, Germany, EU, US public bodies | No | Yes |
| Many invoices at once | No | Yes, from a CSV (paid) |
| Price | Free | Figure free on the page; letter $1.50 |

## When the Commissioner's calculator is better

You have one UK debt and want the official government figure, the one an adviser or the Commissioner's own team will recognize, and you will write the letter yourself. It is free and the Commissioner's office will help if you are unsure (its guidance invites you to contact it).

## What the Commissioner says about its tool

Its guidance: "Our interest rate calculator calculates interest to the first two decimal points only." and it treats VAT, disputes and the 6-year limit as this tool does.
