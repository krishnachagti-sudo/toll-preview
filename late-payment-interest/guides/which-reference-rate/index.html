UK and EU guide

Which reference rate applies?

Use the Bank Rate on the reference date before the day the debt became late, plus 8%, and keep it until the debt is paid: an invoice due March 31, 2026 became late on April 1, so it uses the 3.75% Bank Rate of December 31, 2025, 11.75% a year, even after Bank Rate moves.

The two reference dates

GOV.UK users' guide: "The Bank of England base rate on 31 December will be the "reference rate" for 1 January to 30 June. The Bank of England base rate on 30 June will be the "reference rate" for 1 July to 31 December." And: "add 8% to the "reference rate" that covers the six-month period in which your debt becomes late."

Across a year end

£6,000 due December 20, 2025 and paid February 28, 2026: the debt became late on December 21, 2025, in the second half of 2025, so the reference date is June 30, 2025 (Bank Rate 4.25%). The rate is 12.25% for all 70 days, including those in 2026: £140.96.

Under the EU directive it changes

The directive and Irish law apply the ECB rate in force on January 1 or July 1 to each half-year of the delay, so a long delay uses several rates. Directive, Article 3(2): "for the first semester of the year concerned shall be the rate in force on 1 January of that year; for the second semester of the year concerned shall be the rate in force on 1 July of that year."

Finding the base rate yourself

The Bank of England publishes every Bank Rate change with its date. The rate on a reference date is the last change on or before it. Every half-year since 2003 is worked out in the reference rate table.
