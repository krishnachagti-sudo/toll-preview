UK guide

Do I need a clause to charge late payment interest?

No clause is needed: for a debt between businesses the Late Payment Act gives statutory interest at 8% over the reference Bank Rate (11.75% for debts late from July to December 2026) and a fixed sum. A £1,500 invoice due September 30, 2026 and paid 45 days late earns £21.73 plus £70: £91.73.

Who counts

  • Business to business, including sole traders and freelancers selling to a business.
  • Public authorities buying from a business (they must usually pay within 30 days).
  • Not consumers: money owed by a private person is outside the Act.

When a contract term takes over

GOV.UK: "this is 8% plus the Bank of England base rate for business to business transactions. You cannot claim statutory interest if there's a different rate of interest in a contract."

GOV.UK users' guide: "Statutory interest rate applies (at least 8 percentage points above the Bank of England's reference rate) to the debt unless an agreement has been reached which amounts to a substantial remedy for the late payment of the debt."

Saying it on the invoice from now on

Not required, but it helps: a line such as "Late payments carry interest and fixed compensation under the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998" (more on the clause page).

What to send first

A claim letter with an interest invoice, not a court claim (see the letter template). If that fails, the next step is a court claim (Money Claim Online).

Please read

  • Not legal advice. The figures follow the official rules and rates quoted, read on the date shown; a court decides any dispute.
  • Business-to-business debts only: the UK Act, the Irish regulations, § 288(2) BGB and the EU directive do not cover money owed by consumers.
  • Statutory interest does not apply where your contract sets its own late-payment rate (UK: a "substantial remedy"); use your own terms instead.
  • No interest or compensation is worked out for an invoice paid late because of a genuine dispute.
  • US public-body rules (federal, Texas, Florida local government) apply only to payments by those bodies; between businesses in the US, only the rate in your agreed terms applies, within your state's limits.

Questions

Can I charge interest on a late invoice if my contract says nothing?

Yes, for a debt between businesses: statutory interest at 8% over the reference Bank Rate applies without a clause. On £1,500 paid 45 days late that is £21.73 plus £70.

When does a contract clause replace statutory interest?

When it sets its own rate that amounts to a substantial remedy for late payment (GOV.UK users' guide); a court decides whether it does.
