# The Small Business Protections Bill

The bill introduced on May 19, 2026 would cap payment terms at 60 days for large firms paying smaller suppliers and make interest at 8% above Bank of England base rate mandatory (GOV.UK). Until it is law, the 1998 Act applies: on £8,000 due September 15, 2026 you can claim £139.07 interest and £70 on November 8.

- Payment terms cap: 60 days
- £8,000 today: £139.07

## What was announced

- GOV.UK, May 19, 2026: the Small Business Protections Bill, formally the Commercial Payments Bill, was due to be introduced in the House of Lords that day.
- Reforms named: "a clear 60-day cap on payment terms on all large firms paying smaller suppliers, mandatory interest on late payments, set at 8% above the Bank of England base rate", and a ban on withholding retention payments in construction.
- New powers for the Small Business Commissioner to investigate, adjudicate disputes and fine persistent late payers.
- Boards or audit committees of persistently late-paying large companies to publish explanations.

GOV.UK has also published factsheets on the bill and a consultation page, dated July 24, 2026, titled "Late payments: tackling poor payment practices". This page does not follow the bill's later stages; check Parliament's bill page for its status.

## What you can claim today

The 1998 Act already gives 8% over the reference Bank Rate (11.75% for debts late from July to December 2026) and £40, £70 or £100, without a clause. The bill would make that interest mandatory rather than new.
