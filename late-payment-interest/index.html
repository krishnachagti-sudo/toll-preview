Which rate, and why

Each law sets its own rate: a reference rate (Bank Rate, the ECB rate, the Basiszinssatz or the Treasury rate) on a fixed date plus a margin, and most add a fixed sum by the size of the debt. The page uses the rate in force for each period of the delay.

UK, Ireland, Germany, the EU and US public bodies

UK

8% plus the Bank of England Bank Rate on December 31 (debts late January to June) or June 30 (July to December). The rate stays fixed until the debt is paid. Fixed sum: £40, £70 or £100 by the size of the debt.

Ireland, Germany, EU

The ECB rate on January 1 or July 1 plus 8 points (Germany: the Basiszinssatz plus 9), applied to each half-year of the delay. Fixed sum: €40, or €40, €70 or €100 in Ireland.

US public bodies

Federal agencies: the Treasury rate, 360-day year, compounded every 30 days for up to a year. Texas: the fiscal-year rate, days over 365. Florida local government: 1% a month from 30 days after the due date.

What it does not cover

  • Not legal advice. The figures follow the official rules and rates quoted, read on the date shown; a court decides any dispute.
  • Business-to-business debts only: the UK Act, the Irish regulations, § 288(2) BGB and the EU directive do not cover money owed by consumers.
  • Statutory interest does not apply where your contract sets its own late-payment rate (UK: a "substantial remedy"); use your own terms instead.
  • No interest or compensation is worked out for an invoice paid late because of a genuine dispute.
  • US public-body rules (federal, Texas, Florida local government) apply only to payments by those bodies; between businesses in the US, only the rate in your agreed terms applies, within your state's limits.
