## The sample letter (made-up firms)

**Claim for statutory interest and compensation**

**Invoice INV-1042: £4,800.00 due August 31, 2026**

Invoice INV-1042 for £4,800.00 was due for payment by August 31, 2026. It is still unpaid: on November 8, 2026 it was 69 days overdue.

The Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998 gives us the right to statutory interest on this business debt at 8% a year over the Bank of England base rate on the reference date. Interest began to run on September 1, 2026, so the reference date is June 30, 2026, when Bank Rate was 3.75%. The rate is 11.75% a year, simple interest, and stays the same until the debt is paid.

Statutory interest from September 1, 2026 to November 8, 2026 (69 days) at 11.75% a year: £106.62. The Act also gives fixed compensation for a debt of this size: £70. We claim £176.62 in all, as set out in the enclosed interest invoice.

Interest continues to run at £1.55 a day until the invoice is paid.

We may also claim the reasonable costs of recovering the debt if it remains unpaid.

Please pay £4,976.62 (the invoice and the claim) by November 22, 2026, to sort code 00-00-00, account 00000000 (sample). If you believe any part of this claim is wrong, please tell us by that date.

Yours faithfully, Sample Studio Ltd (made up)

## Tone

- State facts and figures; the law gives the right, so the letter does not need to argue.
- Give a date and say what you will do next only if you will do it (a court claim is the usual next step).
- Keep the customer: a short call before the letter often gets the invoice paid; the letter then claims only what the law gives.
- Send it by email and keep a copy with the date sent.

## Steps

1. **Facts first.** The invoice number, amount and due date, and whether it is paid or how many days overdue it is.
2. **The statutory line.** The Act, the reference date, the Bank Rate on it and the rate that follows, with the interest for the days late.
3. **The fixed sum.** £40, £70 or £100 by the size of the debt.
4. **Interest going forward.** The daily amount, so the customer knows the cost of waiting.
5. **What to pay and by when.** The total, a date and how to pay, with the interest invoice enclosed.
