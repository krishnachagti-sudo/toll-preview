Wording for contracts and invoices

Late payment clause examples

On a £2,000 invoice paid 30 days late, a clause that keeps the statutory right gives £19.32 interest at 11.75% plus £70; a US clause at 1.5% a month gives $30.00 on $2,000.

UK: keep the statutory right

Payment is due within 30 days of the invoice date. If payment is late, we may claim interest and fixed compensation under the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998.

Naming the Act does not change the rate: it tells the customer what late payment costs. A clause that sets a lower rate can replace statutory interest if it is a substantial remedy (GOV.UK users' guide), so stating your own low rate can cost you money.

US: a monthly rate

Invoices are due within 30 days. Balances unpaid after the due date accrue interest at 1.5% per month (18% per year), simple, from the due date until paid.

On $2,000 paid 30 days late: $30.00. A rate is enforceable only if the customer agreed to it, and state usury or late-fee limits may cap it; this page does not check a state's limit.
