UK business-to-business debts

The Late Payment Act 1998 in brief

The Act gives a supplier statutory interest on a late business invoice at 8% a year over the Bank of England base rate on the reference date (11.75% for debts late from July to December 2026), plus a fixed sum of £40, £70 or £100. On £3,000 due September 30, 2026 and paid November 8 that is £37.66 and £70.

When a payment is late

GOV.UK: "If you do not agree a payment date, the law says the payment is late 30 days after either: the customer gets the invoice; you deliver the goods or provide the service (if this is later)".

GOV.UK: "If you agree a payment date, it must usually be within 30 days for public authorities or 60 days for business transactions."

The rate and the fixed sums

GOV.UK: "this is 8% plus the Bank of England base rate for business to business transactions. You cannot claim statutory interest if there's a different rate of interest in a contract."

Fixed sums: £40 for a debt up to £999.99, £70 for £1,000 to £9,999.99 and £100 for £10,000 or more, once for each payment. Reasonable recovery costs can be claimed as well.

When your contract sets its own rate

GOV.UK users' guide: "Statutory interest rate applies (at least 8 percentage points above the Bank of England's reference rate) to the debt unless an agreement has been reached which amounts to a substantial remedy for the late payment of the debt." Whether a contract rate is a substantial remedy is for a court; this tool does not decide it.

Disputes, time limits and interest on interest

  • Disputes (Small Business Commissioner): "You cannot charge interest and compensation on an invoice which has been paid late due to a dispute."
  • Six years: "You can only collect interest on a late payment up to a maximum of 6 years from the date following the end of the contracted payment terms."
  • No interest on interest: "You can only charge interest and compensation on the original invoice which has been delayed or remains unpaid."
  • No reminder needed: "In cases of late payment, you are entitled to claim interest for the late payment without a reminder."

What is changing

A bill to cap payment terms for large firms at 60 days and make interest mandatory was introduced on May 19, 2026. It is not law on this page's date; see the bill page.

Please read

  • Not legal advice. The figures follow the official rules and rates quoted, read on the date shown; a court decides any dispute.
  • Business-to-business debts only: the UK Act, the Irish regulations, § 288(2) BGB and the EU directive do not cover money owed by consumers.
  • Statutory interest does not apply where your contract sets its own late-payment rate (UK: a "substantial remedy"); use your own terms instead.
  • No interest or compensation is worked out for an invoice paid late because of a genuine dispute.
  • US public-body rules (federal, Texas, Florida local government) apply only to payments by those bodies; between businesses in the US, only the rate in your agreed terms applies, within your state's limits.

Questions

When is a business payment late in the UK?

On the agreed date; with no agreed date, 30 days after the customer gets the invoice or you deliver the goods or service, whichever is later (GOV.UK).

My client paid very late but refuses to pay interest. Can I still claim it?

Yes, if the debt was not disputed and your contract sets no other rate: the right to statutory interest needs no reminder and can be claimed after payment, for up to 6 years. On £3,000 paid 39 days late it is £37.66 plus £70.

Does the Act apply to consumers?

No. It covers payments between businesses (and public bodies buying from businesses).
