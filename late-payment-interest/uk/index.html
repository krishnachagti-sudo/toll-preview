UK late payment interest calculator

How the £4,800 figure is worked out: 4,800 x 11.75% x 63 / 365 = £97.35, plus £70 because the debt is between £1,000 and £9,999.99.

The rate this half-year and last

  • Debts late from July 1 to December 31, 2026: 11.75% (Bank Rate 3.75% on June 30, 2026).
  • Debts late from January 1 to June 30, 2026: 11.75% (Bank Rate 3.75% on December 31, 2025).
  • Bank Rate is 4% since November 5, 2026. That does not change the rate on debts already late: it counts only if it is still the Bank Rate on December 31, 2026.

Every half-year since 2003 is in the reference rate table.

What GOV.UK says

GOV.UK: "this is 8% plus the Bank of England base rate for business to business transactions. You cannot claim statutory interest if there's a different rate of interest in a contract."

GOV.UK's own example uses a 0.5% base rate: "If your business were owed £1,000 and the Bank of England base rate were 0.5%: the annual statutory interest on this would be £85 (1,000 x 0.085 = £85)". It rounds the daily amount to 23p; this calculator keeps the exact figure and rounds only the total.

Please read

  • Not legal advice. The figures follow the official rules and rates quoted, read on the date shown; a court decides any dispute.
  • Business-to-business debts only: the UK Act, the Irish regulations, § 288(2) BGB and the EU directive do not cover money owed by consumers.
  • Statutory interest does not apply where your contract sets its own late-payment rate (UK: a "substantial remedy"); use your own terms instead.
  • No interest or compensation is worked out for an invoice paid late because of a genuine dispute.
  • US public-body rules (federal, Texas, Florida local government) apply only to payments by those bodies; between businesses in the US, only the rate in your agreed terms applies, within your state's limits.

Steps

  1. Find the due date. The last day of the agreed payment terms; with no agreed date, 30 days after the customer got the invoice or the goods or service, whichever is later (GOV.UK).
  2. Pick the reference rate. Interest starts the day after the due date. Use the Bank Rate on December 31 if that day is from January 1 to June 30, or on June 30 if it is from July 1 to December 31, and add 8%.
  3. Work out the interest. Debt x rate x days late / 365, simple interest. The rate does not change while the debt is unpaid.
  4. Add the fixed sum. £40 up to £999.99, £70 from £1,000 to £9,999.99, £100 from £10,000, once for each payment.

Questions

What is the UK statutory interest rate on late payments?

8% a year plus the Bank of England base rate on the reference date. For debts that became late from July 1 to December 31, 2026 it is 11.75%: 8% plus the 3.75% Bank Rate on June 30, 2026.

Which base rate applies to my invoice?

The Bank Rate on December 31 for debts that become late from January 1 to June 30, and the Bank Rate on June 30 for July 1 to December 31. It stays fixed until the debt is paid.

How do I work out daily statutory interest?

Multiply the debt by the yearly rate and divide by 365. At 11.75%, £1,000 earns £0.32 a day, £16.10 after 50 days.

How long can I claim statutory interest for?

Up to 6 years from the day after the payment terms end (Small Business Commissioner).

Can I claim interest on a disputed invoice?

No. The Small Business Commissioner says interest and compensation cannot be charged on an invoice paid late because of a dispute.

Do I include VAT in the amount?

Yes. The Small Business Commissioner says to use the invoice total including VAT.
