# UK statutory interest reference rates

Statutory interest means interest the law gives on a late business debt: in the UK, 8% a year plus the Bank Rate on the reference date. For debts that became late from July 1 to December 31, 2026 it is 11.75% (Bank Rate 3.75% on June 30, 2026); from January 1 to June 30, 2026 it was 11.75%.

- July to December 2026: 11.75%
- Half-years listed: 48

## The rule

GOV.UK users' guide: "The Bank of England base rate on 31 December will be the "reference rate" for 1 January to 30 June. The Bank of England base rate on 30 June will be the "reference rate" for 1 July to 31 December."

And: "add 8% to the "reference rate" that covers the six-month period in which your debt becomes late."

Bank Rate rose to 4% on November 5, 2026. Debts already late keep their rate; the first half-year that 4% can touch is January to June 2027, and only if it is still the Bank Rate on December 31, 2026 (the next decision is December 17, 2026). See [the 2027 rate page](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/late-payment-interest/rate-2027/).

## Questions

### What does statutory interest mean?

Interest the law gives a supplier on a late business payment without any clause in the contract: in the UK, 8% a year over the Bank of England base rate on the reference date.

### Where is the reference rate published?

Nowhere as one figure: it is the Bank of England Bank Rate in force on June 30 or December 31, which the Bank publishes in its Bank Rate history. This table reads it from there.
