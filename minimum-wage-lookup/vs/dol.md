# DOL State Minimum Wage Table vs an Address Lookup

The U.S. Department of Labor's state table (updated November 5, 2026) gives each state's basic rate and says that some local governments set higher rates it does not show; this lookup starts from that table and adds the city and county rates from each place's own page, by address and date.

## What DOL's table gives

- Every state's basic minimum wage and its employer-size or receipts tiers.
- Notes on yearly adjustments and scheduled increases (Michigan on January 1, 2027; Florida's formula from 2028).
- A separate table for tipped employees.

## What it does not show

- City and county rates (DOL says so on the page).
- Which rate applies at a street address or ZIP code.
- Rates on a past or future date.

## When DOL is the better choice

When you only need state-level rates, DOL's table is official and free.

> Not legal advice. The rate shown is the highest general minimum wage we hold for the place and date; industry, hotel, health care, youth, training and other special rates can differ. Every rate names the official page it was read from and the date we read it. Places and states whose pages we could not read are listed as not covered, never guessed. Employers covered by the federal Fair Labor Standards Act pay at least the federal $7.25; state laws differ on which employers they cover.
