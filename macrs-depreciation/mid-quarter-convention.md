# The mid-quarter convention and the 40% test

You must use the mid-quarter convention when more than 40% of the year's depreciable basis is placed in service in the last 3 months of the tax year. Placing a $50,000 forklift in service in November 2026 next to a $60,000 lathe from March makes 45.45% of the year's basis, so mid-quarter applies; exactly 40% stays half-year.

- Last 3 months share: 45.45%

## What is the 40% test?

> Use this convention if the mid-month convention does not apply and the total depreciable bases of MACRS property you placed in service during the last 3 months of the tax year (excluding nonresidential real property, residential rental property, any railroad grading or tunnel bore, property placed in service and disposed of in the same year, and property that is being depreciated under a method other than MACRS) are more than 40% of the total depreciable bases of all MACRS property you placed in service during the entire year.

## Example: a forklift bought in November 2026

Bonus elected out for 5- and 7-year property, so the convention shows in year 1.

| Forklift placed in service | Share in last 3 months | Convention | Year 1 total |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: |
| November 16, 2026 | 45.45% | Mid-quarter | $17,500.00 |
| September 15, 2026 | 0.00% | Half-year | $18,574.00 |

Moving the same forklift into September changes year 1 by $1,074.00, because mid-quarter cuts the first-year rate on the fourth-quarter asset and lifts it on the first-quarter one.

## Publication 946's own example

tax year 2025: $5,000.00 placed in service October to December / $10,000.00 for the year = 50.00%, more than 40%: mid-quarter for every asset in the test classes. Year-1 deduction $1,357.10: Section 179 $0.00, special allowance $0.00, MACRS $1,357.10.

## Steps

1. **List the year's additions.** Take every MACRS asset placed in service in the tax year, leaving out residential rental and nonresidential real property, property placed in service and disposed of in the same year, and property depreciated under a method other than MACRS.
2. **Reduce for Section 179 and personal use.** Use each asset's basis after the Section 179 deduction and without the personal-use part. Do not reduce it for the special depreciation allowance.
3. **Add up the last 3 months.** Total the bases of the assets placed in service in the last 3 months of the tax year (October to December for a calendar year).
4. **Divide.** Divide that total by the total for the whole year. More than 40% means mid-quarter for every asset in the test; 40% or less means half-year.

## Questions

### Is exactly 40% mid-quarter?

No. The rule is more than 40%. $40,000.00 placed in service October to December / $100,000.00 for the year = 40.00%, not more than 40%: half-year.

### Does bonus depreciation reduce the basis in the test?

For purposes of determining whether the mid-quarter convention applies, the depreciable basis of property you placed in service during the tax year reflects the reduction in basis for amounts expensed under section 179 and the part of the basis of property attributable to personal use. However, it does not reflect any reduction in basis for any special depreciation allowance.

### Does mid-quarter matter if I take 100% bonus?

Less in year 1, but the convention still sets the schedule for classes elected out of bonus, for states that do not follow federal bonus, and for the year you later sell the asset.

### Can Section 179 keep me out of mid-quarter?

It can: the test uses the basis after Section 179, so expensing part of a fourth-quarter asset lowers its share. Run the test with and without it.
