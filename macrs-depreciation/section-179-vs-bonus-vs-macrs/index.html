Section 179, bonus depreciation or regular MACRS?

For a $50,000 machine placed in service in March 2026, Section 179 and 100% bonus both deduct $50,000 in year 1, while regular MACRS deducts $7,145 in year 1 and the rest over 7 more years. The difference is in the limits: Section 179 cannot exceed your business income, so with $20,000 of income you expense $20,000 and bonus takes the remaining $30,000.

The total cost you can deduct each year after you apply the dollar limit is limited to the taxable income from the active conduct of any trade or business during the year. You can elect, for any class of property, not to deduct any special depreciation allowances for all property in such class placed in service during the tax year.

What about later years and a sale?

Taking the whole cost in year 1 leaves nothing to deduct in later years. A vehicle over 6,000 pounds is still listed property: if its business use later drops to 50% or less, excess depreciation is recaptured (Publication 946, chapter 5). The tool does not compute sales or recapture; work those with your preparer.

Not tax or legal advice. Many states do not follow federal bonus or Section 179; elect out for every class to get a starting point for those states.

Once you have chosen, book it with the year-end journal entry.

Questions

Is Section 179 or bonus depreciation better?

For one asset in 2026 both can deduct the whole cost in year 1. Section 179 is chosen asset by asset but is limited by business income and the dollar limit; bonus applies to a whole class unless you elect out and has no income limit.

Can I use both on one asset?

Yes. Section 179 comes off first and bonus applies to what is left: with $20,000 of Section 179 on a $50,000 machine, bonus deducts the other $30,000.

Why would I choose regular MACRS?

To spread the deduction when you expect higher income in later years, or as the starting point for a state that does not follow federal bonus or Section 179.
