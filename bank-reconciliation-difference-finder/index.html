Example: a made-up bakery's October bank statement and ledger

At October 31, 2026 the statement shows $12,711.13 and the books $12,915.98: a difference of $204.85. 11 lines match (1 as a split) and 5 lines are on one side only. Timing explains $99.85 (1 outstanding check and 1 deposit in transit); the other $105.00 (the books are higher) comes from 1 likely transposition and 1 bank line missing from the books. The running balances first disagree on October 5, 2026.

Is the difference a transposition?

Free, in your browser. Type the difference; add the amounts from the period (one per line) to see which of them could have produced it.

$90.00 is divisible by 9 ($90.00 / 9 = $10.00), so a transposition is possible: two adjacent digits 1 apart in the $100 and $10 places. Amounts that become it with one swap: $450.00 (vs $540.00).

How it works

  • Matching: the same amount to the cent within 7 days (0 to 60), or the same check number; splits only when exactly one set fits.
  • Errors before timing: swapped digits, slides, sign flips, wrong amounts and entries made twice are looked for first.
  • The break date: the first date the running balances disagree.

What it does not cover

It never posts or adjusts anything in your books, and a transposition hint is an arithmetic clue, not proof. A break before the files start needs an earlier month's export.

Read how it works in full

Matching

A bank line and a books line match when the amount is the same to the cent and the dates are no more than 7 days apart (you can set 0 to 60), or when they carry the same check or reference number up to 120 days apart. Closer dates, matching payee words and a shared reference win. Then one bank deposit is matched to two to four books entries that add up to it, or one books entry to several bank lines, but only when exactly one set fits. Leftover equal amounts up to 92 days apart are matched with a date flag.

Errors before timing

What is left is checked for errors first: two amounts that are the same digits with two neighbours swapped (the gap is always a multiple of 9), ten times apart (a slide), the same amount the other way round (the balance moves by twice it), a different amount on the same reference, a books entry that repeats a matched one, a line twice in the statement. Books lines left over are outstanding checks, payments or deposits in transit, aged; bank lines left over are missing from the books.

The break date

The difference that is not timing is followed date by date. It starts at the gap between the two opening balances and moves on each date the books miss or get wrong something the bank has. The first such date is when the running balances broke. If the opening balances already differ, the break is before the files start: export an earlier month and run it again.

Please read
  • Not accounting or tax advice. The tool compares two files and explains the difference; it never posts, adjusts or changes anything in your books.
  • A transposition, slide or sign-flip hint is an arithmetic clue, not proof: check each one against the bank statement before you correct anything.
  • Talk to your accountant before you post a reconciliation adjustment or change anything in a closed or filed period.
  • Files are processed in memory and not kept.
