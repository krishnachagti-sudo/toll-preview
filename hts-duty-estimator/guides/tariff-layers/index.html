US tariff layers, explained

A US duty is the HTS base rate plus every Chapter 99 layer for the goods' origin: on November 8, 2026 that means the Section 301 forced-labor duty on goods of 60 economies (17 at 10%, 38 at 12.5%, 5 topped up), the China lists (7.5% to 100%), Brazil (25%) and Section 232 on named products, while the IEEPA duties and the section 122 surcharge no longer apply.

The 60 economies

  • 10%: Argentina, Bangladesh, Cambodia, Canada, Ecuador, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, India, Indonesia, Jordan, Malaysia, Mexico, Pakistan, Sri Lanka, Trinidad and Tobago, United Kingdom.
  • 12.5%: Algeria, Angola, Australia, Bahamas, Bahrain, Brazil, Chile, China, Colombia, Costa Rica, Dominican Republic, Egypt, Guyana, Hong Kong, Iraq, Israel, Kazakhstan, Kuwait, Libya, Morocco, New Zealand, Nicaragua, Nigeria, Norway, Oman, Peru, Philippines, Qatar, Russia, Saudi Arabia, Singapore, South Africa, Thailand, Turkiye, United Arab Emirates, Uruguay, Venezuela, Vietnam.
  • Topped up to 10% with the general rate: European Union, Taiwan. To 12.5%: Japan, South Korea, Switzerland.

The Section 301 duties add to each other and to the base rate unless a note exempts the goods; goods covered by Section 232 are exempt from the 60-economy duty (U.S. note 52(f)).

Estimate, not a customs ruling: U.S. Customs and Border Protection decides the classification, value, origin and duty at entry, and the importer is responsible for them. This is not legal or customs advice. Rates are from the USITC 2026 HTS Revision 22 (effective 10/30/2026) and the Federal Register actions read on 2026-11-08. A layer we have not read is refused, never guessed: Section 232, the section 338 duties on Canada, Russia, safeguards and quotas are not computed, and antidumping and countervailing duties are never included.

Questions

Why does my duty rate have several parts?

The HTS line sets a base rate; Chapter 99 adds duties by origin or product (Section 301, Section 232), each under its own heading, and they are added together.

Did the Supreme Court strike down the tariffs?

Executive Order 14389 of February 20, 2026 ended the additional duties imposed under IEEPA; it states that Section 232 and Section 301 duties are not affected.
