# HS codes after de minimis

The $800 de minimis exemption is suspended indefinitely (courier and cargo from June 24, 2026, mail from July 24, 2026), so every parcel needs an HTS code, an origin and an entry: a $50 candle from China now pays $10.00 in duty plus a $2.77 informal-entry MPF.

- $50 candle, duty: $10.00
- Informal MPF: $2.77
- Formal MPF minimum: $34.58

## The rules that apply

- Informal entry is for shipments of $2,500 or less, except articles over $250 classified in Chapter 99, subchapters III and IV (19 CFR 143.21).
- MPF on a formal entry is 0.3464% of value, at least $34.58 and at most $670.86; an automated informal entry is $2.77 (fiscal year 2027, from October 1, 2026).
- Mail uses a new postal informal entry process (FR 2026-12669); its fees are not computed here.
- Your carrier or broker may add its own fees for clearing the parcel.

> Estimate, not a customs ruling: U.S. Customs and Border Protection decides the classification, value, origin and duty at entry, and the importer is responsible for them. This is not legal or customs advice. Rates are from the USITC 2026 HTS Revision 22 (effective 10/30/2026) and the Federal Register actions read on 2026-11-08. A layer we have not read is refused, never guessed: Section 232, the section 338 duties on Canada, Russia, safeguards and quotas are not computed, and antidumping and countervailing duties are never included.

## Questions

### Is there still a $800 duty-free limit for US imports?

No. CBP rules suspended the de minimis exemption indefinitely for all modes other than mail from June 24, 2026 and for mail from July 24, 2026.

### When must an entry be formal?

Above $2,500, and above $250 for articles classified in Chapter 99, subchapters III and IV (19 CFR 143.21), which now covers goods of the 60 economies in the Section 301 action.
