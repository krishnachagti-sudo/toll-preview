# Owner-operator per diem on Schedule C

Owner-operators deduct per diem as business meals on Schedule C line 24b, at 80% because interstate truck operators under DOT hours-of-service rules get the higher limit: in 2026, 240 days x $80 + 40 x $60 = $21,600.00, x 80% = $17,280.00.

- 2026 deduction: $17,280.00
- Full per diem: $21,600.00
- Full day / partial day: $80 / $60

## The numbers your preparer asks for

- Tax software that asks for your meal expenses and applies the limit itself (for example by ticking "subject to DOT hours of service"): enter the full per diem, $21,600.00.
- A form line that asks for the deductible amount (Schedule C line 24b): enter $17,280.00. Never take 80% of a figure that already had it applied.

## The rest requirement

Per diem is for days you are away from your tax home overnight, or long enough to need sleep or rest. Publication 463 gives the example of a truck driver who leaves the terminal and returns the same day with an hour off to eat: that driver is not traveling away from home. A day away with relief from duty long enough to sleep counts even if it is not dusk to dawn.

## Two numbers, not one

Spreadsheets that use $64 a day ($80 x 80%) invite a double cut: tax software that asks about hours of service takes 80% again, and $17,280.00 becomes $13,824.00. Give software the full per diem and Schedule C line 24b the deductible amount.

## Keep the record

Carriers need keep duty-status records and ELD back-ups only 6 months (49 CFR 395.8(k) and 395.22(i)), so export your logs every month or quarter. Keep them with load sheets for at least 3 years after filing, 6 if income was under-reported by more than 25% ([IRS: How long should I keep records?](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/how-long-should-i-keep-records)).

## Steps

1. **Count the nights away.** Log each day you were away from your tax home long enough to need sleep or rest. A day you left or got back is a departure or return day; a day away from 12:01 a.m. to midnight is a full day.
2. **Price each day.** Use the IRS transportation-industry rate in force that day: $80 in the continental US and $86 outside it since October 1, 2024, and 3/4 of it on departure and return days.
3. **Apply the limit once.** Meals during DOT hours-of-service periods are 80% deductible. Take 80% of the full per diem, or let tax software take it, never both.
4. **Report it.** The deductible amount goes on Schedule C line 24b. Incidental-expenses-only days ($5) go on line 24a. Keep the log and your ELD records.

## Questions

### Where do owner-operators claim per diem?

On Schedule C line 24b (deductible meals), at 80% for meals during DOT hours-of-service duty. The Schedule C instructions name interstate truck operators under DOT regulations.

### Do I need to sleep away from home to claim per diem?

Yes. Publication 463 says you are traveling away from home if your duties keep you away substantially longer than an ordinary day's work and you need sleep or rest; napping in your car does not satisfy the rest requirement.

### Can I switch between the special rate and local rates?

Not within a year. Publication 463 says that if you use the special rate for any trip, you must use it for all trips that year; Rev. Proc. 2019-48 sec. 4.06(2) lets you switch only from January 1.
