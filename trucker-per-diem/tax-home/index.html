The tax home rule for truck drivers

You can claim per diem only for days away from your tax home. If you have no main place of business and no place you regularly live, Publication 463 calls you an itinerant whose tax home is wherever you work, so the deduction is $0. It uses 3 factors for drivers without a main place of business; meeting only 1 makes you an itinerant.

Factors in Publication 463
3
At stake for 240 + 40 days in 2026
$17,280.00

What is a tax home?

Publication 463: "Generally, your tax home is your regular place of business or post of duty, regardless of where you maintain your family home." If you have no regular or main place of business because of the nature of your work, your tax home may be the place where you regularly live. Per diem is for days you are traveling away from that place long enough to need sleep or rest.

The three factors

For a driver without a regular or main place of business, Publication 463 uses three factors:

  • You perform part of your business in the area of your main home and use that home for lodging while doing business there.
  • You have living expenses at your main home that you duplicate because your business requires you to be away from it.
  • You have not abandoned the area where your historical place of lodging and your claimed main home are; or family members live at the main home; or you often use it for lodging.

All three: your tax home is the home where you regularly live. Two: you may have a tax home, depending on all the facts and circumstances. One: you are an itinerant, your tax home is wherever you work, and you cannot deduct travel expenses.

What is at stake

For a driver with 240 full days and 40 departure or return days in 2026, the per diem deduction is $17,280.00 at 80%. Without a tax home it is $0. The worksheet assumes you have one and says so; whether you do is for you and your preparer. More on the deduction itself: owner-operator per diem on Schedule C.

Evidence that helps

  • A lease, mortgage or utility bills at the home you claim, showing costs you keep paying while on the road.
  • Days you spend and work from that area: loads that start or end near home, time at home between runs.
  • A days-away log that shows when you left home and came back.

The log is the core record for per diem: see what to write in a days-away log.

Questions

Can I claim per diem if I live in my truck?

Not if you have no regular place of business and no place where you regularly live. Publication 463: you are "considered an itinerant (a transient) and your tax home is wherever you work. As an itinerant, you can't claim a travel expense deduction because you are never considered to be traveling away from home."

What are the three tax home factors?

Publication 463: you do part of your business near your main home and lodge there while doing it; you have living costs at that home that you duplicate because work takes you away; and you have not abandoned the area, have family living at the home, or often lodge there.

Is my home terminal my tax home?

It can be. Publication 463's example: a driver who lives in Tucson but whose employer's terminal is in Phoenix has Phoenix as his tax home, and cannot deduct meals or lodging in Phoenix or the trip home to Tucson.
