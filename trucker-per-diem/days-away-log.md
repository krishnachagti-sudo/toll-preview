# What to write in a days-away log

A per diem log needs one line per day away: the date, where you stopped to sleep or rest, whether you left or got back that day, and the load or trip it was for. Two November 2026 trips logged this way, 12 days and 5 days with one carrier-paid day, come to $1,227.00 of per diem and a $981.60 deduction.

- Per diem, two trips: $1,227.00
- Days logged: 17
- Left out (carrier paid): 1

## What does the IRS want the log to show?

Publication 463: "If you use the standard meal allowance, you must still keep records to prove the time, place, and business purpose of your travel." For a driver, that is when you were away, where you stopped to rest, and which load it was for. You do not need meal receipts for the per diem amount.

## How long to keep it

Keep the log, the ELD exports and load sheets for at least 3 years after you file, 6 if income was under-reported by more than 25% ([IRS: How long should I keep records?](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/how-long-should-i-keep-records)).

## Please read

- Figures computed from the IRS notices named on each row, Rev. Proc. 2019-48 and Publication 463; not tax advice. Whether you are away from your tax home, in the transportation industry and subject to DOT hours of service is your call or your preparer's.
- Partial days use 3/4 of the daily rate, as Rev. Proc. 2019-48 sec. 6.04 requires for the self-employed per diem method; Publication 463 also describes other methods, so ask your preparer if you use one.
- Enter the full per diem in tax software that applies the 80% limit itself; enter the deductible figure only where the form asks for the deductible amount (Schedule C line 24b).
- Company (W-2) drivers cannot deduct unreimbursed meals under current law; days your carrier or employer reimbursed cannot also be deducted.
- This worksheet counts what you log; keep your ELD records, load sheets and receipts as evidence for at least 3 years (6 if income is under-reported by over 25%).

## Steps

1. **Date.** One line per calendar day away, so full days and departure or return days can be counted.
2. **Where you rested.** The city or truck stop where you stopped to sleep or rest, and whether it was in the lower 48 or outside (Canada, Alaska, Hawaii).
3. **Left or got back.** Mark the day you left your tax home and the day you returned: those are the 3/4 days.
4. **Business purpose.** The load, trip or bill of lading number that took you there.
5. **Paid days.** Mark any day a carrier or customer paid you per diem: it cannot also be deducted.

## Questions

### Do I need receipts for trucker per diem?

Not for meals. Publication 463: with the standard meal allowance you must still keep records to prove the time, place and business purpose of your travel. The log is that record.

### Can my ELD logs be my per diem log?

They show time and place, which helps. Export them: carriers have to keep duty-status records and ELD back-ups only 6 months (49 CFR 395.8(k) and 395.22(i)), and the IRS can ask for 3 years or more. Add the load numbers and mark the days you left and got home.

### What if a carrier paid per diem for a day?

Leave it out. Rev. Proc. 2019-48 sec. 6.06: a day that was reimbursed cannot also be deducted. The worksheet takes a "reimbursed" day and drops it.
