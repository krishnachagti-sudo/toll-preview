# Accountable plan mileage reimbursement

Under an accountable plan, mileage paid at or below the IRS rate is not wages. For 10,000 business miles in 2026, 5,000 before July 1 and 5,000 after, up to **$7,425.00** stays out of W-2 wages (5,000 x $0.725 + 5,000 x $0.76), if the employee accounts for each trip and returns any excess (IRS Publication 463).

## What are the three accountable plan rules?

Pub 463 says the reimbursement or allowance arrangement must include all of these:

- Business connection: the expenses were paid or incurred while performing services as an employee.
- Adequate accounting: the employee accounts to the employer for the expenses within a reasonable period of time.
- Return of excess: the employee returns any excess reimbursement or allowance within a reasonable period of time.

For mileage, adequate accounting means a record made at or near the time, with the time, place and business purpose of each trip. The elements are on [IRS mileage log requirements](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/mileage-reimbursement/irs-mileage-log-requirements/).

## What counts as a reasonable period of time?

It depends on the facts, but Pub 463 treats these as reasonable in every case:

Safe-harbor periods from Pub 463.

| Action | Within |
| --- | --- |
| Employee receives an advance | 30 days of the expense |
| Employee accounts for the expense | 60 days after it was paid or incurred |
| Employee returns any excess | 120 days after the expense was paid or incurred |
| Employee responds to a periodic statement (at least quarterly) | 120 days of the statement |

## What happens to an allowance above the IRS rate?

Pub 463: "If your allowance is more than the federal rate, your employer must include the allowance amount up to the federal rate under code L in box 12 of your Form W-2. This amount isn’t taxable. However, the excess allowance will be included in box 1 of your Form W-2."

Worked by the calculator: three trips paid at 80 cents a mile come to $696.00; at the 2026 IRS rates for their dates they are $657.00. So $657.00 goes under code L in box 12 and $39.00 in box 1. The trips are on [the July 2026 true-up page](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/mileage-reimbursement/mileage-true-up-july-2026/).

## What if the plan does not meet the rules?

Pub 463: reimbursements that fail any of the three rules are generally treated as paid under a nonaccountable plan, and the employer reports those as pay. An employee cannot turn a nonaccountable plan into an accountable one by accounting and returning the excess on their own; the employer decides which kind of plan it runs.

## Can employees deduct mileage their employer did not reimburse?

Mostly not. Notice 2026-10 says the business standard mileage rate "cannot be used to claim an itemized deduction for unreimbursed employee travel expenses, except for certain educator expenses". It names Armed Forces reservists, fee-basis state or local government officials and certain performing artists as able to deduct them as an adjustment to income instead.

## Questions

### What makes a mileage reimbursement plan accountable?

Pub 463 lists three rules: the expenses have a business connection, the employee adequately accounts for them within a reasonable period of time, and the employee returns any excess within a reasonable period of time.

### What is a reasonable period of time?

Pub 463 treats these as reasonable in any case: an advance within 30 days of the expense, accounting within 60 days after it, returning any excess within 120 days after it, or complying within 120 days of a periodic (at least quarterly) statement.

### Is mileage reimbursement taxable to the employee?

Not when it is paid under an accountable plan at or below the federal rate: Pub 463 says the allowance won't be included in box 1 of the employee's Form W-2. Amounts above the federal rate are wages in box 1.

### Can employees deduct unreimbursed mileage in 2026?

Most cannot. Notice 2026-10 says the business rate cannot be used to claim an itemized deduction for unreimbursed employee travel expenses, except for certain educators; Armed Forces reservists, fee-basis state or local government officials and certain performing artists deduct them as an adjustment to income.
