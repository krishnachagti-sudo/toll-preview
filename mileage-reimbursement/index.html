A heading line, then one trip a line. It finds columns such as Date, Miles, Employee, Vehicle, Destination, Business purpose, Purpose, Parking, Tolls, Paid amount, Paid date and Recorded on. Download a blank template.

What the file needs, and app exports

A heading line and one trip a line, separated by commas, tabs or semicolons, or the first sheet of an Excel file. Two columns are required: one headed Date (or Trip date, Day, Travel date, Start date) and one headed Miles (or Mileage, Distance, Business miles, Miles driven, Total miles, Mi). Dates may be written 2026-07-14, 7/14/2026 or 7/14/26. Other columns are read when their heading is one of the names above; the page lists the rest as not used.

From MileIQ, Gridwise, Stride or another mileage app, open its export here. If the preview says it needs a date column and a miles column, rename those two headings to Date and Miles, and the place and reason columns to Destination and Business purpose, then open it here again.

The rules

Two dates, not one: the trip and the payment.

Rates are from the IRS standard mileage rates table and Announcement 2026-11 (and GSA for federal travel), cited on each line. Sources read October 31, 2026.

What it does not cover

Which trips are business, whether a log is adequate, Schedule A limits, actual expenses and 2027 rates before the IRS sets them.

Read the full notes
  • Rates are from the IRS standard mileage rates table and Announcement 2026-11 (and GSA for federal travel), cited on each line. The standard rate is optional: actual expenses may be used instead, and you cannot use it with five or more vehicles in business use at the same time, or for a car you leased and once used actual expenses on (Pub 463).
  • This tool computes amounts from the trips you enter. It does not decide which trips are business, medical or charity, or whether a log is adequate; its completeness check lists objective gaps only. A log is only as good as the records behind it: the IRS expects records kept at or near the time, and treats a weekly log as timely (Pub 463).
  • Medical and charity amounts are inputs to Schedule A; deduction limits are not applied. Rates for 2027 are added only once the IRS announces them.
  • Not tax or legal advice. Sources, read October 31, 2026: IRS standard mileage rates (updated July 28, 2026), Announcement 2026-11, Notice 2026-10, Publication 463 (2025), GSA POV rates (updated October 23, 2026).

The trip date

Each mile takes the rate of the day it was driven: 72.5 cents to June 30, 2026, 76 cents from July 1.

Read the full rule

The IRS sets its optional standard mileage rates by period. For 2026: 72.5 cents a business mile, 20.5 cents medical or moving and 14 cents charity from January 1 to June 30 (Notice 2026-10, IR-2025-128); 76, 23.5 and 14 cents from July 1 to December 31 (Announcement 2026-11, IR-2026-29). Each mile takes the rate of the day it was driven, so a trip that runs over July 1 is entered as two legs. The charity rate is fixed at 14 cents by law (IRC 170(i)).

The date an allowance is paid

An allowance paid before July 1, 2026 stays at the old rate, even for July miles.

Read the full rule

Announcement 2026-11 applies the new rates to employer mileage allowances only when they are paid on or after July 1, 2026 for miles driven on or after July 1. A June 28 trip reimbursed July 5 stays at 72.5 cents; so does an advance paid in June for July miles. The 2011 and 2022 mid-year changes had the same rule, and it is applied to them too. Give a paid date and the line says which date set its rate.

Above the federal rate

Up to the federal rate goes in W-2 box 12 code L; the excess is wages in box 1.

Read the full rule

Pub 463: when an allowance is more than the federal rate, the employer reports the amount up to the federal rate in W-2 box 12 with code L (not taxable) and the excess in box 1 as wages. The trip file shows both per employee. Most W-2 employees cannot deduct unreimbursed mileage; Form 2106 is only for Armed Forces reservists, qualified performing artists, fee-basis state or local officials and impairment-related work expenses.

Does a log I rebuild later count?

The IRS asks for records made at or near the time; a log kept weekly counts as timely.

Read the full answer

The IRS asks for records made at or near the time. Publication 463 says you should record each business use "at or near the time" and that "A timely kept record has more value than a statement prepared later when there is generally a lack of accurate recall." A log kept weekly counts as timely. If your records are incomplete, Pub 463 says you must prove each missing element with your own detailed written or oral statement plus other supporting evidence, such as receipts or paid bills.

The PDF shows the trips you entered, priced by date, and its completeness check lists gaps it can see. It cannot make a log rebuilt from memory into a timely record, and it does not say whether the IRS will accept it. If your file has a Recorded on column, the check flags any trip written down more than 7 days after it happened.
