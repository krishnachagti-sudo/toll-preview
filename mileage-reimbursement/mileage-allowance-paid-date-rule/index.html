Paid date or trip date: which IRS mileage rate applies

For a mileage deduction, use the rate for the date the miles were driven. For an employer mileage allowance, Announcement 2026-11 applies the 76 cent rate only when the miles were driven on or after July 1, 2026 and the allowance was also paid on or after July 1; anything else keeps 72.5 cents.

What does Announcement 2026-11 say?

New rate: "The revised standard mileage rates set forth in this announcement apply to deductible transportation expenses paid or incurred for business, medical, or moving expense purposes on or after July 1, 2026, and to mileage allowances that are paid both (1) to an employee on or after July 1, 2026, and (2) for transportation expenses paid or incurred by the employee on or after July 1, 2026."

Old rate: "The standard mileage rates set forth in Notice 2026-10 continue to apply to deductible transportation expenses paid or incurred for business, medical, or moving expense purposes before July 1, 2026, and to mileage allowances paid (1) to an employee before July 1, 2026, or (2) with respect to transportation expenses paid or incurred by the employee before July 1, 2026."

Which rate applies in each case?

The only way to the 76 cent rate for an allowance is both dates on or after July 1. An advance paid in June for July driving stays at 72.5 cents.

What does it mean for payroll?

If allowances for July trips were paid at the old rate after July 1, the employee is owed the difference. The July 2026 true-up guide works it out per employee from a trip file. Whether the allowance is taxable is a separate question, answered in accountable plan mileage reimbursement.

Questions

Do I use the date paid or the date driven for the mileage rate?

For a deduction, the date driven. For an employer mileage allowance in 2026, the 76 cent rate needs both: miles driven on or after July 1, 2026 and the allowance paid on or after July 1, 2026. Otherwise it is 72.5 cents.

An allowance was paid on June 30 for a July 2 trip. Which rate?

72.5 cents: under Announcement 2026-11 an allowance paid before July 1, 2026 keeps the earlier rate. 100 miles is $72.50.

Has the IRS used this rule before?

Yes. The IRS table shows the same allowance rule with each mid-year change: July 1, 2011 (Announcement 2011-40), July 1, 2022 (Announcement 2022-13), July 1, 2026 (Announcement 2026-11).
