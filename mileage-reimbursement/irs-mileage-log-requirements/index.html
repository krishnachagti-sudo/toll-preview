IRS mileage log requirements

For each business use of a car, record the miles, the date, the business destination and the business purpose, plus the total miles for the year and the date you started using the car for business, at or near the time (IRS Publication 463, Table 5-1); a log kept weekly counts as timely.

What each entry needs

Pub 463, Table 5-1, for car expenses.
ElementWhat Pub 463 says
AmountFor car expenses, the cost of the car and any improvements, the date you started using it for business, the mileage for each business use, and the total miles for the year.
TimeFor car expenses, the date of the use of the car.
PlaceYour business destination.
Business purposeBusiness purpose for the expense.
  • At or near the time. You should record the elements of an expense or of a business use at or near the time of the expense or use and support it with sufficient documentary evidence.
  • Weekly is timely. If you maintain a log on a weekly basis that accounts for use during the week, the log is considered a timely kept record.
  • No estimates. You can’t deduct amounts that you approximate or estimate.
  • Parking and tolls. In addition to using the standard mileage rate, you can deduct any business-related parking fees and tolls.

  • A business trip with no destination or no business purpose.
  • An entry recorded more than 7 days after the trip, when the file has a recorded-on column.
  • Odometer readings that do not match the miles, run backwards, or overlap the trip before.
  • The same date, miles and destination twice.
  • A trip between the home and regular workplace you name: commuting.
  • No odometer readings for the year, so no total miles; no business start date; five or more vehicles in business use at the same time.

Questions

What does an IRS mileage log need?

For each business use: the miles, the date, the business destination and the business purpose; plus the total miles for the year and the date you started using the car for business, recorded at or near the time (Pub 463, Table 5-1).

Does a log have to be written the same day?

No. Pub 463 says: "If you maintain a log on a weekly basis that accounts for use during the week, the log is considered a timely kept record."

Does a log I rebuild later count?

Pub 463 asks for records made at or near the time: "A timely kept record has more value than a statement prepared later when there is generally a lack of accurate recall." If your records are incomplete, it says you must prove each missing element with your own detailed statement and other supporting evidence. A log made in the calculator shows the trips you entered and lists the gaps it can see; it does not make a rebuilt log timely.

Can I log only part of the year?

Pub 463 allows sampling: "You can keep an adequate record for parts of a tax year and use that record to prove the amount of business or investment use for the entire year." You must show by other evidence that the sampled periods are representative of the year.

Are commuting miles deductible?

No. Pub 463: "You can’t deduct commuting expenses no matter how far your home is from your regular place of work."
