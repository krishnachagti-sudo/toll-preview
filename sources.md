# Sources and how we check the answers

Every answer comes from a named official source and a stated data date, with no AI in between. Data on this page was read between 2026-10-30 and 2026-11-08; each tool's source, edition and checks are below.

## How does every answer get made?

- The tools are deterministic: the same input gives the same answer, every time. Nothing in an answer is generated or estimated by a model.
- The data is read from an official source by a person, stored with its edition and the day it was read, and changes only when the data is updated.
- An answer names the data date or the source it used, and each tool reports a data version.
- A date, year or place the data does not cover is refused with the reason, not guessed.

[The data updates page](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/updates/) lists each change to the data and rules, with its date.

## How do we check the answers?

These are the checks in each tool's test suite. Only what a test asserts is listed. Where a tool has no official worked example to compare with, the list says what is checked instead.

**1099 threshold tracker**

- Every federal line for 2025 and 2026 is compared with the IRS instructions, with the revision and source recorded; penalties, the CF/SF states, California's lines and the IRIS CSV columns are checked the same way.
- The 2027 projection is checked against the arithmetic in 26 U.S.C. 6041(h) (rounded to the nearest $100) and marked provisional.
- A payee is in at exactly the line and out a cent below it, for every box and year.

**Business days**

- Easter Sunday is compared with published dates for nine years from 1971 to 2100.
- England and Wales holidays are compared with the official lists for 2020 to 2025 and 2027, and the 2026 list is checked in full.
- United States holidays are checked in full for 2026, and for the years where rules changed: Juneteenth from 2021, Martin Luther King Jr. Day from 1986, Veterans Day in 1971 to 1977, New Year's Day on a Saturday.
- India is compared with the DoPT memoranda for 2025, 2026 and 2027, name by name, and the weekday printed in each memorandum must agree with the calendar. The Bakrid move to 28 May 2026 is checked.
- Counting days and adding days must agree with each other, forwards and backwards.

**Natural-language date resolver**

- Calendar days, weeks, months, quarters and years are checked phrase by phrase from a fixed reference date: 90 days from Monday, November 2, 2026 is Sunday, January 31, 2027.
- Month ends are clamped and leap years kept: 1 month after January 31, 2027 is February 28, and 1 year after February 29, 2028 is February 28, 2029.
- "Next Friday", "last Tuesday" and dates with no year are checked on both readings, and the second reading is given only when it differs.
- Business days must equal the Business days tool's own add for US federal holidays; England and Wales, India, weekends only and your own days off are checked too.
- Rolling (following, preceding, modified following), ISO weeks and the refusal of times, unknown words and out-of-range dates are checked.

**Delivery date and holiday order-by calculator**

- Every USPS 2026 send-by date and weekday is compared with the USPS page as recorded, and the postal-holiday rule must give exactly the closures USPS lists.
- USPS delivery times are compared with the USPS services page.
- UPS and FedEx tables are checked to be absent, with the reason recorded, so the tool never invents them.

**Fix a CSV export (leading zeros, dates, encoding) with a saved recipe**

- The Windows-1252 table in the engine equals the WHATWG index dated 2024-09-18, and the quoted facts are dated 2026-11-08.
- Decoding, encoding with and without a BOM, garbled-accent repair, RFC 4180 parsing and writing, and delimiter detection are each checked.
- What each date proves about its order, the 2029 rule for two-digit years, mixed and unproven date columns, ZIP codes, amounts, code widths and long IDs are checked.
- A recipe applied to next month's file reports drift, and the same file and recipe always give the same bytes.
- Limits and refusals are checked, and the worked examples equal what run() returns.

**FMLA eligibility checker**

- The thresholds (12 months or 52 weeks, 1,250 hours, 50 employees within 75 miles, 7 years, 20 workweeks, 5 business days) and the quoted sections of Part 825 are pinned to eCFR and dol.gov as read on 2026-11-08.
- The 12-month test is checked on the anniversary and the day before, a leap-day hire, seasonal periods in weeks, 51 and 52 weeks, and breaks of seven years kept by USERRA, an agreement or the employer.
- The hours window, logs, USERRA credit, missing records and flight crew are checked, and so are 75 and 75.1 miles, staff abroad and the 300 employees of FAB 2023-1 example 5.
- The notice due date skips weekends, Veterans Day, Thanksgiving and closed days; the rolling balance reproduces the 825.200(c) example; calendar, fixed, forward and military caregiver years and the ledger CSV are checked.

**Exchange rate on a date**

- The ECB file parser and the rate arithmetic are tested on a sample file: units per euro, cross rates, the 7-day lookback to the day, rounding that is exact on the decimal value.
- When the real downloaded files are present, the worked examples and the currency list are compared with them. Treasury and HMRC values read on 2026-11-02 are pinned against the real files.
- IRS yearly averages are tested against the stored IRS table, including the cells the IRS prints irregularly, which are refused rather than guessed.

**GPSR listing checker**

- The quoted rule texts, the Article 2(2) exclusions and the read date 2026-11-08 are pinned to the EUR-Lex and Commission texts as read.
- The 27 Member States carry the official EU languages europa.eu lists, and the channel field names are pinned to each channel's page.
- CSV reading (commas, semicolons, tabs, quotes, line breaks, BOM), header matching and Shopify variant rows are each checked.
- Each Article 19 item is checked on its own: manufacturer, responsible person by country, picture, identifier, GTIN check digits, warnings and their language per market, exclusions and defaults.
- The responsible person operation is checked for EU, non-EU, UK, EEA and Northern Ireland cases, the worked examples equal run(), and formula-like cells are written as text.

**Hours calculator and timesheet**

- The federal rules held are compared with the text read from Cornell LII and dol.gov on 2026-11-07: 29 CFR 785.48, 785.18, 785.19, 778.104 and 778.105, and DOL Fact Sheets #21 and #23.
- A worked week is reproduced: 46 hours with 6 of overtime, and $18.50 x 40 + $27.75 x 6 = $906.50.
- A biweekly period of 30 and 50 hours gives 10 hours of overtime, never averaged (29 CFR 778.104), and moving the workweek start moves the overtime.
- Daylight-saving nights in America/New\_York are checked: 10 PM to 6 AM is 9 hours on October 31, 2026 and 7 hours on March 13, 2027.
- Rounding to 5, 6 and 15 minutes, nearest and in up and out down, is checked punch by punch, and the same timesheet input gives the same PDF bytes.

**IFTA quarterly return calculator**

- Each of the four quarters on file is final, from the Oregon DOT copy with the New York State Tax cross-check. Florida for the 1st quarter of 2026 is held back where the copies disagree.
- A two-state return is worked out by hand in the test and compared with the engine, with miles-per-gallon rounding and surcharges checked.
- Due dates for 2026 are compared with the Oregon DOT calendar, and the late-interest example from the Idaho State Tax Commission ($100 over 3 months late is $1.25) is reproduced.

**Image to size**

## Questions

### Where does the data behind an answer come from?

From a named official source: a government agency, a central bank or a standards body. This page lists each source with its edition and the day it was read.

### How do I know an answer is current?

Each tool has a data version that names the data it used, and an answer that depends on a source names it. The data updates page lists when data went in or a rule changed.

### Is there any AI in the answers?

No. The same input gives the same answer, worked out by fixed rules from the data above.

### What if a source is not available for a date or a year?

The tool refuses and says why, and the call costs nothing. It does not guess.
