The plate costs $3.05. On a $14.00 menu price that is 21.8%; at a 30% target the price is $3.05 / 0.30 = $10.17.

Why one price list beats a cost per dish

Chicken breast here is one line on a price list that every recipe reads. When the case price changes, the menu re-cost prices every dish that uses it at once, and lists the dishes that now miss your target.

Costs are only as current as the prices entered and only as true as the yields. A target food cost % is your choice; ranges such as 28% to 35% are rules of thumb, not a standard.

Steps

  1. Price the packs. For each item write the pack size, unit and price you pay, and the yield: the share left after trimming and cooking.
  2. Cost per usable unit. Pack price / pack size / yield gives the cost of a usable pound, ounce or cup.
  3. Price each component. Multiply each component's portion by its usable cost, converting units exactly (16 oz to the pound, 3 teaspoons to the tablespoon).
  4. Add up the plate. The sum is the plate cost. Divide by the menu price for the food cost %, or by your target to get a price.

Questions

How do I calculate plate cost?

Price every component on the plate at its usable cost and add them up. Here the chicken, potatoes, green beans, butter, cream and oil come to $3.05.

Why does yield matter?

Trim and cooking loss raise the cost of what reaches the plate: chicken breast at $3.29 a pound with a 92% yield costs $3.58 per usable pound.
