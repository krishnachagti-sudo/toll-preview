Recipe and plate costing

Recipe cost calculator

Keep one ingredient price list: pack size, pack price and yield. Every recipe and sub-recipe reads it, so when butter or salmon goes up you change one price and see every plate cost, food cost % and the menu price that meets your target, with the dishes that now miss it listed first.

Single calculations on this page are free. A menu re-cost is $0.01 a recipe ($0.05 at least), and never more than $2.99 in a month.

Your price list and recipes

Paste from a spreadsheet (tabs or commas) or type. The first row names the columns.

Columns: ingredient, pack size, pack unit, pack price. Optional: yield % (usable after trim), previous price (to see what moved), grams per cup or a USDA weight (to use a by-weight item by the cup).

One row per line: recipe, portions, menu price, ingredient, qty, unit. Leave the recipe columns blank to carry on the one above. A sauce or dough is a recipe too: use it by the portion, or say what a batch makes (2 qt) and use it by the cup or ounce.

Download the sample workbook (free): Prices, Lines and Menu sheets with live formulas. Opens in Excel, or in Google Sheets with File, Import.

How the numbers are worked out

Cost per usable unit

Pack price / pack size gives the as-purchased cost per unit. Divide by the yield (the share left after trimming and cooking loss) for the edible portion cost: salmon at $11.00 a pound with a 64% yield costs $17.19 per usable pound. Yield % is usable weight / purchased weight x 100.

Recipe, portion, plate

Each line's quantity is converted to the pack's unit and priced at the edible cost. A sub-recipe is costed first, then used by the portion or by volume or weight of its batch. The batch cost / portions is the cost per portion; food cost % is that / menu price x 100, and the price at a target is cost / target.

Exact units

Every factor is exact as NIST Handbook 44 (2026) defines it: 1 lb = 453.59237 g, 1 gallon = 231 cubic inches = 128 fl oz, 1 cup = 8 fl oz, 1 tablespoon = 1/2 fl oz, 1 teaspoon = 1/3 tablespoon. Cups to grams needs a weight per cup: yours, or USDA's household weight for that food.

  • Costs are only as current as the prices you enter, and only as true as your yields. Weigh a trim test for the yield of your own cuts.
  • USDA cup weights are averages for the food named; packing, sifting and brand change them. Weigh your own for the items that matter most.
  • A target food cost % is your choice. Figures like 28% to 35% are rules of thumb, not a standard.
  • Sources, read November 8, 2026: NIST Handbook 44 (2026), Appendix C; USDA FoodData Central, SR Legacy household weights; yield and edible portion cost as taught in Recipe and Menu Costing (Egan, LibreTexts, CC BY 4.0).
