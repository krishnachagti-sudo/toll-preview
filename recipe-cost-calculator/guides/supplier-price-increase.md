# A supplier raised prices: what to reprice

Butter up 21.1%, cream up 7.3% and salmon up 20.2% move pan-seared salmon from 27.1% to 32.1% food cost; 2 of 4 dishes now miss a 30% target and 2 moved more than 1 point.

- Salmon plate now: 32.1%
- Over target: 2 of 4
- Salmon price at 30%: $20.30

Butter reaches three dishes through two sauces and the shortbread: no dish lists butter directly except the cookies, which is why a price list that sub-recipes read beats a cost typed into each recipe.

## Three ways to fix a dish

- Price: move it to the price at target (salmon: $20.30, $1.30 more than today).
- Portion: the same price with a smaller portion of the item that rose. Re-cost with the new amount to see the food cost.
- Swap: a cheaper cut or product. Put it on the price list and re-cost.

> Costs are only as current as the prices entered and only as true as the yields. A target food cost % is your choice; ranges such as 28% to 35% are rules of thumb, not a standard.

## Steps

1. **Update one price list.** Put the new pack price beside the old one (a "previous price" column).
2. **Re-cost everything.** Every recipe and sub-recipe that uses the item re-costs; nothing is re-typed.
3. **Rank the damage.** Sort by points moved and by how far each dish is over target.
4. **Choose a fix per dish.** Raise the price to the price at target, trim the portion, or swap the ingredient.
