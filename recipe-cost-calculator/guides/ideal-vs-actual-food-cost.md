# Actual against ideal food cost

Actual food cost is opening inventory + purchases - closing inventory over food sales: $8,400.00 + $21,600.00 - $7,900.00 = $22,100.00, 38.1% of $58,000.00, 8.1 points ($4,700.00) above the 30% the recipes say.

## Where the gap comes from

- Recipe costs on old prices: re-cost from the latest invoices first, or the ideal is wrong, not the kitchen.
- Trim and yields not in the costing: a 64% yield costed as 100% understates salmon by more than half.
- Over-portioning: a 7 oz portion served as 8 oz is 14% more of that item.
- Waste and spoilage: what goes in the bin is bought but never sold.
- Comps, staff meals and theft: food that leaves without a sale.
- The count itself: a stock count valued at old prices, or missed storage, moves the actual.

Start with the first: a [menu re-cost](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/recipe-cost-calculator/) on current prices gives an ideal you can trust, and the [yield guide](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/recipe-cost-calculator/guides/edible-portion-cost/) covers the second.

> Costs are only as current as the prices entered and only as true as the yields. A target food cost % is your choice; ranges such as 28% to 35% are rules of thumb, not a standard.

## Questions

### How do I calculate actual food cost?

Opening inventory + purchases - closing inventory = cost of food sold; divide by food sales for the percentage.

### What is ideal food cost?

The food cost your recipe costs say you should have: each dish's plate cost times the number sold, over food sales.
