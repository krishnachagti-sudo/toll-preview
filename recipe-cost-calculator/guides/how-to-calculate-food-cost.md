# How to calculate food cost

Food cost % = ingredient cost / menu price x 100: the lemon chicken plate's lines add up to $3.79, which on its $12.50 menu price is 30.3%; at a 30% target it would sell for $12.63.

- Plate cost: $3.79
- Food cost: 30.3%

## The formulas

```
Yield % = usable weight / purchased weight x 100
Edible cost = purchased cost / yield
Line cost = amount x edible cost per unit
Plate cost = sum of line costs / portions
Food cost % = plate cost / menu price x 100
Price at target = plate cost / target
```

## Steps

1. **Cost per unit as purchased.** Pack price / pack size: a 40 lb case of chicken thigh at $139.60 is $3.49 a pound.
2. **Edible cost.** Divide by the yield: at 90% usable, $3.49 / 0.90 = $3.88 per usable pound.
3. **Line cost.** Usable cost x the amount in the recipe, in the same unit: 7 oz is 0.4375 lb.
4. **Plate cost.** Add every line, sauces and sides included, and divide a batch by its portions.
5. **Food cost %.** Plate cost / menu price x 100.

## Questions

### How do I calculate food cost for a recipe?

Multiply each ingredient's amount by its edible cost per unit, add them up, then divide by the number of portions.

### How is food cost calculated in a restaurant for a month?

Opening inventory + purchases - closing inventory, divided by food sales. Compare it with the food cost your recipes say to find waste and over-portioning.
