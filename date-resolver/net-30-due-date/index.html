Net 15, 30, 45 and 60 due dates for late-2026 invoices

The due dates that move

  • Invoice Mon, Nov 2, 2026, net 60: Friday, January 1, 2027 is not a business day, so the next one is Monday, January 4, 2027.
  • Invoice Fri, Nov 27, 2026, net 15: Saturday, December 12, 2026 is not a business day, so the next one is Monday, December 14, 2026.
  • Invoice Fri, Nov 27, 2026, net 30: Sunday, December 27, 2026 is not a business day, so the next one is Monday, December 28, 2026.
  • Invoice Tue, Dec 1, 2026, net 60: Saturday, January 30, 2027 is not a business day, so the next one is Monday, February 1, 2027.
  • Invoice Tue, Dec 15, 2026, net 60: Saturday, February 13, 2027 is not a business day, so the next one is Tuesday, February 16, 2027.
  • Invoice Thu, Dec 31, 2026, net 30: Saturday, January 30, 2027 is not a business day, so the next one is Monday, February 1, 2027.
  • Invoice Thu, Dec 31, 2026, net 45: Sunday, February 14, 2027 is not a business day, so the next one is Tuesday, February 16, 2027.

The US business days used here skip weekends and the federal holidays of 5 U.S.C. 6103 on the days OPM observes them. Your terms, not this table, decide whether a due date moves.

Questions

How do you count net 30?

Thirty calendar days after the invoice date, with the invoice date itself not counted. Weekends and holidays count as days.

What if a net 30 due date falls on a weekend?

It depends on your terms. Counted plainly, the due date can be a Saturday or Sunday; some terms move it to the next business day. For an invoice dated November 27, 2026 that is Sunday, December 27, 2026 or Monday, December 28, 2026.

Does net 30 mean the end of next month?

No. "Net 30" counts days from the invoice date; terms such as "end of month" or "EOM" count from the month end. The tool reads "end of next month" as the last day of the next month.
