# Washington paid sick leave

Washington: at least 1 hour for every 40 hours worked, full-time, part-time, temporary or seasonal (RCW 49.46.210(1)(a)). A part-time employee working 22 hours a week, paid every two weeks through 2027, earns 28.60 hours of sick time.

## The rule

Washington: sick leave rule by employer size, as quoted below

| Employer | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| All employers | 1 hour per 40 hours worked; carryover may be limited to 40 hours |

- Unused balances of 40 hours or less carry over; an employer need not carry over more than 40. (RCW 49.46.210(1)(j))
- Where a local ordinance is more generous than state law, the ordinance applies. (WA L&I)
- Front-loading is allowed if it meets the accrual, use and carryover rules. (RCW 49.46.210(1)(a))
- Use may start 90 calendar days after the first day of work. (WA L&I)
- No cash-out required at separation (some construction workers excepted). (RCW 49.46.210(1)(k))
- Reinstated on rehire within 12 months. (RCW 49.46.210(1)(k))

## Washington in its own words

- "An employee shall accrue at least one hour of paid sick leave for every forty hours worked as an employee." [RCW 49.46.210, Paid sick leave](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=49.46.210) (RCW 49.46.210(1)(a))
- "accrued and unused paid sick leave carries over to the following year, but an employer is not required to allow an employee to carry over paid sick leave in excess of 40 hours." [RCW 49.46.210, Paid sick leave](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=49.46.210) (RCW 49.46.210(1)(j))
- "If a local ordinance requires more generous paid sick leave benefits for employees than state law, those requirements will apply." [Washington Department of Labor & Industries, Paid Sick Leave](https://lni.wa.gov/workers-rights/leave/paid-sick-leave/) (WA L&I)
- "An employer may provide paid sick leave in advance of accrual provided that such front-loading meets or exceeds the requirements of this section for accrual, use, and carryover of paid sick leave." [RCW 49.46.210, Paid sick leave](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=49.46.210) (RCW 49.46.210(1)(a))
- "You may begin using earned paid sick leave 90 calendar days after your first day of work with your employer." [Washington Department of Labor & Industries, Paid Sick Leave](https://lni.wa.gov/workers-rights/leave/paid-sick-leave/) (WA L&I)
- "Paid sick leave balances generally do not have to be cashed out or paid when employment ends, unless another state law or a collective bargaining agreement requires it." [Washington Department of Labor & Industries, Paid Sick Leave](https://lni.wa.gov/workers-rights/leave/paid-sick-leave/) (RCW 49.46.210(1)(k))
- "When there is a separation from employment and the employee is rehired within 12 months of separation by the same employer, whether at the same or a different business location of the employer, previously accrued unused paid sick leave shall be reinstated" [RCW 49.46.210, Paid sick leave](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=49.46.210) (RCW 49.46.210(1)(k))

## Questions

### How much sick leave do employees earn in Washington?

At least 1 hour for every 40 hours worked, full-time, part-time, temporary or seasonal. (RCW 49.46.210(1)(a)).

### Does unused sick leave have to be paid out when someone leaves in Washington?

No cash-out required at separation (some construction workers excepted). (RCW 49.46.210(1)(k)).

### When can a new employee in Washington start using sick leave?

Use may start 90 calendar days after the first day of work. (WA L&I).

### Does sick leave carry over to the next year in Washington?

Unused balances of 40 hours or less carry over; an employer need not carry over more than 40. (RCW 49.46.210(1)(j)).
