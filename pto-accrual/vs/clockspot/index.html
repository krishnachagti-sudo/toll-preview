Clockspot and this tool

Clockspot's free calculator (fact-checked May 24, 2026) projects one balance at a fixed weekly schedule; this tool runs each paycheck's real hours, so a Maryland employee at a 10-person employer alternating 30 and 20 hours every two weeks earns 13 hours of unpaid leave in 2027, not 21.67, because no leave accrues in a two-week period under 24 hours (Md. Lab. & Empl. 3-1304(c)(5)).

  • Made by Conyso
  • Exact, no AI
  • Official sources, dated
  • Files you upload are not kept
  • SecurityTerms

What Clockspot's page says

  • A free calculator that projects one employee's sick balance by state from hours a week and weeks to project
  • Fact-checked May 24, 2026, with a methodology page
  • Its state list includes California, Connecticut, Massachusetts, Michigan, Nebraska, Nevada, New Mexico, New York, Rhode Island and San Francisco
  • It says three-tier ordinances (Chicago, Seattle, D.C.) and the unpaid variant for the smallest Maryland and Massachusetts employers are not modeled, and that the projection assumes no leave is used

Read on November 9, 2026: Clockspot, PTO Accrual Calculator by State.

What this tool does

  • Two banks per employee, paycheck by paycheck: your PTO policy and the state or city sick law, with the yearly cap, balance cap, waiting period and year-end carryover applied.
  • Every rule quoted from the state's or city's own statute or agency page with the date read; places we could not read are listed as not covered.
  • The payout owed at separation with the state's vacation rule.
  • The same answers over a REST API and MCP, $0.01 a lookup or $0.01 an employee in a batch (at least $0.10), free on the web page, never more than $9.90 in a month.

Clockspot's page notes that the smallest-employer unpaid variant and three-tier ordinances are not modeled; this tool works out D.C.'s three tiers, Seattle's three tiers, Maryland's and Oregon's unpaid tiers, and a PTO policy bank beside the sick bank, for one employee on the page or many over the API.

When Clockspot is better

You need a quick projection for California, New York, Massachusetts or another place whose official site we could not read; Clockspot lists them and we do not.

Worked out from each state's or city's own published text, quoted with the date we read it. Not legal advice. The limits shown are the most an employer may apply; your written policy, a union contract or a local ordinance can give more. Places whose official sites we could not read are listed as not covered rather than guessed.
