Example: Washington, 44 hours every two weeks for 26 paychecks, an 80-hour PTO policy, 8 sick hours used

20.6 hof sick time at year end

Over 26 paychecks every two weeks (January 8, 2027 to December 24, 2027) and 1,144 hours worked, the PTO bank earns 80 hours and ends at 80 hours; the separate Washington sick bank earns 28.60 hours at 1 hour per 40 hours worked and ends at 20.60 hours.

What is owed when someone leaves

ExampleIllinois · 820 ILCS 115/5

Example: Illinois, 64 unused PTO hours and 20 sick hours at $28.00 an hour

Paying out 64 hours of PTO at $28.00 an hour is $1,792.00 gross (and 20 hours of sick time would be $560.00). In Illinois, unused vacation at separation must be paid, as earned vacation counts as wages (820 ILCS 115/5; 56 Ill. Adm. Code 300.520); unused sick leave under the state law need not be paid out (IDOL PLAWA FAQ).

PTO hours per paycheck

Examplearithmetic

Example: 120 PTO hours a year

120 hours a year is 4.62 hours a paycheck every two weeks (120 / 26), 5.00 twice a month, 2.31 weekly and 10.00 monthly; per hour worked it is 0.0577 hours (120 / 2,080).

One place's rules, quoted

ExampleSeattle · SMC 14.16

Example: Seattle, WA

Seattle, WA (SMC 14.16): Tier 3: 250 or more FTEs worldwide: 1 hour per 30 hours worked; carryover may be limited to 72 hours. Tier 2: 50 to 249 FTEs: 1 hour per 40 hours worked; carryover may be limited to 56 hours. Tier 1: 1 to 49 FTEs: 1 hour per 40 hours worked; carryover may be limited to 40 hours.

What it does not cover

Legal advice, places whose official sites we could not read, and what your own policy or a union contract gives beyond the law.

  • Worked out from each state's or city's own published text, quoted with the date we read it. Not legal advice.
  • The limits shown are the most an employer may apply; your written policy, a union contract or a local ordinance can give more.
  • Places whose official sites we could not read are listed as not covered rather than guessed.
  • Sick time is worked out from the hours worked so far in the plan year (hours divided by the rate, to the thousandth of an hour), so it never drifts across paychecks.
