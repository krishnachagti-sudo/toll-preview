# New York wage garnishment limit

In New York, a creditor garnishment may take the least of 10% of gross pay, 25% of disposable earnings, or the amount above 30 times the New York minimum wage; for a weekly paycheck with $1,000.00 of disposable earnings on November 13, 2026 that is $125.00, against $250.00 under the federal rule.

- Weekly, $1,000 disposable: $125.00
- Every two weeks, $2,000 disposable: $250.00
- Federal limit on the same weekly pay: $250.00
- New York minimum wage used: $16.00 an hour

## The New York rule

N.Y. CPLR 5231(b), as read on November 8, 2026:

"an income execution for installments therefrom of not more than ten percent thereof may be issued ... provided, however, that (i) no amount shall be withheld ... unless the disposable earnings of the judgment debtor for that week exceed the greater of thirty times the federal minimum hourly wage ... or thirty times the state minimum hourly wage prescribed in section \[652\] of the labor law ...; (ii) the amount withheld ... shall not exceed twenty-five percent of the disposable earnings of the judgment debtor for that week, or, the amount by which the disposable earnings ... exceed the greater of thirty times the federal minimum hourly wage ... or thirty times the state minimum hourly wage ..., whichever is less; (iii) if the earnings of the judgment debtor are also subject to deductions for alimony, support or maintenance ... the amount withheld ... shall not exceed the amount by which twenty-five percent of the disposable earnings ... exceeds the amount deducted ...; and (iv) no amount shall be imposed in judgments arising from a medical debt action brought by a hospital ... or a health care professional ..."

> Amounts and section numbers the statute writes in words are shown as figures in square brackets.

- The state minimum wage depends on where the employee works: New York City, Long Island and Westchester, or the rest of the state.
- The 10% is of the money the debtor receives; the tool applies it to gross earnings for the period.

## Where the employee works

CPLR 5231(b) uses the state minimum wage, which differs by region. Gross is 1.25 times disposable.

| Region | Minimum wage | Weekly, $400 disposable | Weekly, $1,000 disposable |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Rest of New York State | $16.00 | $0.00 | $100.00 |
| New York City, Long Island and Westchester County | $17.00 | $0.00 | $100.00 |

No amount may be withheld on a judgment from a medical debt action by a hospital or a health care professional (CPLR 5231(b)(iv)).

## Questions

### How much can be garnished from a paycheck in New York?

For a creditor judgment, the least of 10% of gross pay, 25% of disposable earnings, or the amount above 30 times the New York minimum wage. On $1,000.00 of weekly disposable earnings that is $125.00; on $2,000.00 every two weeks it is $250.00.

### Is the limit taken from gross or net pay?

From disposable earnings: gross pay minus only the deductions the law requires, such as income taxes, Social Security and Medicare. Voluntary deductions such as health premiums and 401(k) stay in.

### What if the employee already has child support withheld?

Support withheld this pay period counts against the same limit, so the creditor gets only what is left of $125.00 on $1,000.00 a week, which can be nothing.
