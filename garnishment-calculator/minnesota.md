# Minnesota wage garnishment limit

In Minnesota, a creditor garnishment may take 10%, 15% or 25% of disposable earnings by weekly income band, and never more than the amount above 40 times the higher of the federal and Minnesota minimum wage; for a weekly paycheck with $1,000.00 of disposable earnings on November 13, 2026 that is $250.00, against $250.00 under the federal rule.

- Weekly, $1,000 disposable: $250.00
- Every two weeks, $2,000 disposable: $500.00
- Federal limit on the same weekly pay: $250.00
- Minnesota minimum wage used: $11.41 an hour

## The Minnesota rule

Minn. Stat. 571.922, as read on November 8, 2026:

"Unless the judgment is for child support, the maximum part of the aggregate disposable earnings of an individual for any pay period subjected to garnishment may not exceed the lesser of: (1) 25 percent of the debtor's disposable earnings, if the debtor's weekly income exceeds 80 times the greater of the hourly wage described in paragraph (b); (2) 15 percent ... if the debtor's weekly income exceeds 60 times, but is less than or equal to 80 times ...; or (3) ten percent ... if the debtor's weekly income exceeds 40 times, but is less than or equal to 60 times ... (b) The amount by which the debtor's disposable earnings exceed the greater of: (i) 40 times the hourly wage described in section 177.24, subdivision 1, paragraph (a), clause (4); or (ii) 40 times the federal minimum hourly wages ..."

- The tool reads "weekly income" as weekly disposable earnings; if the court or your counsel reads it as gross, the percentage tier can be higher.
- The statute counts work weeks in the pay period, with extra workdays as a fraction of a week; enter the weeks if your semimonthly or monthly period differs from 13/6 or 13/3.
- Child support judgments: 50%, 55%, 60% or 65% (571.922(c)), the same as federal.

## Questions

### How much can be garnished from a paycheck in Minnesota?

For a creditor judgment, 10%, 15% or 25% of disposable earnings by weekly income band, and never more than the amount above 40 times the higher of the federal and Minnesota minimum wage. On $1,000.00 of weekly disposable earnings that is $250.00; on $2,000.00 every two weeks it is $500.00.

### Is the limit taken from gross or net pay?

From disposable earnings: gross pay minus only the deductions the law requires, such as income taxes, Social Security and Medicare. Voluntary deductions such as health premiums and 401(k) stay in.

### What if the employee already has child support withheld?

Support withheld this pay period counts against the same limit, so the creditor gets only what is left of $250.00 on $1,000.00 a week, which can be nothing.
