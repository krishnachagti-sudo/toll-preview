Wage garnishment calculator

Enter one paycheck and get the most you may withhold for a garnishment order under federal law and your state's own statute.

Example

Worked example

Example: California, paid every two weeks, $3,000 gross, $621 required deductions, $150 child support already withheld

$152.64to withhold for a creditor this two-week pay period

On $2,379.00 disposable earnings paid every two weeks in California, the most a creditor garnishment may take is $302.64 (California's limit); after $150.00 already withheld for support or earlier orders, $152.64 is left, so withhold $152.64 this two-week pay period.

  • Gross earnings this pay period$3,000.00you entered
  • Deductions required by law$621.00you entered
  • Disposable earnings$2,379.00gross less required deductions
  • Federal limit$594.7515 U.S.C. 1673
  • California limit$302.64Cal. Code Civ. Proc. 706.050, form WG-002
  • Already withheld for support$150.00you entered
  • Withhold for this order$152.64the lower limit less what is already withheld

The $245.00 of health insurance and 401(k) on that stub stays in disposable earnings: only deductions the law requires come off.

Where the numbers come from

The federal limit is the lower of 25% of disposable earnings and the amount above 30 times the federal minimum wage; a covered state's own statute can set less, and the lower figure wins.

What it does not cover

States we have not read show the federal limit with a warning, because their own law may allow less. Where the order you were served sets a lower amount, the order controls.

Read the sources and the rules in full
  • Federal: 15 U.S.C. 1672 to 1677 and the Department of Labor's Fact Sheet #30 (revised December 2024), read November 8, 2026: the lesser of 25% of disposable earnings or the amount above 30 times $7.25 a week; support 50% to 65%; federal debts 15%.
  • States: the statute or official court form of each covered state, quoted and dated on its page. States we have not read show the federal limit with a warning, because their own law may allow less.
  • IRS levies: Publication 1494 (Rev. 12-2025) for 2026.
  • Not legal advice. Follow the order you were served: where it sets a lower amount, the order controls. Ask the court or agency that issued it, or a lawyer, about anything it does not answer.
