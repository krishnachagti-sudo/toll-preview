# Iowa wage garnishment limit

In Iowa, a creditor garnishment may take the federal limit, and no more in a calendar year for each creditor than a cap set by the employee's expected earnings; for a weekly paycheck with $1,000.00 of disposable earnings on November 13, 2026 that is $250.00, against $250.00 under the federal rule.

- Weekly, $1,000 disposable: $250.00
- Every two weeks, $2,000 disposable: $500.00
- Federal limit on the same weekly pay: $250.00

## The Iowa rule

Iowa Code 642.21, as read on November 8, 2026:

"The disposable earnings of an individual are exempt from garnishment to the extent provided by the federal Consumer Credit Protection Act ... The maximum amount of an employee's earnings which may be garnished during any one calendar year is \[$250\] for each judgment creditor ... When the employee's earnings are reasonably expected to be more than \[$12,000\], the maximum amount ... for each creditor is as follows: \[$12,000\] or more, but less than \[$16,000\], not more than \[$400\] ... \[$16,000 to under $24,000: $800\] ... \[$24,000 to under $35,000: $1,500\] ... \[$35,000 to under $50,000: $2,000\] ... \[$50,000\] or more, not more than ten percent of an employee's expected earnings."

> Amounts and section numbers the statute writes in words are shown as figures in square brackets.

- The yearly cap is per judgment creditor per calendar year, so the tool needs the employee's expected earnings for the year and what this creditor has already received this year.
- Support (chapter 252D, sections 598.22, 598.23 and 627.12) is outside the yearly cap.
- An employer may not discharge an employee because of garnishment for indebtedness (642.21(2)(c)).

## Questions

### How much can be garnished from a paycheck in Iowa?

For a creditor judgment, the federal limit, and no more in a calendar year for each creditor than a cap set by the employee's expected earnings. On $1,000.00 of weekly disposable earnings that is $250.00; on $2,000.00 every two weeks it is $500.00.

### Is the limit taken from gross or net pay?

From disposable earnings: gross pay minus only the deductions the law requires, such as income taxes, Social Security and Medicare. Voluntary deductions such as health premiums and 401(k) stay in.

### What if the employee already has child support withheld?

Support withheld this pay period counts against the same limit, so the creditor gets only what is left of $250.00 on $1,000.00 a week, which can be nothing.
