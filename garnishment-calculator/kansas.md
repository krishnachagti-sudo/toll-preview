# Kansas wage garnishment limit

In Kansas, a creditor garnishment may take the lesser of 25% of disposable earnings or the amount above 30 times the federal minimum wage (the federal rule), and no more than the claim; for a weekly paycheck with $1,000.00 of disposable earnings on November 13, 2026 that is $250.00, against $250.00 under the federal rule.

- Weekly, $1,000 disposable: $250.00
- Every two weeks, $2,000 disposable: $500.00
- Federal limit on the same weekly pay: $250.00

## The Kansas rule

K.S.A. 60-2310, as read on November 8, 2026:

"The maximum part of such earnings of any wage earning individual which may be subjected to wage garnishment for any workweek or multiple thereof may not exceed the lesser of: (1) Twenty-five percent of the individual's aggregate disposable earnings for that workweek or multiple thereof; (2) the amount by which the individual's aggregate disposable earnings for that workweek or multiple thereof exceed an amount equal to 30 times the federal minimum hourly wage, or equivalent multiple thereof for such longer period; or (3) the amount of the plaintiff's claim as found in the order for garnishment."

- Also capped at the plaintiff's claim in the order: enter the balance owed.
- No one creditor may issue more than one garnishment against the same debtor's earnings in any 30-day period (60-2310(b)).

## Questions

### How much can be garnished from a paycheck in Kansas?

For a creditor judgment, the lesser of 25% of disposable earnings or the amount above 30 times the federal minimum wage (the federal rule), and no more than the claim. On $1,000.00 of weekly disposable earnings that is $250.00; on $2,000.00 every two weeks it is $500.00.

### Is the limit taken from gross or net pay?

From disposable earnings: gross pay minus only the deductions the law requires, such as income taxes, Social Security and Medicare. Voluntary deductions such as health premiums and 401(k) stay in.

### What if the employee already has child support withheld?

Support withheld this pay period counts against the same limit, so the creditor gets only what is left of $250.00 on $1,000.00 a week, which can be nothing.
