# Washington wage garnishment limit

In Washington, a creditor garnishment may take for a consumer debt, the lesser of 20% of disposable earnings or the amount above 35 times the Washington minimum wage; for a weekly paycheck with $1,000.00 of disposable earnings on November 13, 2026 that is $200.00, against $250.00 under the federal rule.

- Weekly, $1,000 disposable: $200.00
- Every two weeks, $2,000 disposable: $400.00
- Federal limit on the same weekly pay: $250.00
- Washington minimum wage used: $17.13 an hour

## The Washington rule

RCW 6.27.150, as read on November 8, 2026:

"(1) ... for each week of such earnings, an amount shall be exempt from garnishment which is the greatest of the following: (a) Thirty-five times the federal minimum hourly wage in effect at the time the earnings are payable; or (b) Seventy-five percent of the disposable earnings of the defendant. ... (3) In the case of a garnishment based on a judgment or other order for the collection of private student loan debt ... the greater of the following: (a) Fifty times the minimum hourly wage of the highest minimum wage law in the state at the time the earnings are payable; or (b) Eighty-five percent of the disposable earnings of the defendant. (4) In the case of a garnishment based on a judgment or other order for the collection of consumer debt ... the greater of the following: (a) Thirty-five times the state minimum hourly wage; or (b) Eighty percent of the disposable earnings of the defendant."

- Which rule applies depends on the debt named in the writ: consumer debt, private student loan, or another judgment.
- The exemptions apply whatever the pay interval (6.27.150(5)); the tool scales the weekly multiple by the weeks in the period.
- Spousal maintenance garnishments (other than a mandatory wage assignment): 50% of disposable earnings exempt (6.27.150(2)).

## Questions

### How much can be garnished from a paycheck in Washington?

For a creditor judgment, for a consumer debt, the lesser of 20% of disposable earnings or the amount above 35 times the Washington minimum wage. On $1,000.00 of weekly disposable earnings that is $200.00; on $2,000.00 every two weeks it is $400.00.

### Is the limit taken from gross or net pay?

From disposable earnings: gross pay minus only the deductions the law requires, such as income taxes, Social Security and Medicare. Voluntary deductions such as health premiums and 401(k) stay in.

### What if the employee already has child support withheld?

Support withheld this pay period counts against the same limit, so the creditor gets only what is left of $200.00 on $1,000.00 a week, which can be nothing.
