Wage garnishment by state

South Dakota wage garnishment limit

In South Dakota, a creditor garnishment may take the lesser of 20% of disposable earnings or the amount above 40 times the higher of the federal and South Dakota minimum wage, less $25 a week for each dependent; for a weekly paycheck with $1,000.00 of disposable earnings on November 13, 2026 that is $200.00, against $250.00 under the federal rule.

The South Dakota rule

S.D. Codified Laws 21-18-51, as read on November 8, 2026:

"The maximum part of the aggregate disposable earnings of a wage earner for any workweek which is subject to garnishment may not exceed the lesser of: (1) Twenty percent of disposable earnings for that week; (2) The amount by which disposable earnings for that week exceed forty times the federal minimum hourly wage ... or applicable state minimum wage if greater ... in effect at the time the earnings are payable less twenty-five dollars per week for each dependent family member residing with the garnishment debtor other than the garnishment debtor himself or herself."

  • The tool takes the $25 per dependent per week off the amount in test (2).

Questions

How much can be garnished from a paycheck in South Dakota?

For a creditor judgment, the lesser of 20% of disposable earnings or the amount above 40 times the higher of the federal and South Dakota minimum wage, less $25 a week for each dependent. On $1,000.00 of weekly disposable earnings that is $200.00; on $2,000.00 every two weeks it is $400.00.

Is the limit taken from gross or net pay?

From disposable earnings: gross pay minus only the deductions the law requires, such as income taxes, Social Security and Medicare. Voluntary deductions such as health premiums and 401(k) stay in.

What if the employee already has child support withheld?

Support withheld this pay period counts against the same limit, so the creditor gets only what is left of $200.00 on $1,000.00 a week, which can be nothing.
