# Colorado wage garnishment limit

In Colorado, a creditor garnishment may take the lesser of 20% of disposable earnings or the amount above 40 times the higher of the federal and Colorado minimum wage; for a weekly paycheck with $1,000.00 of disposable earnings on November 13, 2026 that is $200.00, against $250.00 under the federal rule.

- Weekly, $1,000 disposable: $200.00
- Every two weeks, $2,000 disposable: $400.00
- Federal limit on the same weekly pay: $250.00
- Colorado minimum wage used: $15.16 an hour

## The Colorado rule

Colorado Judicial Branch Form 27, Calculation of the Amount of Exempt Earnings Worksheet (C.R.S. 13-54-104), as read on November 9, 2026:

"EXEMPTION CHART (“Minimum Hourly Wage” means state or federal minimum wage, whichever is greater.) PAY PERIOD Weekly Bi-weekly Semi-monthly Monthly AMOUNT EXEMPT IS THE GREATER OF: 40 x Minimum Hourly Wage or 80% of Disposable Earnings 80 x Minimum Hourly Wage or 80% of Disposable Earnings 86.67 x Minimum Hourly Wage or 80% of Disposable Earnings 173.3 x Minimum Hourly Wage or 80% of Disposable Earnings"

- The exempt amount is the greater of 80% of disposable earnings and 40 times the greater of the state and federal minimum wage a week (80, 86.67 and 173.3 times for biweekly, semimonthly and monthly pay), so at most 20% can be withheld.
- The court form was read in a browser on 2026-11-09 (the statute site does not serve the code as text). The same PDF also prints the line "This form is effective until November 1, 2020 unless extended by the Administrator of the Uniform Consumer Credit Code"; the court still posts it as the current Form 27.

## Questions

### How much can be garnished from a paycheck in Colorado?

For a creditor judgment, the lesser of 20% of disposable earnings or the amount above 40 times the higher of the federal and Colorado minimum wage. On $1,000.00 of weekly disposable earnings that is $200.00; on $2,000.00 every two weeks it is $400.00.

### Is the limit taken from gross or net pay?

From disposable earnings: gross pay minus only the deductions the law requires, such as income taxes, Social Security and Medicare. Voluntary deductions such as health premiums and 401(k) stay in.

### What if the employee already has child support withheld?

Support withheld this pay period counts against the same limit, so the creditor gets only what is left of $200.00 on $1,000.00 a week, which can be nothing.
