# District of Columbia wage garnishment limit

In the District of Columbia, a creditor garnishment may take 25% of the amount by which weekly disposable wages exceed 40 times the D.C. minimum wage, if that is less than the federal limit; for a weekly paycheck with $1,000.00 of disposable earnings on November 13, 2026 that is $66.00, against $250.00 under the federal rule.

- Weekly, $1,000 disposable: $66.00
- Every two weeks, $2,000 disposable: $132.00
- Federal limit on the same weekly pay: $250.00
- District of Columbia minimum wage used: $18.40 an hour

## The District of Columbia rule

D.C. Code 16-572, as read on November 8, 2026:

"the attachment shall become a lien and a continuing levy upon the gross wages due or to become due to the judgment debtor for the amount specified in the attachment to the extent of 25% of the amount by which the judgment debtor's disposable wages for that week exceed 40 times the minimum hourly wage, as prescribed in \[§ 32-1003\] ("minimum hourly wage"), in effect at the time the wages are payable."

- For pay periods other than a week the Mayor sets the multiple by regulation (16-572(1)(B)); that regulation was not read, so the tool scales by weeks in the period as Fact Sheet #30 does.
- Only one attachment on a debtor's wages may be satisfied at a time; the first delivered to the marshal has priority (16-572(3) and (4)).

## Questions

### How much can be garnished from a paycheck in the District of Columbia?

For a creditor judgment, 25% of the amount by which weekly disposable wages exceed 40 times the D.C. minimum wage, if that is less than the federal limit. On $1,000.00 of weekly disposable earnings that is $66.00; on $2,000.00 every two weeks it is $132.00.

### Is the limit taken from gross or net pay?

From disposable earnings: gross pay minus only the deductions the law requires, such as income taxes, Social Security and Medicare. Voluntary deductions such as health premiums and 401(k) stay in.

### What if the employee already has child support withheld?

Support withheld this pay period counts against the same limit, so the creditor gets only what is left of $66.00 on $1,000.00 a week, which can be nothing.
