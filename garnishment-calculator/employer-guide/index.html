Wage garnishment: what an employer does

When a writ arrives for an employee, compute disposable earnings, apply the lesser of the federal and state limits, take off support already withheld, and answer on time: a California employee with $2,400.00 disposable every two weeks and $150.00 of support leaves $161.04 for the creditor this period.

State rules we have read

  • Maine: answer within 20 days of service; the employer may charge $1 per check sent to the creditor; a late answer makes the employer liable for what could have been withheld (14 M.R.S. 3127-B).
  • California: withheld amounts go to the levying officer by the 15th of the next month after each payday (form WG-002).
  • Pennsylvania: wages cannot be attached for ordinary debts (42 Pa.C.S. 8127); on a residential lease attachment the employer may keep up to $5 for bookkeeping.
  • District of Columbia and Delaware: only one attachment on a debtor's wages is satisfied at a time (D.C. Code 16-572(3); 10 Del. C. 4913(b)).

All state limits list the rest.

Not legal advice. Follow the order you were served: where it sets a lower amount, the order controls. Ask the court or agency that issued it, or a lawyer, about anything it does not answer.

Steps

  1. Read the order. Note who issued it (a court, a support agency, the IRS or a federal agency), the debt, the amount and the answer deadline. Calendar the deadline the day it arrives.
  2. Work out disposable earnings. Gross pay for the period minus only the deductions the law requires.
  3. Apply the limits. For a creditor writ, the lesser of the federal limit and your state's; support, tax levies and federal-debt orders have their own limits.
  4. Take off what is already withheld. Support and earlier orders count against the same limit; the new order gets only what is left.
  5. Answer and remit. File the answer the order asks for, with the figures, then withhold each payday and send the money where the order says.
  6. Do not fire for one debt. Federal law bars discharging an employee because earnings were garnished for any one debt (15 U.S.C. 1674).

Questions

Can I fire an employee because of a wage garnishment?

Not for garnishment for any one debt, however many orders there are for it (15 U.S.C. 1674(a); DOL Fact Sheet #30). Some states protect more.

How long do I have to answer a garnishment in Maine?

Within 20 days of service of the order, file the completed form answer with the court and serve copies on the debtor and creditor (14 M.R.S. 3127-B(2)).
