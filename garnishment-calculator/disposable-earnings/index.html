Disposable earnings for a wage garnishment

Disposable earnings are what is left after the deductions the law requires: on $1,850.00 gross with $368.28 of taxes, Social Security and Medicare, disposable earnings are $1,481.72, not the $1,276.22 take-home, because health insurance, 401(k) and union dues are not subtracted.

Steps

  1. Start from gross. Take gross earnings for the pay period: wages, salary, commission and bonus paid in it.
  2. Subtract required deductions. Subtract only amounts the law requires to be withheld: federal, state and local income tax, Social Security, Medicare, and state-mandated items such as state disability insurance.
  3. Leave voluntary deductions in. Do not subtract health premiums, 401(k) contributions, union dues or other voluntary items (Maryland exempts medical insurance; check your state page).
  4. Apply the limit. Apply the lesser of the federal and state limits to the result.

Questions

Is disposable earnings net or gross pay?

Neither exactly: it is gross pay minus only the deductions the law requires, so voluntary deductions such as 401(k) or health premiums are not subtracted.

Are health insurance premiums taken off disposable earnings?

Not under the federal definition, which subtracts only amounts required by law. Maryland exempts medical insurance payments from attachment on top of that.

Does a bonus count?

Yes. Earnings include wages, salary, commission and bonus (15 U.S.C. 1672(a)).
