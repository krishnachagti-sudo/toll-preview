# Florida wage garnishment limit

In Florida, a creditor garnishment may take nothing from a head of family without a signed waiver, and otherwise the federal limit; for a weekly paycheck with $1,000.00 of disposable earnings on November 13, 2026 that is $250.00, against $250.00 under the federal rule.

- Weekly, $1,000 disposable: $250.00
- Every two weeks, $2,000 disposable: $500.00
- Federal limit on the same weekly pay: $250.00

## The Florida rule

Fla. Stat. 222.11, as read on November 8, 2026:

"All of the disposable earnings of a head of family whose disposable earnings are less than or equal to $750 a week are exempt from attachment or garnishment. Disposable earnings of a head of a family, which are greater than $750 a week, may not be attached or garnished unless such person has agreed otherwise in writing. ... Disposable earnings of a person other than a head of family may not be attached or garnished in excess of the amount allowed under the Consumer Credit Protection Act, 15 U.S.C. s. 1673."

- Head of family: a person providing more than one-half of the support for a child or other dependent (222.11(1)(c)).
- A head of family over $750 a week can be garnished only with a signed written waiver in the form the statute sets, and then not beyond the CCPA amount.
- For pay periods other than a week the tool scales $750 by the weeks in the period.

## Questions

### How much can be garnished from a paycheck in Florida?

For a creditor judgment, nothing from a head of family without a signed waiver, and otherwise the federal limit. On $1,000.00 of weekly disposable earnings that is $250.00; on $2,000.00 every two weeks it is $500.00.

### Is the limit taken from gross or net pay?

From disposable earnings: gross pay minus only the deductions the law requires, such as income taxes, Social Security and Medicare. Voluntary deductions such as health premiums and 401(k) stay in.

### What if the employee already has child support withheld?

Support withheld this pay period counts against the same limit, so the creditor gets only what is left of $250.00 on $1,000.00 a week, which can be nothing.
