Child support plus a creditor garnishment

Support is withheld first, and the creditor gets only what is left of the creditor limit: on $1,600.00 of biweekly disposable earnings the federal limit is $400.00, so after $250.00 of support the creditor gets $150.00, and after $400.00 or more it gets nothing.

New York's statute says the same in words: the income execution may not exceed the amount by which 25% of disposable earnings exceeds the support deducted (CPLR 5231(b)(iii)).

Sources

  • 15 U.S.C. 1672 to 1677, U.S. Code on govinfo.gov (read November 8, 2026)govinfo.gov
  • U.S. Department of Labor: Fact Sheet #30, Wage Garnishment Protections of the CCPA (revised December 2024, read November 8, 2026)dol.gov
  • California: Cal. Code Civ. Proc. 706.050, as stated on Judicial Council form WG-002 (Rev. January 1, 2026) (read November 8, 2026)courts.ca.gov
  • New York: N.Y. CPLR 5231(b) (read November 8, 2026)nysenate.gov
  • Pennsylvania: 42 Pa.C.S. 8127 (read November 8, 2026)palegis.us

Not legal advice. Follow the order you were served: where it sets a lower amount, the order controls. Ask the court or agency that issued it, or a lawyer, about anything it does not answer.

Steps

  1. Withhold support first. Support orders come ahead of creditor garnishments in the states read here (for example 42 Pa.C.S. 8127(b), and California's form WG-002 deducts the support order from the creditor amount).
  2. Work out the creditor limit. The lesser of the federal and state creditor limits on disposable earnings, computed as if there were no support.
  3. Subtract the support. Creditor headroom is that limit minus support withheld this period, never below zero.
  4. Answer the writ. Report disposable earnings, the support deducted and the amount for the creditor, which may be $0.00.

Questions

He is already tapped out on child support. What goes on the form?

Report his disposable earnings, the support withheld and $0.00 for the creditor when support is at or above the creditor limit: $400.00 on $1,600.00 every two weeks under the federal rule.
