The garnishment answer form

Most employer answer forms ask for the same figures: gross pay, required deductions, disposable earnings, other orders and the amount you will withhold; for a Maine employee with $800.00 of weekly disposable earnings and $50.00 of support that is $146.00 a week, and Maine's answer is due within 20 days of service.

Sources

  • U.S. Department of Labor: Fact Sheet #30, Wage Garnishment Protections of the CCPA (revised December 2024, read November 8, 2026)dol.gov

Not legal advice. Follow the order you were served: where it sets a lower amount, the order controls. Ask the court or agency that issued it, or a lawyer, about anything it does not answer.

Steps

  1. Calendar the deadline. Find the answer deadline on the order and put it in the calendar the day it is served (Maine: 20 days, 14 M.R.S. 3127-B(2)).
  2. Employment facts. Whether the person works for you, how often they are paid, and gross pay for the period.
  3. Disposable earnings. Gross minus the deductions the law requires.
  4. Other orders. List support and earlier garnishments and what each takes this period.
  5. Amount withheld. The amount for this order, which may be less than the order asks for, or $0.00.
  6. Sign and deliver. Sign, keep a copy, and send it where the order says (court, creditor, debtor).

Questions

What happens if I do not answer in time?

In Maine, a late answer is a default that makes the employer liable for the amount that could properly have been withheld, plus interest (14 M.R.S. 3127-B(5)).
