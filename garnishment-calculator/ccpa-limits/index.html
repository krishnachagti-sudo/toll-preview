CCPA wage garnishment limits

Under the CCPA the weekly maximum is the lesser of 25% of disposable earnings or the amount above $217.50 (30 x $7.25); paid every two weeks, nothing may be taken at or below $435.00, and 25% applies from $580.00.

What the law says

  • Disposable earnings are the earnings left after deducting amounts required by law to be withheld. (15 U.S.C. 1672(b))
  • Earnings means pay for personal services, whether called wages, salary, commission, bonus or otherwise, and includes periodic payments under a pension or retirement program. (15 U.S.C. 1672(a))
  • The creditor limit does not apply to support orders, chapter 13 bankruptcy court orders or debts for any state or federal tax. (15 U.S.C. 1673(b)(1))
  • A state law that prohibits garnishment or allows less is not affected; the law that gives the smaller garnishment applies. (15 U.S.C. 1677; DOL Fact Sheet #30)
  • The CCPA has no rules on which of several garnishments comes first; state or other federal law decides, and questions go to the court or agency that issued the order. (DOL Fact Sheet #30)
  • No employer may discharge an employee because their earnings have been garnished for any one debt, however many orders there are for it. (15 U.S.C. 1674(a); DOL Fact Sheet #30)
  • The limit applies to the aggregate disposable earnings garnished for the week, so amounts already garnished count against it. (15 U.S.C. 1673(a))

Questions

25% of disposable earnings or 30 times minimum wage: which one?

Whichever is less. 25% of disposable earnings, or disposable earnings minus 30 times the federal minimum wage ($217.50 a week); the smaller figure is the most a creditor can take (15 U.S.C. 1673(a)).

How much can be garnished from a biweekly paycheck?

Nothing at or below $435.00 of disposable earnings; the amount above $435.00 between there and $580.00; 25% at $580.00 or more.

Do the CCPA limits apply to child support or tax levies?

No. Support orders have their own limits (50% to 65%), and the creditor limit does not apply to chapter 13 bankruptcy orders or state or federal tax debts (15 U.S.C. 1673(b)).

Does the CCPA say which garnishment comes first?

No. Fact Sheet #30 says the CCPA has no rules on priority; state or other federal law decides.
