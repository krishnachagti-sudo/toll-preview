# Child support garnishment limits

A worker supporting another spouse or child can have up to 50% of disposable earnings withheld for support (55% if more than 12 weeks in arrears), and 60% or 65% if not; on $1,600.00 of disposable earnings that is $800.00 to $1,040.00, and a creditor then gets only what is left of the $400.00 federal limit.

- Support limit at 50% of $1,600.00: $800.00
- Supporting another family: 50% (55% in arrears)
- Not supporting another family: 60% (65% in arrears)
- Creditor room after $300 support: $100.00

## What is left for a creditor

Biweekly disposable earnings of $1,600.00: the federal creditor limit is $400.00. Support withheld this period is $300.00, so the creditor writ gets $100.00. With support of $400.00 or more, the creditor gets nothing this period.

## Questions

### How much can be garnished for child support?

Up to 50% of disposable earnings if the worker supports another spouse or child, up to 60% if not, plus 5% more when payments are over 12 weeks in arrears (15 U.S.C. 1673(b)(2)).

### Does child support count against a creditor garnishment?

Yes. The creditor limit is on the aggregate garnished, so support withheld first uses it up: on $1,600.00 every two weeks the federal creditor limit is $400.00; with $300.00 of support already withheld, $100.00 is left.
