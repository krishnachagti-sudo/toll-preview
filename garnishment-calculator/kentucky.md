# Kentucky wage garnishment limit

In Kentucky, a creditor garnishment may take the lesser of 25% of disposable earnings or the amount above 30 times the federal minimum wage (the federal rule); for a weekly paycheck with $1,000.00 of disposable earnings on November 13, 2026 that is $250.00, against $250.00 under the federal rule.

- Weekly, $1,000 disposable: $250.00
- Every two weeks, $2,000 disposable: $500.00
- Federal limit on the same weekly pay: $250.00

## The Kentucky rule

KRS 427.010, as read on November 8, 2026:

"the maximum part of the aggregate disposable earnings of an individual for any workweek which is subjected to garnishment may not exceed the lesser of either: (a) Twenty-five percent (25%) of his disposable earnings for that week, or (b) The amount by which his disposable earnings for that week exceed thirty (30) times the federal minimum hourly wage ... In the case of earnings for any pay period other than a week, the multiple of the federal minimum hourly wage equivalent ... as prescribed by regulation by the federal secretary of labor shall apply."

- Kentucky's limit is the federal one.

## Questions

### How much can be garnished from a paycheck in Kentucky?

For a creditor judgment, the lesser of 25% of disposable earnings or the amount above 30 times the federal minimum wage (the federal rule). On $1,000.00 of weekly disposable earnings that is $250.00; on $2,000.00 every two weeks it is $500.00.

### Is the limit taken from gross or net pay?

From disposable earnings: gross pay minus only the deductions the law requires, such as income taxes, Social Security and Medicare. Voluntary deductions such as health premiums and 401(k) stay in.

### What if the employee already has child support withheld?

Support withheld this pay period counts against the same limit, so the creditor gets only what is left of $250.00 on $1,000.00 a week, which can be nothing.
