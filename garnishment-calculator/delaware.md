# Delaware wage garnishment limit

In Delaware, a creditor garnishment may take 15% of wages (85% is exempt), if that is less than the federal limit; for a weekly paycheck with $1,000.00 of disposable earnings on November 13, 2026 that is $187.50, against $250.00 under the federal rule.

- Weekly, $1,000 disposable: $187.50
- Every two weeks, $2,000 disposable: $375.00
- Federal limit on the same weekly pay: $250.00

## The Delaware rule

10 Del. C. 4913, as read on November 8, 2026:

"Eighty-five percent of the amount of the wages for labor or service of any person residing within the State shall be exempt from mesne attachment process and execution attachment process under the laws of this State; but such limitation shall be inapplicable to process issued for the collection of a fine or costs or taxes due and owing the State."

- The 85% exemption is of the amount of the wages; the tool applies it to gross wages for the period.
- It applies to persons residing in Delaware.
- On any amount of wages due, only one attachment may be made (4913(b)).

## Questions

### How much can be garnished from a paycheck in Delaware?

For a creditor judgment, 15% of wages (85% is exempt), if that is less than the federal limit. On $1,000.00 of weekly disposable earnings that is $187.50; on $2,000.00 every two weeks it is $375.00.

### Is the limit taken from gross or net pay?

From disposable earnings: gross pay minus only the deductions the law requires, such as income taxes, Social Security and Medicare. Voluntary deductions such as health premiums and 401(k) stay in.

### What if the employee already has child support withheld?

Support withheld this pay period counts against the same limit, so the creditor gets only what is left of $187.50 on $1,000.00 a week, which can be nothing.
