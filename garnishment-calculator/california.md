# California wage garnishment limit

In California, a creditor garnishment may take the lesser of 20% of disposable earnings or 40% of the amount above 48 times the applicable hourly minimum wage a week; for a weekly paycheck with $1,000.00 of disposable earnings on November 13, 2026 that is $75.52, against $250.00 under the federal rule.

- Weekly, $1,000 disposable: $75.52
- Every two weeks, $2,000 disposable: $151.04
- Federal limit on the same weekly pay: $250.00
- California minimum wage used: $16.90 an hour

## The California rule

Cal. Code Civ. Proc. 706.050, as stated on Judicial Council form WG-002 (Rev. January 1, 2026), as read on November 8, 2026:

"Calculate the maximum amount that may be withheld from the employee's disposable earnings, which is the lesser of the following two amounts: 20 percent of disposable earnings for that week; or 40 percent of the amount by which the employee's disposable earnings that week exceed the applicable minimum wage. ... For a daily or weekly pay period, multiply the applicable hourly minimum wage by 48. For a biweekly pay period, multiply the applicable hourly minimum wage by 96. For a semimonthly pay period, multiply the applicable hourly minimum wage by 104. For a monthly pay period, multiply the applicable hourly minimum wage by 208."

- The applicable minimum wage is the local minimum wage where the employee works when it is higher than the state rate (WG-002).
- A Wage and Earnings Assignment Order for support is deducted from the amount to withhold for the earnings withholding order (WG-002).
- Withheld amounts go to the levying officer by the 15th of the next month after each payday (WG-002).
- leginfo.legislature.ca.gov refused the request (403), so the rule is taken from the Judicial Council's mandatory form and the courts' self-help guide, both state sources.

## Questions

### How much can be garnished from a paycheck in California?

For a creditor judgment, the lesser of 20% of disposable earnings or 40% of the amount above 48 times the applicable hourly minimum wage a week. On $1,000.00 of weekly disposable earnings that is $75.52; on $2,000.00 every two weeks it is $151.04.

### Is the limit taken from gross or net pay?

From disposable earnings: gross pay minus only the deductions the law requires, such as income taxes, Social Security and Medicare. Voluntary deductions such as health premiums and 401(k) stay in.

### What if the employee already has child support withheld?

Support withheld this pay period counts against the same limit, so the creditor gets only what is left of $75.52 on $1,000.00 a week, which can be nothing.
