# Pennsylvania wage garnishment limit

In Pennsylvania, wages in an employer's hands cannot be garnished for ordinary debts; for a residential lease judgment the most is 10% of net wages, which on a weekly paycheck of $1,250.00 gross with $250.00 of taxes is $100.00 (42 Pa.C.S. 8127).

- Weekly, $1,000 disposable: $100.00
- Every two weeks, $2,000 disposable: $200.00
- Federal limit on the same weekly pay: $250.00

## The Pennsylvania rule

42 Pa.C.S. 8127, as read on November 8, 2026:

"The wages, salaries and commissions of individuals shall while in the hands of the employer be exempt from any attachment, execution or other process except upon an action or proceeding: (1) Under 23 Pa.C.S. Pt. IV (relating to divorce). (2) For support. (3) For board for four weeks or less. (3.1) For amounts awarded to a judgment creditor-landlord arising out of a residential lease ... The sum attached shall be no more than 10% of the net wages per pay period of the judgment debtor-tenant or a sum not to place the debtor's net income below the poverty income guidelines ..., whichever is less. ... "net wages" shall mean all wages paid less only the following items: (i) Federal, State and local income taxes. (ii) F.I.C.A. payments and nonvoluntary retirement payments. (iii) Union dues. (iv) Health insurance premiums. (4) Under ... the Pennsylvania Higher Education Assistance Agency Act. (5) For restitution to crime victims, costs, fines or bail judgments ..."

- Residential lease judgments: at most 10% of net wages, and never so much that net income falls below the federal poverty guidelines; the tool computes the 10% and leaves the poverty test to you (the guideline was not read).
- Board for four weeks or less, the Higher Education Assistance Agency Act and criminal restitution, costs, fines or bail can reach wages; the statute sets no percentage for them, so the federal limit is shown.
- Support has first priority and criminal restitution orders second (8127(b)).
- For a residential lease attachment the employer may deduct up to $5 for the extra bookkeeping (8127(c)(1)).
- The employer may not take adverse action because wages were attached (8127(e)).

## What can reach wages in Pennsylvania

- Support, and actions under the divorce code: the federal support limits apply.
- Board for four weeks or less, the Higher Education Assistance Agency Act, and criminal restitution, costs, fines or bail: no percentage in 8127, so the federal limit is the ceiling.
- Residential lease judgments: at most 10% of net wages (wages less income taxes, F.I.C.A., nonvoluntary retirement, union dues and health insurance premiums), and not below the federal poverty guidelines.
- Any other debt: nothing. Answer the writ that no wages are subject to it.

## Questions

### How much can be garnished from a paycheck in Pennsylvania?

For ordinary debts, nothing: 42 Pa.C.S. 8127(a) exempts wages in the employer's hands except for support, divorce, board, residential lease judgments, the Higher Education Assistance Agency and criminal restitution. A residential lease judgment takes at most 10% of net wages.

### Is the limit taken from gross or net pay?

From disposable earnings: gross pay minus only the deductions the law requires, such as income taxes, Social Security and Medicare. Voluntary deductions such as health premiums and 401(k) stay in.

### What if the employee already has child support withheld?

Support withheld this pay period counts against the same limit, so the creditor gets only what is left of $100.00 on $1,000.00 a week, which can be nothing.
