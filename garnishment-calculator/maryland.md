# Maryland wage garnishment limit

In Maryland, a creditor garnishment may take the lesser of 25% of disposable wages or the amount above 30 times the Maryland minimum wage, after medical insurance payments come off; for a weekly paycheck with $1,000.00 of disposable earnings on November 13, 2026 that is $250.00, against $250.00 under the federal rule.

- Weekly, $1,000 disposable: $250.00
- Every two weeks, $2,000 disposable: $500.00
- Federal limit on the same weekly pay: $250.00
- Maryland minimum wage used: $15.00 an hour

## The Maryland rule

Md. Code, Com. Law 15-601.1, as read on November 8, 2026:

"The following are exempt from attachment: (1) The greater of: (i) 75 percent of the disposable wages due; or (ii) 30 times the State minimum hourly wage in effect at the time the wages are due, multiplied by the number of weeks during which the wages due were earned; and (2) Any medical insurance payment deducted from an employee's wages by the employer. (c) The amount subject to attachment shall be calculated per pay period."

- Medical insurance payments deducted by the employer are exempt too, so the tool takes them off disposable wages first.
- Semimonthly and monthly periods use 13/6 and 13/3 weeks unless you enter the weeks the wages were earned.

## Medical insurance comes off first

Maryland also exempts medical insurance payments the employer deducts. Biweekly gross of $2,000.00 with $500.00 of taxes and $80.00 of health insurance: disposable wages are $1,500.00, the Maryland base is $1,420.00, and the most to withhold is $355.00 (federal limit $375.00).

## Questions

### How much can be garnished from a paycheck in Maryland?

For a creditor judgment, the lesser of 25% of disposable wages or the amount above 30 times the Maryland minimum wage, after medical insurance payments come off. On $1,000.00 of weekly disposable earnings that is $250.00; on $2,000.00 every two weeks it is $500.00.

### Is the limit taken from gross or net pay?

From disposable earnings: gross pay minus only the deductions the law requires, such as income taxes, Social Security and Medicare. Voluntary deductions such as health premiums and 401(k) stay in.

### What if the employee already has child support withheld?

Support withheld this pay period counts against the same limit, so the creditor gets only what is left of $250.00 on $1,000.00 a week, which can be nothing.
