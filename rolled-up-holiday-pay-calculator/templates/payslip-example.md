Holiday accrued this period: 35 x 12.07% = 4.2245 hours (4 hours 13 minutes), to the nearest hour: 4. Tax, National Insurance and pension are worked out on the gross by your payroll software.

## Getting the line right

- Show it on its own. GOV.UK: "Employers should tell their workers if they intend to start using rolled-up holiday pay and for this payment to be clearly marked as a separate item on each payslip."
- Pay it with the work. "The holiday pay should be paid at the same time as the worker is paid for the work done in each pay period."
- On top of pay at or above the minimum wage. "Rolled-up holiday pay is to be paid in addition to the worker’s normal salary, which should be at National Minimum Wage or above."
- Label the rate when it is not 12.07%, for a contract above 5.6 weeks.
- Round each pay period. The calculator's default is the nearest penny; GOV.UK's worked-example steps can differ by a few pence, and the calculator shows both.

> Worked out under GOV.UK's guidance for Great Britain; Northern Ireland has its own rules and is not covered. Whether a worker is an irregular-hours or part-year worker depends on the contract. Not legal advice.

## Questions

### How should rolled-up holiday pay show on a payslip?

As a separate item on each payslip, paid at the same time as the pay for the work done in that pay period (GOV.UK).

### Is rolled-up holiday pay taxed?

The Security Industry Authority's GOV.UK guidance tells approved contractors to make sure holiday pay is included in workers' gross pay and is subject to tax and National Insurance.
