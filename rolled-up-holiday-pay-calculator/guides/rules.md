# Rolled-up holiday pay rules

Rolled-up holiday pay is allowed only for irregular-hours and part-year workers, in leave years beginning on or after 1 April 2024: it is 12.07% of total pay in each pay period, paid with the work and shown as a separate payslip item; of GOV.UK's examples, 2 of 4 below qualify.

- Rate: 12.07%
- Leave years from: 1 April 2024

## The rules, in GOV.UK's words

- "The regulations allow employers to use rolled-up holiday pay as an additional method for calculating holiday pay for irregular hour and part-year workers only, for leave years beginning on or after 1 April 2024."
- "The calculation of holiday pay by employers is 12.07% of a worker’s total pay as 12.07% is the proportion of statutory annual leave in relation to the working weeks of each year"
- "Employers using rolled-up holiday pay should calculate it based on a worker’s total pay in a pay period."
- "Employers should tell their workers if they intend to start using rolled-up holiday pay and for this payment to be clearly marked as a separate item on each payslip."
- "The holiday pay should be paid at the same time as the worker is paid for the work done in each pay period."
- "Rolled-up holiday pay is to be paid in addition to the worker’s normal salary, which should be at National Minimum Wage or above."
- "For regular-hours workers (full- or part- time), an employer cannot include an amount for holiday pay in the hourly rate (known as ‘rolled-up holiday pay’)."

## Security contractors

The Security Industry Authority's guidance for approved contractors: "You will be breaking the law if you use rolled-up holiday pay for anyone who does not fall into these categories." Its assessors check that holiday pay is separately identified on payslips and that contractors make sure that holiday pay is included in your workers’ gross pay and is subject to tax and National Insurance payments.

> Worked out under GOV.UK's guidance for Great Britain; Northern Ireland has its own rules and is not covered. Whether a worker is an irregular-hours or part-year worker depends on the contract. Not legal advice.

## Steps

1. **Who.** The worker is an irregular-hours worker (paid hours wholly or mostly variable under the contract) or a part-year worker.
2. **When.** The leave year began on or after 1 April 2024.
3. **How much.** 12.07% of the worker's total pay in the pay period, or the higher percentage for a longer contract.
4. **How shown.** A separate item on each payslip, paid at the same time as the pay for the work, on top of pay at or above the National Minimum Wage.
5. **Tell them.** Tell workers before starting it, check whether it varies the contract, and for agency workers put it in the Key Information Document.

## Questions

### Can rolled-up holiday pay be used for part-time staff on fixed hours?

No, unless they are part-year workers. GOV.UK: "For regular-hours workers (full- or part- time), an employer cannot include an amount for holiday pay in the hourly rate (known as 'rolled-up holiday pay')."

### Does the employer have to use rolled-up holiday pay?

No. Employers can keep the 52-week reference period and pay holiday when it is taken.
