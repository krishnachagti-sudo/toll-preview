# 12.07% explained

12.07% is 5.6 weeks of statutory leave divided by the 46.4 working weeks left in the year (5.6 ÷ 46.4 = 0.12069, so 12.07%); £1,000 of pay gives £120.70 of rolled-up holiday pay and 68 hours worked accrues 8 hours of holiday.

- Rate: 12.07%
- On £1,000: £120.70

## The formula

GOV.UK: "The 12.07% figure is based on the fact that all workers are entitled to 5.6 weeks’ leave. This means that a worker’s total working weeks in a year is 46.4 (52 weeks in a year minus 5.6 weeks of leave). 12.07% of 46.4 is 5.6."

```text
5.6 ÷ (52 - 5.6) = 5.6 ÷ 46.4 = 0.120689... = 12.07%
```

## Pay: the holiday pay line

Rolled-up holiday pay at 12.07%, exact to the penny and by GOV.UK's worked-example steps.

| Example | Total pay | 12.07%, nearest penny | GOV.UK steps |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| £1,000 in a month | £1,000.00 | £120.70 | £120.70 |
| Hana: 35 hours at £10.42 | £364.70 | £44.02 | £44.06 |
| Mark: 20 hours at £11, 20 at £12 | £460.00 | £55.52 | £55.52 |

GOV.UK's tables divide the pay by 100 and round to the penny before multiplying by 12.07, which can move the answer a few pence either way. The calculator's default is 12.07% of the whole pay to the nearest penny; it can round up instead, or follow GOV.UK's steps.

## Hours: the holiday accrued

68 x 12.07% = 8.2076 hours (8 hours 12 minutes), to the nearest hour: 8. GOV.UK's Jill works 68 hours in June and accrues 8 hours. The hours can be rounded down (to zero if it is less than 30 minutes) but will be rounded up to one hour if it is 30 minutes or more than 30 minutes.

> Worked out under GOV.UK's guidance for Great Britain; Northern Ireland has its own rules and is not covered. Whether a worker is an irregular-hours or part-year worker depends on the contract. Not legal advice.

## Questions

### Where does 12.07% come from?

It is 5.6 weeks of statutory leave divided by 46.4 working weeks (52 minus 5.6). GOV.UK: "12.07% of 46.4 is 5.6."

### Why does GOV.UK's example give £44.06 when 12.07% of £364.70 is £44.02?

GOV.UK's table rounds the pay ÷ 100 step to the penny first (£3.65), then multiplies by 12.07: £44.0555, so £44.06. Multiplying the whole pay by 12.07% gives £44.01929, so £44.02. The calculator shows both.
