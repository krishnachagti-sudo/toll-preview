# Does rolled-up holiday pay include overtime?

Yes: GOV.UK bases rolled-up holiday pay on total pay in the pay period, so a week of £384.00 basic and £92.16 overtime gives £57.47 (12.07% of £476.16), not £46.35 on basic pay alone.

- With overtime: £57.47
- On basic alone: £46.35

## What GOV.UK says

- "Employers using rolled-up holiday pay should calculate it based on a worker’s total pay in a pay period."
- "The calculation of holiday pay by employers is 12.07% of a worker’s total pay as 12.07% is the proportion of statutory annual leave in relation to the working weeks of each year"
- "the calculation for rolled-up holiday pay applies to a worker’s total pay in a pay period, regardless of differing hourly rates of pay."

## Bonuses and commission

GOV.UK's rolled-up section speaks of total pay in the period and lists no exclusions. For the 52-week method, its holiday pay page says: "‘Normal’ rate of pay includes commission, regular overtime payments, and any payments related to length of service or professional qualifications. It does not usually include bonus payments." The calculator adds every pay line you give it; leave a line out only on advice.

> Worked out under GOV.UK's guidance for Great Britain; Northern Ireland has its own rules and is not covered. Whether a worker is an irregular-hours or part-year worker depends on the contract. Not legal advice.

## Questions

### Is 12.07% holiday pay calculated on basic pay or total pay?

Total pay. GOV.UK: "Employers using rolled-up holiday pay should calculate it based on a worker's total pay in a pay period."

### Do different hourly rates matter?

No. GOV.UK's Mark works 20 hours at £11 and 20 at £12: 12.07% of the £460 total, £55.52 by its steps.
