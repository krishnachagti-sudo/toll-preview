# A zero-hours year, month by month

Over a year of 958 hours paid £12,262.40, a zero-hours worker on rolled-up pay gets £1,480.09 of holiday pay in 12 monthly lines and accrues 117 hours of holiday; 5.6 weeks of their average 20.65-hour working week is 115.62 hours, close to 12.07% of all their hours (115.6306) before each month is rounded.

## The 5.6-week check

958 hours ÷ 46.4 working weeks = 20.647 hours a week; x 5.6 weeks = 115.62 hours. Rounding each month to the hour gives 117. 12.07% of all the pay is £1,480.07; the monthly lines add up to £1,480.09.

## Starters and leavers

GOV.UK: "If an irregular hour worker or part-year worker does not take their accrued holiday entitlement by the time they leave employment, they should be paid for this untaken holiday (known as ‘payment in lieu’)." "If annual leave is carried over where a worker is paid using rolled-up holiday pay, the leave will already have been paid at the time the work was done."

For a team, run each month's payroll through [the calculator](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/rolled-up-holiday-pay-calculator/).

## Questions

### How much holiday does a zero-hours worker get in a year?

12.07% of the hours they work, accrued each pay period and rounded to the nearest hour. That is 5.6 weeks of their average working week.

### What about starters and leavers?

They accrue from their first pay period. A leaver is paid in lieu for accrued holiday not taken; with rolled-up pay, holiday already paid with the work is not paid again.
