# Is rolled-up holiday pay legal?

Yes: rolled-up holiday pay is legal in Great Britain for irregular-hours and part-year workers in leave years beginning on or after 1 April 2024, paid as 12.07% of total pay and shown separately on each payslip; it is still not allowed for regular-hours workers, full or part time.

- Leave years from: 1 April 2024
- Rate: 12.07%

## What GOV.UK says now

- "The regulations allow employers to use rolled-up holiday pay as an additional method for calculating holiday pay for irregular hour and part-year workers only, for leave years beginning on or after 1 April 2024."
- "For regular-hours workers (full- or part- time), an employer cannot include an amount for holiday pay in the hourly rate (known as ‘rolled-up holiday pay’)."
- "You will be breaking the law if you use rolled-up holiday pay for anyone who does not fall into these categories."

GOV.UK's example workers and one casual worker with an older leave year.

| Worker | Type | Legal to roll up? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kevin: hospitality, hours vary each week | irregular-hours | yes |
| Paul: rotating 15 and 20-hour weeks, no overtime | regular-hours | no |
| Melanie: seasonal farm work, unpaid in winter | part-year | yes |
| A casual worker whose leave year began 1 October 2023 | irregular-hours | no |

- Kevin: hospitality, hours vary each week: This is an irregular-hours worker with a leave year from 1 April 2026, so rolled-up holiday pay can be used, as a separate payslip item paid with the work.
- Paul: rotating 15 and 20-hour weeks, no overtime: This is a regular-hours worker: rolled-up holiday pay is not allowed. Holiday is paid when it is taken, at a week's pay.
- Melanie: seasonal farm work, unpaid in winter: This is a part-year worker with a leave year from 1 April 2026, so rolled-up holiday pay can be used, as a separate payslip item paid with the work.
- A casual worker whose leave year began 1 October 2023: This is an irregular-hours worker, but the leave year began on 1 October 2023: rolled-up holiday pay can be used from the first leave year beginning on or after 1 April 2024.

## Conditions that come with it

- "Employers should tell their workers if they intend to start using rolled-up holiday pay and for this payment to be clearly marked as a separate item on each payslip."
- "The holiday pay should be paid at the same time as the worker is paid for the work done in each pay period."
- "Rolled-up holiday pay is to be paid in addition to the worker’s normal salary, which should be at National Minimum Wage or above."

> Worked out under GOV.UK's guidance for Great Britain; Northern Ireland has its own rules and is not covered. Whether a worker is an irregular-hours or part-year worker depends on the contract. Not legal advice.

## Questions

### Is rolled-up holiday pay legal in the UK now?

Yes for irregular-hours and part-year workers, for leave years beginning on or after 1 April 2024 (GOV.UK). Not for regular-hours workers.

### What if an old answer says rolled-up holiday pay is unlawful?

Check its date. GOV.UK's current guidance allows it for irregular-hours and part-year workers from leave years beginning on or after 1 April 2024.

### What happens if a worker should have had rolled-up pay and did not?

GOV.UK: workers can carry over their whole leave entitlement if they did not receive rolled-up holiday pay that they were entitled to.
