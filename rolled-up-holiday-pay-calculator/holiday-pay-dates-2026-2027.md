# Holiday pay dates, November 2026 to December 2027

From November 2026 to December 2027 England and Wales have 10 bank holidays and Scotland 12, starting with 30 November 2026; for irregular-hours workers a bank holiday is not extra paid leave unless the contract gives it, and the National Living Wage rises to £13.21 on 1 April 2027.

- England and Wales bank holidays: 10
- Scotland bank holidays: 12

## What the dates mean

- Bank holidays: GOV.UK says they do not have to be given as paid leave, and an employer can include them in statutory annual leave.
- With rolled-up pay, holiday was paid with the work, so a bank holiday taken off is not paid again as holiday unless the contract says otherwise.
- The minimum wage rise on 1 April 2027 raises the pay the 12.07% is worked out on for workers paid at the minimum.
- Records: From 6 April 2026, employers must keep detailed records of annual leave and holiday pay for a minimum of 6 years from the date they were made.

> Northern Ireland bank holidays are not listed: this calculator covers Great Britain only. GOV.UK's timeline says the dates for the zero-hours contract reforms will be set after consultation.

> Worked out under GOV.UK's guidance for Great Britain; Northern Ireland has its own rules and is not covered. Whether a worker is an irregular-hours or part-year worker depends on the contract. Not legal advice.

## Questions

### Do zero-hours workers get paid bank holidays?

Bank holidays do not have to be given as paid leave; an employer can include them in the 5.6 weeks of statutory leave (GOV.UK). An irregular-hours worker accrues 12.07% of hours worked either way.
