# What is rolled-up holiday pay?

Rolled-up holiday pay is an extra 12.07% of total pay added to every payslip for irregular-hours and part-year workers, instead of paying holiday when it is taken: on £1,000 of pay that is £120.70, and on £500 it is £60.35.

- On £1,000: £120.70
- On £500: £60.35

## Meaning

GOV.UK: "Rolled-up holiday pay allows employers to include an additional amount with every payslip to cover a worker’s holiday pay, as opposed to paying holiday pay when a worker takes annual leave."

## In short

- Who: irregular-hours workers (paid hours wholly or mostly variable) and part-year workers.
- From: leave years beginning on or after 1 April 2024.
- How much: 12.07% of total pay in the pay period, or more with a longer contract.
- How shown: a separate payslip item, paid with the work.
- Optional: employers can pay holiday when it is taken instead, using the 52-week average.

The [12.07% figure](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/rolled-up-holiday-pay-calculator/guides/12-07-explained/) is 5.6 weeks of leave over 46.4 working weeks.

> Worked out under GOV.UK's guidance for Great Britain; Northern Ireland has its own rules and is not covered. Whether a worker is an irregular-hours or part-year worker depends on the contract. Not legal advice.
