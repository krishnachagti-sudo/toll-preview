# Rolled-up holiday pay vs the 52-week reference period

Rolled-up pays 12.07% of total pay every pay period (£361.19 over the 10 weeks below), while the 52-week method pays the average of the last 52 paid weeks when leave is taken (£374.07 for a week of leave, from the 8 paid weeks here).

- Rolled-up over 10 weeks: £361.19
- A week of leave, 52-week method: £374.07

## Which suits

From GOV.UK's guidance.

| Question | Rolled-up | 52-week reference period |
| --- | --- | --- |
| When is holiday paid? | With the work, every pay period | When the leave is taken |
| What is the payslip line? | 12.07% of total pay, a separate item | A week's average pay per week of leave |
| Admin each pay run | One multiplication per worker | A look-back of up to 104 weeks for each leave |
| Pay when leave is taken | Nothing more: already paid | The average |
| Leave carried over | Already paid when the work was done | Paid when taken |
| Off sick or on statutory leave | Average of total earnings per pay period in the 52-week relevant period | Those weeks are skipped in the average |
| Who can use it | Irregular-hours and part-year workers, leave years from 1 April 2024 | Irregular-hours and part-year workers |

GOV.UK: "Employers that do not want to use rolled-up holiday pay for irregular hour and part-year workers can continue to use the existing 52-week reference period to calculate holiday pay for irregular hour workers if they choose to do so"

> Worked out under GOV.UK's guidance for Great Britain; Northern Ireland has its own rules and is not covered. Whether a worker is an irregular-hours or part-year worker depends on the contract. Not legal advice.

## Questions

### Which holiday pay method should I use for irregular-hours workers?

Either is allowed for irregular-hours and part-year workers in leave years from 1 April 2024: rolled-up pay adds 12.07% to every payslip; the 52-week method pays a week's average pay when leave is taken.

### What happens to rolled-up pay when the worker is off sick?

GOV.UK: their rolled-up holiday pay for that period is worked out from the average of their total earnings in each pay period during the 52-week relevant period.
