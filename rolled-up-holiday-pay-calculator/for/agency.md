# Agency workers' holiday pay

An agency worker on variable hours paid £512.40 for a week gets £61.85 of rolled-up holiday pay (12.07%) if they meet the irregular-hours or part-year definition, and accrues 5 hours of holiday for 40 hours worked.

- Holiday pay that week: £61.85
- Hours accrued: 5

## When the 12.07% rules apply

GOV.UK: "The accrual method to work out entitlement will apply to an agency worker if the agency worker’s arrangements fall within the meanings of both a ‘worker’ (as already defined) and either an ‘irregular hours worker’ or a ‘part-year worker’, as per the new definition in the Working Time Regulations."

An agency worker who is a worker but neither irregular-hours nor part-year continues to accrue one twelfth of their entitlement at the start of each month in their first year.

## Telling the worker

GOV.UK: "Employers of agency workers must include this information in the agency worker’s Key Information Document." The line must still be a separate item on each payslip and paid with the work.

## The amount

£512.40 x 12.07% = £61.84668, so £61.85. GOV.UK's worked-example steps give £61.80.

> Worked out under GOV.UK's guidance for Great Britain; Northern Ireland has its own rules and is not covered. Whether a worker is an irregular-hours or part-year worker depends on the contract. Not legal advice.

## Questions

### Do agency workers get rolled-up holiday pay?

They can if their arrangements make them an irregular-hours or part-year worker. GOV.UK says employers of agency workers must include it in the agency worker's Key Information Document.

### How does an agency worker with fixed hours accrue holiday?

If they are a worker but not an irregular-hours or part-year worker, they accrue one twelfth of their entitlement at the start of each month in their first year (GOV.UK).
