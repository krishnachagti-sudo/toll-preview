# Part-year workers' holiday pay

A seasonal worker who works 26 weeks of 40 hours at £510.00 a week gets £61.56 of rolled-up holiday pay each week (£1,600.56 over the season) and accrues 5 hours of holiday a week, about 125.5 hours over the season.

- Holiday pay a week: £61.56
- Hours accrued a week: 5

## The definition

GOV.UK: "A worker is a part-year worker, in relation to a leave year, if, under the terms of their contract, they are required to work only part of that year and there are periods within that year (during the term of the contract) of at least a week which they are not required to work and for which they are not paid." Its example is Melanie, a seasonal farm worker paid only in spring and summer.

## Over the season

26 weeks of 40 hours at £510.00 a week.

| Item | Each week | Over 26 weeks |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Pay for work | £510.00 | £13,260.00 |
| Rolled-up holiday pay (12.07%) | £61.56 | £1,600.56 |
| Holiday hours accrued (rounded each week) | 5 | 130 |

Rounding each week to the hour gives 130 hours over the season; 12.07% of all 1040 hours is 125.528. GOV.UK rounds per pay period.

> Worked out under GOV.UK's guidance for Great Britain; Northern Ireland has its own rules and is not covered. Whether a worker is an irregular-hours or part-year worker depends on the contract. Not legal advice.

## Questions

### Who is a part-year worker?

Someone whose contract requires them to work only part of the year, with periods of at least a week in it that they are not required to work and are not paid for (GOV.UK).

### How much holiday does a part-year worker get?

They accrue 12.07% of the hours they work in each pay period (more with a longer contract), for leave years beginning on or after 1 April 2024.
