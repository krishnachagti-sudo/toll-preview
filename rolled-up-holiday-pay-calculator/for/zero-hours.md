# Zero-hours holiday pay

A zero-hours worker paid £403.20 for a week gets £48.67 of rolled-up holiday pay (12.07%) as a separate payslip line, and accrues 4 hours of holiday for 31.5 hours worked; over the 4 weeks below that is £117.04 and 9 hours.

- Holiday pay that week: £48.67
- Hours accrued: 4

## Who counts

GOV.UK: "A worker is an irregular hours worker, in relation to a leave year, if the number of paid hours that they will work in each pay period during the term of their contract in that year is, under the terms of their contract, wholly or mostly variable." Its example is Kevin, a hospitality worker on a casual (zero-hours) contract whose hours change every week.

## Four weeks of a casual worker

Made-up weeks for one worker paid weekly at £12.80 an hour, holiday pay at 12.07%.

| Pay week | Hours | Pay | Holiday pay line | Hours accrued |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Week to 9 Oct | 18 | £230.40 | £27.81 | 2 |
| Week to 16 Oct | 31.5 | £403.20 | £48.67 | 4 |
| Week to 23 Oct | 0 | £0.00 | £0.00 | 0 |
| Week to 30 Oct | 26.25 | £336.00 | £40.56 | 3 |

A week with no work has no pay, so no holiday pay line and no accrual. With rolled-up pay, the worker is not paid again when they take the leave: it was paid with the work.

For a whole team at once, paste the payroll into [the calculator](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/rolled-up-holiday-pay-calculator/): it adds the holiday pay column and the hours to every row.

> Worked out under GOV.UK's guidance for Great Britain; Northern Ireland has its own rules and is not covered. Whether a worker is an irregular-hours or part-year worker depends on the contract. Not legal advice.

## Questions

### What is the holiday pay rate for zero-hours workers?

12.07% of their total pay in each pay period if the employer rolls it up, or a week's average pay from the last 52 paid weeks when leave is taken. Their holiday accrues at 12.07% of hours worked.

### Is a zero-hours worker an irregular-hours worker?

Usually: GOV.UK's example Kevin, on a casual or zero-hours contract with hours that vary each pay period, would qualify if his contract says his hours will be wholly or mostly variable.
