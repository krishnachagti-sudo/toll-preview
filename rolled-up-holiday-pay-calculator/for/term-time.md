# Rolled-up holiday pay for term-time workers

A term-time worker whose contract has unpaid school holidays is a part-year worker, so rolled-up pay can be used: a month of 96 hours paid £1,238.40 gets £149.47 of holiday pay (12.07%) and accrues 12 hours of holiday.

- Holiday pay that month: £149.47
- Hours accrued: 12

## Part-year, even on fixed hours

GOV.UK: "A worker is a part-year worker, in relation to a leave year, if, under the terms of their contract, they are required to work only part of that year and there are periods within that year (during the term of the contract) of at least a week which they are not required to work and for which they are not paid." It adds: "This includes part-year workers who may have fixed hours."

The check for a school catering assistant on fixed hours with unpaid holidays: This is a part-year worker with a leave year from 1 September 2026, so rolled-up holiday pay can be used, as a separate payslip item paid with the work.

## Pay spread across the year

GOV.UK: "The regulations require that there must be a period of at least one week 'for which they are not paid' which means that it would still be possible for a worker to be paid 'during' that period so long as there is no expectation of them working in that period and nor are they receiving payment 'for' that period."

## Holidays already paid

GOV.UK: "If annual leave is carried over where a worker is paid using rolled-up holiday pay, the leave will already have been paid at the time the work was done."

> Worked out under GOV.UK's guidance for Great Britain; Northern Ireland has its own rules and is not covered. Whether a worker is an irregular-hours or part-year worker depends on the contract. Not legal advice.

## Questions

### Can term-time workers get rolled-up holiday pay?

Yes if they are part-year workers: the contract requires work for only part of the year, with periods of at least a week they are not required to work and are not paid for. Fixed hours do not stop someone being a part-year worker.

### What if their pay is spread over 12 months?

GOV.UK says the unpaid period must be one "for which they are not paid": being paid during it is possible so long as no work is expected and the pay is not for that period. Check what the pay is for in the contract.
