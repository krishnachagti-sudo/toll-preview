Average your worker's weeks

Weekly pay, most recent week first; 0 for a week with no pay.

Open the 52-week calculator

The rule

  • "Where a worker has irregular hours or works part of the year, employers can calculate their holiday pay using an average from the last 52 weeks in which they have worked and have earned pay."
  • "Any weeks that are before the 104 complete weeks prior to the first day of the worker’s holiday are not included."
  • "The reference period must not include weeks where the worker received no pay or weeks when a worker was for any amount of time on sick leave or statutory leave, such as maternity leave."

Worked out under GOV.UK's guidance for Great Britain; Northern Ireland has its own rules and is not covered. Whether a worker is an irregular-hours or part-year worker depends on the contract. Not legal advice.

Sources

Steps

  1. List the weeks. Start with the last complete week (Sunday to Saturday, or the worker's own pay week) ending on or before the first day of leave, and go back.
  2. Skip weeks. Leave out weeks with no pay and any week with sick leave or statutory leave such as maternity leave; count earlier weeks instead.
  3. Stop. Stop at 52 counted weeks, or at 104 weeks back, whichever comes first.
  4. Average. Add up the pay in the counted weeks and divide by how many there are.

Questions

How far back does the 52-week reference period go?

Up to 104 complete weeks before the first day of the holiday. With fewer than 52 paid weeks in that time, the average is over the paid weeks found.

What if the worker started 20 weeks ago?

Use the complete weeks there are. On the first 20 weeks of GOV.UK's example that is £158.42 a week (£3,010.00 ÷ 19 paid weeks = £158.42 a week).
