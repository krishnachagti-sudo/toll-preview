# California rent increase cap

California caps a rent increase at 5% plus the regional CPI change, at most 10%, over any 12 months (Civ. Code § 1947.12 (Tenant Protection Act)). For an increase taking effect March 1, 2027 the figure is 8.7% in the Los Angeles Area, 8.1% in the Riverside Area, 8.2% in the San Diego Area, 8.8% in the San Francisco Area and 8.6% in all other counties; on $2,000 rent in Los Angeles County that is $2,174. Written notice: 30 days for an increase of 10% or less, by January 30, 2027.

## Units outside the cap

- A unit issued a certificate of occupancy within the past 15 years (rolling).
- A single-family home not owned or controlled by a corporation or real estate investment trust.
- A duplex where the owner lived in one unit when the tenant moved into the other, while the owner still lives there.
- Housing restricted as affordable by deed, government agreement or other recorded document, or under a subsidy agreement.
- A mobilehome, unless owned and offered for rent by the park's owner or manager.
- A dorm.
- A unit under a more protective local rent control ordinance (that ordinance applies instead).

## What the official source says

Civ. Code § 1947.12 (Tenant Protection Act): "Annual rent increases are capped at "5% plus the percentage change in the cost of living," up to a maximum of 10%."

Civ. Code § 1947.12 (Tenant Protection Act): "In areas where April data is not published, March data is used."

Civ. Code § 1947.12 (Tenant Protection Act): "For all other counties, the change in the cost of living is measured using the April California consumer price index data published by the California Department of Industrial Relations."

Civ. Code § 1947.12 (Tenant Protection Act): "Landlords cannot raise rent more than 10% total or 5% plus the percentage change in the cost of living – whichever is lower – over a 12-month period."

Civ. Code § 1947.12 (Tenant Protection Act): "If the rent increase is 10% or less, landlords must provide notice 30 days before the increase can take effect. If the rent increase is more than 10%, the landlord must provide notice 90 days before it can take effect."

Civ. Code § 1947.12 (Tenant Protection Act): "Units issued a certificate of occupancy within the past 15 years (this is a rolling timeline, so tenants will gain protection once their building turns 15)"

Civ. Code § 1947.12 (Tenant Protection Act): "It is not enough for a landlord to call, text, or email that they plan on raising the rent."

Civ. Code § 1947.12 (Tenant Protection Act): "If the tenants of a unit move out and new tenants move in, the landlord may establish the initial rent to charge."

Civ. Code § 1947.12 (Tenant Protection Act): "Local rent increase caps do not apply to buildings that have a certificate of occupancy issued after February 1, 1995, or to most single-family homes or condos."

## Questions

### What is the AB 1482 cap for 2026-27?

For increases taking effect August 1, 2026 to July 31, 2027: Los Angeles Area 8.7%, Riverside Area 8.1%, San Diego Area 8.2%, San Francisco Area 8.8% and All other counties 8.6% (California Attorney General).

### How much notice for a rent increase in California?

30 days if the increase (with any others in the past 12 months) is 10% or less, 90 days if it is more than 10% (Civ. Code § 827, as the Attorney General describes it).

### Is my building exempt from AB 1482?

Units issued a certificate of occupancy within the past 15 years are exempt (a rolling count), as are single-family homes not owned by a corporation or REIT, owner-occupied duplexes, deed-restricted affordable housing and dorms; a unit under a stricter local ordinance follows that ordinance.
