# Maine rent increase rules

Maine sets no percentage cap in the sections read; a landlord must give at least 45 days' written notice of an increase, and 75 days if it is 10% or more in 12 months (14 M.R.S. § 6015). For a 15% increase starting March 1, 2027, serve the notice by December 16, 2026.

- Serve notice by: December 16, 2026
- Notice: 75 days
- Cap: None in the sections read

## The rule

14 M.R.S. § 6015: "rent or mandatory recurring fees charged for residential estates may be increased by the landlord only after providing at least 45 days' written notice to the tenant."

14 M.R.S. § 6015: "If rent charged for a residential estate is increased by the landlord by 10% or more, the landlord must provide at least 75 days' written notice to the tenant."

14 M.R.S. § 6015: "If the landlord increases rent more than once in a 12-month period, and the increases add up to a total increase of 10% or more, the landlord must provide at least 75 days' written notice prior to any increase that brings the total increase in rent to 10% or more."

## Notes

- The 75-day rule does not apply to housing kept affordable by a recorded document or under subsidy program restrictions (14 M.R.S. § 6015(2)).
- Rent may not be increased while the unit breaches the warranty of habitability (14 M.R.S. § 6016).
- No percentage cap appears in the sections read.

## Questions

### How much notice to raise rent in Maine?

At least 45 days' written notice; at least 75 days if the increase, alone or with others in 12 months, is 10% or more (14 M.R.S. § 6015).

### Is there a rent cap in Maine?

No percentage cap appears in the sections read; rent may not be raised while the unit breaches the warranty of habitability (14 M.R.S. § 6016).
