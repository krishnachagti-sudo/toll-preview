# Raising rent legally in five steps

For an Oregon unit at $2,000, a new rent of $2,150 from March 1, 2027 is within the 10% cap ($2,200), and a notice dated November 20, 2026 meets the 90-day deadline of December 1, 2026. The five checks below apply in every covered place.

## Where people slip

- Using last year's figure: every cap here is published for a window, and the start date picks it.
- Counting from the wrong rent: California and Washington count every increase in 12 months against the lowest rent of that period.
- A short notice: a late notice in California, Oregon or Washington moves the earliest start date, it does not shorten the period.
- Assuming an exemption: Oregon and Washington ask for the facts of an exemption in the notice itself.

## Steps

1. **Check coverage.** Find whether the unit is under a state cap or a local ordinance, and whether an exemption (building age, owner occupancy, affordable housing) applies.
2. **Compute the cap.** Take the figure in force on the start date and apply it to the right base rent.
3. **Pick the start date.** Choose a date that leaves the full notice period, after the first year of the tenancy and 12 months after the last increase where those rules apply.
4. **Serve the notice.** Give written notice by an allowed method with the content the rule asks for (amount, new rent, date; Washington's form).
5. **Keep proof.** Keep a copy, the date and the method of service, and update the lease record.

## Questions

### Can I raise rent during the first year of a lease?

Not in Oregon (no increase in the first year of a tenancy) or Washington (none in the first 12 months); elsewhere a fixed-term lease usually holds the rent until it ends.
