# New York rent increase rules

New York has no statewide cap; for a renewal with an increase of 5% or more, the notice is 30, 60 or 90 days by the length of the tenancy (Real Prop. Law § 226-c). For a tenant of two years or more and a new rent from March 1, 2027, serve it by December 1, 2026.

- Serve notice by: December 1, 2026
- Notice: 90 days
- Cap: None in the sections read

## The rule

A landlord who will renew a tenancy with a rent increase of 5% or more, or not renew it, must give written notice: 30 days for occupancy under one year with no lease of at least a year; 60 days for occupancy of one to two years or a lease of one to two years; 90 days for occupancy over two years or a lease of at least two years, whichever is longer.

If the notice is late, the tenancy continues on its existing terms until the notice period has run.

The notice must also say whether the unit is covered by the Good Cause Eviction law, and for a covered unit justify any increase above the local rent standard.

## Questions

### How much notice to raise rent in New York?

For an increase of 5% or more: 30 days if the tenant has been there under a year (and has no lease of a year or more), 60 days for one to two years, 90 days for two years or more (Real Prop. Law § 226-c).

### Is there a rent cap in New York?

No statewide cap. Rent-stabilized apartments follow the Rent Guidelines Board, and in New York City and opted-in municipalities an increase above the Good Cause local rent standard must be justified.
