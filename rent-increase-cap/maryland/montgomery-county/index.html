Montgomery County rent stabilization law (DHCA)

Montgomery County, Maryland rent increase cap

A rent-stabilized unit in Montgomery County, Maryland may rise 5.2% for an increase between July 1, 2026 and June 30, 2027 ($2,104 on $2,000 rent), once every 12 months. Every landlord, stabilized or exempt, owes 90 days' written notice: by December 1, 2026 for a new rent from March 1, 2027.

The rules

DHCA: "CPI-U +3%, or A flat 6%, whichever is lower."

DHCA: "Maximum allowable rent increase is 5.2%"

DHCA: "These rates remain in effect until June 30, 2027."

DHCA: "For both regulated and exempt units, landlords must provide a written 90-day notice before increasing rent."

DHCA: "Rent for regulated units may only be increased once every 12 months."

DHCA: "Unless otherwise exempt, all County-licensed residential rental units that are at least 23 years old are rent-stabilized under Montgomery County Law."

Notes

  • Banked increases (unused allowance, up to 10% in total) and fair-return increases are not worked out here.
  • The 90-day notice must be delivered by US mail or in person with a signed receipt.
  • Rules of municipalities inside the county that have their own laws were not checked.

Questions

What is the Montgomery County rent increase limit for 2026?

5.2% for rent-stabilized units from July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027 (CPI-U of 2.2% plus 3%, capped at 6%).

Which Montgomery County units are rent-stabilized?

County-licensed rental units at least 23 years old, unless exempt; a unit turns 23 on January 1 of the 23rd year after its SDAT year built.
