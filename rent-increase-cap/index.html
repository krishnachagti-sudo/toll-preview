The rules

How much, from which date, with how much notice.

Three questions, each answered from the official source for the place, with the date it was read.

What it does not cover

Legal advice, states without a row, local ordinances other than the four listed, and checks of public records.

Read the full list
  • Not legal advice. We are not a law firm, and this tool does not review your lease, your building or your case.
  • Figures are as published on the dates shown, for the windows shown; an increase taking effect outside a published window is refused, not guessed.
  • Exemptions (building age, owner occupancy, local coverage) are applied from your answers; the tool does not check public records.
  • Local ordinances other than the City of Los Angeles, unincorporated Los Angeles County, New York City and Montgomery County, Maryland were not checked.
  • States without a row are not covered: the tool refuses them rather than guess.

Late rent instead? See the rent late fee checker. A rental in Ontario, Canada? See the Ontario rent increase calculator.

How much

A state cap, a CPI region or a local ordinance, each with its own ceiling.

Read the full rules

California caps an increase at 5% plus the regional CPI change (at most 10%) over any 12 months, Oregon and Washington at 7% plus CPI (at most 10%) for each calendar year, DC rent-controlled units at CPI-W plus 2%. Local ordinances in Los Angeles, unincorporated LA County, New York City and Montgomery County set their own, lower figures.

From which date

The figure in force on the day the new rent starts, from its published window.

Read the full rules

Every figure is published for a window: August to July in California, the calendar year in Oregon and Washington, May to April in DC, October to September for New York City leases, July to June for the local ordinances. The page picks the figure in force on the day the new rent starts.

With how much notice

30 to 90 days by place, increase or tenancy, with the last day to serve it.

Read the full rules

30 or 90 days in California by the size of the increase, 90 in Oregon and Washington, the first rent day more than 60 days out in DC, 30, 60 or 90 days in New York by the length of the tenancy, 45 or 75 in Maine, 60 before the lease ends in Delaware and Virginia, 90 in Montgomery County.
