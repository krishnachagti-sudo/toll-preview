# Cleaning production rates, converted

A production rate is square feet an hour: 1,500 sq ft vacuumed in 25 minutes is 3,600 sq ft an hour (16.67 minutes per 1,000 sq ft), so 25,000 sq ft at that rate takes 6.94 hours.

- Sq ft per hour: 3,600
- Hours for 25,000 sq ft: 6.94

## Measure your own rates

- Time one task at a time (vacuuming, mopping, restrooms) on a normal visit, from the start of the task to the end, including moving between rooms.
- Measure the area the task covered, or count the fixtures or panes.
- Do it on three visits; use the slowest, or the average if they are close.
- Time a first deep clean separately: it takes longer than routine visits.
- Re-time when the equipment changes (a wider vacuum, a ride-on scrubber) or the building does.

[ISSA's Cleaning Times](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/cleaning-bid-calculator/issa-612-explained/) publishes task times for estimating; it is a paid, copyrighted book, and no official public source publishes cleaning production rates, so no rate is built into the calculator.

> Production rates, minutes per fixture or pane, extras and the target range are the figures you enter; this tool supplies no industry production rates or market prices. A bid is a business decision: check it against your own costs, your timed rates and the local market. Not legal, tax or accounting advice.

## Questions

### How many square feet can a cleaner clean per hour?

It depends on the task, the floor and the equipment, so time your own crew. At 10 minutes per 1,000 sq ft a task runs at 6,000 sq ft an hour; at 20 minutes, 3,000.

### How do I turn minutes per 1,000 sq ft into sq ft per hour?

Divide 60,000 by the minutes: 60,000 / 12 = 5,000 sq ft an hour.
