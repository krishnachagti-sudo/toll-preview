How each phase is priced

  • Rough clean: debris out, a first sweep and dusting while trades are still on site. Usually the fastest per square foot, often with a haul-away or dumpster charge.
  • Final clean: the detail clean before handover: every surface, fixture, floor and pane. The slowest phase, and the one where restrooms and glass belong as counted lines.
  • Touch-up: after the punch list, dust and marks left by the last trades. A quick pass over the whole area.
  • Each phase's hours times the billed rate ($33.91 an hour here) is its price; extras carry overhead and margin like labor.

Glass priced per pane is inside the final clean here: 48 panes at 6 min each is $162.79, $3.39 a pane.

The bid is free on the calculator page; a proposal PDF costs $3.00 a job, a corrected one for the same job is free for 30 days, and proposals never cost more than $19 in a month.

Production rates, minutes per fixture or pane, extras and the target range are the figures you enter; this tool supplies no industry production rates or market prices. A bid is a business decision: check it against your own costs, your timed rates and the local market. Not legal, tax or accounting advice.

Questions

How much should I charge for post-construction cleaning per square foot?

Work it out from your hours and divide by the square feet at the end. At the sample rates on this page the 12,000 sq ft job is $1,297.35, $0.108 per sq ft; your own rates and wage give your own figure.

Should rough, final and touch-up be priced separately?

Yes, as separate lines: they happen weeks apart and a general contractor may award or cancel one of them. Here they are $519.57, $676.04 and $101.74.

How much to charge per hour for post-construction cleaning?

The billed rate is the loaded wage plus supplies and overhead, divided by one minus the margin. In this example the loaded wage is $22.43 an hour and the billed rate $33.91 an hour.
