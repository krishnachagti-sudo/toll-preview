# Fully loaded hourly rate for cleaners: BLS wages and payroll costs by state

A cleaner's hour costs more than the wage: the BLS May 2025 national median for janitors, $17.71, is $19.17 with the employer's federal payroll taxes alone (8.25%) and $23.02 with BLS's average benefit costs for service jobs (29.96%); the worked office bills that hour at $29.20 after supplies, overhead and margin.

- US median wage: $17.71
- With payroll taxes: $19.17
- With BLS benefits: $23.02

## What loading leaves out

- State unemployment and workers' compensation: your rates depend on your state, class code and claims; enter them in the itemized burden.
- FUTA stops once a worker earns $7,000 in a year; charging it on every hour is the most it can be.
- Paid leave, health insurance and retirement, if you offer them (the BLS option includes average amounts).
- City and county minimum wages, which can be above the state rate.

## Questions

### What hourly rate should I charge for cleaning?

Start from the loaded cost of the hour, then add supplies, overhead and margin. In the worked office, $20.36 loaded becomes $29.20 billed.

### Should I bid cleaning by the hour or by the square foot?

Either way, build the price from hours: square feet divided by your production rate. Per square foot is then a check on the hourly build, not a substitute for it.
