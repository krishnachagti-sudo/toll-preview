How it works

How the bid is built, hours first.

Your rates give the hours, the wage and burden give a loaded cost, and overhead and margin give the price.

What it does not cover

Industry production rates, market prices, your local tax rates, or legal, tax and accounting advice.

Read the full list
  • Production rates, minutes per fixture or pane, extras and the target range are the figures you enter; this tool supplies no industry production rates or market prices.
  • Wages are BLS May 2025 estimates; payroll tax rates are from IRS Publication 15 (2026); benefit costs from BLS ECEC (June 2026). State unemployment, workers' compensation and local minimum wages vary: enter your own.
  • A bid is a business decision: check it against your own costs, your timed rates and the local market. Not legal, tax or accounting advice.

Hours from your rates

Square feet over your rate, or a count times your minutes each. No rate is built in.

Read the full rules

An area task takes square feet divided by your production rate in square feet an hour. A count task takes the count times your minutes each, over 60. Restrooms and glass are counted, not measured, because two buildings of the same size can have very different fixture and window counts. No rate is built in: ISSA's Cleaning Times is a paid, copyrighted publication, and no official source publishes cleaning production rates.

A loaded hourly cost

Your wage or a BLS figure, plus payroll taxes from IRS Pub 15 and your own rates.

IRS Publication 15 (2026)

Read the full rules

The wage is yours, or a BLS May 2025 figure for your state. Itemized burden adds the employer's 6.2% social security and 1.45% Medicare and the 0.6% FUTA from IRS Publication 15 (2026), plus your state unemployment, workers' comp and benefit rates. Or use BLS's June 2026 figure for private service occupations: $5.47 of benefits on $18.26 of wages an hour worked, 29.96%.

Price, then the check

Margin is a percent of the price: 15% margin is a 17.65% markup on cost.

Read the full rules

Supplies are a percent of loaded labor, overhead a percent of that, and the margin a percent of the price: a 15% margin is a 17.65% markup on cost. A monthly price is the visit price times visits a week times 52 weeks over 12 months. The $/sq ft is the price over the cleanable square feet; compare it with your own range rather than a number from a blog.

Sources, read November 8, 2026

BLS wages and benefit costs, IRS Publication 15 and DOL state minimum wages.

Read the full list

BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics (May 2025, through the BLS Public Data API), IRS Publication 15 (2026), BLS Employer Costs for Employee Compensation, Table 4 (June 2026) and DOL State Minimum Wage Laws (updated November 5, 2026).
