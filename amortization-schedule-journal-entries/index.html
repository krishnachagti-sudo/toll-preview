How it works

  • Straight-line, three ways: equal months, half-month or actual days; the last month takes the rounding, so the balance ends at zero.
  • Loans, payment by payment: interest on the opening balance, principal the rest; the level payment matches Excel's PMT to the cent.
  • Entries and the tie-out: one balanced journal per date, and a rollforward per account checked against your GL.

What it does not cover

Fixed assets (use the MACRS depreciation tool), whether an item is a prepaid or when revenue is earned, and the 12-month rule's exceptions. Nothing is posted to your books.

Read how it works in full

Straight-line, three ways

Equal months: the amount over the months of the term, the start month counting in full. Half-month: half a month in the first and last calendar month, full months between. Actual days: each month's days over the days of the term. Every month is rounded to the cent and the last month takes the difference, so the balance ends at exactly zero. A cancellation books the month it ends in, then refunds and writes off (or recognizes) what is left.

Loans, payment by payment

Interest is the opening balance times the rate: a twelfth of the yearly rate (30/360), or the days since the last payment over 365 or 360. Principal is the payment less the interest. The level payment matches Excel's PMT to the cent, the last payment clears what is left, and a lender's own rows can replace the computed ones.

Entries, files and the tie-out

Each date gets one balanced journal. QuickBooks Online: the journal entry import columns (Journal No., Journal Date, Account Name, Debits, Credits, Description, Name). Xero: the manual journal fields, debits positive and credits negative, at most 300 lines a file. The rollforward per balance sheet account (opening, additions, amortization, cancellations, closing) is checked against the GL balances you give.

Please read
  • Not accounting or tax advice. The tool does straight-line arithmetic on the terms you enter; whether an item is a prepaid, when revenue is earned (ASC 606) and which convention your policy uses are your accountant's call.
  • The 12-month rule check tests only the duration test in 26 CFR 1.263(a)-4(f)(1); it does not check the exceptions, the election to capitalize or economic performance for accrual-method taxpayers.
  • Nothing is posted to your books. Import the file into a test company, or one month first, and check it before importing the rest.
  • Schedules are processed in memory and not kept.

Fixed assets follow their own tax rules: for depreciation, use the MACRS depreciation tool.
