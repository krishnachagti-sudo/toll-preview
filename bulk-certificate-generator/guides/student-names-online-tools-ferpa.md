# Student names and online certificate makers

The US Department of Education's Student Privacy Policy Office says a teacher should first check with the school or district that an online tool is approved, and that a tool collecting personal information from education records under FERPA's school-official exception must meet 4 conditions, including staying under the school's direct control.

> General information, not legal advice. The US Department of Education asks teachers to check with their school or district before using an online tool with student data; this tool makes no claim about FERPA compliance.

## The four conditions, in short

From the Student Privacy Policy Office FAQ, a service collecting personally identifiable information from education records under the school-official exception must:

- provide a service the school would otherwise do with its own staff;
- be under the school's direct control for the use and upkeep of that information;
- use it only in line with the school or district's annual FERPA notice;
- not re-disclose it or use it for anything else.

The FAQ also asks teachers to talk to their IT staff first, because some tools bring security or privacy risks.

## Questions to ask any certificate maker

- Where is the list sent, and is it stored? For how long?
- Who can see it, and which other companies (sub-processors) handle it?
- Can you get it deleted, and how?
- Does it need an account, a Google or Microsoft sign-in, or Drive permissions?
- Does your district already approve it?

## How this tool handles the list

The preview and the free proof are made by the page in your browser: the list is not sent to make them. The clean PDF is made on the server from the list sent with the download, in memory, and the list is discarded with the answer; it is not stored or logged. That answers the first three questions; the last one is still your district's call.

## What to tell your principal or tech coordinator

"I want to print certificates from our class list. The tool draws the preview in the browser, makes the final PDF in memory and keeps nothing; can I use it, or is there an approved tool I should use instead?"
