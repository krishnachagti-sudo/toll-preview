How it works

  • Each name fitted on its own: measured with the font's widths and set as large as its line allows; every change is listed.
  • Where the list goes: the preview and proof are made in your browser; the clean PDF is made in memory and the list discarded.
  • Ready for the printer: half an inch inside the edge; print at 100% (Actual size).

What it does not cover

Letters outside the standard Times and Helvetica fonts (such as Ł, Cyrillic or Chinese) are flagged, not drawn. It does not decide whether your school allows an online tool with student data: ask your school or district.

Read how it works in full

Each name fitted on its own

A mail merge sets one font size for every certificate, so a long name runs past the line. Here each name is measured with the font's own character widths and set as large as its line allows; only a long name is made smaller, a very long one goes onto two lines, and every change is listed before you print.

Where the list goes

The preview and the free proof are drawn by this page in your browser, so the list is not sent to make them. The clean PDF is made on the server from the list you send with the download, in memory, and the list is discarded with the answer: it is not stored or logged.

Ready for the printer

Every line stays at least half an inch inside the paper edge, beyond the quarter inch most desktop printers cannot reach. Print at 100% (Actual size); the PDF asks the print dialog for no scaling. Card stock: print one test page first and check the paper type setting.

  • Names print in the standard Times and Helvetica fonts, so letters such as é, ñ, ø and š print as written. Letters outside those fonts (for example Ł, Cyrillic or Chinese) are flagged on the certificate they are on.
  • The designs and wording are this tool's own. Change any line; leave a line empty to leave it off.
  • General information, not legal advice. The US Department of Education asks teachers to check with their school or district before using an online tool with student data: its Student Privacy Policy Office FAQ (read November 8, 2026).
