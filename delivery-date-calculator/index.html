Delivery dates and holiday order-by dates

Find when an order arrives, or the last moment to order for delivery by a date such as Christmas, with every holiday and closure it skipped named.

Which carriers?

Tell it when you stop taking same-day orders, the days you ship, the days you are closed and how long you take to pack. It works out when an order placed now arrives, or the last moment to order for delivery by a date such as Christmas, and names every holiday and closure it skipped.

Which carriers? USPS is built in: its delivery days, postal holidays, service times and 2026 holiday send-by dates. UPS, FedEx or any other carrier works through "Another carrier": tick the days of the week it delivers, say whether it skips US federal holidays, and enter its transit days, or its send-by date copied from its own holiday page. For Canada Post or Royal Mail, choose "Another carrier" and paste its holiday dates from its own site into "Carrier closures" (over the API you list them as carrierClosures). The page's time zones are US only.

What do you want to know?

For Christmas 2026 use December 24: USPS's send-by dates are for delivery before December 25.

The rules

Two calendars, not one.

Processing days count on your calendar. Transit days count on the carrier's.

What it does not cover

Delivery guarantees, UPS and FedEx dates, time zones outside the US, and legal advice.

Read the full list

Dates are estimates from your settings and the carriers' published calendars, not delivery guarantees.

UPS and FedEx publish their own dates on ups.com and fedex.com; pick "Another carrier" and enter the date from their page.

The page's time zones are US only.

Under the FTC's Mail, Internet, or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule you need a reasonable basis for any shipping time you state, so show a ships-by date as well as an arrives-by date. This is not legal advice.

Your shipping days

Orders count from the day they are received. Processing days count on the days you ship, minus your closures and federal holidays.

Read the full rules

Orders count from the day they are received: the order day if it is a shipping day and before your cutoff, otherwise the next shipping day. Processing days are counted on your calendar: the weekdays you ship, your closures and, if you follow them, the federal holidays as OPM observes them. When a holiday falls on a Saturday, OPM gives the Friday off, so Friday, July 3, 2026 is a closed day for you.

The carrier's delivery days

Transit days count on the carrier's calendar. USPS delivers Monday to Saturday and closes on postal holidays.

usps.com: holiday shipping dates

Read the full rules

Transit days are counted on the carrier's calendar. USPS delivers Monday to Saturday and closes on postal holidays, which do not follow OPM's Fridays: Post Offices are open with normal delivery on the Friday before a Saturday holiday and closed on the Saturday, and a Sunday holiday closes the Monday. Priority Mail Express delivers every day.

For any other carrier, pick "Another carrier" and tick the days of the week it delivers: transit days are counted on those days only, and on US federal holidays it is treated as closed unless you untick that box. It is also closed on any dates you list under "Carrier closures", such as the holidays Canada Post or Royal Mail publish.

Holiday send-by dates

USPS's dates are its recommended send-by dates for delivery before December 25.

usps.com, read October 31, 2026

Read the full rules

USPS's holiday dates are its recommended send-by dates for delivery before December 25, read from usps.com on October 31, 2026. UPS and FedEx publish their own dates on ups.com and fedex.com; pick "Another carrier" and enter the date from their page.

For your site

An order-by banner for any store.

Run "Last day to order" with your settings, then press "Make the banner code". You get a few lines of plain HTML and JavaScript to paste into any site: Shopify (a Custom Liquid section), WooCommerce, Squarespace, BigCommerce or a hand-built page.

Fixed moments

The deadlines are written into the code as fixed moments, worked out from your store's clock, so the visitor's time zone cannot shift them.

No key, no server

It never holds a key and calls no server: the paid run happens once, here or from your code, and the banner costs nothing per page view.

Moves on by itself

It shows the first deadline still open, counts down in its last two days, moves to the next faster service when one passes, and says plainly when every deadline has passed.

Run it again on changes

It reads the visitor's clock only to tell whether a deadline has passed. If you change your cutoff, processing days or closures, run it again and paste the new code.
