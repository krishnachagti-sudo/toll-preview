Shipping dates, per call

Delivery dates and holiday order-by dates

Tell it when you stop taking same-day orders, the days you ship, the days you are closed and how long you take to pack. It works out when an order placed now arrives, or the last moment to order for delivery by a date such as Christmas, and names every holiday and closure it skipped.

Each run costs a fraction of a cent. Without an account you get 5 free runs a day.

What do you want to know?

For Christmas 2026 use December 24: USPS's send-by dates are for delivery before December 25.

Two calendars, not one

Your shipping days

Orders count from the day they are received: the order day if it is a shipping day and before your cutoff, otherwise the next shipping day. Processing days are counted on your calendar: the weekdays you ship, your closures and, if you follow them, the federal holidays as OPM observes them. When a holiday falls on a Saturday, OPM gives the Friday off, so Friday, July 3, 2026 is a closed day for you.

The carrier's delivery days

Transit days are counted on the carrier's calendar. USPS delivers Monday to Saturday and closes on postal holidays, which do not follow OPM's Fridays: Post Offices are open with normal delivery on the Friday before a Saturday holiday and closed on the Saturday, and a Sunday holiday closes the Monday. Priority Mail Express delivers every day. For another carrier you say which days it delivers.

Dates are estimates from your settings and the carriers' published calendars, not delivery guarantees. USPS's holiday dates are its recommended send-by dates for delivery before December 25, read from usps.com on October 31, 2026. UPS and FedEx publish their own dates on ups.com and fedex.com; pick "Another carrier" and enter the date from their page. Under the FTC's Mail, Internet, or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule you need a reasonable basis for any shipping time you state, so show a ships-by date as well as an arrives-by date. This is not legal advice.

An order-by banner for any store

Run "Last day to order" with your settings, then press "Make the banner code". You get a few lines of plain HTML and JavaScript to paste into any site: Shopify (a Custom Liquid section), WooCommerce, Squarespace, BigCommerce or a hand-built page.

  • The deadlines are written into the code as fixed moments, worked out from your store's clock, so the visitor's time zone cannot shift them.
  • It never holds a key and calls no server: the paid run happens once, here or from your code, and the banner costs nothing per page view.
  • It shows the first deadline still open, counts down in its last two days, moves to the next faster service when one passes, and says plainly when every deadline has passed.
  • It reads the visitor's clock only to tell whether a deadline has passed. If you change your cutoff, processing days or closures, run it again and paste the new code.
