FMLA, state by state

FMLA and state leave overlay

For one employee's leave in one state: the state leave laws that apply with the FMLA, which run at the same time, and the total weeks the job is protected and the state pays.

Worked example

Example: California, birth parent, leave from January 11, 2027, 10 weeks of pregnancy disability, hired June 3, 2024, 1,980 hours, 200 employees

22 weeks protected18 of them paid by the state

  • Pregnancy disability: 10 weeks protected, 10 paid10 wkprotected by FMLA 10, PDL 10; paid by SDI 10
  • Bonding: 12 weeks protected, 8 paid12 wkprotected by FMLA 2, CFRA 12; paid by PFL 8
  • Job-protected in total22 wk18 of them paid by the state

For a birth parent's leave in California starting January 11, 2027 at an employer with 200 employees, the FMLA, PDL, CFRA, SDI and PFL apply (SDI and PFL only if the state's conditions are met); PDL and CFRA run at the same time as the FMLA where the reason qualifies under both; together they protect 22 weeks of leave (10 for pregnancy disability, then 12 for bonding), 18 weeks of them paid by the state.

Case plan for one employee

The same overlay, plus a designation note you can adapt and a CSV of the parts of the leave. $1.00 per employee; updates for the same employee and state are free for 12 months. Needs an account.

What the designation note says

What is designated, how many weeks count against the FMLA, and the PTO rule during state-paid weeks.

Where the rules come from

Each state rule is quoted word for word from that state's own paid leave program, labor department or legislature site, read on November 8, 2026; every answer shows the quote and the link. Leave that qualifies under the FMLA and a state law counts against both (29 CFR 825.701(a)).

What it does not cover

  • Hawaii, Massachusetts, Rhode Island and Virginia: their official sites could not be read.
  • Whether a condition qualifies, the earnings test and benefit amounts.
  • Local ordinances and contracts that give more.
Read the coverage, the notes and the prices

The federal tests come from the FMLA eligibility checker and 29 CFR 825.701(a): leave that qualifies under the FMLA and a state law counts against both.

Covered: California, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, the District of Columbia, Iowa, Maine, Maryland, Minnesota, Montana, New Jersey, New York, Oregon and Washington. Could not be read from their official sites: Hawaii, Massachusetts, Rhode Island and Virginia. Other states: the FMLA only.

  • Not legal advice. Worked out from each state's own published text, quoted with the date we read it, and from 29 CFR Part 825.
  • States whose official sites we could not read are named as not covered, never filled in from someone else's chart.
  • Whether a condition qualifies, the earnings test and benefit amounts are decided by the employer and the state program; local ordinances and contracts can give more.
  • The FMLA coverage test assumes the employer had its current headcount for at least 20 workweeks this year or last.
