FMLA and Minnesota family and medical leave

In Minnesota, the employer may require Minnesota Paid Leave to run concurrently with the FMLA. For a birth parent at a 100-person employer, hired in June 2024 and out from January 11, 2027 with 10 weeks of pregnancy disability, the laws together protect 20 weeks (20 paid); at a 20-person employer, where the FMLA does not apply, 20 weeks.

Worked totals

Each row is the engine's answer for an employee hired June 3, 2024 with 1,980 hours in the last 12 months, leave from January 11, 2027, everyone in the state and within 75 miles, and the earnings test met. A birth counts 10 weeks of pregnancy disability, then bonding.

The birth at 100 employees: 10 weeks of pregnancy disability protected by FMLA and Minnesota Paid Leave and Pregnancy and parenting leave, then 10 weeks of bonding protected by FMLA and Minnesota Paid Leave and Pregnancy and parenting leave. Why the weeks add up this way.

  • A birth parent's pregnancy disability is counted under each state law's own-health (medical) leave unless the state names pregnancy separately (California PDL, Oregon OFLA, Iowa, Montana, Minnesota).
  • The date benefits began was not read on a Minnesota site (paidleave.mn.gov refused our reads and mn.gov/deed showed a bot check), so the tool applies the statute to any leave date and says so.

Minnesota in its own words

Read the law's own words (6 quotations)
  • "The total number of weeks that an applicant may take benefits in a single benefit year for a serious health condition is the lesser of 12 weeks, or 12 weeks minus the number of weeks within the same benefit year that the applicant received benefits for bonding, safety leave, family care, and qualifying exigency plus eight weeks." Minn. Stat. 268B.04, Financial eligibility; benefits
  • "Ninety calendar days from the date of hire, an employee has a right and is entitled to reinstatement as provided under this subdivision" Minn. Stat. 268B.09, Employment protections
  • "An employer may require leave taken under this chapter to run concurrently with leave taken for the same purpose under section 181.941 or the Family and Medical Leave Act" Minn. Stat. 268B.27, Relationship to other leave
  • "The length of the leave shall be determined by the employee, but must not exceed 12 weeks, unless agreed to by the employer." Minn. Stat. 181.941, Pregnancy and parenting leave
  • "a female employee for prenatal care, or incapacity due to pregnancy, childbirth, or related health conditions." Minn. Stat. 181.941, Pregnancy and parenting leave
  • ""Employer" means a person or entity that employs one or more employees" Minn. Stat. 181.940, Definitions

Federal side

FMLA eligibility (50 employees within 75 miles, 12 months, 1,250 hours) is worked out by the FMLA eligibility checker. Leave that qualifies under the FMLA and a state law counts against both (29 CFR 825.701(a)); state-paid weeks that the FMLA also covers must still be designated as FMLA leave, and no one can force PTO to run during them (DOL Opinion Letter FMLA2025-01-A).

Not legal advice. Worked out from each state's own published text, quoted with the date we read it, and from 29 CFR Part 825. States whose official sites we could not read are named as not covered, never filled in from someone else's chart. Whether a condition qualifies, the earnings test and benefit amounts are decided by the employer and the state program; local ordinances and contracts can give more.

Questions

Does Minnesota Paid Leave run at the same time as the FMLA in Minnesota?

An employer may require Minnesota Paid Leave to run concurrently with FMLA leave for the same purpose.

Does Pregnancy and parenting leave run at the same time as the FMLA in Minnesota?

An employer may require Minnesota Paid Leave to run with this leave (268B.27); with FMLA, 29 CFR 825.701(a) counts leave that qualifies under both against both.

How many weeks does Minnesota Paid Leave pay in Minnesota?

medical: 12 weeks; family: 12 weeks, at most 20 weeks combined (benefit year).
