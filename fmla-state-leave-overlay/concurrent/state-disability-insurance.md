# FMLA and state disability insurance

Yes, when the FMLA also covers the absence: state disability insurance pays and the FMLA protects the job, so a New Jersey birth parent with 10 weeks of disability has those weeks paid by TDI and counted as FMLA leave (22 protected, 22 paid in all with bonding), and a California one has them paid by SDI (22 protected, 18 paid).

State disability insurance we read

| State | Program | Weeks it pays | Job protection |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| California | SDI | up to 52 weeks | none from SDI; FMLA, CFRA or PDL protect the job |
| New Jersey | TDI | as certified (for a birth, usually 4 weeks before and 6 after) | while benefits are paid, from July 17, 2026, when NJFLA does not cover the leave |
| New York | Disability benefits (DBL) | not read (26 weeks combined with PFL in 52) | not read |
| Rhode Island | TDI | not covered: the state's site refused our reads | not covered |
| Hawaii | TDI | not covered: the state's site refused our reads | not covered |

- "If eligible, you can get benefit payments for up to 52 weeks." [California EDD, Am I Eligible for Disability Insurance Benefits?](https://edd.ca.gov/en/disability/Am_I_Eligible_for_DI_Benefits/)
- "Earned at least $300 with State Disability Insurance (SDI) deducted from your paycheck." [California EDD, Am I Eligible for Disability Insurance Benefits?](https://edd.ca.gov/en/disability/Am_I_Eligible_for_DI_Benefits/)
- "Your job is now protected by TDI/FLI law during your leave if you receive TDI/FLI benefits from the State or a private plan, and your leave is not already covered under the NJ Family Leave Act." [New Jersey Department of Labor, NJ Expands Job-Protected Leave for Workers](https://nj.gov/labor/myleavebenefits/worker/job-protection-information/more-protections.shtml)
- "There are no minimum employer size requirements or work history requirements with your current employer. Eligibility for TDI/FLI benefits is based on recent earnings." [New Jersey Department of Labor, NJ Expands Job-Protected Leave for Workers](https://nj.gov/labor/myleavebenefits/worker/job-protection-information/more-protections.shtml)
- "Benefits are usually payable up to four weeks before the expected date of delivery, and up to six weeks after you give birth (or up to eight weeks after a Caesarean delivery)." [New Jersey Department of Labor, Division of Temporary Disability and Family Leave Insurance, Maternity Coverage](https://www.nj.gov/labor/myleavebenefits/worker/maternity)

- "If State law provides half-pay for employees temporarily disabled because of pregnancy for six weeks, the employee would be entitled to an additional six weeks of unpaid FMLA leave (or accrued paid leave)." [29 CFR 825.701, Interaction with State laws (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/section-825.701)

> Not legal advice. Worked out from each state's own published text, quoted with the date we read it, and from 29 CFR Part 825. States whose official sites we could not read are named as not covered, never filled in from someone else's chart. Whether a condition qualifies, the earnings test and benefit amounts are decided by the employer and the state program; local ordinances and contracts can give more.
