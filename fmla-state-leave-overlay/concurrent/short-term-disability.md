# FMLA and short-term disability

It can: leave under a short-term disability plan is FMLA leave for a serious health condition if the employer designates it, so 8 weeks on disability benefits leave 4 weeks of FMLA; because those weeks are paid, neither the employee nor the employer can require PTO to be substituted (29 CFR 825.207(d)).

- "Because leave pursuant to a disability benefit plan is not unpaid, the provision for substitution of the employee's accrued paid leave is inapplicable, and neither the employee nor the employer may require the substitution of paid leave." [29 CFR 825.207, Substitution of paid leave (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/section-825.207)
- "employers and employees may agree, where state law permits, to have paid leave supplement the disability plan benefits" [29 CFR 825.207, Substitution of paid leave (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/section-825.207)

Weeks of FMLA left after disability-plan leave is designated

| Weeks on disability benefits | FMLA weeks left | PTO during the paid weeks |
| ---: | ---: | --- |
| 2 | 10 | only by agreement, to top up |
| 4 | 8 | only by agreement, to top up |
| 6 | 6 | only by agreement, to top up |
| 8 | 4 | only by agreement, to top up |
| 10 | 2 | only by agreement, to top up |
| 12 | 0 | only by agreement, to top up |

## Questions

### Can an employer require PTO while an employee is on short-term disability?

Not under the FMLA substitution rule: disability-plan leave is not unpaid, so neither side can require substitution; they may agree to top up the benefit with PTO where state law allows (29 CFR 825.207(d)).
