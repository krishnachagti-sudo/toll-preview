# State agency pages and this overlay

Each state agency publishes the official rules for its own program, and the overlay quotes them; what it adds is the case: a Colorado employee at a 100-person employer out for their own health gets 12 protected, 12 paid because FAMLI and the FMLA run together, worked out with the federal tests in one answer.

## Agency pages we read

State program pages, read November 8, 2026

| Agency | What the page gives |
| --- | --- |
| [Colorado FAMLI](https://famli.colorado.gov/individuals-and-families/how-famli-works/famli-and-fmla) | a FAMLI and FMLA page: FAMLI is designed to run concurrently with the FMLA |
| [Washington Paid Leave](https://paidleave.wa.gov/find-out-how-paid-leave-works/) | how Paid Leave works, the 16 and 18 week caps and the 2026 job protection rules, plus a notice form |
| [New Jersey MyLeaveBenefits](https://nj.gov/labor/myleavebenefits/worker/job-protection-information/more-protections.shtml) | the July 17, 2026 job protection changes, NJFLA before and after |
| [CT Paid Leave](https://www.ctpaidleave.org/how-ct-paid-leave-works/ct-paid-leave-and-fmla) | a CT Paid Leave and FMLA page with both eligibility tests |

Some agencies refused our reads (Massachusetts, Rhode Island, Hawaii, Virginia, and Minnesota's program site), so those states are not covered here; their own sites remain the source.

## When the agency page is better

You have one employee in one state and need to file a claim, check benefit amounts, contribution rates or forms. The agency decides claims; the overlay never does.
