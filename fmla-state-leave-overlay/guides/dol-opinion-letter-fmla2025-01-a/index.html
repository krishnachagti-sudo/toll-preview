Primary source

DOL Opinion Letter FMLA2025-01-A, explained

If leave under a state paid leave program also qualifies under the FMLA, it must be designated as FMLA leave, and neither side can force employer paid leave to run during the state-paid weeks: an employee with 8 weeks of state benefits inside a 12-week FMLA leave has 4 weeks the employer may still require PTO for (where state law allows).

What the letter says

Read the law's own words (3 quotations)
  • "First, where an employee takes leave under a state or local paid family or medical leave program, if the leave is covered by the FMLA, it must be designated as FMLA leave and the employee must be given notice of the designation, which should include the amount of leave to be counted against the employee's FMLA leave entitlement." U.S. Department of Labor, Wage and Hour Division, Opinion Letter FMLA2025-01-A (January 14, 2025)
  • "Because the substitution provision does not apply, neither the employee nor the employer may use the FMLA substitution provision to unilaterally require the concurrent use of employer-provided paid leave during the portion of the leave that is compensated by the state or local program." U.S. Department of Labor, Wage and Hour Division, Opinion Letter FMLA2025-01-A (January 14, 2025)
  • "the employer and the employee may agree, where state law permits, to use the employee's employer-provided accrued paid leave to supplement the payments under a state or local leave program." U.S. Department of Labor, Wage and Hour Division, Opinion Letter FMLA2025-01-A (January 14, 2025)

Worked example

The same rule already applied to disability plans and workers' compensation (29 CFR 825.207(d) and (e)); the letter extends it to state and local paid leave programs.

Not legal advice. Worked out from each state's own published text, quoted with the date we read it, and from 29 CFR Part 825. States whose official sites we could not read are named as not covered, never filled in from someone else's chart. Whether a condition qualifies, the earnings test and benefit amounts are decided by the employer and the state program; local ordinances and contracts can give more.
