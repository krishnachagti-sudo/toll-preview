# Designation note for FMLA with state leave

Template: this leave from January 11, 2027 qualifies under the FMLA and PDL and CFRA; 12 weeks count against the FMLA entitlement and the leave is protected for 22 weeks in all (the California birth example).

```
To: Sample Employee
Leave starting January 11, 2027, for a birth: the birth parent's pregnancy disability, then bonding.
This leave qualifies under the Family and Medical Leave Act and is designated as FMLA leave. 12 weeks will be counted against your FMLA entitlement of 12 weeks.
It also qualifies, and is counted at the same time, under: Pregnancy Disability Leave (PDL); California Family Rights Act (CFRA). Leave that qualifies under the FMLA and a state law counts against both (29 CFR 825.701(a)).
You may apply for benefits under: State Disability Insurance (SDI); Paid Family Leave (PFL). While a state program pays you, we will not require you to use accrued paid leave, and you cannot be required to; we may agree in writing to use it to top up the state payments where state law allows (DOL Opinion Letter FMLA2025-01-A).
In total this leave is job-protected for 22 weeks (10 for pregnancy disability, then 12 for bonding).
The formal FMLA designation notice is DOL form WH-382; give it in writing within five business days of having enough information (29 CFR 825.300(d)).
```

## What the FMLA requires of the notice

**Read the law's own words (3 quotations)**

- "the employer must notify the employee whether the leave will be designated and will be counted as FMLA leave within five business days absent extenuating circumstances." [29 CFR 825.300, Employer notice requirements (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/section-825.300)
- "The designation notice must be in writing." [29 CFR 825.300, Employer notice requirements (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/section-825.300)
- "the employer must notify the employee of the number of hours, days, or weeks that will be counted against the employee's FMLA leave entitlement in the designation notice." [29 CFR 825.300, Employer notice requirements (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/section-825.300)

The formal designation notice is DOL form WH-382. The note adds what the form does not: the state laws counted at the same time and the PTO rule while the state pays. Washington and New York set their own notice steps (shown on their pages).
