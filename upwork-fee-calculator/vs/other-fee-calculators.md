# Upwork fee calculators compared

Of the calculators read on November 8, 2026, Millo's page describes the flat 10% fee from May 3, 2023 and Indy's the old 20% / 10% / 5% tiers by lifetime billings, while Upwork's own page says fees "can vary between 0% and 15%"; Elevate Pay's covers the withdrawal fee but compares it with its own account. None carries the payment through conversion to a local bank.

## What other Upwork fee calculators says (read November 8, 2026)

**Millo.** “Upwork Fee Calculator Updated for new 2023 Fees”; “Beginning May 3, 2023 Upwork will charge a flat 10% fee on all new client contracts.”

**Indy.** “$0-$500 = 20% Upwork service fee”; it asks you to “Enter the lifetime Upwork billings for this client”. Indy itself costs “$25.00 per month”.

**Elevate Pay.** “See what Upwork's new $2.99 withdrawal fee costs you each month and each year, and what the same withdrawals cost through Elevate Pay.” The calculator offers withdrawals of $500, $1,000, $2,500 or $5,000 a month, monthly, twice monthly or weekly, and compares them with an Elevate Pay account. “Receive $500 or more from Upwork into your Elevate Pay account in a calendar month and one $2.99 reward lands in your account”.

**Upwork.** “Fees can vary between 0% and 15%”.

## What this calculator adds

- One chain from what the client paid to what your bank receives: the service fee, the withdrawal fee, the provider's fee and its conversion margin, at a dated ECB or US Treasury reference rate.
- Every fee quoted from the company's own page with the date read; a fee we could not read there (Upwork's own withdrawal fees) is never assumed, you type it.
- The rate to bill to keep a target, Connects per job won, and fixed fees a year by how often you withdraw.
- Free on the calculator's page; $0.002 a calculation over the API or MCP; a statement of many payments with its CSV is $0.001 a payment ($0.25 at least), never more than $2.49 a month.

## When other Upwork fee calculators is the better choice

Elevate Pay sells a payout account and its calculator is built around it; the others are fine for a quick gross-up if you set the fee to your contract's own figure.

> An estimate from the fees each company publishes, read on its own page on the date shown; your contract and your account's fee page win. Upwork's own withdrawal fees could not be read on Upwork's pages, so they are left out rather than guessed. Providers convert at their own base rate; the dated reference rate stands in for it.
