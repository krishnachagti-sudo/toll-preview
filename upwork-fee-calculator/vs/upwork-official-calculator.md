# Upwork's fee calculator compared

Upwork's own calculator applies the service fee and stops at the payout ("bill your client $500, you'll get a $450 payout" at 10%); it shows no withdrawal fee or currency conversion, so the same $500 taken to an Indian bank through PayPal is about 40,997.18 INR, before Upwork's withdrawal fee, at PayPal's published 3.0%.

## What Upwork's own fee calculator says (read November 8, 2026)

“The Upwork Fee Calculator can help you determine your take-home earnings from working on Upwork.”

“The rate calculator applies the freelancer service fee to the amount of money that you would make from a job.”

“Fees can vary between 0% and 15%”

“If you work on a contract with a 10% fee and bill your client $500, you'll get a $450 payout.”

The page's calculator takes an amount and a service fee (starting at 10%). It shows no withdrawal fee, no currency conversion and no date of last update; its footer reads "© 2015 - 2025 Upwork® Global LLC".

## The same $500, to the bank

$500.00 billed at a 10% Upwork fee leaves $450.00; converted through PayPal at its published 3% conversion fee, about 40,997.18 INR reaches the bank at 93.922526 INR per dollar (87.3% of the gross at that rate).

## What this calculator adds

- One chain from what the client paid to what your bank receives: the service fee, the withdrawal fee, the provider's fee and its conversion margin, at a dated ECB or US Treasury reference rate.
- Every fee quoted from the company's own page with the date read; a fee we could not read there (Upwork's own withdrawal fees) is never assumed, you type it.
- The rate to bill to keep a target, Connects per job won, and fixed fees a year by how often you withdraw.
- Free on the calculator's page; $0.002 a calculation over the API or MCP; a statement of many payments with its CSV is $0.001 a payment ($0.25 at least), never more than $2.49 a month.

## When Upwork's own fee calculator is the better choice

It is the authority on the service fee itself. If all you need is the fee on one contract, in dollars, its own page answers that; and the fee your contract shows always wins over any calculator.

> An estimate from the fees each company publishes, read on its own page on the date shown; your contract and your account's fee page win. Upwork's own withdrawal fees could not be read on Upwork's pages, so they are left out rather than guessed. Providers convert at their own base rate; the dated reference rate stands in for it.
