The rules

The rules it applies, hour by hour.

Every other state, Nevada included, gets the federal weekly rule only and is named in the results: Nevada's statute (NRS 608.018) and its Labor Commissioner's site refused our reads, so its daily rule is not guessed.

What it does not cover

Exemptions, wage orders, union agreements, meal and rest premiums, and Nevada's daily rule.

Read the full notes
  • An audit aid worked out from the rows given and the rules quoted with the date they were read. Not legal, tax or payroll advice.
  • It assumes every row is a non-exempt employee covered by the FLSA and by the state rule shown. It does not decide exemptions, wage orders, union agreements, or whether an alternative workweek was validly adopted. A shift belongs to the date in its row (its workday).
  • The regular rate is the FLSA one. Pay not in the file, and California's own regular-rate and meal or rest premium rules, are not counted.
  • Code TT is the FLSA premium only: hours over 40 in the workweek x one-half x the FLSA regular rate (IRS FS-2026-13, Q12 and Q16). California daily and double-time premium on hours not over 40 is not qualified overtime.
  • Sources, read November 8, 2026: 29 CFR Part 778 (eCFR); California Labor Code 510, 511 and 500 (from the Legislature's own data download); Alaska DOLWD Pamphlet 100 (October 2025); Colorado COMPS Order #40 (effective February 1, 2026); KRS 337.050 and 337.285; IRS FS-2026-13 (August 2026).

Federal, everywhere

Over 40 hours in a fixed workweek at 1.5 times the regular rate.

Read the full rule

Over 40 hours in a fixed workweek of 168 hours at 1.5 times the regular rate (29 CFR 778.101 and 778.105). Each workweek stands alone (778.104). The regular rate is all straight-time pay in the week, including extra pay such as a nondiscretionary bonus or shift pay, divided by all hours worked (778.109, 778.115).

California

Over 8 in a day, over 40 in the week, and the seventh day; over 12 a day at 2x.

Read the full rule

Labor Code 510(a): over 8 hours in a workday, over 40 in the workweek, and the first 8 hours on the seventh day of work in the workweek at 1.5x; over 12 hours in a day and over 8 on that seventh day at 2x. The seventh-day rule needs all seven days of one workweek worked, not seven days in a row. Under an alternative workweek (511): over the scheduled hours up to 12 at 1.5x, over 12 at 2x, and a day beyond the scheduled workdays at 1.5x for 8 hours and 2x after.

Alaska, Colorado, Kentucky

Each with its own daily or seventh-day rule, read from the state's own text.

Read the full rule

Alaska AS 23.10.060(b): over 8 a day and over 40 a week, the weekly count leaving out the daily overtime hours; not for employers of fewer than four. Colorado COMPS Order #40 Rule 4: over 40 a workweek, 12 a workday or 12 consecutive hours, whichever pays more. Kentucky KRS 337.050: all time on the seventh day of work in the workweek at 1.5x, unless the week is 40 hours or fewer; KRS 337.285: over 40 a week.
