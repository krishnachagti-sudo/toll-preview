# Which states have daily overtime

Of the 4 state laws read for this tool, daily overtime applies in California (over 8 hours; 2x over 12), Alaska (over 8, for employers of four or more) and Colorado (over 12 in a workday or 12 consecutive hours), and Kentucky pays time and a half for a seventh day; Nevada has a daily rule in NRS 608.018 that we could not read, so it is not stated here.

- State laws read: 4
- Daily overtime among them: CA, AK, CO
- Seventh-day rule: CA, KY

## The texts

Alaska AS 23.10.060(b): "An employee is entitled to overtime compensation for hours worked in excess of eight hours a day. An employee is also entitled to overtime compensation for hours worked in excess of 40 hours a week; in determining whether an employee has worked more than 40 hours a week, the number of hours worked shall be determined without including hours that are worked in excess of eight hours in a day because the employee has or will be separately awarded overtime compensation based on those hours." Subsection (d)(1) leaves out "an employee employed by an employer employing fewer than four employees in the regular course of business".

Colorado COMPS Order #40, Rule 4.1.1 to 4.1.2: "Employees shall be paid time and one-half of the regular rate of pay for any work in excess of any of the following, except as provided in exemptions or variances in Rule 2: (A) 40 hours per workweek; (B) 12 hours per workday; or (C) consecutive hours without regard to the start and end time of the workday." "Whichever of the three calculations in Rule 4.1.1 results in the greater payment of wages shall apply in any particular situation." The official PDF prints item (C) without a number. Rule 4.1.5 ("when 12 consecutive hours are worked for purposes of the Rule 4.1.1 requirement of overtime after 12 hours") and the CDLE 2026 poster ("12 daily or consecutive") give 12, which the auditor applies.

Kentucky KRS 337.050(1): "Any employer who permits any employee to work seven (7) days in any one (1) workweek shall pay him at the rate of time and a half for the time worked on the seventh day."

California: [Labor Code 510(a), worked on the calculator page](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/overtime-auditor/california-overtime-calculator/).

## Not read

Nevada has a daily overtime rule in NRS 608.018, but the Legislature's site (leg.state.nv.us, HTTP 403) and the Labor Commissioner's site (labor.nv.gov, request rejected) refused this network on 2026-11-08. It is not applied; Nevada rows get the federal weekly rule only.

Every other state and DC: the state's own overtime law was not read for this tool, so rows there get the federal weekly rule only (over 40 hours in the workweek at 1.5 times the regular rate).

## Questions

### Which states have daily overtime?

Of the laws read for this tool: California (over 8 hours a day, 2x over 12), Alaska (over 8 a day, employers of four or more) and Colorado (over 12 in a workday or 12 consecutive hours). Nevada has a daily rule in NRS 608.018, but its statute and labor department sites refused our reads, so we do not state it.

### Does daily overtime count toward weekly overtime?

Not twice. California (Labor Code 510(a)) and Alaska (AS 23.10.060(b)) leave daily overtime hours out of the weekly count; Colorado pays whichever of its three calculations pays more (COMPS Rule 4.1.2). The auditor works it the same way: daily first, then the straight hours left over 40.

### What is the federal rule?

Over 40 hours in a workweek at 1.5 times the regular rate (29 CFR 778.101). The FLSA has no daily overtime.
