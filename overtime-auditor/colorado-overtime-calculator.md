# Colorado overtime calculator

In Colorado, 13 hours in a workday at $21.50 gives 1.00 overtime hour because overtime starts after 12 hours in a workday or 12 consecutive hours, as well as after 40 in the workweek, whichever pays more; a 14-hour run across midnight split over two days still gives 2.00.

- 13-hour workday: 1.00 overtime hour
- 14 hours across midnight: 2.00 overtime hours
- 53-hour week: 13.00 overtime hours

## The rule

COMPS Order #40, Rule 4.1.1: "Employees shall be paid time and one-half of the regular rate of pay for any work in excess of any of the following, except as provided in exemptions or variances in Rule 2: (A) 40 hours per workweek; (B) 12 hours per workday; or (C) consecutive hours without regard to the start and end time of the workday." Rule 4.1.2: "Whichever of the three calculations in Rule 4.1.1 results in the greater payment of wages shall apply in any particular situation." Rule 4.1.5: "In calculating when 12 consecutive hours are worked for purposes of the Rule 4.1.1 requirement of overtime after 12 hours, meal periods may be subtracted, but only if the meal periods comply with the Rule 5.1 requirements for meal periods."

> The official PDF prints item (C) without a number. Rule 4.1.5 ("when 12 consecutive hours are worked for purposes of the Rule 4.1.1 requirement of overtime after 12 hours") and the CDLE 2026 poster ("12 daily or consecutive") give 12, which the auditor applies. The auditor joins punch pairs with gaps of up to an hour into one run, with the gap not counted; give clock times for the rule to apply. The poster says: "Use new version released by each December".

## Questions

### When does Colorado require overtime?

Over 40 hours a workweek, over 12 in a workday, or over 12 consecutive hours without regard to when the workday starts, at 1.5 times the regular rate; whichever calculation pays more applies (COMPS Order #40, Rules 4.1.1 and 4.1.2).

### Does Colorado have double time?

COMPS Order #40 Rule 4 sets time and one-half only; the auditor applies no double time in Colorado.

### Do daily and weekly overtime stack in Colorado?

No. A week of one 13-hour day and four 10-hour days is 53 hours: the daily calculation gives 1 hour and the weekly 13, so 13.00 hours are overtime, not 14.
