The regulation's own example

A California week with a bonus

California has its own rules for some bonuses and for meal and rest premiums. The Labor Commissioner's pages could not be read for this tool, so the auditor uses the FLSA regular rate and says so.

Worked out by the auditor from the hours shown and the rules quoted with the date read. It assumes a non-exempt employee covered by the rule shown; a wage order, contract or policy may require more. Not legal, tax or payroll advice.

Steps

  1. Add the straight-time pay. Every hour at its own rate, plus nondiscretionary bonuses, shift pay and similar pay for the week.
  2. Divide by all hours. Divide by every hour worked that week, overtime hours included (29 CFR 778.109).
  3. Add the premium. Half the regular rate for each overtime hour and the full rate again for each double-time hour.

Questions

What is the regular rate?

29 CFR 778.109: "The regular hourly rate of pay of an employee is determined by dividing his total remuneration for employment (except statutory exclusions) in any workweek by the total number of hours actually worked by him in that workweek for which such compensation was paid."

How does a bonus change overtime?

29 CFR 778.110(b): "a production bonus of $46 for the week, the regular hourly rate of pay is $13 an hour (46 hours at $12 yields $552; the addition of the $46 bonus makes a total of $598; this total divided by 46 hours yields a regular rate of $13). The employee is then entitled to be paid a total wage of $637 for 46 hours" The auditor gives $13.00 and $637.00 for the same week.

Two jobs at two rates in one week?

29 CFR 778.115: "Where an employee in a single workweek works at two or more different types of work for which different nonovertime rates of pay (of not less than the applicable minimum wage) have been established, his regular rate for that week is the weighted average of such rates." In a timesheet file, put each row's own rate; the auditor weights them by hours.
