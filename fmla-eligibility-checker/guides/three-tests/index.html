FMLA guide

The three FMLA eligibility tests

An employee is eligible for FMLA leave only if all three tests are met on the facts: at least 12 months with the employer, at least 1,250 hours worked in the 12 months before leave starts, and 50 employees within 75 miles of the worksite; of three employees checked here, the remote analyst is eligible, the rehire fails the 12 months (8 months) and the part-timer fails the hours (1,196).

Hours worked, not hours paid

The 1,250 hours are hours worked under the FLSA, so paid vacation and sick time do not count. See the 1,250-hour test.

Worked out from the facts shown under 29 CFR Part 825. State family and medical leave laws are not covered; an employee who is not eligible under the FMLA may still have leave under state law. Not legal advice.

Steps

  1. Coverage. Is the employer covered? Private employers with 50 or more employees in 20 or more workweeks of this or last calendar year; public agencies and schools at any size (29 CFR 825.104, 825.105(e)).
  2. 12 months. Has the employee worked for the employer at least 12 months, not necessarily in a row, as of the day leave starts (825.110(b), (d))?
  3. 1,250 hours. At least 1,250 hours actually worked in the 12 months before leave starts (825.110(a)(2), (c))?
  4. 50 within 75 miles. At least 50 employees within 75 surface miles of the worksite, counted when notice is given; for a teleworker, the office they report to (825.111).

Questions

Who is eligible for FMLA leave?

An employee of a covered employer who has worked for it at least 12 months, has at least 1,250 hours of service in the 12 months before the leave, and works where the employer has 50 or more employees within 75 miles (29 CFR 825.110(a); DOL Fact Sheet #28).

Is employer coverage the same as employee eligibility?

No. A covered employer must post the FMLA notice even if no employee is eligible (29 CFR 825.300(a)(2)); each employee must still meet the three tests.
