# FMLA eligibility for remote employees

A remote employee's worksite is the office they report to or get assignments from, never their home, and that office's count includes the teleworkers assigned to it: an analyst working from home far from headquarters but assigned from there counts the 300 employees within 75 miles of headquarters and is eligible, while the same analyst assigned from the regional office counts 45 and is not.

- Assigned from headquarters: 300
- Assigned from the regional office: 45

## The rule

29 CFR 825.111(a)(2): **An employee's personal residence is not a worksite** for employees who work at home, as under telecommuting; their worksite is the office to which they report and from which assignments are made. The Wage and Hour Division's Field Assistance Bulletin 2023-1 (February 9, 2023) applies it to telework, and adds that the count around that office includes the teleworkers who report to it or receive assignments from it.

## The example

Based on the bulletin's Example 5: an analyst in data processing works from home more than 75 miles from headquarters, and gets her projects from a manager at headquarters. Her worksite is headquarters.

Sites and surface miles from headquarters, as the engine counts them.

| Site | Employees | Miles from HQ | Counted |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Headquarters | 210 | 0 | yes |
| Suburban office | 90 | 28 | yes |
| Regional office | 45 | 140 | no |

With 32 months of service, 1,612 hours in the 12 months before December 1, 2026, and 300 employees within 75 surface miles of Headquarters, this employee is eligible for FMLA leave; the eligibility notice is due by November 13, 2026.

## Setting the reporting office

- The bulletin calls it fact specific: where the employee reports to work, or where their assignments are made.
- Keep the reporting office in the employee's record from hire, so the count is not argued after a leave request.
- An office closed for an emergency stays the worksite of the staff sent home (the bulletin's Example 4).

> Worked out from the facts shown under 29 CFR Part 825. State family and medical leave laws are not covered; an employee who is not eligible under the FMLA may still have leave under state law. Not legal advice.

## Questions

### Is a remote employee's home a worksite for FMLA?

No. 29 CFR 825.111(a)(2) says an employee's personal residence is not a worksite; the worksite is the office to which they report and from which assignments are made.

### Do remote employees count toward the 50 employees?

Yes, at the office they report to or receive assignments from: the count includes teleworkers who report to or receive assignments from that worksite (DOL Field Assistance Bulletin 2023-1).

### Does a remote worker 300 miles from headquarters qualify?

Distance from home does not matter. If they report to headquarters and 50 or more employees work within 75 miles of it, the worksite test is met.
