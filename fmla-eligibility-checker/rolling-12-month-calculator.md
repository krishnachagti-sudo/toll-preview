## The rule

Under the rolling method, **each time an employee takes FMLA leave the remaining leave entitlement would be any balance of the 12 weeks which has not been used during the immediately preceding 12 months** (29 CFR 825.200(c)). Employers on this method may need to work out the balance each time leave is asked for, and employees can fall in and out of protection.

> Worked out from the facts shown under 29 CFR Part 825. State family and medical leave laws are not covered; an employee who is not eligible under the FMLA may still have leave under state law. Not legal advice.

## Questions

### How is the FMLA rolling 12-month period calculated?

Each time leave is used, look back 12 months from that day: the employee may use whatever is left of 12 workweeks after the FMLA leave taken in those 12 months (29 CFR 825.200(b)(4) and (c)).

### When does FMLA leave come back under the rolling method?

Hours used on a day come back on the same date a year later. In the regulation's example, leave used from February 1, 2008 is recouped day by day from February 1, 2009.

### What if an employee asks for more leave than is left?

Only the part covered by the remaining balance on each day is FMLA-protected. In the regulation's example, six weeks asked for when four are left: only the first four are protected.
