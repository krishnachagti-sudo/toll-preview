What the check follows

Three tests, each as of the day leave starts: 12 months of employment, not necessarily in a row; 1,250 hours actually worked in the 12 months before; and 50 employees within 75 surface miles of the office the employee reports to (29 CFR 825.110, 825.111).

What it does not cover

  • State family and medical leave laws: see the state leave overlay.
  • Whether a reason for leave qualifies.
  • Whether employers are joint or integrated employers.
Read the three tests in full, the sources and the prices

12 months, not in a row

The 12 months need not be consecutive. Service before a break of seven years or more need not be counted, unless the break was USERRA-covered military service or a written agreement covered the rehire (29 CFR 825.110(b)). One continuous job is measured to its 12-month anniversary; separate periods are added up in weeks, where any part of a week on the payroll counts and 52 weeks equal 12 months. Both tests are made as of the day leave starts (825.110(d)).

1,250 hours actually worked

Hours of service are counted under the Fair Labor Standards Act's rules for hours worked, in the 12 months immediately before the leave starts (825.110(a)(2), (c)). Paid time off is not hours worked. An employer that kept no accurate record must be able to show the employee fell short, so an estimate below 1,250 is not enough to deny leave (825.110(c)(3)).

50 within 75 miles of the office they report to

A teleworker's home is not a worksite: their worksite is the office they report to or get assignments from, and that office's count includes the teleworkers assigned to it (825.111(a)(2); DOL Field Assistance Bulletin 2023-1). The 75 miles are surface miles by the shortest route (825.111(b)), counted when the employee gives notice (825.110(e)).

  • The eligibility notice is due within five business days of the request, or of learning the leave may qualify, absent extenuating circumstances, and must give at least one reason when the employee is not eligible (825.300(b)). Part 825 does not define business days: this page counts Monday to Friday less US federal holidays and any closed days you add.
  • Worked out from the facts entered under 29 CFR Part 825 as published on eCFR. The headcount, worksite and hours are yours; the tool cannot check them.
  • State family and medical leave laws are not covered. An employee who is not eligible under the FMLA may still have leave under state law.
  • The tool does not decide whether a reason for leave qualifies, or whether employers are joint or integrated employers. Not legal advice.
  • Sources, read November 8, 2026: 29 CFR Part 825 on eCFR (current to November 2, 2026: 825.104, 825.105, 825.110, 825.111, 825.200, 825.205, 825.300), DOL Fact Sheet #28 (revised March 2025), DOL Field Assistance Bulletin 2023-1 (February 9, 2023) and DOL FMLA forms (WH-381, WH-382).
