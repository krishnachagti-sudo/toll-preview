# The FMLA 1,250-hour test

An employee needs at least 1,250 hours actually worked in the 12 months before the leave starts: for leave starting December 1, 2026 that is December 1, 2025 to November 30, 2026, and a part-timer averaging 24 hours a week for 52 weeks has 1,248, 2 hours short; 24.04 hours a week is the least that reaches 1,250.

- Needed: 1,250
- 24 hours a week: 1,248
- Least weekly average: 24.04

## The rule

The employee must have **at least 1,250 hours of service during the 12-month period immediately preceding the commencement of the leave** (29 CFR 825.110(a)(2)), counted as of the date the leave is to start (825.110(d)). Hours are hours worked under the Fair Labor Standards Act: any accurate record of actual hours worked may be used, whatever the payroll system shows (825.110(c)(1)).

## Records and USERRA

- No accurate record (often exempt staff): the employer must be able to clearly show the employee fell short (825.110(c)(3)).
- Back from USERRA-covered military service: the hours the employee would have worked are added to the hours actually worked (825.110(c)(2)).
- Airline flight crew have their own test (825.801). This tool does not work it out.

> Worked out from the facts shown under 29 CFR Part 825. State family and medical leave laws are not covered; an employee who is not eligible under the FMLA may still have leave under state law. Not legal advice.

## Questions

### Which 12 months count for the 1,250 hours?

The 12 months immediately before the leave starts. For leave starting December 1, 2026 that is December 1, 2025 to November 30, 2026 (29 CFR 825.110(a)(2) and (d)).

### Does paid time off count toward the 1,250 hours?

No. Hours of service are counted under the FLSA rules for hours worked (29 CFR 825.110(c)(1)), so vacation, sick and holiday hours that were paid but not worked are left out.

### What if we do not track an exempt employee's hours?

Then the employer has the burden of showing the employee did not work 1,250 hours (29 CFR 825.110(c)(3)). An estimate below 1,250 is not enough to deny leave.
