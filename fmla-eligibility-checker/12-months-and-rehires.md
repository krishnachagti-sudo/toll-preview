# The FMLA 12-month rule and rehires

The 12 months need not be in a row: separate periods are added up, but service before a break of seven years or more is dropped unless the break was military (USERRA) service or a written agreement covered the rehire, so an employee rehired on March 2, 2026 after leaving on August 31, 2018 has 8 months on November 16, 2026 and does not qualify, while one who left on June 30, 2020 has 325 weeks and does.

- Break over 7 years: 8 months
- Break under 7 years: 325 weeks

## How the engine counts

- One continuous job: the 12 months are met on the anniversary of the first day, so a hire on November 10, 2025 qualifies for leave starting November 10, 2026, not November 9.
- Separate periods: each week with any day on the payroll counts, including paid and unpaid leave while on the payroll, and 52 weeks equal 12 months (825.110(b)(3)).
- A break of seven years or more drops the service before it (825.110(b)(1)), unless USERRA service or a written rehire agreement explains it (825.110(b)(2)). An employer may count older service, but then for everyone with a similar break (825.110(b)(4)).
- USERRA service itself counts as employment (825.110(b)(2)(i)).

> Worked out from the facts shown under 29 CFR Part 825. State family and medical leave laws are not covered; an employee who is not eligible under the FMLA may still have leave under state law. Not legal advice.

## Questions

### Do the 12 months of FMLA service have to be consecutive?

No. 29 CFR 825.110(b) says the 12 months need not be consecutive months.

### Does a rehired employee's earlier service count?

Yes, unless it ends before a break in service of seven years or more. Even then it counts if the break was USERRA-covered service or a written agreement, such as a collective bargaining agreement, covered the rehire (29 CFR 825.110(b)(1) and (2)).

### How are separate periods added up?

In weeks: a week with any day on the payroll counts as a week, and 52 weeks equal 12 months (29 CFR 825.110(b)(3)).
