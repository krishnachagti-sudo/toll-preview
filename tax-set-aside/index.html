The rules

How the amount is worked out

An estimate for planning, not tax advice, from tax year 2026 (US) and 2026 to 2027 (UK) published rates.

What it does not cover

Tax credits, US local taxes, more than one state, and UK savings, dividends and the High Income Child Benefit Charge.

Read the full list
  • Estimate for planning, not tax advice. It uses tax year 2026 (US) and 2026 to 2027 (UK) published rates, with the sources listed under each result. You remain responsible for your estimated payments.
  • Assumes one resident state, the standard deduction (plus any tips, overtime or senior deduction you enter) and no credits; it leaves out US local taxes, and UK savings, dividends and the High Income Child Benefit Charge.
  • Tips, overtime and senior deductions apply only if you qualify; you confirm eligibility. They do not reduce self-employment tax.
  • We do not move money; you decide what to transfer.
  • UK: first-year payments on account can make your January bill about 150% of one year's tax. Each UK answer also shows the amount that covers it.

Why not a flat 25%

A flat 25% is too much early in the year and too little later on.

Read the full rule

A flat 25% is too much on your first payments of the year and too little later on. That is because the tax on a payment depends on what you earned before it: early in the year your first profit is covered by the standard deduction (in the UK, the Personal Allowance) and taxed in the lowest brackets, and later payments land in higher brackets. Give the payment and what you have earned so far this year, and it works out the tax this one payment adds: self-employment tax, federal income tax and state tax in the US, or Income Tax, Class 4 and student loan in the UK, split exactly wherever the payment crosses a bracket or a threshold.

The tax this payment adds

The year's tax with and without this payment; the difference is what it costs.

Read the full rule

It works out the year's tax twice: once on your profit so far, once with this payment added, using the same rules both times. The difference is what this payment costs you in tax. That is why the same $3,000 needs $424 (14.1%) at the start of the year and $915 (30.5%) at $90,000, single in Texas: by then the payment lands in a higher bracket. A payment that crosses a bracket, the Social Security wage base, the Additional Medicare line or a UK band is split at the line.

What is in it

US 2026 federal, self-employment and state tax; UK 2026 to 2027 Income Tax, Class 4 and student loan.

Read the full rule

US, 2026: self-employment tax (15.3% of 92.35% of profit, Social Security stopping at $184,500 counting W-2 wages), Additional Medicare Tax, federal income tax with the standard deduction, the QBI deduction, and the tips, overtime and senior deductions you qualify for; state income tax for the states listed. UK, 2026 to 2027: Income Tax (Scottish bands in Scotland), the Personal Allowance taper, Class 4 National Insurance and student loan through Self Assessment.
