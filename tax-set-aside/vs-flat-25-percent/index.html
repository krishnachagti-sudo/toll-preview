Is a flat 25 percent set-aside enough?

A flat 25% is more than a single Texas freelancer with $25,000 of 2026 profit owes ($4,103 against $6,250), but at $150,000 in California it is $8,827 short of the $46,327 owed. The right share moves with profit, state and other income.

Where does 25 percent fall short?

Self-employment tax alone is 15.3% on 92.35% of profit, about 14.1% of it, before any income tax. Income tax then climbs through the brackets, so a fixed share is too much early on and too little later.

When is a flat percentage the better choice?

  • Your income is steady and near a level you have checked, so the rate barely changes.
  • You want no tool at all: one transfer rule is easy to keep.
  • You round up on purpose and true up at filing time.

When is Endwise the better choice?

  • Your income is uneven, or crosses a bracket during the year.
  • You live in a state with an income tax, or have a W-2 job too.
  • You want the exact amount for each payment, with the working: $0.10 a payment, capped at $2.49 a month; the percentage alone is shown free to signed-out visitors.

Questions

Is 25 percent enough to set aside for self-employment taxes?

For a single filer with no other income in 2026, 25% covers the federal and self-employment tax at lower profits in Texas and falls short from about $150,000 of profit. In California it falls short from about $100,000. A W-2 job on top makes it short sooner.

When is a flat percentage good enough?

When income is steady, near a profit level where you have checked the rate, and you have no other income that moves you into a higher bracket.
