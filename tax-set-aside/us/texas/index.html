Texas: tax set-aside per payment, 2026

In Texas, a single filer with $40,000 of net profit so far should set aside $692 (23.1%) of a $3,000 payment in 2026: $424 self-employment tax, $268 federal income tax and $0 state income tax (Texas has none).

Texas income tax

Texas has no state income tax on wages or business profit, so a Texas freelancer sets aside federal tax only: self-employment tax and federal income tax.

What is in the amount

  • Self-employment tax: 15.3% of 92.35% of profit (12.4% Social Security up to the $184,500 wage base, 2.9% Medicare on all of it), and nothing under $400 of net earnings.
  • Federal income tax: 2026 brackets, the $16,100 standard deduction for a single filer, half of self-employment tax deducted, and the 20% QBI deduction.
  • The amount is the tax this payment adds: the year's tax with it, less the year's tax without it.

Estimate for planning, not tax advice. It uses the published 2026 rates (UK: 2026 to 2027), the standard deduction and no credits, and one resident state; it leaves out local taxes. Tips, overtime and senior deductions apply only if you qualify, and never reduce self-employment tax. We do not move money; you decide what to transfer.

Questions

How much should I set aside for taxes on 1099 income in Texas?

It depends on what you have earned so far. For a single filer in 2026, a $3,000 payment needs $424 (14.1%) at the start of the year, $692 (23.1%) after $40,000 of profit and $915 (30.5%) after $90,000.

Is 25% enough to set aside for taxes in Texas?

Not always. A single Texas filer needs 14.1% of a $3,000 payment at the start of 2026 but 30.5% once profit passes $90,000, so a flat 25% is too much early in the year and too little later.
