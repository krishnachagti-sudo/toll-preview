## Illinois income tax

Illinois taxes income at a flat 4.95% on federal AGI less the personal exemption: $2,925 a person for 2026 (two on a joint return), plus $1,000 for each person 65 or older. Above $250,000 of AGI ($500,000 joint) there is no exemption. Estimated tax is paid in four equal parts on April 15, June 15 and September 15, 2026 and January 15, 2027.

## What is in the amount

- Self-employment tax: 15.3% of 92.35% of profit (12.4% Social Security up to the $184,500 wage base, 2.9% Medicare on all of it), and nothing under $400 of net earnings.
- Federal income tax: 2026 brackets, the $16,100 standard deduction for a single filer, half of self-employment tax deducted, and the 20% QBI deduction.
- The amount is the tax this payment adds: the year's tax with it, less the year's tax without it.

## Questions

### How much should I set aside for taxes on 1099 income in Illinois?

It depends on what you have earned so far. For a single filer in 2026, a $3,000 payment needs $424 (14.1%) at the start of the year, $830 (27.7%) after $40,000 of profit and $1,053 (35.1%) after $90,000.

### Is 25% enough to set aside for taxes in Illinois?

Not always. A single Illinois filer needs 14.1% of a $3,000 payment at the start of 2026 but 35.1% once profit passes $90,000, so a flat 25% is too much early in the year and too little later.
