# Fourth quarter 2026 estimated tax, due January 15, 2027

The fourth 2026 federal estimated tax payment is due **Friday, January 15, 2027**, for income from September 1, 2026 to December 31, 2026. You can skip it if you file your 2026 return by February 1, 2027 and pay everything owed with it.

## When exactly is it due?

Form 1040-ES (2026) lists the 4th payment on January 15, 2027. January 15, 2027 is a Friday and not a federal holiday, so it does not move: a deadline on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday moves to the next business day (26 U.S.C. 7503), and the Business days engine finds no such day here. A payment mailed and postmarked by the due date counts as paid on time.

| 3 | June 1, 2026 to August 31, 2026 | Tuesday, September 15, 2026 |
| 4 | September 1, 2026 to December 31, 2026 | Friday, January 15, 2027 |

## Can I skip the January payment?

Yes. Form 1040-ES: you don't have to make the payment due January 15, 2027 if you file your 2026 tax return by February 1, 2027 and pay the entire balance due with your return. The form names February 1, 2027 because January 31, 2027 is a Sunday.

Farmers and fishermen (at least two-thirds of gross income for 2025 or 2026 from farming or fishing) can instead pay all of their estimated tax by January 15, 2027, or file by March 1, 2027 and pay the total tax due.

## How much should I pay?

Enough that the four payments together reach the smaller of 90% of your 2026 tax or 100% of your 2025 tax (110% if your 2025 AGI was over $150,000, or $75,000 married filing separately).

Income that arrives in December still counts for this payment. A $3,000 payment on December 10, 2026, with $55,000 of profit already, needs **$691.53** set aside (23.1%) in Texas, single.

## How do I pay it?

- Your IRS Online Account: balance and estimated payments, with your payment history.
- IRS Direct Pay: a transfer from a checking or savings account, at no cost.
- Debit card, credit card or digital wallet: through IRS.gov/Payments; the card processors charge a fee.
- EFTPS: online or by phone from a bank account, no fee, but you must enroll first.
- By mail with a Form 1040-ES voucher; the postmark date counts as the payment date.

## What if I pay late or too little?

Form 1040-ES: a penalty may be applied if you didn't pay enough estimated tax or didn't pay on time, figured on each underpayment for the days it stays unpaid. The IRS rate for individual underpayments is 7% for the fourth quarter of 2026. The rate for the first quarter of 2027 was not yet posted on the IRS page when checked on November 6, 2026.

States set their own dates: California's last 2026 installment is also January 15, 2027 (see [California set-aside](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/tax-set-aside/us/california/)). ## Questions

### When is the Q4 2026 estimated tax payment due?

Friday, January 15, 2027. It covers income received from September 1, 2026 to December 31, 2026.

### Can I skip the January 15, 2027 payment?

Yes, if you file your 2026 tax return by February 1, 2027 and pay the entire balance due with it (Form 1040-ES).

### What if I farm or fish?

If at least two-thirds of your gross income for 2025 or 2026 is from farming or fishing, Form 1040-ES lets you pay all of your estimated tax by January 15, 2027, or file your 2026 return by March 1, 2027 and pay the total tax due.

### How much should the fourth payment be?

With equal quarters, a quarter of the year's required payment. A single filer in Texas with $60,000 of 2026 profit owes about $12,037 for the year, so $3,009 a quarter. On the prior-year safe harbor, 100% of a $9,000 tax for 2025 is $2,250 a quarter.
