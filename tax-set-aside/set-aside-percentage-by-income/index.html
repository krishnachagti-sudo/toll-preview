What share of freelance income to save for tax, 2026

A single freelancer with $40,000 of 2026 profit and no other income owes 18.6% of it in Texas (self-employment and federal income tax); at $80,000 in California it is 24.2%. The share rises with profit, and the next dollar is taxed above the average.

Single filer, 2026, standard deduction, no W-2 wages, the QBI deduction where it applies. Federal income tax, self-employment tax and the state's income tax, as a share of the year's profit.

Texas has no state income tax, so its column is federal and self-employment tax only; the same column applies in the other no-tax states the tool covers. Each state's method is on its own page, for example California.

Questions

What percentage should a freelancer save for taxes?

For a single filer in 2026 with no other income: about 15.1% of profit at $20,000 in Texas, 18.6% at $40,000 and 22.4% at $100,000. State income tax adds to that: at $80,000, 24.2% in California against 20.8% in Texas.

Why does the share go up with income?

Self-employment tax is a flat 15.3% on 92.35% of profit up to the Social Security wage base, but income tax is charged in brackets, so each extra dollar of profit is taxed at a higher rate than the average.

Should I use the year average or the rate on the next payment?

The rate on the next payment. At $80,000 of profit so far in Texas, the next $1,000 needs $230.51, 23.1%, more than the 20.8% year average.
