Before you sign

Asked for an unconditional waiver before you are paid

Ask to sign the conditional waiver instead: it takes effect only when the payment clears, and you hand over the unconditional one after that. In Texas the statute settles it: Property Code 53.283 says no one may require an unconditional waiver until you have received payment in good and sufficient funds. Arizona's and California's unconditional forms warn on their face that they are enforceable even if you have not been paid.

What does the generator do if I ask for it anyway?

It refuses by default. Asked for an unconditional progress waiver with the payment not yet received, it declines in Arizona, California and Texas and says why; you can make it only by confirming that you want it before the payment clears.

What can I send the GC?

I have attached the conditional waiver for this payment, on the statutory form. It takes effect as soon as the check clears. I will send the unconditional waiver for the same amount the day the funds clear.

Steps

  1. Offer the conditional form. Send the conditional waiver for the same payment, filled in with the check maker, amount and payee. It becomes effective only when the bank pays the check.
  2. Point to the form's own notice. The unconditional forms in Arizona, California and Texas print a notice that the document is enforceable against you even if you have not been paid.
  3. Swap when the money clears. Once the payment has cleared, sign the unconditional waiver for the same amount and hand it over.
  4. Keep dated copies. Keep a copy of every waiver you sign, with the date and the payment it was for.

Questions

Can a general contractor make me sign an unconditional lien waiver before paying me?

In Texas, no. Property Code 53.283: "A person may not require a claimant or potential claimant to execute an unconditional waiver and release for a progress payment or final payment amount unless the claimant or potential claimant received payment in that amount in good and sufficient funds." In the other states covered here, the statute quoted does not forbid the request, but the unconditional form is effective when signed.

What do I sign in Florida, where there is no conditional form?

Florida's two statutory forms say the payment was received. Florida Statutes 713.20(7): "A lienor who executes a lien waiver and release in exchange for a check may condition the waiver and release on payment of the check." And 713.20(6): "A person may not require a lienor to furnish a lien waiver or release of lien that is different from the forms in subsection (4) or subsection (5)."

What if I already signed an unconditional waiver and was not paid?

Then the document says you were paid, and Arizona's and California's forms say it is enforceable against you even if you were not. Talk to a construction attorney in the project's state before you do anything else.
