# Does a lien waiver need to be notarized?

Of the 5 states whose statutory lien waiver forms are read here, only Wyoming's form must be notarized, and Texas required a notary only for original contracts entered into before January 1, 2022. Arizona, California and Florida print no notary block on their forms. A contract can still ask for a notarized waiver.

## Why did Texas change in 2022?

H.B. 2237 (87th Leg., 2021), secs. 35, 37 and 38: the act struck "and notarized" from Property Code 53.281(b). Its section 37 says: "The changes in law made by this Act apply only to an original contract entered into on or after the effective date of this Act." Section 38: "This Act takes effect January 1, 2022." The original contract is the owner's contract with the prime contractor, not your subcontract.

## What about the other states?

AIA lists Georgia, Massachusetts, Michigan, Mississippi, Missouri, Nevada and Utah as states that also regulate lien waiver forms. Their statutes have not been read here, so this page says nothing about notarization in them. Every other state has no statutory form; see [the state coverage table](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/lien-waiver/state-coverage/).

## Questions

### Does a Texas lien waiver need to be notarized?

Only under an original contract entered into before January 1, 2022. H.B. 2237 removed "and notarized" from Property Code 53.281(b), and its section 37 applies that change to original contracts entered into on or after January 1, 2022.

### Does a Wyoming lien waiver need a notary?

Yes. The form in Wyoming Statutes 29-10-101(b) ends with a notary's acknowledgment.

### My GC wants the waiver notarized but my state does not require it. Do I have to?

The statutes quoted here do not stop a contract from asking for a notary. Whether your subcontract requires one is a question about your contract. In Florida, 713.20(6) says no one may require a lienor to furnish a waiver that is different from the statutory forms.
