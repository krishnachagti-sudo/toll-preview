# Florida lien waiver forms

Florida sets out 2 lien waiver forms in Florida Statutes 713.20: one for a progress payment and one for the final payment. They are optional ("may be in substantially the following form"), but no one may require a lienor to sign a different form (713.20(6)).

- Statutory forms: 2
- Notary: Not on the form
- Source read: November 2, 2026

## What the statute says

- **Fla. Stat. 713.20(2)**: "A right to claim a lien may not be waived in advance. A lien right may be waived only to the extent of labor, services, or materials furnished."
- **Fla. Stat. 713.20(6)**: "A person may not require a lienor to furnish a lien waiver or release of lien that is different from the forms in subsection (4) or subsection (5)."
- **Fla. Stat. 713.20(7)**: "A lienor who executes a lien waiver and release in exchange for a check may condition the waiver and release on payment of the check."
- **Fla. Stat. 713.20(8)**: "A lien waiver or lien release that is not substantially similar to the forms in subsections (4) and (5) is enforceable in accordance with the terms of the lien waiver or lien release."

## Questions

### Does Florida have a statutory lien waiver form?

Yes: two statutory lien waiver forms in Fla. Stat. 713.20. The statute's words: "A right to claim a lien may not be waived in advance. A lien right may be waived only to the extent of labor, services, or materials furnished."

### Can a Florida lien waiver be conditional?

The statutory forms have no conditional wording. Florida Statutes 713.20(7): "A lienor who executes a lien waiver and release in exchange for a check may condition the waiver and release on payment of the check."
