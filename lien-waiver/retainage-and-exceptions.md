# Retainage, change orders and disputed amounts

A progress waiver in Arizona, California, Florida and Texas leaves out retention by its own words, but the final waivers are different: the Texas and Florida final forms have no space for exceptions, and California's final forms release fully executed change orders unless you list them. Of the 15 statutory forms read here, 3 final forms reserve nothing at all.

## What does each form leave out?

## Where does it go wrong?

- Signing a final waiver for a progress payment: the final forms cover the whole job.
- Leaving the California exception lines blank when a change order is still unpaid.
- Signing the Texas or Florida final form while retention or extras are still owed: those forms have no place to list them.
- A through date later than the work the payment covers: the release reaches every day up to it.

## Checklist before you sign

- Is this the last payment on the job? If not, use the progress form.
- Is any retention, change order or extra still unpaid? Make sure the form leaves it out, or list it where the form has a space.
- Does the amount match the check, not the pay application?
- Has the payment cleared? If not, sign the conditional form ([why](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/lien-waiver/unconditional-before-payment/)).

## Questions

### Does a progress lien waiver release my retainage?

Not on the statutory forms read here. Arizona's and Texas's progress forms leave out retention and pending modifications and changes, California's list retentions as an exception, Florida's progress form does not cover any retention, and Wyoming's form reserves an amount withheld as retainage.

### Does a final lien waiver release pending change orders?

It can. California's final forms say rights under a written change order fully executed before signing "are waived and released by this document, unless listed as an Exception below." The Texas and Florida final forms have no exception space. Arizona's conditional final form has a space for disputed claims.

### Should I sign a final waiver for a progress payment?

The final forms cover the whole job. For a payment that is not the last one, the progress form is the one that keeps retention and later work out of the release.
