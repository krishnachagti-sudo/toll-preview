# The through date and the amount on a lien waiver

The through date is the last day of work or materials the waiver releases, and the amount is the payment it is for: the check, not the pay application. Only 5 of the 15 statutory forms read here have a through date (the Arizona and California progress forms and Florida's progress form); Texas's progress forms point to the attached statement or pay request instead.

## A worked example

A subcontractor in Arizona billed $18,400 for work through October 31, 2026, and the GC sends a check for $16,560 after holding back 10% retention. On the conditional progress form: the amount of check is $16,560, the through date is October 31, 2026, and the form itself leaves out the retention: "does not cover any retention, pending modifications and changes or items furnished after that date."

## Common mistakes

- Writing the pay application total instead of the check amount.
- Using today's date as the through date when the payment covers an earlier period.
- Leaving the amount still owed blank on a form that has a space for it (Arizona and California final forms, Wyoming).

## Questions

### What is the through date on a lien waiver?

The last day of labor, services or materials the waiver covers. Florida's progress form releases work "furnished through" that date, and "This waiver and release does not cover any retention or labor, services, or materials furnished after the date specified."

### What amount goes on a conditional waiver?

The amount of the check you are getting for it. The Arizona, California and Texas conditional forms ask for the maker of the check, the amount and who it is payable to.

### What if the through date is later than the work paid for?

Then the waiver releases more than the payment covers. Put the last day of the work the payment is for.
