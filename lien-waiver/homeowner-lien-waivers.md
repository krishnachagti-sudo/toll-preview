# Lien waivers for homeowners

Yes: when you pay a contractor, collect a lien waiver for that payment, the conditional one with the payment and the unconditional one once it has cleared. Each waiver releases only the signer's own rights, so subcontractors and suppliers who worked on the job sign their own. The conditional forms of 3 states (Arizona, California and Texas) tell you, in their own words, to check that the signer was actually paid before relying on them.

## What do the forms tell an owner?

- **California**: "A PERSON SHOULD NOT RELY ON THIS DOCUMENT UNLESS SATISFIED THAT THE CLAIMANT HAS RECEIVED PAYMENT."
- **Texas**: "Before any recipient of this document relies on this document, the recipient should verify evidence of payment to the signer."
- **Arizona**: "Before any recipient of this document relies on it, that person should verify evidence of payment to the undersigned."

## Who signs a waiver for my job?

Whoever could claim a lien for work or materials on your property, and only for their own rights: California's form "waives and releases lien, stop payment notice, and payment bond rights the claimant has". A waiver from the contractor does not release a supplier the contractor has not paid.

## Steps

1. **With each payment.** Ask the contractor for a conditional waiver for that payment, on your state's statutory form where it has one.
2. **From everyone who worked on the job.** Ask for waivers from the subcontractors and suppliers too: each waiver releases only its signer's rights.
3. **After the payment clears.** Collect the unconditional waiver for the same payment.
4. **At the end.** Make the last payment against final waivers, and keep every waiver with your records.

## Questions

### Should I get a lien waiver before paying my contractor?

Collect a conditional waiver with the payment and an unconditional one once the payment has cleared. The conditional waiver takes effect only on payment, so it does not harm the contractor to give it with the invoice.

### Does my contractor's waiver cover the subcontractors?

No. The statutory forms release the rights of the person who signs them, so a subcontractor or supplier who worked on your job signs its own waiver.

### In Florida, can I hold back money until a check clears?

Florida Statutes 713.20(7) lets a lienor condition a waiver on payment of the check, and adds that without a payment bond protecting the owner, the owner may withhold the amount of an unpaid check from payments to the contractor until the condition is met.
