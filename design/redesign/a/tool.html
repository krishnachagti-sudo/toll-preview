Two dates, not one

The trip date sets the rate. For employer allowances, the date it was paid can matter too.

The trip date

The IRS sets its optional standard mileage rates by period. For 2026: 72.5 cents a business mile, 20.5 cents medical or moving and 14 cents charity from January 1 to June 30 (Notice 2026-10, IR-2025-128); 76, 23.5 and 14 cents from July 1 to December 31 (Announcement 2026-11, IR-2026-29). A trip that runs over July 1 is entered as two legs.

The date an allowance is paid

Announcement 2026-11 applies the new rates to employer mileage allowances only when they are paid on or after July 1, 2026 for miles driven on or after July 1. A June 28 trip reimbursed July 5 stays at 72.5 cents; so does an advance paid in June for July miles. The 2011 and 2022 mid-year changes had the same rule.

Above the federal rate

Pub 463: when an allowance is more than the federal rate, the employer reports the amount up to the federal rate in W-2 box 12 with code L (not taxable) and the excess in box 1 as wages. The trip file shows both per employee.

Does a log I rebuild later count?

The IRS asks for records made at or near the time, and a log kept weekly counts as timely (Pub 463). The PDF's completeness check lists gaps it can see. It cannot make a log rebuilt from memory into a timely record, and it does not say whether the IRS will accept it.

This tool computes amounts from the trips you enter. It does not decide which trips are business, medical or charity. Medical and charity amounts are inputs to Schedule A; deduction limits are not applied. Rates for 2027 are added only once the IRS announces them. Not tax or legal advice.
