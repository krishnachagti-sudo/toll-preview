Reduce an image to a size in KB

Set the limit and the tool keeps the highest JPEG quality that fits, shrinking the picture only when it must. In our test a 12-megapixel photo kept its full 4,032 × 3,024 px from 240 KB up, and had to be made smaller at 200 KB and below.

THE FLOOR

Quality never goes below 35%

Between neighbouring limits the tool can trade one for the other: a 12-megapixel photo at 200 KB came out at 3,427 × 2,570 px and 48% quality, and at 240 KB at its full size and 40%.

How the tool gets to the limit

Quality first. Pixels only if it must.

1

Save at full size, quality 92%

If that already fits, that is the result.

fits → done
2

Search quality 35% to 92% at full size

Halving the gap seven times, keeping the highest quality that fits.

3

Still too big at 35%: 15% smaller, search again

Past a tenth of the width, it says the limit is too small for the photo.

What happens to the file

The result is always a JPEG. See-through areas of a PNG or WebP become white, a sideways phone photo is turned upright, and camera details such as location are not copied into the new file. A JPEG that is already under your limit is handed back as it is, details included.

Photos over 16 megapixels are first drawn at 16 megapixels, because phones and some browsers refuse larger canvases.

All of it happens inside your browser tab: the page has no code that sends your photo anywhere.

Steps

Four steps, one page.

Open Image to size
  1. 01

    Choose the photo.

    Drop in a JPEG, PNG or WebP. It stays in your browser.

  2. 02

    Set the limit.

    Pick a size such as 50 KB, or type the limit the form gives you.

  3. 03

    Let it search.

    Full size first, quality down to 35%; only then a smaller picture.

  4. 04

    Download.

    The result shows its size, pixels and quality next to the original.
