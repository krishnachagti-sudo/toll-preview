Reduce an image to a size in KB

To reduce an image to a size in KB, set the limit and let the tool keep the highest JPEG quality that fits, shrinking the picture only when it must. In our test a 12-megapixel photo kept its full 4,032 × 3,024 px from 240 KB up, and had to be made smaller at 200 KB and below.

Pixels and quality trade places

Between neighbouring limits the tool can trade one for the other: a 12-megapixel photo at 200 KB came out at 3,427 × 2,570 px and 48% quality, and at 240 KB at its full size and 40%.

Busy photos shrink furthest

Photos with detail everywhere need more bytes for the same pixels than photos with plain areas, which is why the busy scene shrinks furthest.

How the tool gets to the limit

Quality first, pixels only when it must.

  1. Save at full sizeJPEG quality 92%fits: done
  2. Search the quality35% to 92%, 7 halvingsfits: done
  3. Shrink by 15% and search againdown to a tenth of the width, then: limit too smallfits: done
Read the exact steps
  1. It first saves your photo at its full size at JPEG quality 92%. If that already fits, that is the result.
  2. If not, it tries quality levels between 35% and 92% at full size, halving the gap seven times, and keeps the highest quality that fits.
  3. Only when even 35% quality is too big does it make the picture 15% narrower and shorter and search the quality again, step by step, down to a tenth of the width. Past that it says the limit is too small for the photo.

Always a JPEG

See-through areas become white; a sideways phone photo is turned upright; camera details such as location are not copied. A JPEG already under your limit is handed back as it is, details included.

Over 16 megapixels

Photos over 16 megapixels are first drawn at 16 megapixels, because phones and some browsers refuse larger canvases.

Inside your browser tab

All of it happens inside your browser tab: the page has no code that sends your photo anywhere.

Steps

Do it in four steps.

Open Image to size
  1. 1

    Choose the photo.

    Open Image to size and drop in a JPEG, PNG or WebP. It stays in your browser.

  2. 2

    Set the limit.

    Pick a size such as 50 KB, or type the limit the form gives you in KB.

  3. 3

    Let it search.

    The tool keeps the full size and lowers JPEG quality as far as 35%; only if that is not enough does it make the picture smaller.

  4. 4

    Download.

    Download the JPEG. The result shows its size, pixels and quality next to the original.
