# W-9s and backup withholding

Ask every contractor for a Form W-9 before you pay. If a payee does not give you a TIN, reportable payments are subject to backup withholding at 24%: on a $2,500 invoice you would hold back $600 and send it to the IRS. Once you withhold any amount, the 1099 is required whatever the total.

## Why collect the W-9 before the first payment?

- It gives the legal name and TIN that go on the 1099.
- It says how the payee is taxed, which decides whether the corporate exemption applies.
- Without a TIN, reportable payments are subject to backup withholding at 24%, which you must withhold and deposit.

## When should I send W-9 requests?

Before the first payment, and in any case before year end, so the forms can go out by **February 1, 2027**. The tracker's scan lists every payee that needs a form and has no TIN.

## Questions

### What is the backup withholding rate?

24%, per the IRS General Instructions for Certain Information Returns.

### When does backup withholding apply?

The IRS General Instructions list the cases: the payee does not give a TIN, the IRS tells you the TIN is incorrect, the IRS notifies you of underreporting, or the payee fails to certify where certification is required.

### Do I need a 1099 if I withheld from a payment under $2,000?

Yes. Backup withholding in any amount means the form is filed whatever the total, with the tax withheld shown in box 4.

### Can I check a TIN before I file?

The IRS TIN Matching program lets a payer or authorized agent match TIN and name combinations with IRS records before filing.
