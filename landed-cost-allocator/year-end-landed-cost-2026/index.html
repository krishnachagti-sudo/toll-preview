Year-end landed cost true-up, 2026

December 31, 2026 is a Thursday: before then post every late invoice for this year's shipments; on container CNT-2611, a $318.50 port storage bill adds $214.43 to the stock on hand and $104.07 to cost of goods sold for the units already sold.

The same container was received at $17,715.76 landed. The storage bill came after; without it the year-end stock value would be short by $214.43. The same check applies on December 31, 2027.

The amounts are the worked shipment's, entered as a merchant would from its invoices; nothing here is a rate. Not tax, accounting or customs advice.

Sources

  • IRS Publication 538 (01/2022), Accounting Periods and Methods (page reviewed April 30, 2026, read November 8, 2026)irs.gov

Steps

  1. List the shipments received this year. With every invoice booked against each.
  2. Chase the missing invoices. Freight, drayage, storage, duty and broker bills that have not arrived; ask for them before the close.
  3. Post the late ones. Mark each late and give the units on hand at year end, so on-hand units take their part and sold units' part is a variance.
  4. Value the stock on hand. Units on hand x their landed unit cost, per SKU.
  5. Hand over the files. The per-SKU CSV per shipment is the trail your accountant needs.
