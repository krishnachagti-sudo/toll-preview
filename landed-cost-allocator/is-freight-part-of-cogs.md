# Is freight part of cost of goods sold?

Inbound freight is part of what the inventory cost and reaches cost of goods sold only as each unit sells: IRS Publication 538 counts "transportation or other charges incurred in acquiring the goods" in the cost of merchandise, so here $1,840.00 of ocean freight adds $0.6369 to each T-shirt's cost, expensed when that T-shirt sells.

## What Publication 538 says

- "For merchandise purchased during the year, cost means the invoice price minus appropriate discounts plus transportation or other charges incurred in acquiring the goods."
- "You must reduce the cost of inventory by a trade (or quantity) discount."
- "Under the uniform capitalization rules, you must capitalize the direct costs and part of the indirect costs for production or resale activities."
- Not subject to those rules, among others: "A small business taxpayer that meets the gross receipts test under section 448(c) and that is not a tax shelter."

Outbound shipping to your customers is a cost of selling, not of acquiring the goods. Whether the uniform capitalization rules apply to your business, and how your books treat freight, are for you and your accountant.
