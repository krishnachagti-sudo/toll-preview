Pennsylvania Act 48 hours

Pennsylvania educators meet Act 48 with 6 college credits, 6 PDE-approved CPE credits, 180 hours or any combination every five years; counting a credit as 30 hours, 3 credits and 90 hours make the 180, while 2 CPE credits and 100 hours leave 20 hours to go.

One credit counted as 30 hours: the FAQ makes 6 credits, 6 CPE credits and 180 hours equal alternatives and allows any combination.

PDE's words

  • "Educators must earn six semester credits of collegiate study; OR six CPE credits of PDE-approved continuing professional education courses; OR 180 hours of continuing professional education programs, activities or learning experiences through a PDE approved provider; OR any combination of these every five years to maintain an active certification status." Pennsylvania Department of Education, General Act 48 FAQ
  • "The period begins as of the effective date upon issuance of the educator's initial Instructional certificate and is renewed after the end date if all requirements are met." Pennsylvania Department of Education, General Act 48 FAQ
  • "Legislation passed as of July 13, 2016 stipulates up to a maximum of 50 excess hours earned within the last two years of the compliance period may be attributed to the succeeding five-year period." Pennsylvania Department of Education, General Act 48 FAQ
  • "It is the responsibility of the educator to monitor their record of credits or hours in PERMS. It is also the educator's responsibility to contact the provider(s) to correct any discrepancies." Pennsylvania Department of Education, General Act 48 FAQ
  • "If a certificate holder does not complete the requirements of Act 48 within the prescribed period the certificate remains valid but becomes inactive." Pennsylvania Department of Education, General Act 48 FAQ
  • "Once an educator with an inactive certificate meets the remainder of their Act 48 requirements and reports those credits or hours to PERMS, the system will automatically return the status back to active overnight." Pennsylvania Department of Education, General Act 48 FAQ
  • "At least 12 months prior to the end of the five-year period, PDE will send a notice regarding any remaining credits or hours still needed to fulfill requirements, if applicable." Pennsylvania Department of Education, General Act 48 FAQ

Check your record in PERMS with your PPID; see missing hours in your state portal.

Rules quoted from each state's own education agency page, with the date we read it. Not legal advice: your state agency, LPDC or district decides what counts. Totals come from the activities you enter; this tool does not read PERMS, TEACH, ECOS or any state portal. Questions

What happens if I do not finish my Act 48 hours?

The certificate stays valid but becomes inactive, and you cannot work as a professional or temporary professional employee in a Pennsylvania public school until the hours are reported.
