Missing hours in your state portal

Find out who reports your hours first: in Pennsylvania the provider reports to PERMS and you must check it and ask the provider to fix gaps (PDE writes at least 12 months before your 5-year period ends); Texas does not count hours for you at all, and Tennessee and Montana have you enter your own PDPs or PDUs.

Why a private log helps

A portal shows what was reported; your log shows what you did, with the evidence. The difference is the list of providers to call, and the date each hour was earned tells you whether it still falls inside the cycle.

Rules quoted from each state's own education agency page, with the date we read it. Not legal advice: your state agency, LPDC or district decides what counts. Totals come from the activities you enter; this tool does not read PERMS, TEACH, ECOS or any state portal. States whose official pages we could not read are listed as not covered rather than guessed.

  • Tennessee Department of Education, Professional Development Points (PDP) FAQ for Educators, read November 8, 2026tn.gov
  • Montana Office of Public Instruction, Renew, Update, Advance License, read November 8, 2026opi.mt.gov
  • Wyoming Professional Teaching Standards Board, Workshop or Renewal Credit Notice, read November 8, 2026wyomingptsb.com

Steps

  1. Check who reports. The provider (Pennsylvania), your district (Tennessee approvals), a workshop facilitator (Wyoming) or you (Texas, Montana).
  2. Collect the proof. Certificate of completion, transcript or attendance record, with the date, hours and provider.
  3. Contact the provider, then the district, then the agency. Keep the date and any ticket number of each request.
  4. Log it yourself meanwhile. A private log with the evidence column shows exactly which line is missing and when it was earned.
