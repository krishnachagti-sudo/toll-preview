# Spreadsheet log vs a rules-aware tracker

A spreadsheet totals what you type; it does not know that Texas counts a semester credit as 15 CPE hours and caps independent study at 20 percent (30 of 150), or that New York needs 15 of 100 CTLE hours on language acquisition.

## What we read on their pages

- Google describes Sheets as "Collaborative, AI-powered spreadsheets" (workspace.google.com, read November 8, 2026).
- A teacher on r/teaching logs PD in a Google Sheet with date, hours, topic, delivery and notes.

## When they are the better choice

A spreadsheet is better for teachers who want full control of their columns and already keep one. The tracker is better when unit conversions, caps and required categories are the hard part; its export is a CSV you can open in the same spreadsheet.

## What this tracker does

- Holds the rule for 22 states, each quoted from the state agency and dated November 8, 2026; other states are listed as not covered.
- Converts each activity to the state's unit, applies caps and required topics, and flags activities outside the cycle.
- Free totals on the web; the audit export is $0.99 for a whole renewal cycle.

Open the [Teacher license renewal tracker](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/teacher-license-renewal-tracker/).

> Rules quoted from each state's own education agency page, with the date we read it. Not legal advice: your state agency, LPDC or district decides what counts. Totals come from the activities you enter; this tool does not read PERMS, TEACH, ECOS or any state portal. States whose official pages we could not read are listed as not covered rather than guessed.
