# Is the unit exempt from the guideline?

New buildings, additions and most new basement or second units first occupied for residential purposes after November 15, 2018 are exempt from the guideline (RTA s.6.1), and the landlord must prove it; of the 7 cases below, 3 are exempt.

- Cutoff: November 15, 2018
- Cases exempt: 3 of 7
- Notice still: 90 days
- Burden of proof: Landlord

## The tests

- A building: no part of it was occupied for residential purposes on or before November 15, 2018 (s.6.1(2) para 1).
- An addition: the unit is entirely in an addition no part of which was so occupied (para 2).
- A unit in a detached, semi-detached or row house: the house had at most two units on or before that date; the unit has its own bathroom and kitchen and a door that secures from inside and locks from outside; it became such a unit after that date; and either the owner lived in another unit of the house when it was first occupied, or it was built in unfinished space (s.6.1(3)).
- Then: not for a tenancy agreed on or before November 15, 2018 (s.6.1(4)), but yes for any later tenancy of the unit (s.6.1(5)).

What the exemption lifts: the guideline (s.120), N10 agreements and above-guideline applications (ss.121, 122, 126, 127) and some rent reductions. What it does not lift: 90 days' written notice (s.116) and 12 months between increases (s.119).

Ontario.ca also lists, outside s.6.1: community housing, long-term care homes and commercial property are not under the guideline, and social housing has its own rules. The calculator checks only s.6.1.

## Questions

### Is a building built in 2020 subject to rent control in Ontario?

Not to the guideline if no part of it was occupied for residential purposes on or before November 15, 2018 (RTA s.6.1(2)). The 90-day notice and the 12-month rule still apply.

### Does the exemption depend on when my tenancy started?

No, it depends on when the building, addition or unit was first occupied. One exception: a tenancy agreed on or before November 15, 2018 keeps the guideline (s.6.1(4)).

### Who has to prove the exemption?

The landlord (s.6.1(6)). Ontario.ca suggests keeping building and occupancy permits, new home warranty papers and builder documents, and for a new unit in a house, contractor invoices and before-and-after photos.
