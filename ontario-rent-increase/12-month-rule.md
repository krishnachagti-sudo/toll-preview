# The 12-month rule

Rent can rise only once at least 12 months have passed since the last increase or the start of the tenancy (RTA s.119); a lease that began June 1, 2026 allows the first increase on June 1, 2027, with the N1 served by March 3, 2027, and one that began February 29, 2024 allowed it on February 28, 2025.

- June 1, 2026 lease: first increase: June 1, 2027
- Months between: 12
- Notice: 90 days

Both rules run together: the increase date must be at least 12 months after the last one, and the N1 must be given at least 90 days before it. A notice can be served before the 12 months are up, as long as the increase itself starts on or after the anniversary.

## Questions

### Can a landlord increase rent in Ontario more than once a year?

Not under the guideline: at least 12 months must have elapsed since the last increase, or since the unit was first rented to that tenant (RTA s.119(1)). An increase for added parking or a prescribed service under s.123 does not count (s.119(2)).

### What if the 12 months end on the 31st and the next month is shorter?

Months land on the same day number, or on the last day of a month that has no such day (Legislation Act s.89(6)); a February 29 anniversary falls on February 28 outside leap years (s.89(7)).
