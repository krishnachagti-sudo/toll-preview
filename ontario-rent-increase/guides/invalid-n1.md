- Served by mail on September 5, 2026, the notice counts as given on September 10, 2026: 82 days before December 1, 2026, short of 90. Without 90 days the increase is void and a new notice is needed (s.116(4)); the earliest date this notice allows is December 9, 2026.

## Steps

1. **The form.** A written notice in the Board's form, stating the new rent (RTA s.116(3)). A text message is not the form.
2. **12 months.** The increase date is at least 12 months after the last increase or your move-in (s.119).
3. **90 days.** At least 90 days between the day it counts as given and the increase; mail counts from the fifth day after mailing (s.191(3)).
4. **The amount.** Not above the guideline for the year it takes effect, unless the unit is exempt, you signed an N10, or the Board ordered more (s.120).
5. **The deadline.** Apply to the Landlord and Tenant Board within one year of the increase being first charged; after that it is deemed lawful (s.136(2)) and a void increase you paid for 12 months is deemed valid (s.135.1).

## Questions

### What should I pay while the notice is wrong?

The Act does not let a landlord take a void increase (s.116(4)). If an above-guideline application is pending, you need pay no more than the guideline amount until the order (s.126(5)). Paying the increase for a year without applying can make it lawful (ss.135.1, 136).

### How long do I have to dispute a rent increase in Ontario?

One year after the amount was first charged (RTA s.136; ontario.ca says within 12 months).
