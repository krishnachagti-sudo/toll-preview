The rules

The rules it applies, section by section.

The guideline for the year the increase takes effect, 12 months since the last one, and 90 days' written notice on the Board's form.

What it does not cover

Above-guideline increases, courier, fax and email service, and legal advice.

Sources and notes

Sources, read November 8, 2026: Ontario.ca, Residential rent increases (updated June 23, 2026); Residential Tenancies Act, 2006 (e-Laws, current from September 21, 2026); Legislation Act, 2006 (current from December 11, 2025); LTB forms (form names). Computed from the Residential Tenancies Act, 2006, the Legislation Act, 2006 and ontario.ca, each cited with the date read. Not legal advice. Above-guideline increases need an order of the Landlord and Tenant Board or a signed N10 agreement. Courier, fax and email service depend on the Landlord and Tenant Board's Rules and are not computed here.

  • The guideline for the calendar year the increase takes effect, from ontario.ca's chart: 1.9% for 2027, 2.1% for 2026, capped at 2.5% (RTA s.120). The new rent is rounded down to the cent so it is never above it.
  • 12 months since the last increase, or since the tenancy began (s.119), counted to the same day number or the month's last day (Legislation Act s.89(6)).
  • 90 days' written notice on the Board's form (s.116), the day it is given not counted (Legislation Act s.89(3)). Mail counts as given on the fifth day after mailing (s.191(3)). Courier, fax and email depend on the Board's Rules (s.191(1)(g)) and are not computed.
  • The exemption for buildings, additions and new units in houses first occupied after November 15, 2018 (s.6.1). It lifts the guideline, not the notice or the 12 months.
  • Deposit interest each year at the guideline in effect when due; the landlord may keep what tops the deposit up to the rent (s.106(6), (7)).
