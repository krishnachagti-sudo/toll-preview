# N1 deadline: the last day to serve

For a rent increase on January 1, 2027, serve the N1 by October 3, 2026 in person, or mail it by September 28, 2026; for March 1, 2027, by December 1, 2026 by hand or November 26, 2026 by mail.

- January 1, 2027, by hand: October 3, 2026
- January 1, 2027, by mail: September 28, 2026
- Notice: 90 days
- Mail adds: 5 days

## The three ways the Act names

- Handed to the tenant, or to an apparently adult person in the unit (s.191(1)(a), (c)): given that day.
- Left in the mail box where the tenant's mail is delivered, or where mail is delivered if there is no box (s.191(1)(d), (e)): given that day.
- Sent by mail to the last known address (s.191(1)(f)): given on the fifth day after mailing (s.191(3)).

A notice that reached the tenant some other way still counts if it is proven it came to their attention in time (s.191(2)). See [how the 90 days are counted](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/ontario-rent-increase/guides/n1-notice-timing/).

## Questions

### How much notice does an Ontario landlord need to give for a rent increase?

At least 90 days' written notice in the Board's form (RTA s.116(1), (3)): the day the notice is given does not count and the increase day does (Legislation Act s.89(3)).

### If I mail the N1, does the 90 days start when I mail it?

No. Mail is deemed given on the fifth day after mailing (RTA s.191(3)), so mail it at least 95 days before the increase.

### Can the N1 be sent by email?

The Act names handing it over, the mail box and mail, plus any other means the Board's Rules allow (s.191(1)(g)). Email depends on those Rules and is not computed here.

### What if the notice was late?

An increase without the required notice is void and needs a new notice (s.116(4)). But if the tenant pays it for 12 consecutive months without applying to the Board within a year, it is deemed not void (s.135.1).
