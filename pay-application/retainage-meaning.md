# Retainage: what it means

Retainage (or retention) is the share of each progress payment the payer holds back until the work is complete; at 10% on $100,000.00 of work to date, $10,000.00 is held and $90,000.00 is paid.

[Work it out](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/pay-application/?calc=forward&amount=100%2C000.00&rate=10)

## How it works

- The contract sets the rate (the examples here use 10% and 5%) and when it is paid back, for example at substantial completion or a set number of days after it.
- Each pay application holds the rate on the work completed and stored to date, so the amount held grows with the job.
- It flows down: a GC held 10% by the owner usually holds the same from its subcontractors, and some statutes forbid holding more from a sub than is held from the prime.
- Some states limit it by statute; the covered ones are on the state table.

## Questions

### Is retainage the same as retention?

Yes: both words name the money held back from progress payments until completion. Statutes use both: Washington's RCW 60.28.011 is headed "Retained percentage", Minnesota's 337.10 says "retainage".

### What is 10% retainage?

The payer holds 10% of the work billed to date: $10,000.00 on $100,000.00, paid later when the release condition is met.
