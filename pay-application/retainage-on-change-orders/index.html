Retainage on change orders

Unless the contract says otherwise, a signed change order is part of the contract sum and is held at the same rate as the rest of the work: on an $8,500.00 change order at 10% retainage, $850.00 is held once it is fully billed, $425.00 when half is billed.

How change orders go on the pay application

  • AIA's instructions for its continuation sheet say change orders are usually listed separately, on their own sheet or at the end of the schedule of values, rather than by changing the schedule; the contract sum adjusted by change orders goes on the summary.
  • Bill a change order only once it is executed (signed). One that is approved in principle but unsigned is listed as pending; the tool leaves it out of the totals and flags any amount billed on it.
  • Some statutes set the rate for every item: for Texas public works, Tex. Gov't Code 2252.032 says the retainage rate may not exceed the limit "for any item in a bid schedule or schedule of values for the project, including materials and equipment delivered on site to be installed".

Put signed change orders on the pay application as their own lines; it adds them to the contract sum and holds retainage on them at the contract rate unless you give a rate for that line.

Not legal advice. State rules are quoted from the statute on the state's own site as read on November 8, 2026; your contract, the project's funding and later changes in the law can change the answer.

Questions

Do you hold retainage on change orders?

Usually, at the contract rate, because the change order becomes part of the contract sum and is billed like a line; the contract can say otherwise. On $8,500.00 fully billed at 10% that is $850.00.

What about a deduct change order?

It lowers the contract sum, and any retainage held on that work comes off with it: a -$1,500.00 deduction at 10% is -$150.00 of retainage.
