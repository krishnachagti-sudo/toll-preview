# Front-loading a schedule of values

Front-loading moves value to the lines finished first: on the same $240,000 job and the same first month of work, raising mobilization from $12,000 to $30,000 (and lowering trim and closeout) asks for $35,640.00 instead of $19,440.00, $16,200.00 more now and the same amount less at the end.

| Line | Balanced | Front-loaded |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Mobilization and submittals | $12,000.00 | $30,000.00 |
| Underground and rough-in | $96,000.00 | $96,000.00 |
| Switchgear and panels | $60,000.00 | $60,000.00 |
| Trim, fixtures and devices | $60,000.00 | $48,000.00 |
| Testing and closeout | $12,000.00 | $6,000.00 |
| Month 1 due (10% retainage) | $19,440.00 | $35,640.00 |

## What reviewers look for

- A line whose value is far above what that work costs, especially the first lines billed.
- Closeout and testing lines so small that finishing the job is not worth billing.
- AIA's instructions say the schedule of values should stay consistent through the project, so a front-loaded schedule approved at the start is the one you bill against all job.

## Defensible lines

- Bonds, insurance and permits as their own lines, at their invoiced cost.
- Submittals and shop drawings as a line, billed when approved.
- Mobilization at what it actually costs to set up.
- Closeout (as-builts, O&M manuals, training) large enough to be worth finishing.
