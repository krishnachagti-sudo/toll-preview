The rules

How the numbers are worked out, line by line.

State retainage rules are quoted from each statute on the state's own site, read November 8, 2026.

What it does not cover

The AIA documents themselves, states not listed below, and legal or accounting advice.

Please read
  • Not legal or accounting advice. The arithmetic is exact for the numbers entered; whether they match your contract is yours to check.
  • G702/G703-style: this tool's own layout with the same column logic as AIA's published instructions. It is not the AIA documents and is not affiliated with or endorsed by the American Institute of Architects. If your contract requires the AIA forms, use them.
  • State retainage rules are quoted from each statute on the state's own site, read November 8, 2026. Your contract, the project's funding and later changes in the law can change the answer.
  • States covered: Arizona, Florida, Kansas, Minnesota, Texas, Virginia and Washington, each only for the kinds of project shown. Every other state is listed as not covered rather than guessed at.

Each line

Previous plus this period plus stored; the percent and balance follow.

Read the full rule

Completed and stored to date is previous applications plus this period plus materials stored now. The percent is that total over the scheduled value, and the balance to finish is the scheduled value less the total. A line cannot pass 100%: extra work goes on a change order.

The summary

Earned less retainage, less previous certificates, is the amount due now.

Read the full rule

The contract sum to date is the original sum plus signed change orders. Retainage is held on completed work and on stored materials (each at its own rate if the contract says so). Earned less retainage, less the previous certificates, is the amount due now.

The checks

Sums, lines over 100%, unsigned change orders, certificates and state limits.

Read the full rule

Lines that do not add up to the contract sum, lines over 100%, billing on an unsigned change order, previous certificates that do not match the earlier work less its retainage, a negative amount due, and a retainage rate above the state's limit where the statute was read.
