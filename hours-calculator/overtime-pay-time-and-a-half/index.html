Overtime pay at time and a half

Overtime pay is 1.5 times the regular rate for each hour over 40 in the workweek: at $20.00 an hour, a 45-hour week pays $800.00 for 40 regular hours plus $150.00 for 5.00 overtime hours at $30.00, $950.00 in all.

The formula

gross = rate × min(hours, 40) + rate × 1.5 × max(hours − 40, 0)
$20.00 × 40 + $30.00 × 5 = $800.00 + $150.00 = $950.00

The rule: overtime is due for "hours worked in excess of 40 in a workweek at a rate not less than time and one-half their regular rates of pay" (DOL Fact Sheet #23). The regular rate can include more than the hourly wage (some bonuses, for example); this table uses the hourly rate alone.

When the week is not 40 plus extra

  • Two weeks of 30 and 50 hours still owe 10 overtime hours, see the biweekly example.
  • Paid holidays and vacation are not hours worked, see holiday week overtime.
  • Rounded punches can move a week across 40, see time clock rounding rules.

Questions

How is time and a half calculated?

Multiply the regular hourly rate by 1.5 and pay that for each hour over 40 in the workweek. At $20.00 the overtime rate is $30.00, so 5 overtime hours are $150.00.

Is overtime after 8 hours a day?

Not under federal law: the FLSA counts hours over 40 in a workweek (DOL Fact Sheet #23). Some states add daily overtime; the calculator has a daily threshold setting for those.

Can two weeks be averaged to avoid overtime?

No. "Averaging of hours over two or more weeks is not permitted." (Fact Sheet #23).
