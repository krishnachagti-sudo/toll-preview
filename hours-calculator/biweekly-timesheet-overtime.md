# Biweekly timesheet with overtime

Overtime is counted on each workweek, never on the two-week total: 30.00 hours in the first week and 50.00 in the second is 10.00 hours of overtime, although the 80.00 hours over two weeks average 40 a week.

- Overtime hours: 10.00
- Week 1: 30.00
- Week 2: 50.00
- Gross at $20.00: $1,700.00

## What averaging would cost

At $20.00 an hour, counted per workweek: $1,400.00 regular plus $300.00 overtime = $1,700.00. Counted on 80 hours for the two weeks there would be 0.00 overtime hours and 80:00 at straight time, $1,600.00: $100.00 short. 29 CFR 778.104: "The Act takes a single workweek as its standard and does not permit averaging of hours over 2 or more weeks."

## Steps

1. **Fix the workweek.** Use the employer's fixed workweek of 168 hours; it may start on any day and hour (29 CFR 778.105).
2. **Total each workweek.** Add each day's minutes, after unpaid breaks, within its workweek. Split a shift that runs past the start of the next workweek.
3. **Overtime per week.** Hours over 40 in each workweek are overtime, at not less than 1.5 times the regular rate. Do not average the two weeks.
4. **Add the weeks.** Regular and overtime hours of both weeks give the pay period's totals.

## Questions

### Is overtime calculated per week or per pay period?

Per workweek. Fact Sheet #23: "Averaging of hours over two or more weeks is not permitted."

### Is it overtime after 80 hours in two weeks?

Not under the FLSA. In this example the two weeks total 80.00 hours, nothing over 80, yet 10.00 hours are overtime.

### Does working a weekend mean overtime?

Not by itself. Fact Sheet #23: "The Act does not require overtime pay for work on Saturdays, Sundays, holidays, or regular days of rest, as such."
