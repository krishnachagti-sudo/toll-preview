# A timesheet you can print and sign

A timesheet records each day's in and out times, unpaid breaks and hours, the workweek's total and its overtime, signed by the employee and an approver; the sample below totals 46:00 (46.00 hours), and federal law asks for time cards to be kept 2 years and payroll records 3.

- Sample week: 46.00
- Keep time cards: 2 years
- Keep payroll records: 3 years

## What goes on it

- Employee and employer, and the pay period's first and last day.
- The day and time the workweek starts. Fact Sheet #21 lists it among the records an employer keeps.
- Each day: every in and out time, the unpaid break, and the hours in hours:minutes and decimal.
- Each workweek: total hours, regular hours and overtime hours.
- Rounding, if the clock rounds: the rule used and what it changed.
- A line for the employee to sign that the hours are true, and one for the approver, each with a date.

## Questions

### Is a specific timesheet form required?

No. Fact Sheet #21: "The Act requires no particular form for the records."

### How long must employers keep time cards?

Fact Sheet #21: payroll records for at least 3 years; time cards, as records on which wage computations are based, for 2 years.
