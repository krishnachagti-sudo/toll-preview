Hours worked with a lunch break

Take the unpaid lunch off the time between in and out: 8:45 AM to 5:15 PM with a 30-minute lunch is 8:00 (8.00 hours), the same as punching out at 12:00 PM and back in at 12:30 PM (8.00 hours).

Two ways to enter lunch

  • One pair and a break: 8:45 AM - 5:15 PM with 30 minutes unpaid is 8:00.
  • Two pairs: 8:45 AM - 12:00 PM and 12:30 PM - 5:15 PM is 8:00; the gap between the pairs is the unpaid time.

A 15-minute gap between pairs is flagged by the calculator: 9 to 1 then 1:15 to 5 counts 7:45 as entered, but rest periods of 5 to about 20 minutes count as hours worked under 29 CFR 785.18.

Break lengths on an 8 to 5 day

29 CFR 785.19: "Bona fide meal periods are not worktime. Bona fide meal periods do not include coffee breaks or time for snacks. These are rest periods. The employee must be completely relieved from duty for the purposes of eating regular meals. Ordinarily 30 minutes or more is long enough for a bona fide meal period."

A lunch deducted automatically when it was not taken, or taken while still working, is time worked under 785.19. This page does not decide a particular case. Not legal or payroll advice.

Questions

How do I calculate hours worked with a 30-minute lunch?

End minus start, minus 30 minutes: 8:45 AM to 5:15 PM is 8 hours 30 minutes, less 30 is 8.00 hours.

Is a 15-minute break paid?

Under federal rules rest periods of 5 to about 20 minutes "must be counted as hours worked" (29 CFR 785.18). A bona fide meal period, ordinarily 30 minutes or more with the employee completely relieved from duty, is not work time (785.19).

Do states require a lunch break?

The federal rules do not require one. The DOL's table of state meal-period laws (last updated January 1, 2023) lists 23 jurisdictions that do.
