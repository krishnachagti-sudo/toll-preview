Payroll reference

Time clock rounding rules

Under 29 CFR 785.48(b) an employer may record start and stop times to the nearest 5 minutes, tenth or quarter hour only if it does not, over time, underpay for time actually worked; at the quarter hour, 1 to 7 minutes past rounds down and 8 to 14 rounds up (the "7-minute rule"), and in this week's example nearest rounding pays 38:30 for 38:20 punched while in-up, out-down pays 37:00.

What the regulation says

29 CFR 785.48(b): "It has been found that in some industries, particularly where time clocks are used, there has been the practice for many years of recording the employees' starting time and stopping time to the nearest 5 minutes, or to the nearest one-tenth or quarter of an hour. Presumably, this arrangement averages out so that the employees are fully compensated for all the time they actually work. For enforcement purposes this practice of computing working time will be accepted, provided that it is used in such a manner that it will not result, over a period of time, in failure to compensate the employees properly for all the time they have actually worked."

One punch, rounded each way

At the quarter hour the nearest rule moves a punch by at most 7 minutes either way; always rounding an in punch up can take up to 14 minutes from it.

An example week, both ways

Punched: 38:20. Nearest quarter hour: 38:30 (6 punches for the worker, 4 against). In up, out down: 37:00, -1:20 hours over the week.

This page shows what rounding does to the numbers; it does not decide whether a rounding practice is lawful. A state may be stricter than the federal rule. Not legal advice.

Questions

What is the 7-minute rule?

A name for rounding to the nearest quarter hour: a punch 1 to 7 minutes past a quarter hour rounds down, 8 to 14 rounds up. 29 CFR 785.48(b) does not use the name; it accepts rounding to the nearest 5 minutes, tenth or quarter hour if it does not underpay over time.

Can my employer round my time?

Federal rules accept rounding to the nearest 5 minutes, tenth or quarter hour provided it does not, over a period of time, fail to pay employees for all the time they actually worked (29 CFR 785.48(b)). Some states are stricter.

What does rounding in punches up and out punches down do?

It only ever removes time. On the example week below it pays 37:00 for 38:20 punched, 10 of 10 punches against the worker.
