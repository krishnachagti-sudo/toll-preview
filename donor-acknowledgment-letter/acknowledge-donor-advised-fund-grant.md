# Acknowledging a donor-advised fund grant

Thank the donor who recommended the grant (in the sample, $2,500.00 from Fidelity Charitable (Lee Family Fund)), name the fund's sponsor, and leave out any tax-deductible or receipt language: the sponsoring organization made the grant, and once a donor gives to the fund "the organization has legal control over it" (IRS). The letter says so: "Because Fidelity Charitable (Lee Family Fund) made the grant, this letter is a thank-you and not a receipt for a tax deduction."

## Who gave what

- **The legal donor:** the sponsoring organization. Generally, a donor advised fund is a separately identified fund or account that is maintained and operated by a section 501(c)(3) organization, which is called a sponsoring organization.
- **The advisor:** the person you know. "However, the donor, or the donor's representative, retains advisory privileges with respect to the distribution of funds and the investment of assets in the account."
- **Their deduction:** taken when they gave to the fund, on the sponsor's acknowledgment. IRS Publication 526 says a donor cannot deduct a gift to a donor-advised fund if "you don't have a contemporaneous written acknowledgment from that sponsoring organization that it has exclusive legal control over the assets contributed."

## What the thank-you says

- From: Riverside Food Bank (sample), 22 Market Street, Dayton, OH 45402, EIN 12-3456789
- Date: December 21, 2026
- To: The Lee Family, 8 Park Lane, Oakwood, OH 45419

Dear Lee Family,

Thank you for your first gift to Riverside Food Bank (sample).

We received a grant of $2,500.00 on December 16, 2026 from Fidelity Charitable (Lee Family Fund), made on your recommendation for Annual fund.

Because Fidelity Charitable (Lee Family Fund) made the grant, this letter is a thank-you and not a receipt for a tax deduction.

**No goods or services were provided in exchange for this grant.**

With gratitude, Dana Lee, Executive Director, Riverside Food Bank (sample)

## What it leaves out

- Any line that the gift is tax-deductible to the advisor, and "keep this letter for your tax records".
- The 501(c)(3) line used on receipts (the letter maker omits it on DAF letters).
- Practice, from a DAF sponsor: "Make sure you remove any reference that suggests that the donor is eligible to claim a tax deduction in connection to the donation." Its sample says "Remember, this is not a tax receipt."

## And the sponsor?

Sponsors send grants with their own paperwork and say what, if anything, they want back. The letter maker writes the advisor's thank-you; follow the sponsor's instructions for anything it asks.

## Questions

### Who is the donor of a DAF grant?

The sponsoring organization. Once the donor makes the contribution, the organization has legal control over it. However, the donor, or the donor's representative, retains advisory privileges with respect to the distribution of funds and the investment of assets in the account. (IRS: Donor-advised funds.)

### Should the letter say the gift is tax-deductible?

No. A DAF sponsor's guidance: "A donor who recommends a grant from a donor-advised fund is not eligible to claim a tax deduction in connection to the grant." and "Make sure you remove any reference that suggests that the donor is eligible to claim a tax deduction in connection to the donation."

### Is there mandated wording?

No IRS text is prescribed for the advisor's thank-you; what follows is common practice.
