What the file needs

Three columns: the donor (Donor, Name or Customer), the date (Date, Gift date) and the amount (Amount, Gift amount, Gross). Dates may be written 2026-11-03, 11/3/2026 or Nov 3, 2026.

The Type column picks the letter: "In-kind" or "Stock" with a Description (no amount needed, and none is printed), "DAF grant" or a DAF sponsor column, "Pledge", "Volunteer services". A Benefit value and Benefit description make a ticket or auction letter. In memory of or In honor of, with Notify and Notify address, add a family notice. A refund or a negative amount is set aside.

Optional: Salutation, Fund, Method, Previous gift date and First gift date (for the segments), Address, City, State, ZIP, Email.

The IRS rules

Which sentence each gift gets, and why.

One letter per gift, with the sentence its kind of gift needs.

Read every kind of gift

Each gift gets its own letter with the sentence it needs: no goods or services for a cash gift (or intangible religious benefits for a church), a good faith estimate and the $75 disclosure for a gala ticket, a description without a value for donated goods or stock, a thank-you without receipt language for a donor-advised fund grant, a confirmation for a pledge, and a notice to a memorial family that never shows the amount.

What it does not cover

Form 1098-C for donated vehicles, year-end statements, and tax or legal advice. Your organization sets the values.

Read the full list

Your organization decides the good faith estimate of any goods or services and whether an exception applies; the letters print what you enter.

The donor-advised fund, memorial, pledge and volunteer letters follow common practice; the IRS prescribes no wording for them. A donated vehicle claimed at over $500 follows Form 1098-C rules, which this tool does not produce. The donor's Social Security number is not needed and is not printed.

Not tax or legal advice.

One statement for a donor's whole year is a different document: see year-end giving statements.

The $250 rule

A single gift of $250 or more needs your written acknowledgment before the donor files.

IRS Publication 1771 (Rev. 11-2023)

Read the full rules

A donor cannot deduct a single gift of $250 or more without a written acknowledgment from you, received before they file (IRS Publication 1771). The letter names your organization, gives a money gift's amount, describes a gift of property without valuing it, and says whether you gave anything in return.

Something in return

A ticket or auction item gets a good faith estimate, and over $75 the disclosure too.

IRS: quid pro quo contributions

Read the full rules

A gala ticket or auction item gets a description of what the donor received and your good faith estimate of its value. A payment over $75 that was partly for goods or services also needs the disclosure that only the excess is deductible; the letter gives it.

DAF grants, pledges, volunteers

Thanked without receipt language: none of them is a deductible gift from the donor.

IRS: donor-advised funds

Read the full rules

A donor-advised fund grant comes from the fund's sponsoring organization, which has legal control of the money (IRS), so the advisor's letter is a thank-you without receipt language. A pledge is not a contribution until it is paid (IRS Pub 526), and volunteer time is not deductible, so those letters thank without receipting.

Sources

IRS Publications 1771 and 526 and the IRS pages, each with the date it was read.

Read the sources

Worded from your file and the rules in IRS Publication 1771 (Rev. 11-2023), read November 7, 2026, and IRS Publication 526 and the IRS donor-advised funds page, read November 8, 2026.

Not tax or legal advice. Sources: IRS Publication 1771 (Rev. 11-2023), IRS: written acknowledgments and IRS: quid pro quo contributions (both updated June 28, 2026), IRS Publication 526, IRS: donor-advised funds, and for the DAF letter's practice Fidelity Charitable's guidance.
