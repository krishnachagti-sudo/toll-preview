# "No goods or services were provided in exchange for this contribution"

It is the statement IRS Publication 1771 asks a written acknowledgment to carry when the charity gave the donor nothing in return: "a statement that no goods or services were provided by the organization in return for the contribution, if that was the case"; the letter maker words it "No goods or services were provided in exchange for your contribution."

## The three forms of the statement

IRS Publication 1771, page 3, items 4 to 6, and the sentence the letter maker prints.

| Situation | Pub 1771 | On the letter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nothing given in return | a statement that no goods or services were provided by the organization in return for the contribution, if that was the case | No goods or services were provided in exchange for your contribution. |
| Something given (a ticket, a dinner) | If the organization did provide goods or services in return for the contribution, a description and good faith estimate of the fair market value of the goods or services | In exchange for your payment, we provided goods or services: (description). Our good faith estimate of their fair market value is (amount). |
| Church: only intangible religious benefits | If the organization only provided intangible religious benefits (described later in this publication) in return for the contribution, a statement so providing. | The only goods or services provided in exchange for your contribution were intangible religious benefits. |

If a religious charitable organization provides only "intangible religious benefits" to a donor, the contemporaneous written acknowledgment does not need to describe or value those benefits. It can simply state that the organization provided intangible religious benefits to the donor. Examples include admission to a religious ceremony and a de minimis tangible benefit, such as wine used in a religious ceremony. Benefits that are not intangible religious benefits include education leading to a recognized degree, travel services, and consumer goods.

## When not to use it

- When the donor received something of value: describe it and estimate it (see [the gala ticket letter](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/donor-acknowledgment-letter/examples/)).
- When the gift came from a donor-advised fund: the letter maker says "No goods or services were provided in exchange for this grant" and adds that the letter is not a receipt.
- For a pledge or volunteer time, which are not receipted at all.

## Questions

### What does "no goods or services were provided" mean?

That the charity gave the donor nothing in return for the gift, so the whole gift is a contribution. It is one of the statements IRS Publication 1771 asks a written acknowledgment to carry.

### Is it required on every receipt?

On every written acknowledgment for a gift of $250 or more, in one of three forms: nothing provided, a description and good faith estimate of what was provided, or (for a religious organization) only intangible religious benefits.

### What if the donor got a dinner or a ticket?

Then the letter describes it and gives your good faith estimate of its value instead; over $75, it also says only the excess is deductible.
