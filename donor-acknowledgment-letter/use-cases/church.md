# Church donation receipt letters

A church letter for a gift where the donor received only intangible religious benefits says so in one sentence: the sample for a $300.00 building-fund check reads "The only goods or services provided in exchange for your contribution were intangible religious benefits." Weekly offerings are usually acknowledged once a year on the annual statement instead.

## The filled letter

A household gives a $300 building-fund check. Made up for this page.

- From: Grace Community Church (sample), 100 Main Street, Springfield, OH 45501
- Date: December 14, 2026
- To: John and Mary Smith, 12 Oak Lane, Springfield, OH 45501

Dear John and Mary Smith,

Thank you for your first gift to Grace Community Church (sample).

We received your contribution of $300.00 on December 6, 2026 for Building Fund (Check 1031).

**The only goods or services provided in exchange for your contribution were intangible religious benefits.**

Please keep this letter with your tax records.

With gratitude, Ruth Alvarez, Financial Secretary, Grace Community Church (sample)

## Per gift or once a year?

Pub 1771 requires an acknowledgment for each single gift of $250 or more, and allows one annual summary to cover several. Many churches send a letter for large gifts as they come in and a statement for the year in January. For the year, use [the church contribution statement](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/year-end-giving-statements/templates/church-contribution-statement/).

What counts as intangible religious benefits: Examples include admission to a religious ceremony and a de minimis tangible benefit, such as wine used in a religious ceremony. Benefits that are not intangible religious benefits include education leading to a recognized degree, travel services, and consumer goods.

## Questions

### What does a church receipt say about goods or services?

If the church gave only intangible religious benefits, it may simply say so: "The only goods or services provided in exchange for your contribution were intangible religious benefits." If a religious charitable organization provides only "intangible religious benefits" to a donor, the contemporaneous written acknowledgment does not need to describe or value those benefits. It can simply state that the organization provided intangible religious benefits to the donor.

### Does a church need a letter for every Sunday offering?

No. Separate contributions of less than $250 will not be aggregated. An example of this could be weekly offerings to a donor's church of less than $250 even though the donor's annual total contributions are $250 or more. One annual statement can cover the year: "one contemporaneous written acknowledgment, such as an annual summary, may be used to substantiate several single contributions valued at $250 or more."
