# Thank-you letter for a donor-advised fund grant

A DAF grant is thanked without tax-receipt language: the sponsoring organization made the grant (the IRS says it "has legal control" of the money once given), so the sample thanks The Lee Family for a $2,500.00 grant from Fidelity Charitable (Lee Family Fund) and says the letter is "a thank-you and not a receipt for a tax deduction".

## The filled letter

A family recommends a $2,500 grant from its fund at a national DAF sponsor. Made up for this page.

- From: Riverside Food Bank (sample), 22 Market Street, Dayton, OH 45402, EIN 12-3456789
- Date: December 21, 2026
- To: The Lee Family, 8 Park Lane, Oakwood, OH 45419

Dear Lee Family,

Thank you for your first gift to Riverside Food Bank (sample).

We received a grant of $2,500.00 on December 16, 2026 from Fidelity Charitable (Lee Family Fund), made on your recommendation for Annual fund.

Because Fidelity Charitable (Lee Family Fund) made the grant, this letter is a thank-you and not a receipt for a tax deduction.

**No goods or services were provided in exchange for this grant.**

With gratitude, Dana Lee, Executive Director, Riverside Food Bank (sample)

## Why there is no receipt wording

Generally, a donor advised fund is a separately identified fund or account that is maintained and operated by a section 501(c)(3) organization, which is called a sponsoring organization. Once the donor makes the contribution, the organization has legal control over it. However, the donor, or the donor's representative, retains advisory privileges with respect to the distribution of funds and the investment of assets in the account. (IRS: Donor-advised funds.)

The donor's deduction came when they gave to the fund. IRS Publication 526 says a donor cannot deduct a contribution to a donor-advised fund if "you don't have a contemporaneous written acknowledgment from that sponsoring organization that it has exclusive legal control over the assets contributed." That acknowledgment is the sponsor's, not yours.

Practice, from a DAF sponsor: "Make sure you remove any reference that suggests that the donor is eligible to claim a tax deduction in connection to the donation." Its sample letter says "Remember, this is not a tax receipt."

## Questions

### Should a charity give a tax receipt for a DAF grant?

Not to the advisor. Once the donor makes the contribution, the organization has legal control over it. However, the donor, or the donor's representative, retains advisory privileges with respect to the distribution of funds and the investment of assets in the account. (IRS). A DAF sponsor's guidance says the advisor "is not eligible to claim a tax deduction in connection to the grant".

### Is there required IRS wording for a DAF thank-you?

No. The wording here is common practice; the IRS prescribes none for it.

### Does the DAF sponsor need a letter too?

Many sponsors send the grant with their own paperwork. Follow what the sponsor asks for; the letter maker writes the thank-you to the advisor.
