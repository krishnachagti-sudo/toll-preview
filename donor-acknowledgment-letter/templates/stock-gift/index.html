Stock gift acknowledgment letter

A stock gift letter describes the shares (how many, of which company) and states no value, because IRS Publication 1771 asks for a description "but not the fair market value" of property; the sample thanks Ben Okafor for "50 shares of Example Corp common stock" received December 15, 2026.

The filled letter

A donor transfers 50 shares to the food bank's brokerage account. Made up for this page.

  • From: Riverside Food Bank (sample), 22 Market Street, Dayton, OH 45402, EIN 12-3456789
  • Date: December 21, 2026
  • To: Ben Okafor, 40 River Road, Dayton, OH 45402

Dear Ben Okafor,

Thank you for your first gift to Riverside Food Bank (sample).

We received your gift of 50 shares of Example Corp common stock on December 15, 2026. This letter describes your gift; it does not state its value.

No goods or services were provided in exchange for your contribution.

Riverside Food Bank (sample) is a tax-exempt organization under section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code (EIN 12-3456789).

Please keep this letter with your tax records.

With gratitude, Dana Lee, Executive Director, Riverside Food Bank (sample)

Questions

Should a stock gift letter state the value of the shares?

No. Publication 1771 asks for "a description (but not the fair market value) of any contribution of property". Describe the shares; the donor values them.

Which date goes on the letter?

The date the shares reached you. For the donor's own records, IRS Publication 526 says a properly endorsed stock certificate "is considered delivered on the date of mailing or other delivery to the charity or to the charity's agent".

Does the donor need anything else?

Possibly. IRS Publication 526: "If your total deduction for all noncash contributions for the year is over $500, you must complete Form 8283 and attach it to your Form 1040." That form is the donor's.
