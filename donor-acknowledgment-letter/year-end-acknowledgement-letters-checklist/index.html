Year-end acknowledgment letters: segments and checklist

Close the year's gifts on December 31, export one gift a row, and let each gift's type and segment choose its letter; the sample November batch of 9 gifts has 4 first gifts, 1 lapsed donor back, 2 major gifts, 1 DAF grant, 1 pledge and 1 memorial gift. Letters usually go out by January 31; the legal limit is the donor's filing date.

Steps

  1. Close the year's gifts. Gifts made by December 31 are this year's; a mailed check counts on the day it is mailed (IRS Publication 526).
  2. Export one gift a row. Include a type column for goods, stock, DAF grants and pledges, and a previous gift date if your system has one.
  3. Check the segments. The free preview counts first, repeat, lapsed, major, monthly and memorial gifts and names each gift's sentence.
  4. Fix what it flags. Missing DAF sponsors, memorial gifts with no one to notify, values on goods, vehicles.
  5. Make and send. Email the letters with an address, print the rest, and send memorial notices.
  6. Then the annual statements. In January, one statement per donor for the year.

Questions

Must year-end letters go out by January 31?

January 31 is practice, not law: "Charities typically send written acknowledgments to donors no later than January 31 of the year following the donation." The rule is that a donor must receive the acknowledgment on or before the earlier of: the date on which the donor files the donor's individual federal income tax return for the year of the contribution; or the due date (including extensions) of such return.

How are segments worked out?

From the file: a donor's earliest gift in the list is a first gift (or the gift dated on a First gift date column); a previous gift more than 12 months earlier makes a gift lapsed; $1,000 or more is major; "monthly" or "recurring" in the type, method or fund is monthly.
