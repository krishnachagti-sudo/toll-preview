# Is this QR code safe to open?

Read the link before you open it. The FTC's alert of December 6, 2023 says to inspect the URL of any code in an unexpected place, and not to scan codes in emails or texts you were not expecting, especially ones that urge you to act now.

## What the FTC advises

- Inspect the URL before you open it, and look for misspellings or a switched letter in addresses that look familiar.
- Do not scan a code in an email or text you were not expecting. If it seems real, contact the company through a number or website you know.
- Keep your phone's operating system updated, and protect accounts with strong passwords and multi-factor authentication.

## Why short links make it harder

A dynamic code holds a vendor's short link, such as one on qrco.de, so the address you see does not tell you where you will land. The checker on the tool page follows the redirects and shows each hop before you visit.

## If you print codes for others

- Use a static code that holds your own domain, so customers can see it is yours.
- Check printed codes now and then for stickers placed over them.

## Questions

### How do I check a QR code before opening it?

Look at the address your camera shows before you tap it. The FTC says to inspect the URL of a code in an unexpected place and to look for misspellings or a switched letter.

### Can a QR code on a parking meter be fake?

Yes. The FTC describes scammers covering QR codes on parking meters with their own codes. Pay through the official app or website you know.
