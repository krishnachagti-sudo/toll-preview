# The federal WARN threshold test

Federal WARN covers employers with 100 or more full-time employees and is triggered at a site by a plant closing with 50 or more full-time losses in 30 days, or a mass layoff of 50 or more who are at least 33% of the site (66 at a 200-person site, 330 at 1,000) or 500 or more anywhere; rounds within 90 days add up.

- 200-person site: 66
- 1,000-person site: 330

## Steps

1. **Covered employer.** 100 or more employees not counting part-time staff, or 100 or more working 4,000 hours a week in all, overtime excluded; nonprofits included, regular governments not (20 CFR 639.3(a)).
2. **Single site.** Count each single site of employment on its own; remote and traveling staff at their home base (639.3(i)).
3. **Plant closing.** A shutdown of a site or a unit in it with employment losses for 50 or more full-time employees in any 30 days (639.3(b)).
4. **Mass layoff.** Otherwise, 50 or more full-time losses in 30 days that are at least 33% of the site, or 500 or more (639.3(c)).
5. **90 days.** Rounds each too small add up over any 90 days unless they have separate and distinct causes (639.5(a)(1)(ii)).

## Questions

### How many employees trigger the WARN Act?

An employer of 100 or more full-time employees; then 50 or more full-time losses at a site for a plant closing, or 50 or more who are at least 33% of the site, or 500 or more, for a mass layoff, in any 30 days (20 CFR 639.3).

### Is the 33% counted with part-time employees?

No. Divide the full-time workers laid off by the full-time workers at the site; part-time workers are left out of both (DOL Employer's Guide).
