WARN guide

Adding up layoffs over 90 days

Yes, earlier rounds can count: losses at one site within any 90 days add up when each round is too small, so the DOL's example of 30, 29, 6 and 5 terminations from a site of 180 is 70 losses (38.8%), a mass layoff, and all 70 were owed 60 days' notice, the first by November 3, 2026, unless the employer shows separate and distinct causes.

Questions

We laid off 30 last month; does that count toward WARN?

It can. Look 30 days ahead and behind, and 90 days ahead and behind, at losses taken and expected at the site; rounds each too small that reach the numbers within 90 days count unless they are the result of separate and distinct actions and causes (20 CFR 639.5(a)(1)).

Does splitting a layoff into smaller rounds avoid WARN?

No. Within 30 days the rounds simply add up; within 90 days they add up too unless the employer shows separate and distinct causes and no attempt to evade WARN (20 CFR 639.5(a)(1)(ii)).
