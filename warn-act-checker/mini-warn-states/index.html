Federal WARN for comparison

29 U.S.C. 2101 to 2109; 20 CFR Part 639: 100 or more employees not counting part-time staff; a plant closing of 50 or more at a site, or a mass layoff of 50 or more who are at least 33% of the site, or 500 or more, in any 30 days, with smaller rounds added up over 90 days; 60 days' notice.

States not covered

Alabama, Alaska, Arizona, Arkansas, Colorado, District of Columbia, Florida, Georgia, Hawaii, Idaho, Indiana, Kansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Mississippi, Missouri, Montana, Nebraska, Nevada, New Hampshire, New Mexico, North Carolina, North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, Oregon, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Carolina, South Dakota, Texas, Utah, Vermont, Virginia, West Virginia, Wisconsin, Wyoming. Some of these have notice laws of their own (a 2025 law-firm list names Hawaii, New Hampshire, Vermont and Wisconsin); their sites were not read in this build, so the checker reports their law as not covered.

Worked out from the counts and dates shown under 20 CFR Part 639 and the state sources listed. Whether locations are one single site, whether an exception applies and who the employer is are facts for you and your counsel. Not legal advice.

Questions

Which states have their own WARN law in this checker?

California, New York, New Jersey, Illinois, Iowa, Maine, Maryland, Delaware, Washington, Tennessee, each read on the state's own statute or labor department site on November 8, 2026.

Which states require 90 days of WARN notice?

New York (12 NYCRR 921-2.1) and New Jersey (since April 10, 2023) for layoffs and closings; Maine for a closing or relocation (26 M.R.S. 625-B(6)).

Does Washington have a mini-WARN act?

Yes: RCW 49.45, in force since July 27, 2025, covers employers with 50 or more employees in Washington and needs 60 days' notice of a business closing or a mass layoff of 50 or more.
