# Nonprofits, private companies and public employers under WARN

Nonprofits are covered like any business: a nonprofit with 100 or more full-time employees closing a 100-person clinic with 50 losses owes 60 days' notice (due by November 16, 2026), while regular federal, state, local and tribal governments are not covered; public bodies that run a business separately from the regular government are (20 CFR 639.3(a)(1)).

## The rule

An employer is a business enterprise with 100 or more employees not counting part-time staff, or 100 or more working 4,000 hours a week in all; it includes non-profit organizations, and public or quasi-public entities that take part in a commercial enterprise, supply a service or good on a mercantile basis, or independently manage public assets, and are separately organized from the regular government (639.3(a)(1)).

## States

- New York excludes federal and state government, local governments, school districts, public authorities and tribal governments; private businesses contracting with them are covered (12 NYCRR 921-1.1(e)(6)).
- Delaware excludes governments, school districts and charter schools (19 Del. C. 1903(a)(4)).
- Washington excludes the state, its political subdivisions and tribes (RCW 49.45.010(8)).
- Illinois does not apply to federal, state or local governments (IDOL).

## Questions

### Does WARN apply to nonprofits?

Yes. The term employer includes non-profit organizations of the requisite size (20 CFR 639.3(a)(1)).

### Does WARN apply to public employers?

Regular federal, state, local and federally recognized Indian tribal governments are not covered; public and quasi-public entities that engage in business and are separately organized from the regular government are (20 CFR 639.3(a)(1)).
