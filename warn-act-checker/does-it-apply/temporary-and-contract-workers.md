# Temporary workers and contractors under WARN

Workers hired for a project they knew was temporary are exempt from notice but still counted: closing a temporary project with 10 permanent and 40 temporary workers is a plant closing (50 at the site), and only the 10 permanent workers are entitled to notice (20 CFR 639.3(c)(2), 639.5(c)); contractors paid by another employer are not affected employees at all (639.3(e)).

- Counted: 50
- Notice: 10

## The rules

- Temporary project or facility: no notice if the employees were hired with the understanding that their work was limited to it (639.5(c)(1)); the employer carries the burden of proof (639.5(c)(2)). Recurring seasonal work in agriculture and construction can qualify; permanent staff cannot (639.5(c)(3)).
- Those exempt workers still count toward the thresholds: 10 permanent and 40 temporary workers losing their jobs is a covered plant closing, with notice due to the 10 (639.3(c)(2)).
- Contract employees paid by another employer, and the self-employed, are not affected employees (639.3(e)).

## States

- California exempts project work under Wage Orders 11, 12 and 16 and seasonal employees hired knowing the work was seasonal (EDD).
- Delaware exempts temporary facilities and projects, including construction projects (19 Del. C. 1905(a)(3)).
- Washington exempts construction projects and union-dispatch multiemployer projects (RCW 49.45.030(1)(d)).
- Maryland and Tennessee exclude construction sites and other temporary workplaces.

## Questions

### Does WARN apply to temporary employees?

No notice is required when a temporary facility closes or a project ends and the employees were hired knowing their work was limited to it; the employer must be able to show they clearly understood that (20 CFR 639.5(c)).

### Do independent contractors count for WARN?

Consultant or contract employees with a separate employment relationship with another employer, paid by that employer, or self-employed, are not affected employees of the business they are assigned to (20 CFR 639.3(e)).
