# Remote employees and WARN

Remote employees count at the site they are assigned to as home base, that assigns their work or that they report to (20 CFR 639.3(i)(6)): a headquarters of 128 with 12 remote staff reporting to it has 140, so losing 47 including those 12 is 33.5% and not a mass layoff, while leaving them out gives 35 of 128 and no WARN event.

## The federal rule

For workers whose primary duties require travel, who are outstationed, or who work outside the employer's regular sites, the single site of employment is **the site to which they are assigned as their home base, from which their work is assigned, or to which they report** (20 CFR 639.3(i)(6)). The DOL Employer's Guide gives the same answer for salespeople who travel widely.

## State rules

- New York counts remote workers based at a site at that site (12 NYCRR 921-1.1(e)(7)(i)).
- Maryland treats the whole state as one workplace for remote workers (ESA FAQs).
- Washington counts a mass layoff across all of its sites in the state (RCW 49.45.010(10)).

## Questions

### Do remote employees count for WARN?

Yes, at the single site to which they are assigned as their home base, from which their work is assigned, or to which they report (20 CFR 639.3(i)(6)).

### How does New York count remote workers?

Individuals who work remotely but are based at an employment site are counted at that site (12 NYCRR 921-1.1(e)(7)(i)).

### How does Maryland count remote workers?

For remote workers the whole State of Maryland is one workplace; for teleworkers it is their official duty station (Maryland Department of Labor, ESA FAQs).
