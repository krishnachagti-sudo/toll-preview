# Furloughs, temporary layoffs and WARN

A furlough or temporary layoff is an employment loss under WARN only if it runs more than 6 months, or hours are cut by more than half in each month of a 6-month period (20 CFR 639.3(f)): a furlough starting January 4, 2027 becomes one if it runs past July 4, 2027, and in Washington a short layoff extended past 3 months (April 4, 2027) needs notice when the extension is foreseeable.

- Becomes a loss after: July 4, 2027

## The rules

- Employment loss includes a layoff exceeding 6 months and a reduction in hours of more than 50% during each month of any 6-month period (639.3(f)(1)).
- A layoff first announced as 6 months or less and then extended: notice when the extension becomes reasonably foreseeable, if it was caused by business circumstances not foreseeable at the start; for any other reason the layoff counts from its start (639.4(b)).
- A temporary shutdown of a site is a plant closing only if enough terminations, layoffs over 6 months or hours cuts follow (639.3(b)).

The 6-month line for furloughs starting on these dates (the same day 6 months later).

| Furlough starts | A loss if it runs past |

## States

- Washington: a short-term layoff of 3 months or less that is extended needs notice when the extension becomes foreseeable (RCW 49.45.020(1)(b)).
- New York: a temporary layoff under 6 months with a scheduled return is not subject to notice; one that runs past 6 months is (12 NYCRR 921-1.1(f)(1)(iii)(d)).

## Questions

### Does a furlough trigger WARN?

Only if it is an employment loss: a layoff exceeding 6 months, or a cut in hours of more than 50% in each month of any 6-month period (20 CFR 639.3(f)).

### What if a short layoff is extended past 6 months?

If the extension was caused by business circumstances not reasonably foreseeable at the start, notice is due when the extension becomes foreseeable; otherwise the layoff counts as an employment loss from its start (20 CFR 639.4(b)).
