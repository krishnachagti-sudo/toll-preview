# Part-time employees and WARN

Part-time employees, those averaging under 20 hours a week or employed under 6 of the last 12 months, are left out of the federal counts but still get notice (20 CFR 639.3(h), 639.6(b)): a store losing 45 full-time and 10 part-time employees has 45 federal losses, short of 50, while California counts all 55 and is triggered; the DOL's example of 223 hours in 13 weeks averages 17.2 hours, part-time.

- Federal losses: 45
- California losses: 55

## The definition

A part-time employee is one employed for an average of fewer than 20 hours a week, or employed fewer than 6 of the 12 months before notice is due, including full-time workers; seasonal workers may fall in it (20 CFR 639.3(h)).

The DOL Employer's Guide examples: total hours in 13 weeks divided by 13.

| Example | Hours in 13 weeks | Average | Class |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 1 | 223 | 17.2 | part-time |
| 2 | 265 | 20.4 | full-time |

## States that count them

- California counts full- and part-time employees who have worked 6 of the last 12 months, both for the 75-employee establishment and for the 50 laid off (EDD).
- New Jersey counts part-time employees since April 10, 2023 (Business.NJ.gov).
- Maine counts every person employed at the establishment (26 M.R.S. 625-B).

## Questions

### Who is a part-time employee under WARN?

An employee employed for an average of fewer than 20 hours a week, or employed for fewer than 6 of the 12 months before notice is required, even if full-time (20 CFR 639.3(h)).

### Over what period are the 20 hours averaged?

The shorter of the time the worker has been employed or the most recent 90 days (20 CFR 639.3(h)); the DOL guide divides 13 weeks of hours by 13.

### Do part-time employees get WARN notice?

Yes. They are not counted toward the thresholds, but they are due notice (20 CFR 639.6(b)).
