How to write the lines

Sites: Headcount on the day notice is first due. Full-time means 20 or more hours a week on average and at least 6 of the last 12 months; everyone else is part-time (add a fifth number to split out people with under 6 months). Add closing when the site or a unit in it shuts down, and moves 120 miles for a relocation.

Job losses: Count only employment losses: terminations other than for cause, quitting or retiring; layoffs over 6 months; hours cut by more than half in each month of 6 (20 CFR 639.3(f)). Each person's last day is their date.

The company

New York, Maryland and Washington count the employer's staff in the state; without a line the checker adds up the sites listed there. The hours are overtime excluded, for the federal 4,000-hour test.

The rules

What the check follows, law by law.

Every site is tested against federal WARN and the state's own law where one was read: covered employer, a closing or mass layoff in any 30 days, smaller rounds added up over 90 days, and the notice owed for each last day.

What it does not cover

States not in the table, single-site and exception questions, and who the employer is. Not legal advice.

  • The tool does not decide whether locations are one single site of employment, whether an exception applies (faltering company, unforeseeable business circumstances, natural disaster), or who the employer is. Not legal advice.

Federal WARN

100 or more full-time employees; 50 or more lost at a site in 30 days; 60 days' notice.

20 CFR Part 639

Read the full rules

An employer with 100 or more employees not counting part-time staff, or 100 or more who work 4,000 hours a week in all (20 CFR 639.3(a)). A plant closing is an employment loss for 50 or more full-time employees at a single site in any 30 days; a mass layoff is 50 or more who are at least 33% of the site, or 500 or more (639.3(b), (c)). Smaller rounds within 90 days add up unless they have separate and distinct causes (639.5(a)). 60 days' notice before each person's last day.

Who counts

Part-time staff are left out of the federal counts but still get notice.

Read the full rules

Part-time employees, those averaging under 20 hours a week or employed under 6 of the last 12 months, are left out of every federal count but still get notice (639.3(h), 639.6(b)). A remote or traveling employee belongs to the site that is their home base, assigns their work or that they report to (639.3(i)(6)). Headcounts are taken on the day the first notice is due (639.5(a)(2)).

State laws

Each with its own counts, windows and notice days, read on the state's own site.

Read the full rules

Each state law is read on the state's own statute or labor department site and applied with its own counts, windows and notice days: New York and New Jersey want 90 days, Iowa 30, Maine 90 for a closing. Washington counts a mass layoff across the whole state. A state not in the table below is not covered here.

From your employee list

Every employee's notice dates, from one CSV.

Paste or open a CSV with one row per employee: employee, site, state, hire_date, weekly_hours, separation_date, reason. Leave the separation date empty for people who stay. Reason is layoff (the default), short-layoff (6 months or less), cause, quit, retirement or transfer. The checker classes each person on the day notice is first due and builds the site counts for you. The site summary is free; the CSV of every employee's notice dates is the paid download.
