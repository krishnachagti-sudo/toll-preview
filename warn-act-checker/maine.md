# Maine WARN Act

The Maine severance pay and notice law covers 100 or more employees at a site; at a 120-person site 50 full-time losses at the site trigger it, against 50 for federal WARN, and the Director must be told by January 22, 2027 for a last day of January 15, 2027, while a closing needs 90 days' notice.

- Notice: 90 days
- Losses at 120: 50

## The rules

- A "covered establishment" employs, or employed at any time in the 12 months before, 100 or more persons (625-B(1)(A)).
- Trigger, closing: any number.
- Trigger, relocation of 100 miles or more, any number.
- Trigger, mass layoff (6 months or longer): 50 or more who are at least 33%, or 500 or more.
- A closing or relocation needs 90 days' written notice to the Director; a closing also to the employees and the municipal officers (625-B(6), (6-A)).
- A mass layoff (33% and at least 50, or 500, losing work for at least 6 months) needs notice to the Director as far ahead as practicable and no later than 7 days after the layoff (625-B(6)).
- Severance: one week's pay for each year of service, and partial pay for a partial year, to employees with at least 3 years at the establishment, paid within one regular pay period (625-B(2)).
- Fines: up to $1,000 per employee for unpaid severance; $500 a day for missing notice (625-B(9)).

## Who gets the notice

- The Director of the Bureau of Labor Standards
- For a closing: each employee, and the municipal officers of the municipality where the establishment is

## Questions

### How much notice does Maine require?

90 days under Maine 625-B (26 M.R.S. 625-B), against 60 under federal WARN.

### Which employers does Maine 625-B cover?

A "covered establishment" employs, or employed at any time in the 12 months before, 100 or more persons (625-B(1)(A)).

### Where does the Maine notice go?

The Director of the Bureau of Labor Standards; For a closing: each employee, and the municipal officers of the municipality where the establishment is.
