How much, from which day, on what conditions

How much

Some statutes set a percent (Minnesota 8% of the overdue rent), some a lesser-of (New York: $50 or 5%), some a greater-of (Colorado and North Carolina), Texas a percent by building size, Iowa a daily amount with a monthly total and Oregon 5% for each five-day period. Arizona and California set no number at all.

From which day

The waiting period is counted the way each statute writes it: "two full days" in Texas, "five days" in New York and D.C., seven calendar days in Colorado, 15 in Maine, 30 in Massachusetts, five business days in New Jersey for rent due on the first, and Tennessee's five days that count the due date itself.

On what conditions

Most statutes want the fee in the written lease. Several allow one fee per late payment, keep the fee off the subsidized part of the rent, or forbid an eviction for an unpaid late fee alone. Each check lists the ones that apply, with the words.

What it does not cover

  • Not legal advice. We are not a law firm, and this tool does not review your lease or your case.
  • Rules are as of the statute reading date shown with each state; a later amendment can change the answer. Rows read from a copy of the statute, not the official site, say so.
  • City and county ordinances (other than Chicago's) and federal subsidy program rules were not checked.
  • Where a statute sets no number, or asks for a "reasonable" fee, a court decides what is allowed; the tool does not.
  • States without a row are not covered: the tool refuses them rather than guess.
