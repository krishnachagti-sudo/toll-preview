# Rent late fee limits by state

18 of the 23 jurisdictions whose statute we read cap a rent late fee with a number and 13 make the landlord wait before charging one. On $1,500 monthly rent due December 1, 2026, the limit runs from $50 in Connecticut and New York to $450 in Oregon, whose 5% applies for each five-day period through the end of the month.

- Read: 23
- With a number: 18
- Statutes read: November 8, 2026

Monthly rent due December 1, 2026. Texas: $1,500 in a 4-unit building, $2,500 in a 12-unit building. Oregon: the five-day-period method by the end of the rental period.

## No number in the statute

Arizona, California, Massachusetts, New Jersey and Washington: the sections read set a waiting period, ask for a "reasonable" fee or leave it to the general rule on agreed damages. A court decides what is allowed there.

## Not covered

Alaska, Alabama, Arkansas, Florida, Georgia, Idaho, Illinois, Indiana, Kansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Michigan, Missouri, Mississippi, Montana, North Dakota, Nebraska, New Hampshire, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Carolina, South Dakota, Utah, Vermont, Wisconsin, West Virginia and Wyoming: no late-fee statute was read, so no rule is given and the checker refuses them. Illinois is covered only for Chicago's ordinance.

## Questions

### Which state has the lowest late fee limit?

Of the jurisdictions read, on $1,500 monthly rent: Connecticut and New York, at $50.

### Why are some states missing?

A state is listed only after its late-fee statute text has been read and quoted. The checker refuses the others rather than guess, and many states have no late-fee statute at all in what we read.

### Texas is shown at 12%: is that for every building?

No. 12% of the rent is for a building of 4 or fewer units; 10% for a larger building (Tex. Prop. Code § 92.019(a-1)). The $2,500 column is worked out for a 12-unit building.
