Daily late fees: when the cap bites

A daily late fee is lawful only while the total stays within the statute's limit. At $15 a day from the first lawful fee day on $1,500 rent due December 1, 2026 and paid December 11, the total is over the limit in Hawaii, Iowa, Maryland, Minnesota, Nevada, New York and Chicago; in Texas the 7 days come to $105, within the $180 limit for a 4-unit building.

Connecticut, Iowa and Oregon limits grow with the days late, so their limit is worked out for a December 11 payment. "Cap reached on day" counts days of the daily fee.

What the statutes say about daily fees

  • Texas: "A late fee under this section may include an initial fee and a daily fee for each day any portion of the tenant's rent continues to remain unpaid, and the combined fees are considered a single late fee for purposes of this section."
  • Colorado: "Impose a late fee more than once for each late payment, except that a landlord may impose a late fee more than once for a late payment if the total amount of such late fees does not exceed the amount described in subsection (1)(b) of this section;"
  • Iowa: rent up to $700 a month: $12 a day, $60 a month at most; rent above $700: $20 a day, $100 a month at most (Iowa Code § 562A.9(4); the statute writes the amounts in words).
  • Oregon: a daily charge may not exceed six percent of a reasonable flat amount, and runs "through that rental period only" (ORS 90.260(2)(b)).

What the lease must say

Most of these statutes allow a fee only if the written lease provides for it: Arizona, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, District of Columbia, Hawaii, Maine, Minnesota, Nevada, New Mexico, North Carolina, Oregon, Texas and Virginia. A daily fee the lease does not mention is not covered by that term.

Questions

Can a landlord charge a daily late fee?

No statute read bans one outright, but the total counts against the limit: Texas counts an initial fee plus daily fees as one fee, Colorado allows more than one fee only while the total stays under its cap, Iowa sets a daily and a monthly limit, and Connecticut allows $5 a day up to $50.

Is $10 a day legal in Texas?

Only while the total stays within 12% of the rent (10% in a building of more than 4 units), unless the higher total is within the landlord's uncertain damages. On $1,500 rent in a 4-unit building, $10 a day passes the $180 limit after 18 days.
