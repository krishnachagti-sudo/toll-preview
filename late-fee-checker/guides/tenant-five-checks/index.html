Is my late fee legal? Five checks for tenants

Check five things before you pay a late fee: the state limit, the first fee day, the lease term, how partial or subsidized rent is counted, and get it in writing. Example: Colorado, $1,500 rent due December 1, 2026, a $100 fee charged December 5. For $1,500 monthly rent in Colorado, the most a landlord may charge as a late fee is $75, and only from Tuesday, December 8, 2026 for rent due December 1, 2026 (Colo. Rev. Stat. § 38-12-105). Rent paid on December 5, 2026 is not late enough for a fee.

Partial and subsidized rent

Where a statute works the fee out on the unpaid rent (Colorado, Connecticut, Hawaii, Maryland, Minnesota, Tennessee and Virginia), a partial payment lowers the limit. Colorado, Connecticut, District of Columbia, Minnesota and North Carolina keep the fee off the part of the rent a subsidy pays.

Please read

  • Not legal advice. We are not a law firm, and this tool does not review your lease or your case.
  • Rules are as of the statute reading date shown with each state; a later amendment can change the answer. Rows read from a copy of the statute, not the official site, say so.
  • City and county ordinances (other than Chicago's) and federal subsidy program rules were not checked.
  • Where a statute sets no number, or asks for a "reasonable" fee, a court decides what is allowed; the tool does not.
  • States without a row are not covered: the tool refuses them rather than guess.

Steps

  1. Find your state rule. Look up the limit and the first fee day for your state; if it is not covered here, its statute has not been read.
  2. Compare the lease and the charge. Check that the lease has the late-fee term and that the amount charged matches it and the statute.
  3. Check the dates. Compare the day the fee was charged with the first day the statute allows.
  4. Check partial payments and subsidies. Some statutes work the fee out on the unpaid part, or only on your share of subsidized rent.
  5. Ask in writing and keep copies. Ask for an itemized statement, dispute a fee you think is unlawful in writing, and keep copies.
