Late fees and eviction

Can a landlord evict for late fees?

Colorado, District of Columbia and Oregon say that an unpaid late fee alone does not justify ending a tenancy for nonpayment. On $1,500 Oregon rent, a notice demanding rent only asks for $1,500; adding the late fee would ask for up to $1,950.

What the statutes say

  • Colorado: a landlord may not "remove or exclude a tenant from a dwelling or initiate a court process for the removal or exclusion of a tenant from a dwelling because the tenant fails to pay one or more late fees to the landlord" (§ 38-12-105(1)(d)).
  • District of Columbia: a housing provider shall not "Evict a tenant on the basis of the nonpayment of a late fee" (§ 42-3505.31(c)(4)).
  • Oregon: "Nonpayment of a late charge alone is not grounds for termination of a rental agreement for nonpayment of rent under ORS 90.394, but is grounds for termination of a rental agreement for cause under ORS 90.392 or 90.630 (1)."
  • Delaware: "A late charge is considered as additional rent for the purposes of this Code." (25 Del. C. § 5501(d)).

Delaware treats a late charge as additional rent, so its rule differs; the other covered statutes do not say.

Questions

Can I evict a tenant for not paying a late fee?

Not in Colorado, District of Columbia and Oregon: their statutes forbid it or say it is not grounds for a nonpayment termination. Elsewhere the statutes read do not say; check your state's eviction law.

Should the pay-or-quit notice include the late fee?

Oregon lets the landlord note the late charge on a nonpayment notice only if it is clear the tenant can cure by paying the rent alone. Other states' notice rules were not read here.
