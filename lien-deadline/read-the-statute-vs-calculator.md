# Read the statute or use a calculator?

The statute is the law and the calculator is only arithmetic on it: Florida's 713.08(5) says "not later than 90 days after the final furnishing", which for a last delivery on January 29, 2027 is April 29, 2027. Read the statute when the facts are unusual or the amount is large; use the calculator to count the days and keep every date in one place.

## Where hand counting goes wrong

- Month-based rules: Texas counts to the 15th day of the third or fourth month after a month of work, not a number of days (Tex. Prop. Code 53.052, 53.056).
- End-of-month rules: Virginia counts 90 days from the last day of the month of your last work, capped at 90 days after completion (Va. Code 43-4).
- Lookbacks: Arizona, Montana and Washington let a late notice cover only recent work, so the date that matters is the one the notice still reaches back to.
- Weekends: only some statutes move a deadline off a weekend (Texas 53.003(e)); elsewhere the date stays, and the business day before is the safe day.
- Which clock: the lien runs from last furnishing in Florida and Washington, from completion in Arizona and DC, and the suit from the recording date in most states.

## When reading the statute (and a lawyer) is better

- When a lawyer is drafting the lien or the suit.
- When the facts do not fit the inputs: a terminated contract, disputed completion, a bond, a public project.
- When the amount at stake justifies paying for advice. This calculator is not legal advice.
