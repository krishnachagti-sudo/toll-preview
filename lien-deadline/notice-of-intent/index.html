Notice of intent to lien

A notice of intent to lien warns the owner that a lien is coming; whether one is required, and when, depends on the state. Of the states we have read, Maryland requires a subcontractor's notice of intention to claim a lien within 120 days (for work last done on January 29, 2027, by May 29, 2027), and in the District of Columbia the "notice of intent" is the lien itself, recorded within 90 days after completion (by May 27, 2027 for a project complete on February 26, 2027) (Md. Code, Real Prop. 9-104(a); D.C. Code 40-301.02(a)(1)).

The other states we have read set a preliminary notice early in the job and a lien filing at the end, but no separate pre-lien demand. A demand letter can still be sent anywhere; it does not stop any clock shown here.

  • The total amount earned under the subcontractor’s undertaking to the date hereof is $ ________ of which $ ________ is due and unpaid as of the date hereof. The work done or materials provided under the subcontract were as follows: (insert brief description of the work done and materials furnished, the time when the work was done or the materials furnished, and the name of the person for whom the work was done or to whom the materials were furnished). ________

Not covered

Wisconsin and Colorado notices of intent are often asked about. Their statute sites did not answer or refused our requests on November 8, 2026, so they are not covered here rather than copied from another chart.

Not legal advice and not a law firm. Dates are worked out from each state's own statute text, quoted with the date we read it. Deadlines are strict: a lien recorded one day late is usually lost. Act before the date shown, and earlier when it falls on a weekend or holiday. For disputes, large amounts or anything unusual about your job, ask a construction attorney licensed in the project's state.
