# Billd vs this calculator

Billd's calculator is free with no signup and gives preliminary notice, notice of intent and lien filing dates, labelled "estimates for general guidance only"; this one is free too, adds the suit-to-enforce date (for an Arizona subcontractor on the worked job: suit by December 26, 2027 if the lien is recorded on its last day) and quotes the statute behind each date.

## Side by side

Billd facts from its own site, read November 8, 2026

| What | Billd | This calculator |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Price | Free, no signup | Free on the page; $0.02 a lookup over the API or MCP |
| Inputs | Role (general contractor, subcontractor, supplier), state, first and last furnishing, completion | Six roles, three project types, furnishing, completion, notice of completion, recording and notice-sent dates |
| Dates | Preliminary notice, notice of intent, lien filing | Notice, lien, suit to enforce, copies and Texas months, with weekends handled |
| How it describes them | "estimates for general guidance only and are not legal advice" | Quoted statute and read date on every line; not legal advice |
| States | All states and Washington DC in its list | 10 jurisdictions read on their own sites, plus the Miller Act |

## When Billd is the better choice

- When your state is not covered here yet.
- When you are a supplier also looking at material financing, which Billd offers.

> Not legal advice and not a law firm. Dates are worked out from each state's own statute text, quoted with the date we read it. Deadlines are strict: a lien recorded one day late is usually lost. Act before the date shown, and earlier when it falls on a weekend or holiday. For disputes, large amounts or anything unusual about your job, ask a construction attorney licensed in the project's state.
