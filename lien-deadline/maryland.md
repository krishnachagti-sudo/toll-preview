# Maryland lien deadlines

In Maryland, a subcontractor records the lien within 180 days after the work has been finished or the materials furnished (Md. Code, Real Prop. 9-105(a)); the notice comes first, within 120 days after doing the work or furnishing the materials (Md. Code, Real Prop. 9-104(a)).

Code, Real Prop. 9-104(a); Md. Code, Real Prop. 9-105(a); Md. Code, Real Prop. 9-109).

## The rules

Maryland lien law, read November 8, 2026

| Step | Who | When | Law |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Give the owner notice of intention to claim a lien | subcontractor, sub-subcontractor, supplier to the prime contractor, supplier to a subcontractor, laborer paid wages | Within 120 days after doing the work or furnishing the materials. | Md. Code, Real Prop. 9-104(a) |
| File the petition to establish the mechanic's lien in circuit court | everyone with a lien | Within 180 days after the work has been finished or the materials furnished. | Md. Code, Real Prop. 9-105(a) |
| Petition to enforce the lien | everyone with a lien | Within one year from the day the petition to establish the lien was first filed. | Md. Code, Real Prop. 9-109 |

Weekends and holidays: the Maryland text we read does not move a deadline that falls on a weekend or holiday, so the calculator keeps the statute's date and flags it with the business day before.

- Maryland counts the 120 days from "doing the work or furnishing the materials". We count from your last day; work done more than 120 days before the notice may fall outside it, so the safest notice day is 120 days after your first day.
- On a single family dwelling being built on the owner's land for the owner's own residence, the notice must also reach the owner before the owner has paid the contractor in full, and the lien is limited to what the owner still owes (9-104(a)(2), (f)(3)).

## Maryland in its own words

**Read the statute's words (4 quotations)**

- "A subcontractor doing work or furnishing materials or both for or about a building other than a single family dwelling being erected on the owner’s land for his own residence is not entitled to a lien under this subtitle unless, within 120 days after doing the work or furnishing the materials, the subcontractor gives written notice of an intention to claim a lien substantially in the form specified in subsection (b) of this section." [Md. Code, Real Property 9-104, Notice by subcontractor](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=grp&section=9-104&enactments=false)
- "“Subcontractor” means a person who has a contract with anyone except the owner or his agent." [Md. Code, Real Property 9-101, Definitions](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=grp&section=9-101&enactments=false)
- "In order to establish a lien under this subtitle, a person entitled to a lien shall file proceedings in the circuit court for the county where the land or any part of the land is located within 180 days after the work has been finished or the materials furnished." [Md. Code, Real Property 9-105, Petition to establish lien](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=grp&section=9-105&enactments=false)
- "The right to enforce any lien established under this subtitle expires at the end of one year from the day on which the petition to establish the lien was first filed." [Md. Code, Real Property 9-109, Enforcement](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Laws/StatuteText?article=grp&section=9-109&enactments=false)

## Questions

### When is the notice of intention to claim a lien due in Maryland?

Within 120 days after doing the work or furnishing the materials (Md. Code, Real Prop. 9-104(a)).

### How long do I have to file a mechanics lien in Maryland?

Within 180 days after the work has been finished or the materials furnished (Md. Code, Real Prop. 9-105(a)).

### How long do I have to sue to enforce a Maryland lien?

Within one year from the day the petition to establish the lien was first filed (Md. Code, Real Prop. 9-109).
