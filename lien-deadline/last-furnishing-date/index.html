Which date starts the clock?

The lien clock starts on your last day of work or delivery in Florida, Washington, Minnesota, Maryland, Kansas and Montana, and on the project's completion in Arizona and the District of Columbia; Virginia uses whichever comes first. A Florida subcontractor whose last real work was January 29, 2027 must record by April 29, 2027; counting from a later return visit on March 30, 2027 would give June 28, 2027, a date to rely on only if that visit counts as furnishing under the statute.

Keeping the date provable

  • Keep the delivery ticket or daily log for the last day you put labor or materials into the job.
  • Note whether a later visit was new work under the contract or a warranty or punch-list call; the statutes above name the work or materials furnished, and this calculator does not judge which visit counts.
  • Enter the notice of completion date where Arizona or Montana asks for one: it can end the period sooner.

Not legal advice and not a law firm. Dates are worked out from each state's own statute text, quoted with the date we read it. Deadlines are strict: a lien recorded one day late is usually lost. Act before the date shown, and earlier when it falls on a weekend or holiday. For disputes, large amounts or anything unusual about your job, ask a construction attorney licensed in the project's state.
