# Notice of intent to lien

A notice of intent to lien warns the owner that a lien is coming; whether one is required, and when, depends on the state. Of the states we have read, Maryland requires a subcontractor's notice of intention to claim a lien within 120 days, and in the District of Columbia the "notice of intent" is the lien itself, recorded within 90 days after completion (Md. Code, Real Prop. 9-104(a); D.C. Code 40-301.02(a)(1)).

- [Worked example](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/lien-deadline/?state=MD&role=sub&project=commercial&first=2026-11-02&last=2027-01-29&completion=2027-02-26): For work last done on January 29, 2027, a Maryland subcontractor's notice of intention is due by May 29, 2027; in the District of Columbia, for a project complete on February 26, 2027, the notice of intent is due by May 27, 2027.

## States we have read

Notices of intent in the statutes read November 8, 2026

| State | Notice | When | Law |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Maryland](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/lien-deadline/maryland/) | Notice to owner of intention to claim a lien (subcontractors: anyone without a contract with the owner) | within 120 days after doing the work or furnishing the materials | Md. Code, Real Prop. 9-104(a) |
| [District of Columbia](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/lien-deadline/district-of-columbia/) | Notice of intent, recorded in the land records (it is the lien filing) | during construction or within 90 days after the earlier of completion or termination | D.C. Code 40-301.02(a)(1) |
| [Kansas](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/lien-deadline/kansas/) | Notice of intent to perform, on new residential property sold to a good faith purchaser | filed before the deed is recorded | K.S.A. 60-1103b(b), not modeled |

The other states we have read set a preliminary notice early in the job and a lien filing at the end, but no separate pre-lien demand. A demand letter can still be sent anywhere; it does not stop any clock shown here.

## Maryland's form

- Notice to Owner or Owner’s Agent of Intention to Claim a Lien
- \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ (Subcontractor)
- did work or furnished material for or about the building generally designated or briefly described as \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_
- The total amount earned under the subcontractor’s undertaking to the date hereof is $ \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ of which $ \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ is due and unpaid as of the date hereof. The work done or materials provided under the subcontract were as follows: (insert brief description of the work done and materials furnished, the time when the work was done or the materials furnished, and the name of the person for whom the work was done or to whom the materials were furnished). \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_
- I do solemnly declare and affirm under the penalties of perjury that the contents of the foregoing notice are true to the best of the affiant’s knowledge, information, and belief.
- \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ (Individual) \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_
- on behalf of \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ (Subcontractor)

## Not covered

Wisconsin and Colorado notices of intent are often asked about. Their statute sites did not answer or refused our requests on November 8, 2026, so they are not covered here rather than copied from another chart.

> Not legal advice and not a law firm. Dates are worked out from each state's own statute text, quoted with the date we read it. Deadlines are strict: a lien recorded one day late is usually lost. Act before the date shown, and earlier when it falls on a weekend or holiday. For disputes, large amounts or anything unusual about your job, ask a construction attorney licensed in the project's state.
