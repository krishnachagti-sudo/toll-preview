Washington lien deadlines

For a job you first furnish on Monday, November 2, 2026, last furnish on Friday, January 29, 2027, with the project complete on Friday, February 26, 2027: as a sub-subcontractor on a commercial project in Washington, your notice to owner covers your first day if given by December 31, 2026, the lien must be recorded by April 29, 2027 and the suit to enforce it must be filed by December 29, 2027 (if recorded on the last day, April 29, 2027) (RCW 60.04.031(1); RCW 60.04.091; RCW 60.04.141).

Weekends and holidays: the Washington text we read does not move a deadline that falls on a weekend or holiday, so the calculator keeps the statute's date and flags it with the business day before.

  • No notice is required from anyone who contracts directly with the owner, from laborers claiming only for labor, or from subcontractors who contract directly with the prime contractor (except on an existing owner-occupied home) (60.04.031(2)).
  • If credit is given and its terms are stated in the claim of lien, the 8 months run from the end of the credit (60.04.141).

Washington in its own words

  • "The notice may be given at any time but only protects the right to claim a lien for professional services, materials, or equipment supplied after the date which is sixty days before:" RCW 60.04.031, Notices
  • "In the case of new construction of a single-family residence, the notice of a right to claim a lien may be given at any time but only protects the right to claim a lien for professional services, materials, or equipment supplied after a date which is ten days before the notice is given as described in this subsection." RCW 60.04.031, Notices
  • "Subcontractors who contract for the improvement of real property directly with the prime contractor, except as provided in subsection (3)(b) of this section." RCW 60.04.031, Notices
  • "Who do not contract directly with the owner-occupier or their common law agent shall give notice of the right to claim a lien to the owner-occupier." RCW 60.04.031, Notices
  • "Every person claiming a lien under RCW 60.04.021 shall file for recording, in the county where the subject property is located, a notice of claim of lien not later than ninety days after the person has ceased to furnish labor, professional services, materials, or equipment or the last date on which employee benefit contributions were due." RCW 60.04.091, Recording, time, contents of lien
  • "The lien claimant shall give a copy of the claim of lien to the owner or reputed owner by mailing it by certified or registered mail or by personal service within fourteen days of the time the claim of lien is filed for recording." RCW 60.04.091, Recording, time, contents of lien
  • "No lien created by this chapter binds the property subject to the lien for a longer period than eight calendar months after the claim of lien has been recorded unless an action is filed by the lien claimant within that time in the superior court in the county where the subject property is located to enforce the lien, and service is made upon the owner of the subject property within ninety days of the date of filing the action" RCW 60.04.141, Lien duration

Not legal advice and not a law firm. Dates are worked out from each state's own statute text, quoted with the date we read it. Deadlines are strict: a lien recorded one day late is usually lost. Act before the date shown, and earlier when it falls on a weekend or holiday. For disputes, large amounts or anything unusual about your job, ask a construction attorney licensed in the project's state.

Questions

When is the notice to owner due in Washington?

Any time, but it protects only what you supply after the date 60 days before you mail or deliver it (10 days for new construction of a single-family residence); the date shown is the last day it still covers your first day (RCW 60.04.031(1)).

How long do I have to file a mechanics lien in Washington?

Not later than 90 days after you stopped furnishing labor, services, materials or equipment (RCW 60.04.091).

How long do I have to sue to enforce a Washington lien?

Within eight calendar months after the claim of lien is recorded, and serve the owner within 90 days of filing the action (RCW 60.04.141).
