What is a preliminary notice?

A preliminary notice tells the owner (and often the prime contractor and lender) that you are furnishing labor or materials to the job and may claim a lien if unpaid; it is not a lien. In Arizona it is due 20 days after you first furnish (for a first day of November 2, 2026, by November 22, 2026), in Florida 45 days, and in Texas a notice is due for each month of unpaid work (A.R.S. 33-992.01(C); Fla. Stat. 713.06(2)(a); Tex. Prop. Code 53.056(a-1)).

Who sends it

  • Subcontractors, sub-subcontractors and suppliers, who have no contract with the owner, are the ones the notice is for.
  • Laborers paid wages are exempt in Arizona, Florida, Washington and Montana.
  • Original contractors are exempt except in Arizona; Minnesota's contractor puts its own notice in the contract (514.011, subd. 1).

Not legal advice and not a law firm. Dates are worked out from each state's own statute text, quoted with the date we read it. Deadlines are strict: a lien recorded one day late is usually lost. Act before the date shown, and earlier when it falls on a weekend or holiday. For disputes, large amounts or anything unusual about your job, ask a construction attorney licensed in the project's state.

Questions

Is a preliminary notice a lien?

No. Arizona's form says so in its first line ("this is not a lien"), and Washington's says "THIS IS NOT A LIEN" (A.R.S. 33-992.01(D); RCW 60.04.031(4)). It preserves the right to claim one later.

Do general contractors send a preliminary notice?

Usually not: Florida, Washington, Minnesota and Montana exempt those in a direct contract with the owner. Arizona is the exception among the states read here: every claimant except wage laborers serves the 20-day notice, the original contractor included (A.R.S. 33-992.01(B)).
