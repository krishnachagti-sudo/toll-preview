# Florida notice to owner

Your Florida notice to owner is due before you start or not later than 45 days after you start to furnish labor, services or materials, and in any event before the owner's final payment after the contractor's final affidavit (Fla. Stat. 713.06(2)(a)). The form below is the statute's; the PDF fills it in with your job and adds a proof-of-service log.

## Who sends it, to whom, and how

- Who: Subcontractors and the others the statute names: subcontractor, sub-subcontractor, supplier to the prime contractor, supplier to a subcontractor.
- To: the owner; a sub-subcontractor or a supplier to a subcontractor or sub-subcontractor also serves a copy on the contractor.
- Late: Florida is strict: failing to serve the notice, or serving it late, is a complete defense to enforcing the lien (713.06(2)(a)).

## The form, in the statute's words

- TO AVOID A LIEN AND PAYING TWICE, YOU MUST OBTAIN A WRITTEN RELEASE FROM US EVERY TIME YOU PAY YOUR CONTRACTOR.
- NOTICE TO OWNER
- To (Owner’s name and address) \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_
- The undersigned hereby informs you that he or she has furnished or is furnishing services or materials as follows:
- (General description of services or materials) \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ for the improvement of the real property identified as (property description) \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ under an order given by \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_.
- Florida law prescribes the serving of this notice and restricts your right to make payments under your contract in accordance with Section 713.06, Florida Statutes.
- IMPORTANT INFORMATION FOR YOUR PROTECTION
- Under Florida’s laws, those who work on your property or provide materials and are not paid have a right to enforce their claim for payment against your property. This claim is known as a construction lien.

## The statute's words on timing

**Read the statute's words (3 quotations)**

- "All lienors under this section, except laborers, as a prerequisite to perfecting a lien under this chapter and recording a claim of lien, must serve a notice on the owner setting forth the lienor’s name and address, a description sufficient for identification of the real property, and the nature of the services or materials furnished or to be furnished." [Fla. Stat. 713.06, Liens of persons not in privity](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0713/Sections/0713.06.html)
- "The notice must be served before commencing, or not later than 45 days after commencing, to furnish his or her labor, services, or materials, but, in any event, before the date of the owner’s disbursement of the final payment after the contractor has furnished the affidavit under subparagraph (3)(d)1." [Fla. Stat. 713.06, Liens of persons not in privity](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0713/Sections/0713.06.html)
- "the failure to serve the notice, or to timely serve it, is a complete defense to enforcement of a lien by any person." [Fla. Stat. 713.06, Liens of persons not in privity](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0713/Sections/0713.06.html)

## Steps

1. **Find your date.** Before you start or not later than 45 days after you start to furnish labor, services or materials, and in any event before the owner's final payment after the contractor's final affidavit (Fla. Stat. 713.06(2)(a)).
2. **Fill in the form.** Use the statute's words (Fla. Stat. 713.06(2)(c) asks for may be in substantially the following form and must include the information and the warning contained in the following form): the parties, the property and what you furnish.
3. **Serve it.** On the owner; a sub-subcontractor or a supplier to a subcontractor or sub-subcontractor also serves a copy on the contractor.
4. **Keep proof.** Keep the mailing receipt or signed acknowledgment with a copy of the notice; you will need the date if you record a lien.
