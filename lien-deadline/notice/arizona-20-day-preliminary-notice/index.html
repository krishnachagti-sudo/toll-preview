Arizona 20-day preliminary notice

Your Arizona preliminary twenty day notice is due not later than 20 days after you first furnish labor, services, materials or equipment to the jobsite (A.R.S. 33-992.01(B), (C)). The form below is the statute's; the PDF fills it in with your job and adds a proof-of-service log.

Who sends it, to whom, and how

  • Who: Subcontractors and the others the statute names: original (prime) contractor, subcontractor, sub-subcontractor, supplier to the prime contractor, supplier to a subcontractor.
  • To: the owner, the original contractor, the construction lender (if any) and the person you contracted with.
  • How: first class mail with a certificate of mailing, or registered or certified mail; service is complete when mailed (33-992.01(F)).
  • Late: A late notice still counts, but only for what you furnished within the 20 days before you served it and after (33-992.01(E)).

The statute's words on timing

Read the statute's words (3 quotations)
  • "Except for a person performing actual labor for wages, every person who furnishes labor, professional services, materials, machinery, fixtures or tools for which a lien otherwise may be claimed under this article shall, as a necessary prerequisite to the validity of any claim of lien, serve the owner or reputed owner, the original contractor or reputed contractor, the construction lender, if any, or reputed construction lender, if any, and the person with whom the claimant has contracted for the purchase of those items with a written preliminary twenty day notice as prescribed by this section." A.R.S. 33-992.01, Preliminary twenty day notice
  • "shall be given not later than twenty days after the claimant has first furnished labor, professional services, materials, machinery, fixtures or tools to the jobsite" A.R.S. 33-992.01, Preliminary twenty day notice
  • "The person, however, is entitled to claim a lien only for such labor, professional services, materials, machinery, fixtures or tools furnished within twenty days before the service of the notice and at any time thereafter." A.R.S. 33-992.01, Preliminary twenty day notice

Not legal advice and not a law firm. Dates are worked out from each state's own statute text, quoted with the date we read it. Deadlines are strict: a lien recorded one day late is usually lost. Act before the date shown, and earlier when it falls on a weekend or holiday. For disputes, large amounts or anything unusual about your job, ask a construction attorney licensed in the project's state.

Steps

  1. Find your date. Not later than 20 days after you first furnish labor, services, materials or equipment to the jobsite (A.R.S. 33-992.01(B), (C)).
  2. Fill in the form. Use the statute's words (A.R.S. 33-992.01(D) asks for substantially the following form): the parties, the property and what you furnish.
  3. Serve it. On the owner, the original contractor, the construction lender (if any) and the person you contracted with, by first class mail with a certificate of mailing, or registered or certified mail; service is complete when mailed (33-992.01(F)).
  4. Keep proof. Keep the mailing receipt or signed acknowledgment with a copy of the notice; you will need the date if you record a lien.
