# Florida lien deadlines

In Florida, a subcontractor records the lien not later than 90 days after your final furnishing of labor, services or materials (Fla. Stat. 713.08(5)).

- [Worked example](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/lien-deadline/?state=FL&role=sub&project=commercial&first=2026-11-02&last=2027-01-29&completion=2027-02-26): For a job you first furnish on Monday, November 2, 2026, last furnish on Friday, January 29, 2027, with the project complete on Friday, February 26, 2027: as a subcontractor on a commercial project in Florida, your notice to owner is due December 17, 2026, the lien must be recorded by April 29, 2027 and the suit to enforce it must be filed by April 29, 2028 (if recorded on the last day, April 29, 2027) (Fla. Stat. 713.06(2)(a); Fla. Stat. 713.08(5); Fla. Stat. 713.22(1), (2)).

## The rules

Florida lien law, read November 8, 2026

| Step | Who | When | Law |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Serve the notice to owner | subcontractor, sub-subcontractor, supplier to the prime contractor, supplier to a subcontractor | Before you start or not later than 45 days after you start to furnish labor, services or materials, and in any event before the owner's final payment after the contractor's final affidavit. | Fla. Stat. 713.06(2)(a) |
| Record the claim of lien | everyone with a lien | Not later than 90 days after your final furnishing of labor, services or materials. | Fla. Stat. 713.08(5) |
| Give the owner the contractor's final payment affidavit | original (prime) contractor | At least 5 days before you sue to enforce the lien. | Fla. Stat. 713.06(3)(d)1. |
| Sue to enforce the lien | everyone with a lien | Within 1 year after the claim of lien is recorded (60 days after service, if the owner records a notice of contest). | Fla. Stat. 713.22(1), (2) |

Weekends and holidays: the Florida text we read does not move a deadline that falls on a weekend or holiday, so the calculator keeps the statute's date and flags it with the business day before.

- Laborers serve no notice to owner; contractors in privity with the owner (the prime) serve none either: 713.06 covers lienors not in privity (713.06(1), (2)(a)).
- If the original contract is terminated, a claim of lien for work before the termination is due within 90 days after the termination or 90 days after final furnishing, whichever comes first (713.08(5)). Enter the termination date as your last furnishing date if it is earlier.

## Florida in its own words

**Read the statute's words (7 quotations)**

- "All lienors under this section, except laborers, as a prerequisite to perfecting a lien under this chapter and recording a claim of lien, must serve a notice on the owner setting forth the lienor’s name and address, a description sufficient for identification of the real property, and the nature of the services or materials furnished or to be furnished." [Fla. Stat. 713.06, Liens of persons not in privity](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0713/Sections/0713.06.html)
- "The notice must be served before commencing, or not later than 45 days after commencing, to furnish his or her labor, services, or materials, but, in any event, before the date of the owner’s disbursement of the final payment after the contractor has furnished the affidavit under subparagraph (3)(d)1." [Fla. Stat. 713.06, Liens of persons not in privity](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0713/Sections/0713.06.html)
- "the failure to serve the notice, or to timely serve it, is a complete defense to enforcement of a lien by any person." [Fla. Stat. 713.06, Liens of persons not in privity](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0713/Sections/0713.06.html)
- "The claim of lien may be recorded at any time during the progress of the work or thereafter but not later than 90 days after the final furnishing of the labor or services or materials by the lienor." [Fla. Stat. 713.08, Claim of lien](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0713/Sections/0713.08.html)
- "The contractor shall execute the affidavit and deliver it to the owner at least 5 days before instituting an action as a prerequisite to the institution of any action to enforce his or her lien under this chapter" [Fla. Stat. 713.06, Liens of persons not in privity](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0713/Sections/0713.06.html)
- "A lien provided by this part does not continue for a longer period than 1 year after the claim of lien has been recorded or 1 year after the recording of an amended claim of lien that shows a later date of final furnishing of labor, services, or materials, unless within that time an action to enforce the lien is commenced in a court of competent jurisdiction." [Fla. Stat. 713.22, Duration of lien](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0713/Sections/0713.22.html)
- "The lien of any lienor upon whom such notice is served and who fails to institute a suit to enforce his or her lien within 60 days after service of such notice is extinguished automatically." [Fla. Stat. 713.22, Duration of lien](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0700-0799/0713/Sections/0713.22.html)

## Questions

### When is the notice to owner due in Florida?

Before you start or not later than 45 days after you start to furnish labor, services or materials, and in any event before the owner's final payment after the contractor's final affidavit (Fla. Stat. 713.06(2)(a)).

### How long do I have to file a mechanics lien in Florida?

Not later than 90 days after your final furnishing of labor, services or materials (Fla. Stat. 713.08(5)).

### How long do I have to sue to enforce a Florida lien?

Within 1 year after the claim of lien is recorded (60 days after service, if the owner records a notice of contest) (Fla. Stat. 713.22(1), (2)).
