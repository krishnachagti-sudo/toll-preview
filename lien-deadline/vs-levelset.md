# Levelset vs this calculator

Levelset's calculator page lists notices sent for you at $59 per recipient and lien filing with a subscription; this calculator gives the dates free with the statute quoted on each line (for an Arizona subcontractor starting November 2, 2026: notice by November 22, 2026) and fills the statutory notice as a $2.90 PDF that you serve yourself.

## Side by side

Levelset facts from its own site, read November 8, 2026

| What | Levelset | This calculator |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deadline calculator | Free on its site | Free on this page; $0.02 a lookup over the API or MCP |
| Sending a notice | "Send Notice $59/recipient" (calculator page) | You mail it; the filled statutory form with a proof-of-service log is $2.90 per notice, any number of recipients |
| Filing a lien | "File a Lien (Subscription Required)" | Not offered: dates and the statute only |
| States | All states listed in its state resources | 10 jurisdictions read on their own statute sites, plus the Miller Act; the rest listed as not covered |
| Statute on each date | Not on the calculator page we read | Every date quotes its statute and the day it was read |

## When Levelset is the better choice

- When you want the notice mailed, tracked and filed for you, across every state, and the $59 per recipient is worth not doing it yourself.
- When your state is one this calculator does not cover yet.
- When you manage many projects and want them in one platform with job research.

> Not legal advice and not a law firm. Dates are worked out from each state's own statute text, quoted with the date we read it. Deadlines are strict: a lien recorded one day late is usually lost. Act before the date shown, and earlier when it falls on a weekend or holiday. For disputes, large amounts or anything unusual about your job, ask a construction attorney licensed in the project's state.
