# Mechanics lien deadlines by state

Of the 10 jurisdictions we have read on their own statute sites, District of Columbia, Florida, Montana, Virginia, Washington give a subcontractor 90 days to file the lien. 41 states are not covered because their own sites could not be read or have not been read yet.

- Alaska, California, Colorado, Hawaii, North Carolina, Nevada, New York, West Virginia: The state's own statute site refused our requests on 2026-11-08 (access denied, a bot check or a redirect away from the code), so we read none of its lien law and show no dates. We do not copy the rules from mirrors or vendor charts.
- Arkansas, Connecticut, Idaho, Illinois, Louisiana, Massachusetts, Michigan, Missouri, Nebraska, New Hampshire, New Jersey, Ohio, Oregon, Rhode Island, Utah, Vermont, Wisconsin, Wyoming: The state's own statute site did not answer from our network on 2026-11-08 (timed out or the connection was refused), so we read none of its lien law and show no dates. We do not copy the rules from mirrors or vendor charts.
- Georgia, Mississippi, Tennessee: The state's official code is published through a commercial publisher whose site we cannot read and cite, so we show no dates.
- Alabama, Delaware, Iowa, Indiana, Kentucky, Maine, North Dakota, New Mexico, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, South Carolina, South Dakota: We have not yet read this state's lien statute on its own site into checked rules (some of these sites answer only to a browser script). Until we do, we show no dates rather than guess.

> Not legal advice and not a law firm. Dates are worked out from each state's own statute text, quoted with the date we read it. Deadlines are strict: a lien recorded one day late is usually lost. Act before the date shown, and earlier when it falls on a weekend or holiday. For disputes, large amounts or anything unusual about your job, ask a construction attorney licensed in the project's state.

## Questions

### Can you file a lien after 90 days?

In District of Columbia, Florida, Montana, Virginia, Washington, the recording period is 90 days (from the last furnishing, or from completion), so not after it. Others we have read allow longer: Arizona 120 days after completion, Minnesota 120 days, Maryland 180 days (to petition the court), and Texas until the 15th day of the fourth month after the month of last work.

### Why are some states missing?

Each rule here is quoted from the state's own statute site. Where that site refused or did not answer our requests, or we have not yet read it, the state is listed as not covered rather than copied from another chart.
