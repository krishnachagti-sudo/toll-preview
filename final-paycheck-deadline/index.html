An employee fired (discharged) in Texas on November 13, 2026 at 4:00 pm must be paid by the end of Thursday, November 19, 2026 (6 calendar days after separation). The rule: by the sixth day after the discharge (Tex. Lab. Code 61.014(a)).

In Kansas, a deduction of $900.00 for unreturned equipment (laptop, phone, tools, uniform) from the final paycheck: allowed after written notice. Allowed from final wages after a written notice and explanation, until the property is returned; then the withheld wages must be paid (K.S.A. 44-319). After it, the final week pays -$300.00 for 40 hours (-$7.50 an hour), below $7.25 an hour (the federal minimum wage): at most $310.00 can come out of that week.

What a late check costs

Example: Oregon, $1,840 of final wages due November 27, 2026, paid December 4, $23 an hour

$1,288.00on top of $1,840.00 of wages, 7 days late

  • Final wages unpaid$1,840.00you entered
  • Days late7due Fri, Nov 27, paid Fri, Dec 4
  • Penalty on top of the wages$1,288.008 x $23.00 an hour x 7 days (at most 30)

Paid 7 days late, $1,840.00 of final wages in Oregon can carry $1,288.00 on top of the wages: 8 x $23.00 an hour x 7 days (at most 30). Penalty wages of eight times the regular hourly rate for each day unpaid, up to 30 days; plus a possible $1,000 civil penalty for willful failure.

Where the rules come from

Each covered state's rule is quoted word for word from that state's own statute site or labor department, read on November 8 or 9, 2026; the answer shows the quote and the link. States we could not read there are listed as not covered rather than filled in from someone else's chart.

What it does not cover

  • Local ordinances, union and employment contracts, and industry rules, which can be stricter.
  • State holidays and closed days: add yours under "More".
  • City and county minimum wages in the deduction check.
Read the coverage, the notes and the prices

  • Worked out from each state's own published text, quoted with the date we read it. Not legal advice.
  • Business days skip weekends and US federal holidays; add your state's holidays and closed days if they differ.
  • Local ordinances, union contracts, employment contracts and industry rules can be stricter.
  • The deduction floor uses the higher of the federal minimum wage and the state's basic rate in the U.S. Department of Labor's state table; city and county minimums are not included. 29 CFR 531.35 (eCFR, read November 8, 2026): a deduction for the employer's benefit may not cut into the minimum wage or overtime for the workweek.
