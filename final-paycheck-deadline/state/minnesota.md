# Minnesota final paycheck deadline

In Minnesota, a fired employee must be paid immediately on the employee's written demand; in default 24 hours after it (Minn. Stat. 181.13(a)); one who quits, by the first regular payday; if that is less than 5 days away, the second payday, but within 20 calendar days (Minn. Stat. 181.14 subd. 1). Fired at 4:00 pm on Wednesday, November 18, 2026, with paydays every other Friday, the final paycheck is due by 4:00 pm on Thursday, November 19, 2026; after a resignation that day, by the end of Friday, December 4, 2026.

## Minnesota in words

- "When any employer employing labor within this state discharges an employee, the wages or commissions actually earned and unpaid at the time of the discharge are immediately due and payable upon demand of the employee." [Minn. Stat. 181.13, Penalty for failure to pay wages promptly](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/181.13)
- "If the employee's earned wages and commissions are not paid within 24 hours after demand, whether the employment was by the day, hour, week, month, or piece or by commissions, the employer is in default." [Minn. Stat. 181.13, Penalty for failure to pay wages promptly](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/181.13)
- "shall be paid in full not later than the first regularly scheduled payday following the employee's final day of employment" [Minn. Stat. 181.14, Prompt payment on resignation](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/181.14)
- "If the first regularly scheduled payday is less than five calendar days following the employee's final day of employment, full payment may be delayed until the second regularly scheduled payday but shall not exceed a total of 20 calendar days following the employee's final day of employment." [Minn. Stat. 181.14, Prompt payment on resignation](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/181.14)

## If the check is late

Average daily earnings for each day in default, up to 15 days. [Work out the amount](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/final-paycheck-deadline/waiting-time-penalty/).

## Deductions from the final check

No deduction for lost or stolen property, damage, or any other debt to the employer unless the employee, after the loss or debt arose, voluntarily authorizes it in writing (stating the amount per pay period), or a court holds the employee liable. A purchase or loan the employee authorized in writing before making it may be deducted, including on termination. (Minn. Stat. 181.79).
