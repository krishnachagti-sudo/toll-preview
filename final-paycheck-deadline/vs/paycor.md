# Paycor's final paycheck chart and this lookup

Paycor's chart (last updated December 1, 2025) gives each state's rule in words; this tool works it out to a date and quotes the state: a Delaware layoff on December 31, 2026 with the payday on January 1 is due by the end of Wednesday, January 6, 2027, because the Delaware Code counts 3 business days, where the chart's "3 days after last day worked" would give January 3, 2027.

## What Paycor's page has

Read on Paycor's own page, "Final Paycheck Laws by State in 2026", on November 8, 2026:

- Last updated: December 1, 2025.
- A chart of every state with a column for an employee who quit and one for an employee who was fired
- Lists Alabama, Florida, Georgia and Mississippi as states without specific final paycheck laws
- Answers deductions in general prose (taxes, garnishments and authorized contributions are lawful; state law decides the rest), with no state-by-state deduction column

## What this lookup adds

- The due date and, where the law counts hours, the time, from the last day, the notice and your paydays, with weekends and federal holidays handled.
- Each rule quoted word for word from the state's own statute or labor department site, with the link and the date read.
- A deduction check by state and topic, with the minimum-wage floor worked out on your numbers.
- The late-pay penalty computed where the state sets a formula, and the same answers over an API and MCP.

## Delaware, worked

Their row for Delaware reads: "Whichever is latest: Next scheduled payday or 3 days after last day worked". The Delaware Code says:

- "Whenever an employee quits, resigns, is discharged, suspended, or laid off, the wages earned by the employee become due and payable by the employer on the later of the following:"
- "The next date the wages would be paid through the last day worked under the employer's regular pay cycle as if the employment had not stopped."
- "Three business days after the last day worked."
