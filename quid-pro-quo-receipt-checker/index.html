The whole event

Every ticket level's receipt, the sentence for the invitation or ticket page, and a correction letter for receipts already sent, as text and CSV. Rewording the same event within 90 days is free.

Check a ticket or auction list

One payment a line: Payment, Value (what was received), and if you have them Donor, Event, Kind (ticket, auction, raffle) and Disclosed (yes or no). Each row gets its deductible amount and whether it needed the disclosure; rows marked "no" are counted toward the $10-a-receipt penalty, which stops at $5,000 an event.

Lists are used for that answer and not kept.

The rules it applies

Over $75, disclose; small benefits count as nothing.

Federal rules from IRS Publication 1771 and each year's revenue procedure, read November 8, 2026.

What it does not cover

State raffle and solicitation rules, the good faith estimate itself (you give the value), and tax or legal advice.

Over $75, disclose

A payment over $75 that is partly for goods or services needs a written statement of the value and the deductible part.

Read the full rules

A payment over $75 that is partly a gift and partly for goods or services needs a written statement: that only the excess over the value received is deductible, and your good faith estimate of that value. It is required even when the deductible part is $75 or less, and missing it costs $10 a contribution, up to $5,000 an event or mailing (IRS Publication 1771).

When it counts as nothing

Benefits of insubstantial value, small memberships and intangible religious benefits are left out.

Read the full rules

Benefits of insubstantial value can be ignored: worth no more than 2% of the payment or the year's cap, or only logo items costing no more than the low-cost article limit for a gift of at least the year's minimum. Membership privileges for $75 or less a year, and intangible religious benefits from a religious organization, are also left out.

Raffles and auctions

Raffle tickets are not deductible; an auction winner deducts only what they paid above the value.

Read the full rules

Raffle, lottery and bingo tickets are not deductible (IRS Publication 526). An auction winner can deduct only what they paid above the item's value; a bid at or below it is not a contribution. A sponsor's name or logo is acknowledgment, not a benefit; tickets and advertising are.
