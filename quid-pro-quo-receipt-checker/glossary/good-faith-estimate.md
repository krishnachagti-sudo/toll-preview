# Good faith estimate

A good faith estimate is the charity's figure for the fair market value of the goods or services a donor received, made by any reasonable method applied in good faith; a disclosure for a payment over $75 must give it, as in "In exchange for your contribution, we gave you a cookbook with an estimated fair market value of $60." (IRS Publication 1771).

An organization may use any reasonable method to estimate the fair market value (FMV) of goods or services it provided to a donor, as long as it applies the method in good faith.

The organization may estimate the FMV of goods or services that generally are not commercially available by using the FMV of similar or comparable goods or services.

- A charity provides a one-hour tennis lesson with a tennis professional for the first $500 payment it receives. The tennis professional provides one-hour lessons on a commercial basis for $100. A good faith estimate of the lesson's FMV is $100.
- If the hotel ballroom rents for $2,500, a good faith estimate of the FMV of the right to hold the event in the museum is $2,500.
- A good faith estimate of the FMV of the evening museum tour is $0 even though the artist conducts it.

It goes on the disclosure, which must provide the donor with a good faith estimate of the fair market value of the goods or services.
