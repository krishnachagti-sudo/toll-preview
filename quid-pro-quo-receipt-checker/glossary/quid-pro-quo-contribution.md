# Quid pro quo contribution

A quid pro quo contribution is a payment to a charity partly as a gift and partly for goods or services; above $75 the charity must give a written disclosure. The IRS example: $100.00 for a $40.00 concert ticket is a $60.00 contribution, and the disclosure is required.

An organization must provide a written disclosure statement to a donor who makes a payment exceeding $75 partly as a contribution and partly for goods and services provided by the organization.

A donor gives a charitable organization $100 in exchange for a concert ticket with a fair market value of $40. In this example, the donor's tax deduction may not exceed $60.

## What is not one

- Benefits of insubstantial value (for 2026, worth no more than 2% of the payment or $139, or logo items costing $13.90 or less for a gift of at least $69.50).
- There is no donative element involved in a particular transaction with a charity (for example, there is generally no donative element involved in a visitor's purchase from a museum gift shop).
- Only intangible religious benefits from a religious organization.
- Annual membership benefits for $75 or less.

## Questions

### What is a quid pro quo contribution?

A payment a donor makes to a charity partly as a contribution and partly for goods or services (IRS).

### What is the penalty for not giving the disclosure?

The penalty is $10 per contribution, not to exceed $5,000 per fundraising event or mailing. The charity can avoid the penalty if it can show that the failure was due to reasonable cause.
