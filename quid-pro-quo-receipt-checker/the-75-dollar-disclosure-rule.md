# The $75 disclosure rule

A charity must give a written disclosure when a donor's payment is more than $75 and partly for goods or services: a $75.00 ticket needs none, a $75.01 ticket with a $30.00 dinner does, even though only $45.01 of it is deductible. Missing it costs $10 a contribution, up to $5,000 an event or mailing.

## What the disclosure says and when

- It must inform the donor that the amount of the contribution that is deductible for federal income tax purposes is limited to the excess of money (and the fair market value of property other than money) contributed by the donor over the value of goods or services provided by the organization.
- It must provide the donor with a good faith estimate of the fair market value of the goods or services.

An organization must furnish a disclosure statement in connection with either the solicitation or the receipt of the quid pro quo contribution. If the disclosure statement is furnished in connection with a particular solicitation, it is not necessary for the organization to provide another statement when it actually receives the contribution. The statement must be in writing and must be made in a manner that is likely to come to the attention of the donor. For example, a disclosure in small print within a larger document might not meet this requirement.

## The penalty

$10 a contribution, at most $5,000 for one fundraising event or mailing.

| Receipts without the disclosure at one event | Penalty |
| ---: | ---: |
| 1 | $10.00 |

| 100 | $1,000.00 |
| 300 | $3,000.00 |
| 500 | $5,000.00 |
| 501 | $5,000.00 |

## Questions

### Is the $75 rule per ticket or per payment?

Per payment: Pub 1771 says "An organization must provide a written disclosure statement to a donor who makes a payment exceeding $75 partly as a contribution and partly for goods and services provided by the organization." Two $75 tickets bought in one $150 payment are one payment over $75.

### Is the $75 about the ticket price or the deductible part?

The payment. A donor gives a charitable organization $100 in exchange for a concert ticket with a fair market value of $40. In this example, the donor's tax deduction may not exceed $60.

### What does missing it cost?

The penalty is $10 per contribution, not to exceed $5,000 per fundraising event or mailing. The charity can avoid the penalty if it can show that the failure was due to reasonable cause.
