# Quid pro quo disclosure statement example

Sample: "In exchange for your payment you received dinner and dance, with an estimated fair market value of $60.00. The amount of your contribution that is deductible for federal income tax purposes is limited to the excess of your payment over the value of the goods and services we provided: $90.00." It states the $90.00 deductible limit and the $60.00 good faith estimate, the two things the IRS requires for a payment over $75.

## The filled statement

A $150 gala ticket that includes a dinner and dance the food bank values at $60. Made up for this page.

**Thank you for your payment of $150.00 to Riverside Food Bank for the Harvest Gala on November 14, 2026. In exchange for your payment you received dinner and dance, with an estimated fair market value of $60.00. The amount of your contribution that is deductible for federal income tax purposes is limited to the excess of your payment over the value of the goods and services we provided: $90.00.**

On the invitation or ticket page: Of each $150.00 payment, $60.00 is our good faith estimate of the fair market value of dinner and dance; the amount deductible for federal income tax purposes is limited to $90.00.

- Deductible part: $150.00 paid less $60.00 received = $90.00.
- The payment is over $75 and partly for goods or services, so a written disclosure is required.

## What it must contain

- It must inform the donor that the amount of the contribution that is deductible for federal income tax purposes is limited to the excess of money (and the fair market value of property other than money) contributed by the donor over the value of goods or services provided by the organization.
- It must provide the donor with a good faith estimate of the fair market value of the goods or services.

An organization must furnish a disclosure statement in connection with either the solicitation or the receipt of the quid pro quo contribution. If the disclosure statement is furnished in connection with a particular solicitation, it is not necessary for the organization to provide another statement when it actually receives the contribution.

The statement must be in writing and must be made in a manner that is likely to come to the attention of the donor. For example, a disclosure in small print within a larger document might not meet this requirement.

## Questions

### When is a quid pro quo disclosure required?

An organization must provide a written disclosure statement to a donor who makes a payment exceeding $75 partly as a contribution and partly for goods and services provided by the organization.

### Does it matter that the deductible part is under $75?

No. A donor gives a charitable organization $100 in exchange for a concert ticket with a fair market value of $40. In this example, the donor's tax deduction may not exceed $60. The disclosure is still required because the payment exceeds $75.

### Can the disclosure go on the invitation instead of the receipt?

An organization must furnish a disclosure statement in connection with either the solicitation or the receipt of the quid pro quo contribution. If the disclosure statement is furnished in connection with a particular solicitation, it is not necessary for the organization to provide another statement when it actually receives the contribution.
