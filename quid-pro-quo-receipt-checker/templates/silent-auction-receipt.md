# Silent auction receipt template

A silent auction receipt states the winning bid, the item's fair market value and the difference: a $450.00 bid on an item worth $300.00 is a $150.00 contribution and needs the disclosure, while a bid at or below the value is not a contribution at all (IRS Publication 526).

## A bid above the value

A $450 winning bid on a wine and dinner basket the school values at $300 (the retail prices of its contents). Made up for this page.

**Thank you for your payment of $450.00 to Maple Street School PTA for the Spring Auction. In exchange for your payment you received a wine and dinner basket, with an estimated fair market value of $300.00. The amount of your contribution that is deductible for federal income tax purposes is limited to the excess of your payment over the value of the goods and services we provided: $150.00.**

On the invitation or ticket page: Of each $450.00 payment, $300.00 is our good faith estimate of the fair market value of a wine and dinner basket; the amount deductible for federal income tax purposes is limited to $150.00.

- Deductible part: $450.00 paid less $300.00 received = $150.00.
- The payment is over $75 and partly for goods or services, so a written disclosure is required.

## A bid at the value

Pub 526's example: $600 for a week at a beach house, no more than its fair rental value.

**Thank you for your payment of $600.00 to Maple Street School PTA for the Spring Auction. In exchange you received a week at a beach house, with an estimated fair market value of $600.00. Because your payment is not more than that value, no part of it is deductible as a charitable contribution.**

- The value received ($600.00) is at least the payment ($600.00): no part of it is a charitable contribution, and there is no donative element.

At a fundraising auction conducted by a charity, you pay $600 for a week's stay at a beach house. The amount you pay is no more than the fair rental value. You haven't made a deductible charitable contribution. If you pay more than FMV to a qualified organization for goods or services, the excess may be a charitable contribution. For the excess amount to qualify, you must pay it with the intent to make a charitable contribution.

The person who donated the item gets a different acknowledgment: it describes the item and gives no value; see [the in-kind donation letter](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/donor-acknowledgment-letter/templates/in-kind-donation-letter/).

## Questions

### Do silent auction winners get a tax receipt?

They should get a receipt stating the bid and the item's good faith value; over $75 with something of value received, the disclosure is required.

### What if the winning bid is below the value?

There is no contribution: nothing is deductible. Say so on the receipt rather than calling the payment a donation.
