Gala ticket receipt template

A gala ticket receipt gives the payment, a good faith estimate of the dinner and entertainment, and the deductible part: a $250.00 ticket with $85.00 of benefits is a $165.00 contribution, and because it is over $75 the receipt must say so.

A single ticket

A $250 ticket; the dinner and entertainment are valued at $85, what a comparable public dinner and show would cost. Made up for this page.

Read the rest: A single ticket

Thank you for your payment of $250.00 to Lakeside Arts Council for the Spring Gala on April 17, 2027. In exchange for your payment you received dinner and entertainment, with an estimated fair market value of $85.00. The amount of your contribution that is deductible for federal income tax purposes is limited to the excess of your payment over the value of the goods and services we provided: $165.00.

On the invitation or ticket page: Of each $250.00 payment, $85.00 is our good faith estimate of the fair market value of dinner and entertainment; the amount deductible for federal income tax purposes is limited to $165.00.

  • Deductible part: $250.00 paid less $85.00 received = $165.00.
  • The payment is over $75 and partly for goods or services, so a written disclosure is required.

A table of ten

The same gala, ten seats bought together for $2,500. The value is per seat times ten; the guests' dinners stay in the value received, so the buyer deducts the same $165 a seat.

Read the rest: A table of ten

Thank you for your payment of $2,500.00 to Lakeside Arts Council for the Spring Gala on April 17, 2027. In exchange for your payment you received dinner and entertainment for ten, with an estimated fair market value of $850.00. The amount of your contribution that is deductible for federal income tax purposes is limited to the excess of your payment over the value of the goods and services we provided: $1,650.00.

On the invitation or ticket page: Of each $2,500.00 payment, $850.00 is our good faith estimate of the fair market value of dinner and entertainment for ten; the amount deductible for federal income tax purposes is limited to $1,650.00.

  • Deductible part: $2,500.00 paid less $850.00 received = $1,650.00.
  • The payment is over $75 and partly for goods or services, so a written disclosure is required.
  • The contribution is $250 or more, so the donor needs a written acknowledgment before filing: the receipt below carries its elements.

If a ticket says "contribution"

Even if the ticket or other evidence of payment indicates that the entire price of admission for the special event is a "contribution," this doesn't mean you can deduct the entire amount. If the regular price for the movie is $8, your contribution is $32 ($40 payment − $8 regular price). (IRS Publication 526)

Not using the ticket changes nothing: Whether you use the tickets or other privileges has no effect on the amount you can deduct. However, if you return the ticket to the qualified organization for resale, you can deduct the entire amount you paid for the ticket.

Questions

Is a gala ticket tax deductible?

Only the part above the value of what the guest receives (dinner, entertainment, gifts). The organization gives that value as a good faith estimate.

What if the donor does not attend?

Whether you use the tickets or other privileges has no effect on the amount you can deduct.
