# Intangible religious benefits receipt wording

When a religious organization gives only intangible religious benefits, the acknowledgment need not describe or value them; it can simply say so: "Temple Beth Shalom provided only intangible religious benefits in exchange for your contribution." (IRS Publication 1771).

## Only intangible religious benefits

A $360 payment that comes with admission to High Holiday services. Whether seats are an intangible religious benefit depends on the facts: the IRS example is admission to a religious ceremony for which there is no admission charge. Made up for this page.

**Thank you for your contribution of $360.00 to Temple Beth Shalom on September 1, 2026. Temple Beth Shalom provided only intangible religious benefits in exchange for your contribution.**

- Nothing received counts (only intangible religious benefits from a religious organization), so the full $360.00 is the contribution and no disclosure is required.
- The contribution is $250 or more, so the donor needs a written acknowledgment before filing: the receipt below carries its elements.

## With a dinner as well

A $500 payment that also includes a break-fast dinner valued at $45. A meal is consumer goods, not an intangible religious benefit, so it is valued and disclosed.

**Thank you for your payment of $500.00 to Temple Beth Shalom on September 1, 2026. In exchange for your payment you received a break-fast dinner, with an estimated fair market value of $45.00. You also received intangible religious benefits. The amount of your contribution that is deductible for federal income tax purposes is limited to the excess of your payment over the value of the goods and services we provided: $455.00.**

On the invitation or ticket page: Of each $500.00 payment, $45.00 is our good faith estimate of the fair market value of a break-fast dinner; the amount deductible for federal income tax purposes is limited to $455.00.

- Deductible part: $500.00 paid less $45.00 received = $455.00.
- The payment is over $75 and partly for goods or services, so a written disclosure is required.
- The contribution is $250 or more, so the donor needs a written acknowledgment before filing: the receipt below carries its elements.

## What counts

- Examples include admission to a religious ceremony and a de minimis tangible benefit, such as wine used in a religious ceremony. Benefits that are not intangible religious benefits include education leading to a recognized degree, travel services, and consumer goods.
- The intangible religious benefit must be provided to the donor by an organization organized exclusively for religious purposes and must be of a type that generally is not sold in a commercial transaction outside the donative context.
- For example, a donor who, for a payment, is granted admission to a religious ceremony for which there is no admission charge is provided an intangible religious benefit.

## Questions

### Do churches have to value religious services on receipts?

If a religious charitable organization provides only "intangible religious benefits" to a donor, the contemporaneous written acknowledgment does not need to describe or value those benefits. It can simply state that the organization provided intangible religious benefits to the donor.

### Does a school run by a church count?

Education leading to a recognized degree is not an intangible religious benefit (IRS Publication 1771).
