# Which fundraiser payments are deductible

Raffle, bingo and 50/50 tickets are never deductible; event tickets and auction wins are deductible only above the value received (a $150.00 gala ticket with a $60.00 dinner gives $90.00); a paddle-raise gift, a logo-only sponsorship and a gift with a token premium are deductible in full.

- You can't deduct as a charitable contribution amounts you pay to buy raffle or lottery tickets or to play bingo or other games of chance.
- At a fundraising auction conducted by a charity, you pay $600 for a week's stay at a beach house. The amount you pay is no more than the fair rental value. You haven't made a deductible charitable contribution.
- You pay $65 for a ticket to a dinner dance at a church. Your entire $65 payment goes to the church. The ticket to the dinner dance has an FMV of $25. You can deduct $40 as a charitable contribution to the church.

Who gets which receipt: the winner gets a receipt for what they paid; whoever donated the auction item gets an acknowledgment that describes it without a value ([in-kind letter](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/donor-acknowledgment-letter/templates/in-kind-donation-letter/)).

## Questions

### Are raffle tickets tax deductible?

You can't deduct as a charitable contribution amounts you pay to buy raffle or lottery tickets or to play bingo or other games of chance.

### Is a bake sale purchase a donation?

Not when the price is what the item is worth: There is no donative element involved in a particular transaction with a charity (for example, there is generally no donative element involved in a visitor's purchase from a museum gift shop).
