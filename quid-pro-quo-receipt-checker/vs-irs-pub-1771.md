# Publication 1771 and the checker

IRS Publication 1771 (Rev. 11-2023) is the authority for the rules, free from irs.gov, but it prints 2023 figures ($12.50, $62.50, $125) and says "The dollar amounts are for 2023. Guideline amounts are adjusted for inflation."; the checker applies the same rules with the figures for the year of the payment ($13.90, $69.50 and $139 for 2026) to your own wording.

## What IRS Publication 1771 offers

- The $250 contemporaneous written acknowledgment and what it contains.
- The token, membership benefits and intangible religious benefits exceptions, with 2023 dollar figures.
- The quid pro quo disclosure: over $75, its two elements, when to give it, the $10 penalty up to $5,000.
- Sample acknowledgment sentences.

> From IRS Publication 1771's own page, read November 8, 2026. Plans and features change; check their page.

## What Endwise's checker does

- Checks the receipt text you already send: the estimate, the deductible limit, and a wrong "no goods or services" or "fully deductible" line.
- Applies the token, membership, religious and sponsorship exceptions with each year's IRS limits (2020 to 2027).
- Free single checks; $2.00 for an event's wording at every ticket level; $0.01 a row to check a list, with the penalty exposure; at most $9.00 a month.

## When IRS Publication 1771 is the better choice

- You need the authority to cite to an auditor or board.
- You are reading the rules, not applying them to a receipt.

## When the checker is the better choice

- You want this year's limits applied, not 2023's.
- You have 300 tickets or a receipt template to check against it.
- You want the corrected sentence written.

## Questions

### Is Publication 1771 current?

It is revision 11-2023; its dollar figures are for 2023 and the IRS adjusts them each year in a revenue procedure (for 2027, Rev. Proc. 2026-35).
