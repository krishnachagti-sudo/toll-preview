Are raffle tickets tax deductible?

No: you can't deduct as a charitable contribution amounts you pay to buy raffle or lottery tickets or to play bingo or other games of chance (IRS Publication 526). A $20.00 raffle purchase gives a $0.00 deduction, so the charity's receipt should say the tickets are not deductible.

What to print

Thank you for supporting Riverside Food Bank for the Harvest Gala raffle. The $20.00 you paid for raffle tickets is not deductible as a charitable contribution for federal income tax purposes, because raffle tickets are a chance to win a prize.

On the tickets or the sign: Raffle tickets are not tax-deductible charitable contributions.

A raffle bought together with a gala ticket is separate: the ticket's deductible part is worked out without the raffle, and the raffle money is never added to it.

Raffle prizes and Form W-2G

The requirements in this section apply to church raffles, charity drawings, etc. In the case of one wager for multiple raffle tickets, such as five for $1, the wager is considered as $.20 for each ticket. The minimum threshold amount for payments made in calendar year 2026 is $2,000.

The 2025 threshold ($600) is from Publication 3079 (Rev. 10-2018), which still shows it; the 2026 threshold is from the 01/2026 W-2G instructions; the 2027 figure is the section 6041(a) reporting threshold in Rev. Proc. 2026-35, to be confirmed in the 2027 W-2G instructions. State raffle licensing and wording rules differ and are not covered.

Questions

Are raffle tickets tax deductible?

You can't deduct as a charitable contribution amounts you pay to buy raffle or lottery tickets or to play bingo or other games of chance.

Are raffle tickets deductible if the money goes to a 501(c)(3)?

No. The rule is about what the buyer paid for, a chance to win, not about who receives the money.

When must a charity report a raffle prize?

For prizes paid in 2026: winnings of at least $2,000 (the W-2G instructions' 2026 threshold) and at least 300 times the ticket price. You must withhold federal income tax from the winnings if the winnings minus the wager exceed $5,000. Withhold 24% of the proceeds (the winnings minus the wager).
