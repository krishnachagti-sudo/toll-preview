Are gala tickets tax deductible?

Only the part of a gala ticket above the value of the dinner and entertainment is deductible: a $250.00 ticket with $85.00 of benefits gives a $165.00 deduction, and the charity's receipt must state both numbers because the payment is over $75.

The arithmetic

The eight-ticket case is a real question from a tax forum: the buyer hoped to deduct the guests' tickets in full, but their dinners were benefits too, so the deduction is $25 a ticket, $200.00 in all.

You pay $65 for a ticket to a dinner dance at a church. Your entire $65 payment goes to the church. The ticket to the dinner dance has an FMV of $25. You can deduct $40 as a charitable contribution to the church.

Rules for benefit events

If there is an established charge for the event, that charge is the value of your benefit. If there is no established charge, the reasonable value of the right to attend the event is the value of your benefit. Whether you use the tickets or other privileges has no effect on the amount you can deduct. However, if you return the ticket to the qualified organization for resale, you can deduct the entire amount you paid for the ticket.

Even if the ticket or other evidence of payment indicates that the entire price of admission for the special event is a "contribution," this doesn't mean you can deduct the entire amount.

What the receipt says (for organizers)

Thank you for your payment of $250.00 to Riverside Food Bank for the Harvest Gala. In exchange for your payment you received dinner and entertainment, with an estimated fair market value of $85.00. The amount of your contribution that is deductible for federal income tax purposes is limited to the excess of your payment over the value of the goods and services we provided: $165.00.

If no dinner or gift comes with the ticket price (a "support the gala" gift), the receipt says no goods or services were provided. A tote or program with your logo can still count as nothing under the year's token limits.

Questions

Can I deduct gala tickets I bought for guests?

Only the part of each ticket above the value of that guest's dinner and entertainment; the guests' meals are part of the value received.

What if I do not go?

Whether you use the tickets or other privileges has no effect on the amount you can deduct. However, if you return the ticket to the qualified organization for resale, you can deduct the entire amount you paid for the ticket.

Who decides the value of the dinner?

The charity, as a good faith estimate. If there is an established charge for the event, that charge is the value of your benefit. If there is no established charge, the reasonable value of the right to attend the event is the value of your benefit. (IRS Publication 526)
