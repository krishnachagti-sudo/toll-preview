Are silent auction purchases tax deductible?

Only the part of a winning bid above the item's fair market value is deductible: a $450.00 bid on a $300.00 basket gives $150.00, and a bid at or below the value gives nothing, as in Publication 526's $600 beach house week.

If you pay more than FMV to a qualified organization for goods or services, the excess may be a charitable contribution. For the excess amount to qualify, you must pay it with the intent to make a charitable contribution. (IRS Publication 526)

The disclosure applies to a winning bid like any other payment: over $75 and partly for the item, the receipt states the value and the deductible part. A $70 bid on a $60 dinner needs none, though the receipt should still state both.

Questions

Is a silent auction winning bid tax deductible?

Only the part above the item's fair market value. At a fundraising auction conducted by a charity, you pay $600 for a week's stay at a beach house. The amount you pay is no more than the fair rental value. You haven't made a deductible charitable contribution.

Can the person who donated a week at their vacation home deduct it?

No. The right to use property is a partial interest, which is not deductible, and the winner who pays the fair rental value cannot deduct either (IRS Publication 526).
