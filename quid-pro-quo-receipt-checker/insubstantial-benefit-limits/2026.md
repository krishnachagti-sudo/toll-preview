# Insubstantial benefit limits for 2026

For 2026 a benefit is insubstantial if it is worth no more than 2% of the gift or $139, whichever is less, or if the gift is at least $69.50 and the only items given bear the organization's name or logo and cost it $13.90 or less in all (Rev. Proc. 2025-32, section 4.33).

- Low-cost article: $13.90
- Minimum gift for logo items: $69.50
- Cap on the 2% test: $139.00

## The 2026 figures

Rev. Proc. 2025-32, section 4.33, the $5, $25 and $50 guidelines of Rev. Proc. 90-12 adjusted for 2026; the 2025 figures for comparison.

| Limit | 1990 base | 2026 | 2025 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Low-cost article (logo items, cost to you) | $5 | $13.90 | $13.60 |
| Minimum gift for the logo-item test | $25 | $69.50 | $68.00 |
| Cap on the 2% test | $50 | $139.00 | $136.00 |

## The two tests

- 2% test: the fair market value of the goods and services the donor receives does not exceed the lesser of 2 percent of the donor's payment or $139.00 (for 2026).
- Logo-item test: donor's payment is at least $69.50 (for 2026), the only goods or services the charitable organization provides bear the organization's name or logo (for example, calendars, mugs, or posters), and the cost of these items, in the aggregate, is within the limit for "low-cost articles," which is $13.90 (for 2026).

Good and services are considered to be of insubstantial value if the payment the donor makes occurs in the context of a fundraising campaign, the charitable organization informs the donor as to the amount of the payment that is a deductible contribution. Low-cost articles a charitable organization sends for free to taxpayers who have not ordered these articles are also considered to be of insubstantial value; examples include mailing labels or greeting cards.

## The receipt it allows

A $69.50 gift with a logo mug that cost $13.90: the checker finds the logo-item test met and the full $69.50 deductible.

## Questions

### What are the 2026 insubstantial benefit limits?

Low-cost article $13.90; benefits worth no more than 2% of the payment or $139.00, whichever is less; or, for a payment of at least $69.50, only items with the organization's name or logo costing $13.90 or less in all (Rev. Proc. 2025-32, section 4.33).

### Is the "2% or $50" rule still right?

No. $5, $25 and $50 are the 1990 guidelines of Rev. Proc. 90-12; each year's revenue procedure adjusts them for inflation, to $13.90, $69.50 and $139.00 for 2026.

### Can we give a $69.50 donor a mug and still say no goods or services were provided?

Yes, if the mug bears your name or logo and it and any other such items cost you $13.90 or less in all: If a charitable organization gives a coffee mug bearing its logo that costs the organization $13.90 or less to a donor who contributes $69.50 or more, the organization may state that no goods or services were provided in return for the contribution. The contribution is fully deductible. (Pub 1771's example, with the 2026 figures).

### Is the low-cost article limit the item's value or its cost?

Its cost to the organization: Pub 1771 says "the cost of these items, in the aggregate, is within the limit for low-cost articles".
