The invoice

The invoice, a PDF and two CSVs.

Every line with its service dates, a lines CSV for your accounting app, an entries CSV that shows how each entry was rounded and priced, and a PDF to send. The export is sent once to make them and is not kept.

What you get, and a sample

Open the detailed report you exported from Toggl Track, Clockify, Harvest or Everhour. Choose how to group the lines, how your contract rounds time and what each kind of work is billed at, and see the lines, the total and how far the billed time is from the exact time.

The preview is free on this page and the file does not leave your browser. The full invoice (every line, a lines CSV, an entries CSV and the PDF) costs $0.40; the same client and period again within 30 days is free.

The rules

Where the cents go, line by line.

Lines are worked out from the file you send, with the grouping, rounding and rates you choose. Check them before you send an invoice.

What it does not cover

Employees' time-clock rounding, which is a different rule, and tax or legal advice.

Read the full list
  • Lines are worked out from the file you send, with the grouping, rounding and rates you choose. Check them before you send an invoice.
  • Rounding for freelance and agency work is set by your contract. Rounding employees' time-clock punches is a different rule (29 CFR 785.48): see the hours calculator.
  • Tax is applied at the rate you enter. Not tax or legal advice.
  • Not affiliated with Toggl, Clockify, Harvest or Everhour. Sources, read November 8, 2026: Toggl: Creating invoices, Toggl: Rounding, Toggl: CSV files, Clockify: Invoicing, Clockify: Invoices and reports don't match, Clockify: Time rounding, Harvest API: Time entries.

Each entry, or each line

Rounding each entry up to 15 minutes can add up to 14 minutes 59 seconds an entry; rounding the line adds it once.

Read the full rule

Rounding every entry up to 15 minutes adds up to 14 minutes 59 seconds per entry; rounding the line once adds it once. Toggl rounds each entry separately and, for a group, rounds the subtotal; Clockify rounds each entry first and then adds them up. The preview shows both, and the total rounded once, side by side.

Two decimals, then the rate

Hours to 2 decimals, then times the rate, so hours x rate is the amount shown.

Read the full rule

Clockify's help says its invoices round each entry to 2 decimals before multiplying by the rate, while its reports add the time first and then multiply. Lines here take the line's hours to 2 decimals, so hours x rate is the amount shown; the reconciliation shows Clockify's two ways for the same entries.

A rate for each kind of work

One rate a line; a project with tasks at different rates splits by rate.

Read the full rule

A line holds one rate. When a project's tasks bill at different rates, the project becomes one line per rate. Rates you give win over the file's own billable rate, which wins over the default. Entries with no rate are billed at $0 and flagged.
