# Amazon's prep pages vs the prep checker

Amazon's own Seller Central pages are the rule and cost nothing, but the prep for one product is spread over the 20 help pages the checker reads (prep guidance, bagging, bubble wrap, boxing, cap seal, liquids, softlines, small items, barcodes, expiration dates) and Send to Amazon may show no guidance for a new product; the checker applies all of them to one product at once, with each step linked back to Amazon's page.

## Try the checker on one product

## What they offer

- Prep guidance per product in Send to Amazon, Step 1, when Amazon has classified it; otherwise you pick the category.
- Help pages per prep type (bagging, bubble wrap, boxing, cap seal, opaque covering) and per product type (liquids, granular, fragile, softlines and baby, small and jewelry, adult, sharp, batteries).
- FNSKU labels printed from Seller Central, for thermal printers or six A4 and Letter sheet sizes.
- Free with a seller account. The pages show no date of last change.

## What the prep checker does

- One verdict combining every category a product falls in, as Amazon requires.
- The cap seal decision tree worked for the container.
- The bag checked: opening, warning print size, 1.5 mil, 3-inch limit.
- 2026 and 2027 size tiers side by side.
- Suffocation warning, set and expiration labels (free), FNSKU label PDFs.

## When Amazon's page is the better choice

Amazon's guidance in Send to Amazon wins whenever it shows a requirement for your product, and its pages are the place to check an unusual product (hazmat, TVs, mattresses, plexiglass) the checker does not model. Use Seller Central's own FNSKU printing if you only need product labels.

One product checked on the tool page is free, as are the warning, set and expiration label sheets; a list costs $0.01 a SKU (the first 500 a month, then $0.004); FNSKU label PDFs cost $0.001 a label, at least $0.25 a PDF; never more than $4.99 a month.

> Not affiliated with Amazon. Rules are from Amazon's US Seller Central help pages, read November 8, 2026; Amazon may change them, and product-specific guidance in Send to Amazon wins. Amazon says determining prep is the seller's responsibility.
