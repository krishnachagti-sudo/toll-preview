FBA prep rules

Amazon FBA prep categories

Amazon's prep guidance has 12 categories (adult; apparel on hanger; apparel, fabric, plush, and textiles; baby; fragile, glass; liquids (non-glass); perforated packaging; powder, pellets, and granular; sharp; small; sold as set; no prep needed); 8 of them mean a bag with a suffocation warning, fragile items mean bubble wrap, sharp items their own packaging, and a unit in several categories gets the prep of each.

Where the category is set

In Send to Amazon, Step 1 (Choose inventory to send), open Packing details and click the product's prep category. Some products have no prep requirement yet because they have not been sent in before or not been classified; then you choose the category, or "No prep needed" if the packaging already protects it. Amazon says determining the prep is your responsibility even when no guidance is shown, and it can add prep requirements after damage.

Product types with their own page

Amazon also lists product types whose own help page sets the prep: liquids, pastes, gels and creams; pellets, powders and granular products; fragile products; softlines and baby products; small products and jewelry; adult products; sharp products; and batteries. The checker reads all of them.

Questions

What are Amazon's FBA prep categories?

Adult, Apparel on hanger, Apparel, fabric, plush, and textiles, Baby, Fragile, glass, Liquids (non-glass), Perforated packaging, Powder, pellets, and granular, Sharp, Small, Sold as set and No prep needed.

What if my product is in two categories?

A unit that falls under multiple categories must be prepped for all of them: shampoo and conditioner sold as a set need liquid prep and a "Sold as set" label.
