# A spreadsheet or this tool

A spreadsheet reconciles a 1099-K when you build each line yourself; this tool does the same walkdown with the IRS rules, the $20,000 and 200-transaction check, Boxes 5a to 5l against your books and the duplicate check, free on the page.

A spreadsheet does whatever you build in it. The [free worksheet](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/1099-k-reconciliation/worksheet/) gives you the lines; you still decide each treatment and check each total by hand.

## When they are the better choice

A spreadsheet is better for odd cases a bookkeeper wants to control line by line, or a business with many entities. This tool is better for the standard gross-to-net walkdown done right the first time.

- [Google Sheets: support.google.com/docs, read November 8, 2026](https://support.google.com/docs)
- [IRS: What to do with Form 1099-K, read November 8, 2026](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/what-to-do-with-form-1099-k)

> Computed from IRS instructions and FAQs (each cited with the date read) and the amounts you enter. Not tax or legal advice. You decide which payments are business, personal or in error; the tool applies the IRS reporting text to your classification. State 1099-K thresholds are not covered: check your state revenue department.
