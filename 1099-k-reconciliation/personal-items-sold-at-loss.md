# Personal items sold at a loss on a 1099-K

A personal item sold at a loss is not taxable and the loss is not deductible: the IRS refrigerator bought for $1,000.00 and sold for $700.00 puts $700.00 in the space at the top of Schedule 1, while tickets bought for $500.00 and sold for $900.00 are a $400.00 taxable gain.

- Tickets gain: $400.00
- Refrigerator loss: $300.00
- Box 1a: $1,600.00

Two ways for a loss, by the IRS page: enter the proceeds in the space at the top of Schedule 1 (Form 1040), which keeps you from paying tax you do not owe, or report it on Form 8949 with code L in column (f) and the nondeductible loss in column (g), carried to Schedule D. Report gains and losses separately: a loss on one item cannot offset a gain on another (FAQ Common situations Q4).

## Questions

### Do I pay tax on a 1099-K for things I sold at a loss?

No. The IRS says there is no tax liability on items sold for less than you paid, and you can zero out the reported amount.

### Can I deduct the loss?

No. A loss on the sale of a personal item is not deductible.

### What if I sold some items at a gain and some at a loss?

Report them separately: the IRS says the loss on one cannot offset the gain on another.

### I do not remember what I paid. What now?

The IRS suggests old bank or card statements and the seller's records, and points to Publication 551 on basis.
