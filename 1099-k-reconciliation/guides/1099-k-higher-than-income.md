# Why your 1099-K is higher than your income

Your 1099-K is higher than your income because Box 1a is gross, before refunds, fees and other amounts: $3,000.00 of sales with $120.00 refunded and $87.00 of fees is $3,000.00 on the form but $2,793.00 kept.

- Box 1a: $3,000.00
- Kept: $2,793.00
- Refunds: $120.00
- Fees: $87.00

Your platform pays you the net: sales, less what it refunded, less its fees, less any shipping labels or held amounts. The IRS form counts the gross, each sale on its date, and nothing comes off. The two numbers can only meet in a walkdown.

## Steps

1. **Form total.** Add Box 1a from every form for the year.
2. **Refunds and chargebacks.** Subtract them: they are in Box 1a but go on Schedule C line 2.
3. **Fees.** The platform kept them, but they are an expense (line 10), not a cut in the gross.
4. **Not income.** Take out personal sales at a loss, gifts and reimbursements, duplicates and sales tax paid over.
5. **Reconcile.** What is left is your business gross receipts; what you kept should be close to the payouts.

## Questions

### Why is my 1099-K more than my income?

Box 1a is the gross amount without any adjustment for credits, cash equivalents, discounts, fees, refunds, shipping or any other amounts (Instructions for Form 1099-K). Your income is after those.

### Do I pay tax on the whole 1099-K amount?

No. The IRS says a payment reported on a Form 1099-K is not automatically taxable; fees, credits, refunds, shipping, cash equivalents and discounts can be deducted from the gross.
