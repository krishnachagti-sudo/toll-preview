# 1099-K vs 1099-NEC

A payment made by card or through a payment app goes on Form 1099-K only, never also on Form 1099-NEC (IRS FS-2025-08); so $18,400.00 paid to a freelancer through a marketplace is on the 1099-K, and a $1,500.00 check from a client is on a 1099-NEC.

- On the 1099-K: $18,400.00
- On a 1099-NEC: $1,500.00
- Fees: $1,840.00

The Instructions for Form 1099-K say payments made by payment card or through a third party payment network that would otherwise be reportable under section 6041 or 6041A are reported under section 6050W, on Form 1099-K, and not on Form 1099-MISC or 1099-NEC; the $20,000 and 200-transaction de minimis rule is ignored for deciding which form applies. So a client who pays you through a payment app does not also send a 1099-NEC for it.

## Questions

### Can the same payment be on a 1099-K and a 1099-NEC?

It should not be. The IRS FAQ (FS-2025-08) says a transaction reportable under sections 6041 or 6041A and under 6050W is reported only on Form 1099-K.

### Who files each form?

Form 1099-K is filed by the payment settlement entity (card processor, payment app or marketplace). Form 1099-NEC is filed by the business that paid you by check, cash or bank transfer.

### What if a client sent a 1099-NEC for payments I received through an app?

Then the same income may be reported twice. Count it once on Schedule C and ask the client for a corrected 1099-NEC; keep the records.
