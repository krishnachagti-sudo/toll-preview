# Good Faith Estimates in SimplePractice

SimplePractice includes a Good Faith Estimate form on every plan; its plans start at $49 a month, with $79 and $99 plans above it (pricing page, November 8, 2026). Without an EHR, this generator makes the same 45 CFR 149.610 estimate for $1.00 each: the weekly therapy example, $8,000.00 for 12 months, works out the same way.

- SimplePractice from: $49 a month
- Here: $1.00 per estimate

## In SimplePractice

- From the client's Overview page, select New, then Good faith estimate; fill in the client, provider and service details, and save. Only team members with billing access for the client can create and send it, from the browser version.
- Open it and choose Share: it goes through the Client Portal for the client to sign. A signed estimate cannot be edited or deleted.
- The legal disclaimers appear when the client views the form in the portal, not in the clinician's view; SimplePractice suggests testing with a test client.

> From SimplePractice's help article "Adding a good faith estimate" and its pricing page, checked November 8, 2026. The help center refused our page reader, so its steps were read from SimplePractice's own search listing of the article; the pricing page was read directly. Its blog (March 30, 2023) also says the federal regulation has no provision for clients waiving the estimate.

## When SimplePractice is the better choice

If you already pay for SimplePractice, its form is inside the chart, fills the client from the record and delivers through the portal: that is less work than any standalone tool.

## When a standalone estimate is faster

- You have no EHR, or one without a GFE form.
- You want the due date worked out, or 12 months of sessions counted and totaled.
- Several providers or a facility go on one estimate.
- You need one estimate for a client you see outside the EHR.

## Questions

### How do I create a Good Faith Estimate in SimplePractice?

SimplePractice's help center says: from the client's Overview page choose New, then Good faith estimate, fill in the client, provider and service details and save; then share it through the Client Portal for the client to sign. It is available on all subscription plans and only from the browser version.

### Is a client signature required on a Good Faith Estimate?

Not by 45 CFR 149.610, which asks for a written estimate the patient can save and print. SimplePractice's help center also notes client signatures are not required on its form.
