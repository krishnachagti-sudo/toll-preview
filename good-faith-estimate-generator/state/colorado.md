# Good Faith Estimates in Colorado

In Colorado, unless a medical emergency or an unforeseen, medically necessary service occurs, a self-pay final bill generally may be no more than 15% or $400 above the estimate, whichever is less (C.R.S. 25-49-106(1)(b), as amended by HB25-1317, signed June 4, 2025). On a $2,000 estimate that is $2,300; above $2,666.67 the $400 is the lower figure. The federal Good Faith Estimate rules apply as well.

- Cap on $2,000: $2,300
- Signed: June 4, 2025

## What HB25-1317 changed

The bill corrected the statute's wording so that the $400 is a dollar amount above the self-pay estimate, not a maximum cost for the service, and removed "whichever is less" from the emergency exception. In the legislature's summary: "Existing law permits an individual to request a self-pay estimate of the total cost of an anticipated health-care service (self-pay estimate) from the designated billing or patient services personnel representing the health-care provider or health-care facility providing the service."

> The statute writes the amounts in words ("fifteen percent", and the dollar figure); shown here in figures. Each provider's own estimate is used for the cap; other parts of C.R.S. 25-49-106 were not read for this page.

## Questions

### How much can a Colorado self-pay bill exceed the estimate?

No more than 15% or $400 above the total estimated cost, whichever is less, unless a medical emergency or an additional, unforeseen, medically necessary service occurs (C.R.S. 25-49-106(1)(b), as amended by HB25-1317). The lesser of the two is $400 for any estimate above $2,666.67.

### Does Colorado replace the federal Good Faith Estimate?

No. The federal rule says an estimate issued under a state process that does not meet 45 CFR 149.610 fails to comply (149.610(f)(2)); a Colorado provider gives the federal estimate and is held to the state cap as well.
