## Every required element

- (c)(1)(i): Patient name and date of birth given.
- (c)(1)(ii): Primary service described, scheduled for December 2, 2026.
- (c)(1)(iii): 3 items, grouped by 1 provider.
- (c)(1)(iv): No diagnosis code: fine where none is required for the estimate, such as a first evaluation (CMS GFE FAQs Part 2 Q1).
- (c)(1)(v): Name, NPI (check digit valid), TIN and location for the provider.
- (c)(1)(vi): No separately scheduled items listed.
- (c)(1)(viii): Added in the CMS model notice's words (there may be additional items or services, scheduled or requested separately, not reflected in the estimate).
- (c)(1)(ix): Added in the CMS model notice's words (it is only an estimate, and actual items, services or charges may differ).
- (c)(1)(x): Added in the CMS model notice's words (the right to start patient-provider dispute resolution, where to find how, and that starting it will not adversely affect the quality of care).
- (c)(1)(xi): Added in the CMS model notice's words (the estimate is not a contract and does not require the patient to get the items or services from these providers).

## Questions

### Do dentists have to give Good Faith Estimates?

Yes. CMS says dental and vision providers and facilities are generally subject to the requirement to give GFEs to uninsured (or self-pay) individuals (GFE FAQs Part 5 Q3).

### Does a dental Good Faith Estimate need diagnosis codes?

Not where there is no relevant diagnosis code, such as for certain dental screenings or procedures; the service codes and expected charges are still required (CMS GFE FAQs Part 2 Q1).
