Every required element

  • (c)(1)(i): Patient name and date of birth given.
  • (c)(1)(ii): Primary service described, 52 dates from November 19, 2026 to November 11, 2027.
  • (c)(1)(iii): 2 items, grouped by 1 provider.
  • (c)(1)(iv): No diagnosis code: fine where none is required for the estimate, such as a first evaluation (CMS GFE FAQs Part 2 Q1).
  • (c)(1)(v): Name, NPI (check digit valid), TIN and location for the provider.
  • (c)(1)(vi): No separately scheduled items listed.
  • (c)(1)(viii): Added in the CMS model notice's words (there may be additional items or services, scheduled or requested separately, not reflected in the estimate).
  • (c)(1)(ix): Added in the CMS model notice's words (it is only an estimate, and actual items, services or charges may differ).
  • (c)(1)(x): Added in the CMS model notice's words (the right to start patient-provider dispute resolution, where to find how, and that starting it will not adversely affect the quality of care).
  • (c)(1)(xi): Added in the CMS model notice's words (the estimate is not a contract and does not require the patient to get the items or services from these providers).

Questions

Do therapists have to give a Good Faith Estimate to self-pay clients?

Yes, to clients who are uninsured or not using their insurance, on scheduling at least 3 business days ahead or on request (45 CFR 149.610(b)). CMS names periodic counseling as a recurring service one estimate can cover.

How do I write a Good Faith Estimate for weekly therapy when I do not know how many sessions?

Give the expected scope: the timeframe, the frequency and the total number of sessions (149.610(b)(1)(x)(A)), for up to 12 months. Weekly from November 19, 2026 is 52 sessions within 12 months of the estimate date.

Do I need a diagnosis code on a therapy Good Faith Estimate?

Only where one is required for the estimate; for a first evaluation with no diagnosis yet, CMS says it is not required, but the service codes and charges are (CMS GFE FAQs Part 2 Q1).
