# Good Faith Estimate requirements

A self-pay Good Faith Estimate must contain 10 elements under 45 CFR 149.610(c)(1) (paragraph (vii) is reserved), including each provider's NPI and TIN and four disclaimers, and it is due 1 business day after scheduling when the service is at least 3 business days away, 3 when at least 10 away or after a request. CMS's example: scheduled Monday, January 3, 2022 for Thursday, January 6, 2022, due Tuesday, January 4, 2022.

- Elements: 10
- Disclaimers: 4
- Recurring scope: 12 months

| 149.610(c)(1)(i) | Patient name and date of birth | You |

| 149.610(c)(1)(iii) | Itemized list of items or services, grouped by each provider or facility, for the period of care | You |
| 149.610(c)(1)(iv) | Applicable diagnosis codes, expected service codes and expected charges for each item | You |
| 149.610(c)(1)(v) | Name, NPI and TIN of each provider or facility, and the state and office or facility location | You |
| 149.610(c)(1)(vi) | Items expected to be scheduled separately, with the required disclaimer directly above the list | You list the items; the disclaimer is standard |

## The timing

149.610(b)(1)(vi); business days are Monday to Friday without federal holidays (CMS GFE FAQs Part 5 Q7)

| Situation | Due |
| --- | --- |
| Scheduled fewer than 3 business days ahead | Not required on scheduling (CMS GFE FAQs Part 2 Q5) |
| Scheduled 3 to 9 business days ahead | 1 business day after scheduling |
| Scheduled 10 or more business days ahead | 3 business days after scheduling |
| Requested by the patient | 3 business days after the request |
| Scope changes after it was given | A new one no later than 1 business day before the service (149.610(b)(1)(vii)) |

**Diagnosis codes.** Required only where one is needed for the estimate; service codes and charges are always needed (CMS GFE FAQs Part 2 Q1).

**Delivery.** In writing, on paper or electronically as the patient asks, savable and printable, in clear language (149.610(e)). An estimate given orally must also be given in writing.

**Errors.** A provider acting in good faith that makes an error is not out of compliance if it corrects it as soon as practicable (149.610(f)(3)).

## Steps

1. **Ask.** Ask whether the patient has coverage and whether they want a claim sent to it (149.610(b)(1)(i)-(ii)).
2. **Count.** Count business days from the scheduling date to the service: fewer than 3, no estimate is required on scheduling; 3 to 9, it is due in 1 business day; 10 or more, in 3.
3. **Fill.** Fill the 10 elements: patient, primary service and date, items by provider, codes and charges, each provider's NPI, TIN and location, separately scheduled items, and the four disclaimers.
4. **Deliver.** Give it in writing, on paper or electronically as the patient asks, in a form they can save and print (149.610(e)(1)).

## Questions

### Is a Good Faith Estimate required by law?

Yes, for uninsured or self-pay patients, under section 2799B-6 of the Public Health Service Act and 45 CFR 149.610, for items or services scheduled or requested on or after January 1, 2022 (149.610(g)(1)).

### When is a Good Faith Estimate due?

Not later than 1 business day after scheduling when the service is at least 3 business days away, 3 business days when it is at least 10 away, and 3 business days after a request (149.610(b)(1)(vi)). Business days are Monday through Friday, not including federal holidays, and it is due by 11:59 PM (CMS GFE FAQs Part 5 Q7).

### Does a question about cost count as a request?

Yes: the convening provider must treat any discussion or inquiry about the potential costs of items or services as a request for a good faith estimate (149.610(b)(1)(iv)).

### Can a client waive the Good Faith Estimate?

45 CFR 149.610 contains no waiver provision; we found no CMS statement allowing one.
