What are the next small business tax deadlines?

Dates are for the United States and run from today, November 9, 2026, for the current and the next tax year. A date that moved is marked, and the next section says why.

Which of these dates moved, and why?

When the date in the law or the instructions falls on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday, the deadline is the next day that is none of them. Each move below is worked out with the Business days holiday rules, not by hand.

  • Form 1099-MISC for 2026 payments: send to recipients: Monday, February 1, 2027. January 31, 2027 is a Sunday. A Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday moves the date to the next day that is none of them (26 U.S.C. 7503).
  • Form 1099-NEC for 2026 payments: send to contractors and file with the IRS: Monday, February 1, 2027. January 31, 2027 is a Sunday. A Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday moves the date to the next day that is none of them (26 U.S.C. 7503).
  • IFTA fuel tax return for the fourth quarter of 2026 (December 31): Monday, February 1, 2027. January 31, 2027 is a Sunday. A Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday moves the date to the next day that is none of them (26 U.S.C. 7503).
  • Form 1099-MISC with only box 8 or 10 for 2026: send to recipients: Tuesday, February 16, 2027. Monday, February 15, 2027 is Washington's Birthday. A Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday moves the date to the next day that is none of them (26 U.S.C. 7503).
  • Form 1099-MISC for 2026 payments: file with the IRS on paper: Monday, March 1, 2027. February 28, 2027 is a Sunday. A Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday moves the date to the next day that is none of them (26 U.S.C. 7503).
  • IFTA fuel tax return for the second quarter of 2027 (June 30): Monday, August 2, 2027. July 31, 2027 is a Saturday. A Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday moves the date to the next day that is none of them (26 U.S.C. 7503).
  • IFTA fuel tax return for the third quarter of 2027 (September 30): Monday, November 1, 2027. October 31, 2027 is a Sunday. A Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday moves the date to the next day that is none of them (26 U.S.C. 7503).
  • 2027 estimated tax payment 4 of 4 (Form 1040-ES): Tuesday, January 18, 2028. January 15, 2028 is a Saturday, and Monday, January 17, 2028 is Birthday of Martin Luther King Jr. A Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday moves the date to the next day that is none of them (26 U.S.C. 7503).
  • Federal individual income tax return for 2027 (Form 1040): Tuesday, April 18, 2028. April 15, 2028 is a Saturday, and Monday, April 17, 2028 is D.C. Emancipation Day (observed). A Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday moves the date to the next day that is none of them (26 U.S.C. 7503).

The rule: 26 U.S.C. 7503: Time for performance of acts where last day falls on Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday. A "legal holiday" there means one in the District of Columbia, which is why D.C. Emancipation Day counts (IRS Notice 2011-17: Effect of Emancipation Day on filing and payment deadlines).

Questions

What if a tax deadline falls on a weekend?

It moves to the next day that is not a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday. That is the rule in 26 U.S.C. 7503, and the IRS form instructions and the IFTA agencies say the same. This page and the calendar show the moved date, and say when a date moved and why.

Does a legal holiday move a tax deadline?

Yes. A legal holiday in the District of Columbia counts, so a federal holiday such as Washington's Birthday or Martin Luther King Jr. Day moves a date. So does D.C. Emancipation Day on April 16 (observed on the nearest weekday when it falls on a weekend), as in April 2028.

Are state tax deadlines on this calendar?

No. This calendar is federal only, plus the IFTA fuel tax return, which is filed with your base state or province. State income tax, sales tax and payroll deadlines vary by state and are not included.

Where does each date come from?

Every event names its source: the IRS instructions for Forms 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC, the IRS Form 1040-ES, the Internal Revenue Code, or the IFTA state agency guidance. Dates that move are worked out with the Business days holiday rules.

Is this tax advice?

No. It lists the due dates the sources publish. Your own dates can differ, for example with a fiscal year, a disaster-area extension or a filing extension, so check with your tax preparer.
