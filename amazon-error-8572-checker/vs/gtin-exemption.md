# GTIN exemption or fix the UPC?

If your product already carries a GS1 barcode, a GTIN exemption is not open to you (Amazon lists "Has an existing GS1-approved barcode" as ineligible), so fix the code's match instead: make the brand and GS1 record agree or prove the link; an exemption is the faster path only for a product with no barcode, where Amazon approves a brand owner automatically or within 48 hours, against $30 and a 72-hour wait for one new GS1 US GTIN.

## The two paths

Amazon's help and GS1 US, read November 8, 2026.

|  | GTIN exemption | Fix the UPC |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open to | Products with no product ID or GS1 barcode: private label, handmade, generic, bundles, parts | Any product with a GS1 GTIN |
| Closed to | Products with a GS1 barcode or GTIN; UPC-mandatory brands; products already on Amazon | Codes not licensed to the brand, unless the licensee gives a letter |
| Time | Automatic (brand owner or generic), 48 hours with brand name approval, then 30 minutes | New GS1 codes: wait 72 hours; a GS1 US legal name change: confirmation within 5 business days, then 3 business days to decide |
| Cost | None from Amazon | $30 per single GS1 US GTIN, or a prefix from $250 + $50 a year |
| Scope | Per store and category | Per code, every store and every retailer |
| Can lapse | Amazon may expire exemptions for brands that later get GS1 barcodes | A lapsed GS1 US prefix drops out of the GS1 company database |

## When the exemption is better

- A new private-label or handmade brand with no barcodes yet, selling only on Amazon.
- Generic, unbranded products.
- Replacement parts with no UPC.

## When fixing the UPC is better

- The product already has a GS1 barcode: the exemption is closed.
- You sell, or will sell, in other stores or retailers; GS1 US says its barcodes work across channels.
- Several Amazon stores or categories: one GTIN works in all of them.
