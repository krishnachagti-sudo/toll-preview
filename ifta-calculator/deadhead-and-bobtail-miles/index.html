Guide

Deadhead, bobtail and shop miles

Yes: IFTA counts every mile a qualified vehicle drives, loaded, empty (deadhead), bobtail or to the shop. In the worked example, leaving 300 empty Texas miles off the sheet drops fleet MPG from 6.30 to 5.87 and moves the return from a $28.73 credit to a $21.70 credit, and the odometer chain shows the gap at audit.

6.30

5.87

Do empty miles count on an IFTA return?

Yes. Total Distance in the IFTA Articles of Agreement (R266) is every mile every qualified vehicle drove in the quarter, taxable or not. That includes deadhead after a delivery, bobtail runs without a trailer, trips to the shop or the scale, and miles outside IFTA jurisdictions.

What leaving them off does

The fuel was still burned and is still in your total gallons, so missing miles lower your fleet MPG. A lower MPG turns every jurisdiction's miles into more taxable gallons, so the tax moves even in states you logged correctly.

3rd quarter 2026, diesel, 690 gallons; worked out by the calculator.

Here the error happens to cost money; with another route it could look like a saving. Either way the return is wrong, and Oregon DOT Form 9740 notes that "The average fleet MPGe is typically .50 through 20." A fleet MPG out of line with the truck invites questions.

How auditors find missing miles

They chain the odometer readings: the end of one trip sheet should be the start of the next. The calculator does the same on trip sheets. On its sample fleet it says: "Unit 102: 50 odometer miles between trip T3 and trip T4 are on no trip sheet. Log them (personal or bobtail miles still count) or explain them."

If records fall short, Oregon DOT Form 9740 warns that "At time of audit, failure to maintain adequate records may result in disallowance of tax-paid credits claimed on the IFTA tax returns, assessing fleet miles per gallon to 4.0 mpg, or both." At 4.0 MPG the same 4,350 miles would be 1,088 taxable gallons instead of 690.

Questions

Do deadhead miles count for IFTA?

Yes. IFTA Articles of Agreement R266 defines Total Distance as "all miles or kilometers traveled during the tax reporting period by every qualified vehicle in the licensee's fleet, regardless of whether the miles or kilometers are considered taxable or nontaxable by a jurisdiction."

Do bobtail and personal miles count?

If the tractor is a qualified motor vehicle under your license, its miles count. The calculator's odometer check flags miles between trip sheets and says personal or bobtail miles still count.

Are any miles left out of taxable miles?

Some jurisdictions exempt certain miles, such as off-road miles, from taxable miles; Oregon DOT Form 9740 says not to include off-road nontaxable miles or fuel tax permit miles in taxable miles. They still go in total miles.

What happens if miles are missing at audit?

Oregon DOT Form 9740: "At time of audit, failure to maintain adequate records may result in disallowance of tax-paid credits claimed on the IFTA tax returns, assessing fleet miles per gallon to 4.0 mpg, or both."
