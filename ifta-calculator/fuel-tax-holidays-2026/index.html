IFTA fuel tax holidays in 2026

A fuel tax holiday does not cancel your IFTA return: you still report every mile and gallon, and the holiday changes the rate or the taxable miles for that jurisdiction. In the 4th quarter of 2026 that means Ohio (October 4, 2026 to January 2, 2027, a split rate), Georgia (to November 5, 2026), North Carolina (October 10 to November 30) and Alberta (nil for the quarter).

How does a holiday change the return?

In one of two ways. Where the jurisdiction prints a new rate (Alberta, Indiana) or two rates in the quarter (Ohio), the miles are reported as usual and the rate does the work. Where it suspends the tax for some days (Georgia), its guidance has you take those days' miles out of taxable miles and the tax-free fuel out of tax-paid gallons.

Worked example: Georgia, September 29 and 30

A made-up 3rd quarter 2026 diesel return with 420 Georgia miles, 40 of them on September 29 and 30. Following Georgia's guidance, the 40 miles stay in total miles and come out of taxable miles.

Fleet MPG is the same in both (6.24): all miles and all gallons still count.

Dyed diesel relief orders

In September and October 2026 many jurisdictions waived penalties for dyed diesel on public roads, mostly for agricultural hauling. Most of these orders are not fuel tax holidays and say nothing about IFTA returns. Where a jurisdiction gave IFTA instructions, they differ:

  • Illinois (October 6 to December 31, 2026, qualifying agricultural hauling): all clear and dyed diesel in fleet MPG, all Illinois miles in total miles, qualifying relief-period miles out of taxable miles, only tax-paid clear diesel as tax-paid gallons.
  • Kentucky (from October 8, 2026): untaxed dyed diesel in total volume, reported as non-IFTA volume with the required comment; all highway miles stay taxable.
  • Virginia (October 16 to December 31, 2026): the penalty waiver requires the backup tax to be paid first; all Virginia distance is total and taxable, and dyed diesel with backup tax paid counts as tax-paid gallons.

What the calculator does with holidays

It uses the final IFTA matrix for each quarter, so a holiday printed as the quarter's rate (Alberta in the 4th quarter, Indiana gasoline in the 3rd) is in the numbers once that matrix is on file. It applies one rate per jurisdiction per quarter, so it does not split Ohio's 4th quarter rate; for Georgia-style suspensions, enter taxable miles separately from total miles (the API takes taxableMiles per row).

Questions

Do I still file IFTA during a fuel tax holiday?

Yes. Every notice from Ohio, Georgia, North Carolina, Alberta and Indiana posted by IFTA, Inc. says returns and mileage and fuel reporting are still required.

What is a split rate quarter?

A quarter in which a jurisdiction has two rates, because its tax changed partway through. Ohio says IFTA carriers will see a split rate for the 4th quarter 2026 and the 1st quarter 2027, because its holiday runs from October 4, 2026 to January 2, 2027.

How do I report Georgia miles during its suspension?

Georgia's filing guidance: include the suspension-period miles in total miles but not in taxable miles, and include the tax-free Georgia fuel in total gallons but not in tax-paid gallons.

Does the calculator handle split rates?

No. It applies one final rate per jurisdiction per quarter, so a rate that changes partway through a quarter has to be split by hand. For Georgia-style suspensions you can enter taxable miles separately from total miles, which it does handle.
