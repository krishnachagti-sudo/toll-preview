Fuel tax for interstate carriers

IFTA quarterly return calculator

Each jurisdiction's IFTA tax or credit for the quarter, at the final rates, surcharges included.

  • Made by Conyso
  • Exact, no AI
  • Official sources, dated
  • Files you upload are not kept
  • SecurityTerms

Free on this page · Prepared return from $5.00 a quarter · Re-runs free for 90 days

What you have

One row per jurisdiction a trip crossed. Rows with the same unit and trip id are one trip; the dates, places, route and odometer readings go on its first row.

Blank templates:

From an ELD or fuel card export

Most ELD IFTA reports list miles by unit and jurisdiction; copy them into the trip sheet columns with the trip's dates and odometer readings. Fuel card exports have the date, location, gallons and price of each purchase; paste them under the receipt headers. Fuel type is written as in the matrix: special_diesel, gasoline, propane and so on.

How to write the lines

One jurisdiction per line: its two-letter code, the miles, then the tax-paid gallons bought there (blank or 0 if none). Add a fourth number for taxable miles when some miles were not taxable. Use AK, HI, DC, YT, NT, NU or MX for miles outside IFTA. The gallons box is all the fuel put in the tanks this quarter, wherever it was bought, tax paid or not.

The prepared return (PDF and CSV)

Every error and missing field listed. One $20 top-up covers a year of returns for a small fleet; a preparer buys one return for each licensee and quarter.

Your entries stay in this browser. They are sent only when you prepare the return.

See a sample first: the return for a made-up two-truck fleet, made by this tool. The sample files load with the page.

How each line is worked out

One fleet MPG per fuel from all miles and all fuel; each jurisdiction's taxable miles divided by it, less the tax-paid gallons bought there, times the quarter's rate. A negative line is a credit, and surcharges are owed with no credit. From a trip sheet, it also checks that the odometer readings join up and that every trip and receipt has the fields an auditor asks for.

MPG, taxable gallons and surcharges

One fleet MPG per fuel

All miles, inside and outside IFTA, divided by all the fuel put in the tanks, carried to three decimals and rounded to two. Miles and gallons are rounded to whole numbers first. This is the method printed on Oregon DOT Form 9740; your base jurisdiction's form may round differently.

Taxable gallons, less what you paid

Each jurisdiction's taxable miles divided by that MPG, rounded, less the tax-paid gallons you bought there, times the quarter's rate. A negative line is a credit. Fuel bought in Alaska, Hawaii, D.C. or Mexico counts toward MPG but earns no credit.

Surcharges, with no credit

Indiana, Kentucky and Virginia print surcharge rows in the matrix: in the quarters on file, Kentucky's and Virginia's cover diesel, gasoline and most other fuels, Indiana's only propane. The surcharge is taxable gallons times that rate, owed even when you bought all your fuel there. Rows are read from the matrix each quarter, never assumed.

Due dates

A return is due on the last day of the month after the quarter, moved to the next business day when that is a weekend or legal holiday (Virginia DMV). A return is due even with no miles. Late: interest on each line with tax due for every month or part of a month (0.75% a month for 2025 and 2026), and a penalty of $50 or 10% of the net tax, whichever is greater.

Please read
  • Computed from rates published by IFTA member jurisdictions in the IFTA tax rate matrix, as copied by Oregon DOT and New York State Tax (final rates, sources listed); not affiliated with IFTA, Inc. Your base jurisdiction's return governs.
  • This is a calculation, not tax or legal advice; keep your original trip records and receipts for 4 years from the due or filing date, whichever is later.
  • Record checks compare your records with the IFTA distance and fuel record elements; passing them does not guarantee an audit outcome.
  • Rounding follows Oregon DOT Form 9740 (whole miles and gallons, MPG to two decimals); penalty and interest use the general IFTA rules. Your base jurisdiction may compute or round differently.
Sources, read November 2, 2026
  • IFTA tax rate matrix, final rates, as copied by Oregon DOT: 4Q2025, 1Q2026, 2Q2026 and 3Q2026 (all 15 fuel types)
  • New York State Tax IFTA-105 rate tables, used as the cross-check: 4Q2025, 1Q2026, 2Q2026 and 3Q2026
  • Oregon DOT Form 735-9740 and its instructions (03-26): the column-by-column method, rounding, surcharge, interest and penalty, qualified vehicles
  • Virginia DMV, IFTA tax returns: due dates and the next-business-day rule
  • Idaho State Tax Commission, IFTA interest rates and IFTA records (IVDR): the distance and fuel record elements
  • Oregon DOT, Interstate Operations IFTA: Oregon's weight-mile tax report
Prices

Estimates from totals and the trip sheet check on this page are free. The prepared return (PDF and CSV, every error and missing field listed) is $5.00 for 1 to 5 vehicles, $9.00 for 6 to 25 and $15.00 for 26 or more, per licensee and quarter, with re-runs free for 90 days.

  • Return, 1 to 5 vehicles: $5.00 per quarterly return (all fuel types, PDF and CSV); a repeat for the same licensee and quarter is free for 90 days, and a larger one pays only the difference.
  • Return, 6 to 25 vehicles: $9.00 per quarterly return (all fuel types, PDF and CSV); a repeat for the same licensee and quarter is free for 90 days, and a larger one pays only the difference.
  • Return, 26 or more vehicles: $15.00 per quarterly return (all fuel types, PDF and CSV); a repeat for the same licensee and quarter is free for 90 days, and a larger one pays only the difference.
  • Estimate from totals: $0.03 per estimate.
  • Trip sheet check: $0.03 per check; errors, warnings and missing fields counted, not listed.
  • Late cost: $0.03 per calculation.
  • Rates lookup: $0.01 per lookup.
  • Due dates: $0.01 per lookup.

What this tool does not cover

  • Quarters before the 4th quarter of 2025, and the 4th quarter of 2026 until its matrix is published: the tool refuses them rather than guess.
  • LNG, CNG, electricity, hydrogen and hythane: the matrix copies do not say which unit their rates apply to, and the footnotes that do are on iftach.org, which could not be read.
  • Florida gasoline, diesel, gasohol and biodiesel for the 1st quarter of 2026: the Oregon and New York copies of the matrix disagree, so those lines are refused.
  • Fuels a jurisdiction shows no rate for ("-" in the matrix), except Oregon, which has no IFTA rate for any fuel; Oregon's weight-mile tax is a separate report.
  • Filing or paying the return: that is done in your base jurisdiction's portal.
