Exchange rate on a date

The official rate for a currency pair on any day since January 1999, as published by the European Central Bank. If the ECB did not publish on that day, you get the last fixing before it, and the page tells you which day that was.

Each run costs a fraction of a cent. Without an account you get 5 free runs a day.

From 4 January 1999. Weekends and bank holidays use the last fixing before them.

Where the number comes from

One fixing a day

The ECB sets reference rates for about 30 currencies against the euro at about 16:00 Central European Time on every TARGET working day. Those are the rates in the ECB's own tables, and the ones many accounts and tax offices use.

Pairs without the euro

For two other currencies the page divides one euro rate by the other, on the same day's fixing, then rounds to 6 decimal places. The rounding is exact, with halves rounded away from zero, and the inverse is worked out from the unrounded rate.

Days with no fixing

Weekends and TARGET holidays have no fixing. The tool uses the latest fixing up to 7 days before the date you ask for. If there is none in that window, or the date is before 4 January 1999, it says so and you are not charged.

A working day the ECB has not published yet, such as today before the 16:00 fixing, gets no rate rather than an older one. Ask again after the fixing. That run is free too.

Source: European Central Bank euro foreign exchange reference rates. These are reference rates, not rates you can trade at. Currencies the ECB no longer publishes, such as the Croatian kuna, can still be asked for through the API on dates when it did.
