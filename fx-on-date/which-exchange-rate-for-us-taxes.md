# Which exchange rate to use for US taxes and the FBAR

For a 2025 FBAR, use the Treasury Reporting Rate for 31 December 2025 (0.851 euros per US dollar); for income and expenses on a US tax return, the IRS says to use the exchange rate prevailing when you receive, pay or accrue the item.

## What the agencies say

The IRS: you must report amounts on a US tax return in US dollars. In general, use the exchange rate prevailing (the spot rate) when you receive, pay or accrue the item. It has no official exchange rate and generally accepts any posted exchange rate that is used consistently. To convert with its yearly average table, divide the foreign amount by the rate.

FinCEN, for the FBAR: convert the maximum account value using the Treasury rate for the last day of the calendar year. If no Treasury rate is available, use another verifiable exchange rate and give its source. Record amounts rounded up to the next whole dollar. An FBAR must be filed when one account, or the maximum values of all accounts together, is more than $10,000.

## Questions

### Does the IRS have an official exchange rate?

No. The IRS says it has no official exchange rate and generally accepts any posted exchange rate that is used consistently.

### What rate do I use for a 2025 FBAR?

The Treasury Reporting Rate for 31 December 2025; for the euro that is 0.851 euros per US dollar. If Treasury has no rate for a currency, FinCEN says to use another verifiable rate and give its source.
