# USPS's APV dispute webform vs a prepared packet

USPS's APV dispute webform is the only place to file, free, within 60 days of the notice (a notice dated October 28, 2026 closes on December 27, 2026); it asks for the Revenue Assurance ID, the last 4 digits of the tracking number and a description, but it does not price the adjustment. The checker works out that USPS Ground Advantage at 14 x 12 x 11 in should cost $10.01, or $13.01 with the $3.00 Dimension Noncompliance Fee more than the label, and writes the description.

## Side by side

|  | USPS APV webform | This checker |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Files the dispute | Yes, the only way to file (since October 23, 2024) | No: you paste its text into the webform |
| Price | Free | Check free on the web; packet $0.49 per adjustment |
| Works out the right price | No | Yes, at Notice 123 commercial prices with the DMM rule for the ship date |
| Deadline | Refuses disputes over 60 days | Gives the last day (60 days from the notice) and days left |
| Evidence | USPS may review scan data and images; more evidence to apvsupport@usps.gov | A PDF with the worksheet and your photos |
| Status | From your PC Postage provider after 15 business days | Not tracked |

## When USPS's webform alone is better

Always file there: USPS is the authority and the webform is the place to file. If the reason is simple (a wrong weight you can see on your own scale), the webform alone is enough; this checker only prepares the numbers and the text.

## What the webform asks for

- Shipper's first and last name
- Shipper's email address
- Shipper's telephone number with area code
- Revenue Assurance ID / USPS Adjustment ID number
- USPS tracking number / IMpb (the webform asks for the last 4 digits of the IMpb)
- Reason (weight, package, zone, dimensions)
- Description of dispute
