# How to dispute a USPS APV adjustment

File within 60 days of the notice on USPS's webform with the Revenue Assurance ID, the last 4 digits of the tracking number and a description that prices the parcel both ways; for a notice dated October 28, 2026 the last day is December 27, 2026, and in the sample below the amount to dispute is $13.01.

## A sample description

Made-up ID and tracking number; real Notice 123 prices:

```
Revenue Assurance ID 1234567890, tracking 9400100000000000009021. Reason: Dimensions. The label was bought as USPS Ground Advantage, zone 5, 3 lb, 12 x 10 x 8 in (3 lb). The adjustment uses USPS Ground Advantage, 3 lb, 14 x 12 x 11 in (14 lb). I measured and weighed the parcel again: 12 x 10 x 8 in, 3 lb. At 12 x 10 x 8 in (960 cubic inches) it is not over 1,728 cubic inches, so no dimensional weight applies (DMM 283.1.4). At these measurements it prices at 3 lb, no more than the label, so no adjustment is owed. Please reverse the $13.01 adjustment.
```

555 characters. The packet writes this for your own adjustment, with a PDF of the worksheet and your photos ($0.49 per adjustment).

## Please read

- Not legal advice. USPS decides every APV dispute; a packet does not guarantee a refund.
- Prices are USPS Notice 123 commercial prices for the label's ship date. eBay, Etsy, Pirate Ship and other platforms may charge different rates, so the tool compares the adjustment (the difference), not label totals.
- The zone and USPS's measurements come from your adjustment notice; the tool does not read your account or USPS's scan data.
- Photos you add are processed in memory to build the PDF and are not kept.
- Rules and prices are as read on the dates shown. USPS changes prices next on January 17, 2027; labels from that date are refused until the new Notice 123 is loaded.

## Steps

1. **Find the ID.** Find the Revenue Assurance ID (the USPS Adjustment ID) and the tracking number on your platform's adjustment notice.
2. **Collect evidence.** Weigh the parcel again and measure each side; photograph the box with a ruler or tape and on the scale.
3. **Price both versions.** Price the label as bought and at USPS's measurements (and at yours) with the DMM rule for the ship date.
4. **Write the description.** State the ID, the reason, both measurements, the rule and the amount to refund.
5. **File.** File on USPS's webform (https://apvdisputes.usps.com/) with the ID, the last 4 digits of the tracking number, your contact details and the reason.
6. **Track it.** Check the status with your platform after 15 business days; USPS says most answers take 2-5 business days for most responses, at most 15 business days.
