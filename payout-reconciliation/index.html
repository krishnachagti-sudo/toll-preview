How it splits

By the processor's own labels, proved to the cent.

A payout is a net figure: sales less refunds, chargebacks, fees and reserves, often across several days. Each line is placed, checked and booked the same way.

What it does not cover

Accounting or tax advice, sales tax the payout file does not carry, and posting anything to your books.

Read the full list
  • Files are processed in memory and not kept.
  • This is arithmetic on your processor's own file, placed by the categories and types the processor documents. It is not accounting or tax advice.
  • Stripe, Shopify Payments and Square payout files carry no sales tax collected; PayPal's Sales Tax column is used where it is filled in. Book sales tax from your store's sales report.
  • Check the account names against your chart of accounts before you import, and skip the bank transfer journal if your bank feed already records the deposit.

By the processor's own labels

Each row is placed by the category or type the processor documents. A label it does not have is named, never guessed.

Read the full rules

Each row is placed by the category or type the processor documents: Stripe's reporting_category, Shopify Payments' transaction type, PayPal's T-code description and Square's activity type. A sale's fee goes to fees, so sales are booked gross. A label the processor's list does not have is booked as an other adjustment and named, never guessed.

Proved to the cent

Gross less fee equals net on every row, and a payout's rows add up to its deposit. A gap is its own line.

Read the full rules

Every row's gross less its fee must equal its net, and the rows of a payout must add up to the deposit when the file or you give it. PayPal's running balance must run from the first row to the last. Any gap is shown as its own line, not forced into a total.

A clearing account that returns to zero

The journal books everything against a clearing account that holds exactly the payout until the deposit lands.

Read the full rules

The journal books sales, refunds, chargebacks and fees against a clearing account for the processor, so the clearing account holds exactly the payout. When the deposit lands, matching it from the clearing account to the bank brings the clearing account back to zero.
