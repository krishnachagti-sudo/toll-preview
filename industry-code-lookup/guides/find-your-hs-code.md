# How to find your HS code

Work from the goods to the code in six steps, applying the General Rules of Interpretation: for cotton T-shirts that is chapter 61, heading 6109, subheading 6109.10 and, for a US import, 6109.10.00.12 at a general duty of 16.5%.

- Example: 6109.10.00.12

## Start with the words

## The rules in short

1. Rule 1: titles are for reference; classify by the terms of the headings and the section and chapter notes.
2. Rule 2: an incomplete or unassembled article counts as the article if it has its essential character; a material includes mixtures of it.
3. Rule 3: the most specific heading wins; else the material or part that gives the essential character; else the heading that comes last.
4. Rule 4: goods no rule places go with the goods they are most akin to.
5. Rule 5: fitted cases and normal packing go with the goods.
6. Rule 6: subheadings follow the same rules, compared only at the same level.

## Common mistakes

Stopping at 6 digits for a US entry, using an export number for an import, or keeping a code after a revision; see [tariff code mistakes](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/industry-code-lookup/guides/tariff-code-mistakes/).

> Reference, not a customs ruling or tax advice: a code that exists may still be the wrong one for your goods or business. Only U.S. Customs and Border Protection (by a binding ruling) decides an import classification, and the IRS decides what goes on a return. Each answer names the official table it came from and that table's date; check the official source before you file.

## Steps

1. **Describe the goods.** Write what the item is, what it is made of (by weight), how it is made (knitted or woven, for clothing) and what it is used for.
2. **Find the chapter.** Search the HTS or HS for the words that name the goods. Cotton T-shirts are knitted apparel: chapter 61, Articles of apparel and clothing accessories, knitted or crocheted.
3. **Pick the heading by its terms.** General Rule of Interpretation 1: classification follows the terms of the headings and the section and chapter notes. Heading 6109 is T-shirts, singlets, tank tops and similar garments, knitted or crocheted.
4. **Pick the subheading.** Rule 6: compare only subheadings at the same level. Of cotton gives 6109.10.
5. **Add the national digits.** For a US import, the 10-digit HTS number for men's T-shirts of cotton is 6109.10.00.12, general duty 16.5%.
6. **Check and keep a record.** Check the number exists in the current edition and write down why you chose it. For certainty, ask CBP for a binding ruling.

## Questions

### What are the General Rules of Interpretation?

Six rules at the front of the tariff that decide classification: headings and notes first (rule 1), incomplete and mixed goods (rule 2), the most specific heading or essential character (rule 3), the most akin goods (rule 4), cases and packing (rule 5) and subheadings (rule 6).

### Can I get an official answer?

Yes: a binding ruling from U.S. Customs and Border Protection decides the classification of your goods for a US import.
