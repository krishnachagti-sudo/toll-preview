Schedule B vs HTS

A Schedule B number (Census, 9,779 numbers in 2026) classifies a US export and an HTS number (USITC, 19,831 statistical numbers in chapters 1 to 97) a US import; both are 10 digits built on the same 6-digit HS code, and 6,004 numbers are identical in both, such as 6101.90.0500.

Which one to use

  • Exporting from the US: a Schedule B number; EEI is not needed for an item worth $2,500 or less under one number to one consignee (15 CFR 30.37(a)).
  • Importing into the US: an HTS number, 10 digits.
  • A foreign customs form: that country's code; its first 6 digits are the same HS code.

Reference, not a customs ruling or tax advice: a code that exists may still be the wrong one for your goods or business. Only U.S. Customs and Border Protection (by a binding ruling) decides an import classification, and the IRS decides what goes on a return. Each answer names the official table it came from and that table's date; check the official source before you file.

Questions

What is the difference between a Schedule B number and an HTS code?

Schedule B numbers (Census) classify US exports and HTS codes (USITC) classify US imports; both are 10 digits built on the 6-digit HS code.

Can I put an HTS code on an export filing?

For EEI the regulation names both: a Schedule B number or an HTSUSA commodity classification code (15 CFR 30.37).

Do they share digits?

The first 6 always come from the HS. 6,004 Schedule B numbers are the same 10 digits as an HTS number.
