Each row counts in the year of its own date: a December sale whose refund comes in January is a sale in one year and a refund in the next. The flattener does not move them.

## What the totals are, and are not

- Gross is eBay's Gross transaction amount on the sale rows.
- Fees are Gross minus Net on the sale rows; ad fees and other fees, labels, refunds and claims are their own rows.
- Kept is the sum of eBay's Net amount, plus labels you bought outside eBay if you add them.
- Payouts, transfers, holds and charges move money you already earned and are left out.
- These are records, not a tax return: what is income and what is deductible is for you and a tax professional.

For the 1099-K itself and why it is higher than what you kept, see [eBay's Form 1099-K, box by box](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/1099-k-reconciliation/ebay/) and [Why a 1099-K is higher than income](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/1099-k-reconciliation/guides/1099-k-higher-than-income/).

## Questions

### When does eBay send a Form 1099-K?

The IRS says online marketplaces must report payments when the total for goods or services exceeds $20,000 in more than 200 transactions (IRS, read November 8, 2026). eBay's help says its 1099-K detailed report gives a breakdown of all unadjusted gross payment transactions on the form.

### Which eBay report should I use for taxes?

The transaction report has every row for the dates you choose, with fees, labels and refunds, so yearly totals can be built from it. eBay's monthly financial statement (PDF) and Form 1099-K are other records. What goes on your return is a question for a tax professional.
