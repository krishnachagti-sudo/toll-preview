# What happens if a landlord misses the deposit deadline

A landlord who misses the deadline usually loses the right to keep anything for damage, and in most covered states owes a multiple on top. On a $2,000 deposit with $800 kept, the statutory maximum is $2,500.00 in Texas ($100 plus three times the amount withheld), $2,400.00 in Georgia, $4,000.00 in California and $1,600.00 in Arizona, before attorney's fees where the statute adds them.

## What the statutes say

**Four statutes in their own words**

- **Texas**: "A landlord who fails either to return a security deposit or to provide a written description and itemization of deductions on or before the 30th day after the date the tenant surrenders possession is presumed to have acted in bad faith."
- **Georgia**: "The failure of a landlord to provide the lists and written statements within the time periods specified in Code Section 44-7-34 shall work a forfeiture of all the landlord's rights to withhold any portion of the security deposit or to bring an action against the tenant for damages to the premises."
- **Michigan**: "Failure by the landlord to comply with the notice of damages requirement within the 30 days after the termination of occupancy, constitutes agreement by the landlord that no damages are due and he shall remit to the tenant immediately the full security deposit."
- **Washington**: "If the landlord fails to give the statement and any documentation required by subsection (1) of this section together with any refund due the tenant within the time limits specified in subsection (1) of this section he or she shall be liable to the tenant for the full amount of the deposit."

> North Carolina's remedies section (G.S. 42-55) was not read, so no figure is given for it.

## For tenants: what next?

- Write to the landlord with the move-out date, the statute and the date the statement was due.
- Keep proof of your forwarding address: in Texas, Ohio, Michigan and Pennsylvania the statute ties some rights to it.
- If it is not paid, small claims court is where most deposit cases go; check your court's limits.

## Questions

### Can a landlord keep part of the deposit after missing the deadline?

In New York, Pennsylvania, Georgia, Florida and Michigan, the statute says a late statement or notice forfeits the right to withhold for damages. New York: "If a landlord fails to provide the tenant with the statement and deposit within fourteen days, the landlord shall forfeit any right to retain any portion of the deposit."

### Is the penalty automatic?

No. These are the most a tenant can recover under each statute; several apply only to bad faith or willful conduct, and a court decides. Texas presumes bad faith when the landlord misses the 30th day.

### What should a landlord do once the deadline has passed?

Send the statement and any refund at once, and in a forfeiture state stop deducting for damage: the statutes quoted here tie the right to withhold to meeting the deadline.
