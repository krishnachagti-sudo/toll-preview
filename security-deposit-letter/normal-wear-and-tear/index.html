Normal wear and tear and the security deposit

A landlord may not keep any of the deposit for normal wear and tear: Texas, New York and North Carolina say so in those words, and New York limits deductions to 4 kinds (unpaid rent, damage beyond normal wear and tear, utilities owed to the landlord, and moving and storing the tenant's belongings). Ordinary wear from living in the unit is the landlord's cost; damage beyond it can be deducted at a reasonable cost.

What the statutes say

  • Texas: "The landlord may not retain any portion of a security deposit to cover normal wear and tear."
  • New York: "The landlord may not retain any amount of the deposit for costs relating to ordinary wear and tear of occupancy or damage caused by a prior tenant."
  • North Carolina: "The landlord may not withhold as damages part of the security deposit for conditions that are due to normal wear and tear nor may the landlord retain an amount from the security deposit which exceeds his actual damages."
  • Washington: no withholding "For carpet cleaning unless the landlord documents wear to the carpet that is beyond wear resulting from ordinary use of the premises".

A New York check

A $1,800.00 deposit with a cleaning charge and an administrative fee. The tool's check:

  • Deduction 1 (Professional cleaning): the statute lists rent, damage beyond normal wear and tear, utilities payable to the landlord and moving and storage of belongings; cleaning is kept only as damage beyond normal wear and tear. (§ 7-108(1-a)(b))
  • Deduction 2 (Admin fee): the statute allows only rent, damage beyond normal wear and tear, utilities payable directly to the landlord and moving and storage of belongings. (§ 7-108(1-a)(b))

What is wear and what is damage?

The statutes quoted here do not list examples. Whether a mark, a worn carpet or faded paint is wear or damage is a question of fact you decide and a court can review; the tool does not decide it. It checks that each deduction has the documents and words the statute asks for.

Questions

Can a landlord deduct for normal wear and tear?

Not in Texas, New York or North Carolina. Texas: "The landlord may not retain any portion of a security deposit to cover normal wear and tear."

What can a New York landlord deduct?

"The entire amount of the deposit or advance shall be refundable to the tenant upon the tenant's vacating of the premises except for an amount lawfully retained for the reasonable and itemized costs due to non-payment of rent, damage caused by the tenant beyond normal wear and tear, non-payment of utility charges payable directly to the landlord under the terms of the lease or tenancy, and moving and storage of the tenant's belongings."

Can a Washington landlord charge for carpet cleaning?

Not as a matter of course: RCW 59.18.280 bars withholding "For carpet cleaning unless the landlord documents wear to the carpet that is beyond wear resulting from ordinary use of the premises".
