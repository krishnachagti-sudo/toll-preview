Fla. Stat. § 83.49(3)

Florida security deposit deadline

In Florida a landlord who keeps nothing returns the deposit within 15 days after the rental agreement ends; to keep any of it, the landlord sends a notice of intention to impose a claim by certified mail within 30 days, then pays the rest 30 days after the notice, so a July 31, 2026 move-out means a notice by August 30, 2026 (Fla. Stat. § 83.49(3)).

What the statute says

§ 83.49(3)(a): "If the landlord intends to impose a claim on the deposit, the landlord must, within 30 days after the termination of the rental agreement, provide the tenant written notice by certified mail to the tenant's last known mailing address or by e-mail in accordance with s. 83.505 of his or her intention to impose a claim on the deposit and the reason for imposing the claim."

Read the rest: What the statute says

§ 83.49(3)(a): "Upon the vacating of the premises for termination of the rental agreement, if the landlord does not intend to impose a claim on the security deposit, the landlord must return the security deposit, together with interest if otherwise required, within 15 days after the termination of the rental agreement."

§ 83.49(3)(b): "Unless the tenant objects to the imposition of the landlord's claim or the amount thereof within 15 days after receipt of the landlord's notice of intention to impose a claim, the landlord may then deduct the amount of his or her claim and shall remit the balance of the deposit to the tenant within 30 days after the date of the notice of intention to impose a claim for damages."

The sample move-out

  • August 30, 2026: Send the notice of intention to impose a claim (§ 83.49(3)(a))
  • September 29, 2026: Pay the tenant the rest of the deposit (30 days after the date of the notice, counted from the last day to send it) (§ 83.49(3)(b))
  • The tenant has 15 days after receiving the notice to object in writing.
  • The statute does not say that mailing on the last day is enough, so plan for the tenant to have it by the due date.
  • The due date falls on a Sunday. The statute does not move it to the next business day, and neither does this tool.

Delivery

The notice of intention to impose a claim goes by certified mail to the tenant's last known mailing address, or by e-mail only if both sides signed the § 83.505 e-mail addendum. Keep a copy of an e-mailed notice and evidence it was sent.

If the deadline is missed

A late notice forfeits the right to impose a claim on the deposit (a separate suit for damages stays open); the winner of a deposit suit recovers court costs and attorney's fees (§ 83.49(3)(a), (c)).

"If the landlord fails to give the required written notice within the 30-day period, he or she forfeits the right to impose a claim upon the security deposit and may not seek a setoff against the deposit but may file an action for damages after returning the security deposit to the tenant."

"If either party institutes an action in a court of competent jurisdiction to adjudicate the party's right to the security deposit, the prevailing party is entitled to receive his or her court costs plus a reasonable fee for his or her attorney."

Questions

How long does a landlord have to return a security deposit in Florida?

Fifteen days after the rental agreement ends when nothing is kept. To keep any part, the landlord must send the claim notice within 30 days and pay the balance within 30 days after the notice.

What happens if a Florida landlord misses the deadline?

A late notice forfeits the right to impose a claim on the deposit (a separate suit for damages stays open); the winner of a deposit suit recovers court costs and attorney's fees (§ 83.49(3)(a), (c)).

How must the Florida deposit statement be sent?

The notice of intention to impose a claim goes by certified mail to the tenant's last known mailing address, or by e-mail only if both sides signed the § 83.505 e-mail addendum. Keep a copy of an e-mailed notice and evidence it was sent.
