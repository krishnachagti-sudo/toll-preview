Terms.law and Endwise for a security deposit

Terms.law's calculator covers 8 states and DC and works out interest, the return deadline and potential penalties, with paid attorney-written letters on the side. Endwise covers 13 states, works out the due date from the trigger each statute names (for a July 31, 2026 California move-out, August 21, 2026), checks each deduction and makes a dated itemized letter.

When is Terms.law the better choice?

  • You are in New Jersey, Massachusetts, Maryland, Connecticut or DC, which the deposit tool here does not cover.
  • You need deposit interest worked out from the official rate.
  • You are a tenant who wants a lawyer to write the demand letter.

When is Endwise the better choice?

  • You are a landlord who needs the itemized statement out on time, with each deduction checked against the statute.
  • You are in Texas, Florida, Pennsylvania, Ohio, Georgia, North Carolina, Michigan, Virginia, Washington or Arizona.
  • You want the due date from code or an AI assistant: Endwise answers over REST and MCP.

Questions

Which states does the Terms.law calculator cover?

Its page lists 8 states and DC: California, New York, New Jersey, Massachusetts, Maryland, Illinois, Connecticut and Washington DC.

Does Endwise calculate deposit interest?

It takes the interest owed as an input and adds it to the amount held; it does not look up interest rates.
