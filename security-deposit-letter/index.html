The states

The clock each statute starts, not move-out plus a number.

Each state's days are counted from its own trigger, skipping weekends and holidays only where the statute says to.

What it does not cover

Legal advice, city ordinances, what counts as normal wear and tear, and states without a row.

Read the full list
  • Not legal advice. We are not a law firm, and this tool does not review your case.
  • Rules are as of the statute reading date shown on each result; the rule version is printed on every letter and proof record.
  • Check that the state statute applies to your rental (unit counts, exemptions, local adoption) and whether a city ordinance adds rules; city rules were not checked.
  • The due date depends on the dates you enter (move-out, end of tenancy, the tenant's demand or forwarding address). Wrong dates give a wrong due date.
  • You decide what is damage beyond normal wear and tear and whether a cost is reasonable; the tool only checks that the statute's required documents and words are there.
  • Penalty figures are the statute's maximums on the amounts you entered. Bad faith, willfulness and actual awards are for a court.

The trigger differs

The move-out, the end of the tenancy, the tenant's demand or an abandonment, by state.

Read the full rules

California counts from the day the tenant vacated, Pennsylvania from the earlier of the lease ending and surrender, Ohio, North Carolina, Virginia and Washington from the later of the tenancy ending and the tenant leaving, and Arizona only once the tenancy has ended, possession is back and the tenant has demanded the deposit. Washington's clock runs from the day you learned of an abandonment.

Business days only where written

Arizona skips weekends and its legal holidays; everywhere else counts calendar days.

Read the full rules

Arizona's 14 days exclude Saturdays, Sundays and the legal holidays of A.R.S. § 1-301, including Columbus Day. Everywhere else the statute counts calendar days, and a deadline on a weekend is not moved, because none of these statutes says it is.

Mailing is not always enough

Texas, Washington and Georgia count mailing; elsewhere plan for the tenant to have it.

Read the full rules

Texas presumes a refund on time when postmarked by the due date; Washington and Georgia count first-class mailing. The other statutes do not say, so plan for the tenant to have it by the due date.
