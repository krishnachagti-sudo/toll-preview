## How this is counted

Tax Day is the due date of a calendar-year individual federal income tax return (Form 1040): the 15th day of the 4th month after the year ends, so April 15. When April 15 falls on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday, the due date is the next day that is none of those.

IRS Publication 509 says "legal holiday" means any legal holiday in the District of Columbia. That includes DC Emancipation Day on April 16, observed on the Friday before when it falls on a Saturday and on the Monday after when it falls on a Sunday. The date above applies both the federal holidays and that day. A filing extension (Form 4868) moves the filing date, not the date tax is due.

Weekends are Saturday and Sunday, and the holidays are those on [the US federal holidays 2026 page](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/business-days/us-federal-holidays-2026/). State, company and carrier holidays are not included: add them as your own days off in the [calculator](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/business-days/).

## Questions

### Is Tax Day always April 15?

No. When April 15 falls on a Saturday, Sunday or a legal holiday in the District of Columbia, including DC Emancipation Day, the due date is the next day that is not one of those. This page works the date out each year from the holiday rules.

### Does the count include today?

No. Day 1 is the next business day after today, the way WORKDAY and NETWORKDAYS count from a start date. The date counted to is included when it is a business day.
