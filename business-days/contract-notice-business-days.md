# How to count business days in a contract notice period

Count from the day after notice is given, skip weekends and the holidays your contract names, and end on the last counted day: 10 business days after notice given on Tuesday, December 22, 2026 ends on Thursday, January 7, 2027 with US federal holidays. If the contract instead counts the notice day itself, the period ends a business day earlier, on Wednesday, January 6, 2027.

## Does the notice day count?

Court rules such as Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 6(a) exclude the day of the event and count from the next day, and many contracts are read the same way. If yours says "within 10 business days of the date of notice" and nothing more, read it with the notice day excluded, and write the end date into the notice so nobody has to guess.

## One notice, counted four ways

| Method | Period ends | Days skipped |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10 business days, US federal holidays, notice day excluded | Thu, Jan 7, 2027 | Christmas Day and New Year's Day |
| 10 business days, England and Wales bank holidays | Fri 8 Jan 2027 | Christmas Day, Boxing Day and New Year's Day |
| 10 business days, notice day counted as day 1 (US) | Wed, Jan 6, 2027 | Weekends and the same holidays |
| 10 days under FRCP 6(a)(1) (calendar days, last day moved off a weekend or holiday) | Mon, Jan 4, 2027 | Fri, Jan 1, 2027 is not a business day |

Notice given on Tuesday, December 22, 2026. The same "10 days" ends anywhere from Mon, Jan 4, 2027 to Fri, Jan 8, 2027 depending on the method, which is why the definition matters.

## What does the federal court rule say?

Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 6(a)(1), for periods stated in days: exclude the day of the event that triggers the period, count every day including intermediate Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays, and include the last day, but if the last day is a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday, the period runs until the end of the next day that is not ([Rule 6](https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frcp/rule_6)). Its legal holidays are the federal ones in 5 U.S.C. 6103, any day declared a holiday by the President or Congress, and, for periods measured after an event, state holidays where the court sits.

## Drafting a business-day definition

- Name the weekdays: "Monday to Friday".
- Name the holiday calendar: for example, US federal holidays, or bank holidays in England and Wales.
- Say whose location applies when the parties are in different places.
- Say whether the notice day counts, and what time a business day ends.

> This page explains counting methods; it is not legal advice. Court rules, statutes and your contract decide a real deadline.

## Steps

1. **Read the definition.** Find how the contract defines a business day: which weekdays, which holiday calendar, and whose location.
2. **Find day zero.** Note the day notice is given or received, as the contract says. Most drafting excludes that day; check yours.
3. **Add the business days.** Add the number of business days in the tool, with the region that matches the named holiday calendar and any extra days off the contract names.
4. **Write the date down.** State the resulting date in the notice itself, so both sides work to the same day.

## Questions

### Does the day notice is given count as day 1?

Only if the contract says so. The federal courts' rule for periods in days excludes the day of the event and starts counting the next day. This tool works that way: the start date is not counted.

### Which holidays count in a business-day definition?

Whichever the contract names. Many name the bank holidays of a city or the days banks are open there. Without a definition, people disagree, so name a calendar when you draft.

### Is a federal court deadline counted in business days?

No. Under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 6(a)(1) a period in days counts every calendar day, including weekends and legal holidays, and only the last day moves forward if it lands on a weekend or legal holiday.
