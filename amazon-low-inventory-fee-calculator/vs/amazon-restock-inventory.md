# Amazon's FBA Inventory restock recommendations vs your own plan

Amazon's FBA Inventory is the source of truth for the days of supply and fee it will charge, and its restock recommendations use Amazon's own forecast; your own plan answers what-ifs with your numbers: at 12 a day with 800 units and 16 days of prep, transit and receiving, send by March 3, 2027, 388 units.

## What Amazon says FBA Inventory does

- "The restock recommendations can help you avoid running low on stock and are based on current inventory levels, forecasted demand, open shipments, and your lead time."
- "Recommended minimum level: We provide you with minimum inventory level recommendations, in units and days of supply (DoS), based on our sales forecast (expected average demand)."
- "Total lead time: The time between when you create a shipment to Amazon and when that shipment is received."
- "On the FBA Inventory page, you can either select Inventory report or Restock report in the Reports drop-down menu to generate and download a new report."
- The low-inventory fee pages point to FBA Inventory for the days of supply metric and the "Estimated fee per unit sold" column, and to SKU Economics and Transaction View for fees charged.

## When Amazon's page is better

Always for what was and will be charged: its days of supply use Amazon's own inventory and shipment data, and its recommendations know your open shipments. It is free inside Seller Central.

## When your own plan is better

For what-ifs: a different daily sales figure (a promotion, a season), another prep or transit time, the rule change on February 1, 2027, a file of SKUs, or an API call from your own system. The answer shows the arithmetic and the source of every rate.
