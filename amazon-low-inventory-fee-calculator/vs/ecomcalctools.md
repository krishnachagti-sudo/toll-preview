# EcomCalcTools' low inventory calculator, compared

EcomCalcTools multiplies a fee per unit you type by your monthly sales, while this calculator reads the rate from Amazon's table and charges it only in weeks when both days-of-supply windows are under 28: the same mug pays $30.24 on 84 units at 20 and 25.5 days, and $0.00 once the 90-day figure is 30.

## What EcomCalcTools says about its calculator

- The calculator is free and runs in the browser.
- You type an "Estimated fee per unit"; exposure is monthly units sold x that fee.
- Its page asks you to replace the defaults with your fee preview.
- Pro is $29 a month with 5,000 monthly input row credits for batch SKU audits.
- Its page says it was last updated June 30, 2026.

## Side by side

|  | EcomCalcTools | This calculator |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Price | Free; Pro $29 a month for batches | One SKU free; $0.005 a SKU, at most $4.99 a month |
| Fee per unit | You type it | From Amazon's table by tier, weight and band |
| 28-day test | Not applied to the exposure | Both windows, exact at the edges |
| Exemptions | No | Yes |
| Margin and PPC impact | Yes | No |
| Send-by date | Reorder urgency | A date and a unit count |

## When EcomCalcTools is better

When the question is margin: it turns a fee into a margin and break-even price change, next to its other free ecommerce calculators, and its Pro plan audits SKU profit in batches.

## When this calculator is better

When the question is whether Amazon charges this week, at which rate, and how to stop it: the table, the test, the exemptions and the reorder date.
