# Where to find the low-inventory fees you were charged

Per FNSKU, the low-inventory-level fee column of the SKU Economics report; per order, Transaction View and the FBA fulfillment fee explainer; and from February 1, 2027 the fee is its own line and the Low-Inventory-Level Fee Report lists each order with its trailing and following days of supply and any exemption reason.

## In Amazon's words

- "To make the fee easier to track, the low-inventory-level fee will appear as a standalone fee on the Payment view report, instead of included in FBA fulfillment fee charges."
- "Low Inventory Level Fee report (available starting February 1, 2027)"
- "For excessive inbound delays caused by us or Amazon-managed services, such as the Amazon Partnered Carrier program and Amazon Global Logistics, we'll automatically reimburse you if the low-inventory-level fees are charged."

## Check what it should have been

Take the days of supply from the report into the calculator to see the band and rate it should have used, and whether an exemption applied.

## Steps

1. **Per FNSKU: SKU Economics.** Open the SKU Economics report, pick the store, aggregation level and dates, and read the low-inventory-level fee column.
2. **Per order: Transaction View.** Search the order ID in Transaction View, open the Total, then the FBA Pick & Pack Fee and its Fee explainer (Calculation tab).
3. **From February 1, 2027: the fee report.** Legacy Seller Central: Reports, Fulfillment, Payments, Low-Inventory-Level Fee Report. New Seller Central: Supply chain, Supply chain reports, Payments, Low-Inventory-Level Fee Report.
4. **Days of supply.** FBA Inventory shows the metric and an estimated fee per unit sold.
5. **Inbound delays.** Fees caused by Amazon's receiving delays are reimbursed automatically by the 15th of the following month; check the order in Transaction View.
