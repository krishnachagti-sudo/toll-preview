# Pay per use or a subscription: which costs less

Pay per use costs less when your price per use times your uses a month is below the subscription fee. The break-even is the fee divided by the price per use: with an example $20 a month subscription and the business days tool at $0.0005 a call, it is 40,000 calls a month. A subscription wins only on daily heavy use.

## How do you compare pay per use with a subscription?

Put both on the same period, a month, and compare. Pay per use costs the price of one use times the number of uses. A subscription costs the same fee however much you use it. They meet at one number of uses, the break-even.

```text
break-even uses per month = subscription price per month / price per use
pay per use costs less when: uses per month < break-even
```

Replace the subscription price with the one you are looking at. This guide uses a clearly labelled example, a $20 a month subscription, because the arithmetic is the point.

## What does the break-even look like at Endwise prices?

Business days costs $0.0005 per call, which is $0.0005. Against an example $20 a month subscription:

```text
$20 / $0.0005 = 40,000 calls a month
```

That is about 1,333 calls every day of a 30-day month before the subscription costs less. A person checking a deadline or a project plan makes a handful. An automated job that runs all day can reach it.

## Is pay per use better for seasonal work?

Yes, when the job comes round a few times a year. A subscription bills for the months you do not use it.

[IFTA quarterly return calculator](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/ifta-calculator/) charges $5 for a quarterly return (1 to 5 vehicles). That is 4 returns a year:

```text
4 x $5 = $20 a year
12 x $20 = $240 a year for the example subscription
```

Add a year-end check of 20 payees on the [1099 threshold tracker](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/1099-threshold/), $2, and the whole year is $22. Work that clusters in a season, such as quarterly IFTA or 1099 season, is where pay per use wins by the widest margin.

## When does a pass or a subscription win?

When you use the service every working day. At that point the fixed fee is spread over many uses and each one costs less than its list price.

Year Pass costs $49 once and lasts 12 months. Each month it covers up to $25 of web runs at list prices. It never renews. Spread over the year, $49 is about $4.08 a month, so it pays off if your web runs would otherwise cost more than that each month. Season Pass ($19, 4 months) fits a busy few months. Practice Pass ($149, 12 months) is for bookkeepers and accountants who work for many clients.

> A pass covers use on the tools' web pages. Calls from your own code or an AI assistant (the API and MCP) are paid from your balance, not from the pass.

## What do you get free?

Every tool runs 5 times a day on its page without an account. With an account, the first $0.25 of use each month is free and is spent before your own money. It lapses at the end of the month. That is enough to try a tool and see the answer before you pay anything.

You add money to one balance, from undefined (the smallest pack is $10), and each use is taken from it at the price on the tool's page. Money you add stays until you spend it. Nothing renews.

## Questions

### How do I work out whether pay per use is cheaper than a subscription?

Multiply your price per use by how many uses you expect in a month. If the result is below the subscription price, pay per use costs less. The break-even is the subscription price divided by the price per use.

### When is a subscription cheaper than paying per use?

When you use the service every day, or in large volumes, so that your per-use total passes the monthly fee. A job done a few times a year almost never gets there.

### Is pay per use better for seasonal work?

Usually, yes. A quarterly filing or a once-a-year form check is used 1 to 4 times a year, and a subscription charges for the other months too.

### Does credit expire?

Money you add to your balance stays until you spend it. The free monthly allowance and a pass's monthly allowance lapse at the end of each month.
