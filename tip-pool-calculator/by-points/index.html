The legal side

The method is yours to choose; who is in the pool is not. Employers, managers and supervisors may never receive tips from a pool, and with a tip credit the pool may include only staff who customarily and regularly receive tips (29 CFR 531.54(c)). Who can be in the pool sets out each role.

  • Note: Pay out every collected tip by the regular payday for the workweek (or for the pay period it ends in). (29 CFR 531.54(b)(2))

Not legal or tax advice. Shares are arithmetic on the hours, points and roles shown; the check applies the quoted rules to those facts. Whether someone is a manager or supervisor, or customarily tipped, depends on their duties. State rules are shown only for states whose own site was read; city rules are not covered.

Sources

  • 29 CFR 531.54, Tip pooling (eCFR, current to November 4, 2026, read November 8, 2026)
  • DOL Wage and Hour Division, Fact Sheet #15: Tipped Employees Under the FLSA (read November 8, 2026)

Steps

  1. Give each role points. For example servers 10, bartenders 8, bussers 5, runners 4, hosts 3. The law sets no points; this is house policy.
  2. Add up the points. Total the points of everyone who worked: 55 here.
  3. Value one point. Divide the pool by the points: about $22.55 a point.
  4. Pay each person. Multiply each person's points by the value of a point, round down to the cent and hand out the leftover cents to the largest remainders.

Questions

How does a tip pool point system work?

Add everyone's points, divide the pool by the total points to get the value of one point, then multiply each person's points by that value.

Should points be weighted by hours?

If shifts vary in length, yes: multiply points by hours so a short shift earns less. See the hours x points page.
