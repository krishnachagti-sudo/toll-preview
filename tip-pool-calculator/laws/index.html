Tip pooling laws and tipped wages by state

As of November 5, 2026 (the DOL table's last revision), 7 states require the full state minimum wage before tips (Alaska, California, Minnesota, Montana, Nevada, Oregon, Washington); in the rest the tipped cash wage runs from $2.13 to $14.75 an hour where a single figure is given, and the federal floor is $2.13 with a $5.12 tip credit.

States that allow no tip credit

Alaska ($14.00), California ($16.90), Minnesota ($11.41), Montana ($10.85), Nevada ($12.00), Oregon ($15.55), Washington ($17.13). In these states a pool may include back-of-house staff under the federal rule for employers that take no tip credit (29 CFR 531.54(d)), unless the state's own rule says otherwise.

Managers and back of house

Everywhere in the US, employers, managers and supervisors may not receive tips from a pool (29 CFR 531.54). Back of house may share only when no tip credit is taken (531.54(d)); some states go further, such as Massachusetts and New York, which keep kitchen staff out of pools. See who can be in the pool.

Not covered

Pool rules for 37 states were not read on the state's own site and are not covered: for those, the calculator applies the federal rules and the DOL row, and says so. City minimum wages and ordinances are not covered anywhere.

Not legal or tax advice. Shares are arithmetic on the hours, points and roles shown; the check applies the quoted rules to those facts. Whether someone is a manager or supervisor, or customarily tipped, depends on their duties. State rules are shown only for states whose own site was read; city rules are not covered.

Questions

Which states do not allow a tip credit?

The DOL table revised November 5, 2026 lists Alaska, California, Minnesota, Montana, Nevada, Oregon, Washington (and Guam) as requiring the full state minimum wage before tips.

What is the federal tipped minimum cash wage?

$2.13 an hour, with a maximum tip credit of $5.12 toward the $7.25 federal minimum wage (DOL Fact Sheet #15).
