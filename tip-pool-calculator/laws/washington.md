# Tip pooling in Washington

Washington requires the full state minimum wage of $17.13 before tips, so there is no tip credit (DOL table revised November 5, 2026); pools may include kitchen staff and hourly leads but not salaried-exempt managers or owners.

- Minimum wage: $17.13
- Cash wage: $17.13
- Tip credit: none

## Washington's own rules

- ““Tip crediting” isn’t allowed. Tips are in addition to, and not a part of, an employee’s state hourly minimum wage.” (Washington Department of Labor & Industries: Tips and Service Charges)
- “Cannot include salaried-exempt managers and business owners.” (Washington Department of Labor & Industries: Tips and Service Charges)
- “Can include employees who are not directly serving a customer, such as kitchen staff and hourly lead workers.” (Washington Department of Labor & Industries: Tips and Service Charges)
- “Employees cannot be required to contribute more than they received in tips to a mandatory tip pool.” (Washington Department of Labor & Industries: Tips and Service Charges)
- “Employers may establish tip pools or require employees to “tip out” other employees. We recommend employers create clearly written policies for tip pooling arrangements.” (Washington Department of Labor & Industries: Tips and Service Charges)
- “If nothing is disclosed, or the disclosure is unclear, then the entire service charge must be paid to the employee who provides services to the customer.” (Washington Department of Labor & Industries: Tips and Service Charges)

## The federal floor

“When state law differs from the federal FLSA, an employer must comply with the standard most protective to employees.”

Federal rules apply everywhere: no employer, manager or supervisor may take from a pool (29 CFR 531.54(b), (c)(3), (d)), pooled tips must be paid by the regular payday (531.54(b)(2)), and with a tip credit only customarily tipped staff may share (531.54(c)(1)).

## Questions

### Is tip pooling legal in Washington?

Washington requires the full state minimum wage of $17.13 before tips, so there is no tip credit (DOL table revised November 5, 2026); pools may include kitchen staff and hourly leads but not salaried-exempt managers or owners.

### Can a manager be in a tip pool in Washington?

No. Under the FLSA an employer may not allow managers or supervisors to receive tips from a pool, whether or not it takes a tip credit (29 CFR 531.54), and that applies in every state.
