# Tip pooling in New York

In New York the minimum wage is $17.00 (NYC, Long Island, & Westchester); $16.00 (remainder of NY state), and tipped wages and the tip credit vary by region and type of employer (DOL table revised November 5, 2026); only food service workers who give personal service to patrons may share, so cooks and dishwashers may not; only the card company's pro-rated fee may come off a card tip.

## New York's own rules

- “An employer may require food service workers to participate in a tip pool and may set the percentage to be distributed to each occupation from the tip pool. Only food service workers may receive distributions from the tip pool.” (12 NYCRR 146-2.16(b))
- “Eligibility of employees to receive shared tips, or to receive distributions from a tip pool, shall be based upon duties and not titles. Eligible employees must perform, or assist in performing, personal service to patrons at a level that is a principal and regular part of their duties and is not merely occasional or incidental.” (12 NYCRR 146-2.14(e))
- “Employers may not require directly tipped employees to contribute a greater percentage of their tips to indirectly tipped employees through tip sharing or tip pooling than is customary and reasonable.” (12 NYCRR 146-2.14(f))
- “Employers who operate a tip sharing or tip pooling system must establish, maintain, and preserve for at least six years records” (12 NYCRR 146-2.17(a))
- “The employer must return to the employee the full amount of the tip charged on the credit card, minus the pro-rated portion of the tip taken by the credit card company.” (12 NYCRR 146-2.20)
- “A charge purported to be a gratuity must be distributed in full as gratuities to the service employees or food service workers who provided the service.” (12 NYCRR 146-2.18(a))

Eligibility is based on duties, not titles: the regulation lists wait staff, counter personnel who serve food or beverages, bus persons, bartenders, service bartenders, barbacks, food runners, captains who provide direct food service and hosts who greet and seat guests. A pool the employer requires may include only food service workers; outside food service, pooling is voluntary (146-2.16(a)).

## The federal floor

“When state law differs from the federal FLSA, an employer must comply with the standard most protective to employees.”

Federal rules apply everywhere: no employer, manager or supervisor may take from a pool (29 CFR 531.54(b), (c)(3), (d)), pooled tips must be paid by the regular payday (531.54(b)(2)), and with a tip credit only customarily tipped staff may share (531.54(c)(1)).

## Questions

### Is tip pooling legal in New York?

In New York the minimum wage is $17.00 (NYC, Long Island, & Westchester); $16.00 (remainder of NY state), and tipped wages and the tip credit vary by region and type of employer (DOL table revised November 5, 2026); only food service workers who give personal service to patrons may share, so cooks and dishwashers may not; only the card company's pro-rated fee may come off a card tip.

### Can a manager be in a tip pool in New York?

No. Under the FLSA an employer may not allow managers or supervisors to receive tips from a pool, whether or not it takes a tip credit (29 CFR 531.54), and that applies in every state.
