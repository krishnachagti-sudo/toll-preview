# Tip pooling in Kansas

In Kansas the minimum cash wage for tipped staff is $2.13 and the tip credit is $5.12 against a $7.25 minimum wage (DOL table revised November 5, 2026); the state minimum wage law does not apply to employers covered by the FLSA; nothing read on the state's site (Kansas Statutes 44-1203) adds a tip-pool rule, so the federal pool rules apply.

- Minimum wage: $7.25
- Cash wage: $2.13
- Tip credit: $5.12

## Kansas's own rules

- “The provisions of this section shall not apply to any employers and employees who are covered under the provisions of the federal fair labor standards act” (K.S.A. 44-1203(c))
- “For employees receiving tips and gratuities, the employer shall pay a minimum wage of at least $2.13 an hour.” (K.S.A. 44-1203(b))

## The federal floor

“When state law differs from the federal FLSA, an employer must comply with the standard most protective to employees.”

Federal rules apply everywhere: no employer, manager or supervisor may take from a pool (29 CFR 531.54(b), (c)(3), (d)), pooled tips must be paid by the regular payday (531.54(b)(2)), and with a tip credit only customarily tipped staff may share (531.54(c)(1)).

> Not legal or tax advice. Shares are arithmetic on the hours, points and roles shown; the check applies the quoted rules to those facts. Whether someone is a manager or supervisor, or customarily tipped, depends on their duties. State rules are shown only for states whose own site was read; city rules are not covered.

## Questions

### Is tip pooling legal in Kansas?

In Kansas the minimum cash wage for tipped staff is $2.13 and the tip credit is $5.12 against a $7.25 minimum wage (DOL table revised November 5, 2026); the state minimum wage law does not apply to employers covered by the FLSA; nothing read on the state's site (Kansas Statutes 44-1203) adds a tip-pool rule, so the federal pool rules apply.

### Can a manager be in a tip pool in Kansas?

No. Under the FLSA an employer may not allow managers or supervisors to receive tips from a pool, whether or not it takes a tip credit (29 CFR 531.54), and that applies in every state.
