# Tip pooling in Minnesota

Minnesota requires the full state minimum wage of $11.41 before tips, so there is no tip credit (DOL table revised November 5, 2026); an employer may not require staff to pool or share tips; staff may agree to share among themselves; the card processing fee may not come out of card tips.

- Minimum wage: $11.41
- Cash wage: $11.41
- Tip credit: none

## Minnesota's own rules

- “No employer may directly or indirectly credit, apply, or utilize gratuities towards payment of the minimum wage set by this section or federal law.” (subd. 2)
- “No employer may require an employee to contribute or share a gratuity received by the employee with the employer or other employees or to contribute any or all of the gratuity to a fund or pool operated for the benefit of the employer or employees. This section does not prevent an employee from voluntarily sharing gratuities with other employees. The agreement to share gratuities must be made by the employees without employer coercion or participation” (subd. 3)
- “upon the request of employees, safeguard gratuities to be shared by employees and disburse shared gratuities to employees participating in the agreement” (subd. 3(1))
- “Where a gratuity is received by an employee through a debit, charge, credit card, or electronic payment, the full amount of gratuity indicated in the payment must be distributed to the employee no later than the next scheduled pay period.” (subd. 3a(b))

## The federal floor

“When state law differs from the federal FLSA, an employer must comply with the standard most protective to employees.”

Federal rules apply everywhere: no employer, manager or supervisor may take from a pool (29 CFR 531.54(b), (c)(3), (d)), pooled tips must be paid by the regular payday (531.54(b)(2)), and with a tip credit only customarily tipped staff may share (531.54(c)(1)).

## Questions

### Is tip pooling legal in Minnesota?

Minnesota requires the full state minimum wage of $11.41 before tips, so there is no tip credit (DOL table revised November 5, 2026); an employer may not require staff to pool or share tips; staff may agree to share among themselves; the card processing fee may not come out of card tips.

### Can a manager be in a tip pool in Minnesota?

No. Under the FLSA an employer may not allow managers or supervisors to receive tips from a pool, whether or not it takes a tip credit (29 CFR 531.54), and that applies in every state.
