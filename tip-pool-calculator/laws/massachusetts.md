# Tip pooling in Massachusetts

In Massachusetts the minimum cash wage for tipped staff is $6.75 and the tip credit is $8.25 against a $15.00 minimum wage (DOL table revised November 5, 2026); only wait staff, service employees and service bartenders may share, so cooks and dishwashers may not; the card processing fee may not come out of card tips.

- Minimum wage: $15.00
- Cash wage: $6.75
- Tip credit: $8.25

## Massachusetts's own rules

- “No employer or person shall cause, require or permit any wait staff employee, service employee, or service bartender to participate in a tip pool through which such employee remits any wage, tip or service charge, or any portion thereof, for distribution to any person who is not a wait staff employee, service employee, or service bartender.” (c.149 s.152A(c))
- “Employees who do not serve patrons directly are not "wait staff employees" and, therefore, may not receive tips or service charges.” (Massachusetts Attorney General, Advisory 3/2004 on tips (revised 9/2026))
- “No employer or other person shall demand, request or accept from any wait staff employee, service employee, or service bartender any payment or deduction from a tip or service charge given to such wait staff employee, service employee, or service bartender by a patron.” (c.149 s.152A(b))
- “Any service charge or tip remitted by a patron or person to an employer shall be paid to the wait staff employee, service employee, or service bartender by the end of the same business day, and in no case later than the time set forth for timely payment of wages under section 148.” (c.149 s.152A(e))
- “If an employer or person submits a bill, invoice or charge to a patron or other person that imposes a service charge or tip, the total proceeds of that service charge or tip shall be remitted only to the wait staff employees, service employees, or service bartenders in proportion to the service provided by those employees.” (c.149 s.152A(d))

Only wait staff (who serve food or drink directly or clear tables), service employees outside food service and service bartenders may share. The Attorney General's advisory says kitchen and administrative staff are not wait staff, and that shift supervisors, assistant managers, banquet captains and many maitre d's have managerial responsibility and do not qualify.

## The federal floor

“When state law differs from the federal FLSA, an employer must comply with the standard most protective to employees.”

Federal rules apply everywhere: no employer, manager or supervisor may take from a pool (29 CFR 531.54(b), (c)(3), (d)), pooled tips must be paid by the regular payday (531.54(b)(2)), and with a tip credit only customarily tipped staff may share (531.54(c)(1)).

## Questions

### Is tip pooling legal in Massachusetts?

In Massachusetts the minimum cash wage for tipped staff is $6.75 and the tip credit is $8.25 against a $15.00 minimum wage (DOL table revised November 5, 2026); only wait staff, service employees and service bartenders may share, so cooks and dishwashers may not; the card processing fee may not come out of card tips.

### Can a manager be in a tip pool in Massachusetts?

No. Under the FLSA an employer may not allow managers or supervisors to receive tips from a pool, whether or not it takes a tip credit (29 CFR 531.54), and that applies in every state.
