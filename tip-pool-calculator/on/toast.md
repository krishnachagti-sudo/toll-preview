# How to split tips on Toast

Toast Tips Manager splits a pool by points (optionally times hours) or by job percentages; its own example, a $30 pool with two bartenders on 2 points and a host on 1, pays $10.00, $15.00, $5.00 by points x hours, and this calculator gives the same amounts, then checks who may be in the pool.

## How Toast splits

“When more than one recipient job is in a tip pool, Toast Tips Manager splits the contribution among those jobs using either points or percentages.”

“For hourly splitting, the system multiplies each job's points by hours worked, then divides total tips by total points to find the value of one point.”

“You cannot assign more than 10 points to an individual job, but you can use decimals.”

“With points, that share is redistributed among the other recipients who are clocked in. With percentages, the share returns to the contributor who originally earned the tip.”

## Toast's example, reproduced

“The Server contributing job earns $300 in cash and non-cash tips. The contribution percentage is 10%, so $30 goes into the pool.”

Points weighted by hours: the calculator's result.

| Name | Role | Hours | Points | Share |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Bartender A | Bartender | 4 | 2 | $10.00 |
| Bartender B | Bartender | 6 | 2 | $15.00 |
| Host | Host | 4 | 1 | $5.00 |

Percentages (bartenders 60%, host 40%), by hours within each job.

Toast's article gives Bartender A $10.00, Bartender B $15.00, Host $5.00 for points x hours and Bartender A $7.20, Bartender B $10.80, Host $12.00 for percentages.

## What to check yourself

Toast's article explains the arithmetic; it does not say it checks whether a job may be in the pool. Before you add a job, check it: no managers or supervisors, and with a tip credit no cooks or dishwashers (29 CFR 531.54). [Who can be in the pool](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/tip-pool-calculator/guides/who-can-be-in-the-pool/).

> Not legal or tax advice. Shares are arithmetic on the hours, points and roles shown; the check applies the quoted rules to those facts. Whether someone is a manager or supervisor, or customarily tipped, depends on their duties. State rules are shown only for states whose own site was read; city rules are not covered.
