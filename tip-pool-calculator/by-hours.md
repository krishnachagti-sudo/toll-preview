## The legal side

The method is yours to choose; who is in the pool is not. Employers, managers and supervisors may never receive tips from a pool, and with a tip credit the pool may include only staff who customarily and regularly receive tips (29 CFR 531.54(c)). [Who can be in the pool](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/tip-pool-calculator/guides/who-can-be-in-the-pool/) sets out each role.

- **Note:** Pay out every collected tip by the regular payday for the workweek (or for the pay period it ends in). (29 CFR 531.54(b)(2))

> Not legal or tax advice. Shares are arithmetic on the hours, points and roles shown; the check applies the quoted rules to those facts. Whether someone is a manager or supervisor, or customarily tipped, depends on their duties. State rules are shown only for states whose own site was read; city rules are not covered.

## Steps

1. **Add up the hours.** Total the hours everyone in the pool worked: 48 here.
2. **Find the rate.** Divide the pool by the hours: $1,240.00 / 48 = about $25.83 an hour.
3. **Work out each share.** Multiply each person's hours by the pool and divide by the total hours; round down to the cent.
4. **Hand out the leftover cents.** Give the cents left over one each to the people with the largest remainders, so the shares add up to the pool.

## Questions

### How do you split tips by hours worked?

Divide the total pool by the total hours worked to get a rate per hour, then multiply each person's hours by that rate. Round to the cent and give any leftover cents to the largest remainders so nothing is lost.

### Is there a legal limit on how much a server can be made to pay into a pool?

Not under federal law: "Section 3(m)(2)(A) does not impose a maximum contribution percentage on mandatory tip pools." (29 CFR 531.54(a)). Some states set limits; Delaware caps a required pool at 15% of the main server's tips.
