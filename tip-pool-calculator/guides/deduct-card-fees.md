# Card fees and tips

Under the FLSA an employer may take the card company's fee percentage off a card tip and no more, so $300.00 of card tips at a 2.9% fee pays out $291.30 ($8.70 off); Minnesota and Maine allow no deduction at all, Massachusetts bars any deduction from a tip, and the UK requires tips to be passed on without deductions.

“when tips are charged on customers' credit cards and the employer can show that it pays the credit card company a percentage on such sales as a fee for payment using a credit card, the employer may pay the employee the tip, less that percentage.”

“the employer cannot reduce the amount of tips paid to the employee by any amount greater than the transactional fee charged by the credit card company, regardless of whether or not it takes a tip credit.”

“the amount due the employee must be paid no later than the regular pay day and may not be held while the employer is awaiting reimbursement from the credit card company.”

## Questions

### Can an employer keep the credit card fee out of tips?

Federally, only the actual percentage the card company charges on the tip, and only where the state allows it; the tip must still be paid by the regular payday (DOL Fact Sheet #15). Minnesota and Maine do not allow any deduction.
