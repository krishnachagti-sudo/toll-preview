# Fixing an illegal tip pool

To fix a pool that included people who may not share, re-run each past pool without them and pay the difference: in this week a manager and a cook (with a tip credit) took $1,680.00 of a $4,200.00 pool, and Ana's share goes from $707.37 to $1,178.95.

- **Not allowed:** Hal: a manager or supervisor may not receive tips from the pool, whether or not you take a tip credit. They may keep tips customers give them directly for service they alone provide. (29 CFR 531.52(b)(2); 29 CFR 531.54(c)(3); DOL Fact Sheet #15)
- **Not allowed:** Gus: with a tip credit, the pool may include only employees who customarily and regularly receive tips, so this role is not allowed. Pay everyone in the pool at least the full minimum wage in cash to include them. (29 CFR 531.54(c)(1); 29 CFR 531.54(d); DOL Fact Sheet #15)

## What the DOL says it can cost

“An employer who violates the FLSA by requiring tipped employees to share their tips with a manager or supervisor may be required to return the tips to the employee and pay the full minimum wage.”

## Steps

1. **Stop it now.** Take the people who may not share out of the pool from the next shift.
2. **Re-run each past pool.** For every past pool, split the same tips among only the people allowed to share, using your method.
3. **Work out the difference.** For each person, what they should have had minus what they got.
4. **Get advice.** Talk to an employment lawyer or your state labor agency about paying it back; the DOL may also require the full minimum wage where a tip credit was taken.
5. **Write it down.** Change the written policy and keep the recalculation with the pay records.
