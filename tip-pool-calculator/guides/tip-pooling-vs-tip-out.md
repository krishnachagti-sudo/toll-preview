# Tip pooling vs tip-out

A tip pool puts everyone's tips in one pot and splits it (this night's $1,240.00 by hours pays about $25.83 an hour), while a tip-out has each server pay others a share (2% and 1.5% of $2,000.00 in sales is $70.00); federal law treats both alike, so the same rules on managers and back of house apply.

|  | Tip pool | Tip-out (tip sharing) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Money moves | All tips into one pot, then out by formula | Each server pays named roles a share |
| Usual formula | Hours, points, hours x points or role percentages | A percentage of the server's sales or tips |
| Legal status (federal) | Tips of whoever keeps them (531.54(a)) | The same (531.54(a)) |
| Managers | Never | Never |
| Back of house | Only with no tip credit | Only with no tip credit |

“Section 3(m)(2)(A) does not impose a maximum contribution percentage on mandatory tip pools.”

## The two on the same night

Your $1,240.00 pool over 48 hours pays about $25.83 an hour: Ana $193.75, Ben $155.00, Carla $135.62, Cleo $206.67, Dev $167.92, Ezra $103.33, Fay $129.17 and Gus $148.54 (to the cent, adding up to $1,240.00). No rule is broken on these facts.

On $2,000.00 in sales, the bartender tip-out at 2% is $40.00 and the busser tip-out at 1.5% is $30.00, $70.00 in all; the server keeps $290.00 of $360.00 in tips. No rule is broken on these facts.

## Questions

### What is the difference between tip pooling and tip sharing?

In a pool, tips go into one pot that is split by a formula. In tip sharing or a tip-out, each server gives part of their own tips to others. Federal law treats the amounts the same way: what each person keeps counts as their tips (29 CFR 531.54(a)).
