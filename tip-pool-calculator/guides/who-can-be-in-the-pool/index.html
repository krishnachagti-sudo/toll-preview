Who can be in the tip pool

Under the FLSA (29 CFR 531.54), employers, managers and supervisors may never receive tips from a pool; with a tip credit the pool may include only staff who customarily and regularly receive tips (servers, bartenders, bussers, counter staff), and with no tip credit it may also include cooks and dishwashers; states can be stricter, and the table checks every role in each state whose rules we read.

Three rules

  • Never the employer, managers or supervisors. “203(d).” (29 CFR 531.54(b)(1))
  • With a tip credit, only customarily tipped staff. “The employer may require an employee for whom the employer takes a tip credit to contribute tips to a tip pool only if it is limited to employees who customarily and regularly receive tips” (531.54(c)(1))
  • With no tip credit, back of house too. “An employer that pays its tipped employees the full minimum wage and does not take a tip credit may impose a tip pooling arrangement that includes dishwashers, cooks, or other employees in the establishment who are not employed in an occupation in which employees customarily and regularly receive tips.” (531.54(d))

The states

  • Washingtonpools may include kitchen staff and hourly leads but not salaried-exempt managers or owners.
  • Minnesotaan employer may not require staff to pool or share tips; staff may agree to share among themselves; the card processing fee may not come out of card tips.
  • Massachusettsonly wait staff, service employees and service bartenders may share, so cooks and dishwashers may not; the card processing fee may not come out of card tips.
  • New Yorkonly food service workers who give personal service to patrons may share, so cooks and dishwashers may not; only the card company's pro-rated fee may come off a card tip.
  • Mainewith the tip credit the pool must be only among service employees; without it, a wider group may share; the card processing fee may not come out of card tips.
  • Delawarean employer may require pooling only among direct service employees who served the same customer, up to 15% of the main server's tips.
  • Marylandthe statute says it does not prohibit the pooling of tips; otherwise the federal pool rules apply.
  • District of Columbiathe tipped-wage notice must state the tip-sharing policy and the percentage card fees take off card tips; otherwise the federal pool rules apply.

Other states: not covered here; the calculator applies the federal rules and says so. See all states.

Questions

Can cooks and dishwashers be in a tip pool?

Federally only if the employer takes no tip credit and every pool member gets at least the full minimum wage in cash (29 CFR 531.54(d)). Massachusetts and New York keep them out either way.

Is mandatory tip pooling legal?

Federally yes, for eligible roles: the FLSA lets an employer require a pool and sets no maximum contribution (29 CFR 531.54(a)). Minnesota does not let an employer require one, and Delaware limits it.
