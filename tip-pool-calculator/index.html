Tip pools and tip-outs, 29 CFR 531.54

Tip pool calculator

Split a tip pool to the cent by hours, points or role, and check who may be in it under the federal rules and your state's.

What you get

Split a tip pool by hours, points, hours x points, role percentages or equally. Every share is rounded to the cent and the shares add up exactly to the pool. The same run checks who may be in the pool: managers, supervisors and owners, back of house with or without a tip credit, and card-fee deductions, under the federal rules and your state's own rules where we have read them.

Splits, tip-outs and checks are free on this page. A pay-run record (the payroll CSV, a tip sheet to sign and the check) costs $0.25, and corrections to the same run are free for 30 days.

One line each: bartender 20. They must add up to 100. Inside a role, shares go by hours (or equally when hours are missing).

The rules

What the check follows, rule by rule.

The federal rules for every state, and a state's own rules where its site was read on November 8, 2026.

What it does not cover

City rules, the rules of states not read yet, and whether someone's duties make them a manager.

Read the full list

Not legal or tax advice. The split is arithmetic on the hours, points and roles you enter; the check applies the rules quoted with each finding to the facts you give. Whether someone is a manager or supervisor, or customarily tipped, depends on their duties.

State rules come only from each state's own site, read November 8, 2026: Arizona, Delaware, District of Columbia, Florida, Kansas, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Minnesota, Montana, New York, South Dakota, Virginia and Washington. Every other state gets the federal rules and its row of the DOL wage table, and the answer says its own rules are not covered. City rules are not covered.

Never managers or the employer

No part of staff tips for the employer, managers or supervisors, with or without a tip credit.

DOL Fact Sheet #15

Read the full rules

An employer may not keep any part of staff tips, and may not let managers or supervisors take from a pool, whether or not it takes a tip credit (29 CFR 531.52(b), 531.54(b)). Fact Sheet #15 counts an owner of at least 20 percent who actively manages as a manager. A manager who serves their own tables keeps those tips but stays out of the pool.

Tip credit or full minimum wage

With a tip credit, only customarily tipped staff share. Paying the full minimum wage lets cooks in.

29 CFR 531.50 to 531.60

Read the full rules

With a tip credit, a pool may include only staff who customarily and regularly get more than $30 a month in tips (531.54(c)). Paying everyone the full minimum wage in cash lets the pool include cooks and dishwashers (531.54(d)). Some states allow no tip credit, and some are stricter about who may share.

Card fees and payday

No more than the card company's fee, where the state allows it. Pay out by the regular payday.

DOL tipped wage table

Read the full rules

Take off no more than the card company's fee on the tip, and only where the state allows it: Minnesota and Maine do not; Massachusetts bars any deduction from a tip. Pay out pooled tips by the regular payday (531.54(b)(2)). In the UK, by the end of the month after the customer paid.

Sources, read November 8, 2026: 29 CFR 531.50 to 531.60 on eCFR, DOL Fact Sheet #15, DOL tipped wage table (revised November 5, 2026), the UK code of practice on tips, Ontario's ESA guide on tips and the IRS tipped occupation codes.
