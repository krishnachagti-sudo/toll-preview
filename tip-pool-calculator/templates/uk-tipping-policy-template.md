# UK written tipping policy template

Under the Employment (Allocation of Tips) Act 2023, a UK employer that gets tips more than occasionally must have a written tipping policy saying how tips are accepted, allocated and distributed and how it keeps them fair, must pay them out by the end of the following month, and must keep a three-year record workers can ask to see.

```text
TIPPING POLICY: [Business name], [place of business]

1. How tips are accepted: cash, card and app tips, and service charges [added automatically / voluntary].
2. All tips and service charges are passed to workers in full, with no deductions other than tax.
3. Who shares: all workers at this place of business, including agency workers: [roles].
4. How they are allocated, and why: [factors, e.g. hours worked during the period tips were received;
   type of role; basic pay]. Shares are rounded down to the penny; leftover pence go to the largest remainders.
5. Who allocates: [the employer / an independent tronc operator: name].
6. When they are paid: by the end of the month after the month the customer paid, at the latest.
7. Records: we keep a record of all tips and each worker's share for three years from the date paid.
   You may ask in writing to see the record, once every three months.
8. Questions or disputes: [how to raise a query].
```

## What the code requires

“the employer is required to have a written tipping policy when qualifying tips are paid at or are otherwise attributable to a place of business on more than an occasional and exceptional basis.”

“Pass on all tips and service charges to workers without deductions, except in very limited scenarios, such as deduction of income tax.”

“employers must ensure that all tips are distributed to staff, at the latest, by the end of the month following the month in which the tips are paid by customers (section 27G). For example, if a customer leaves a tip on 23 June, it must be distributed by 31 July at the latest.”

“A tipping record must include detail of all qualifying tips received by the employer at the place of business, and the amount allocated to each worker. This record must be maintained for a period of three years beginning with the date on which the tip was paid.”

“A worker has the right to make a written request – limited to one request per worker in a three month period – to view the tipping record of their employer for a period dating back up to 3 years”

The factors in line 4 are yours to choose from a clear and objective set; the code lists, as examples:

- Type of role / work for example distribution between front of house and backroom workers
- Basic pay (and how workers are engaged)
- Hours worked during period when tips are received
- Individual and/or team performance
- Seniority / level of responsibility
- Length of time served with the employer
- Customer intention

> Not legal or tax advice. Shares are arithmetic on the hours, points and roles shown; the check applies the quoted rules to those facts. Whether someone is a manager or supervisor, or customarily tipped, depends on their duties. State rules are shown only for states whose own site was read; city rules are not covered.
