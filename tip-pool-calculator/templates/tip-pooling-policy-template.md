# Tip pooling policy template

Under 29 CFR 531.54, a US tip pool policy needs to say who is in the pool (never an owner, manager or supervisor; with a tip credit only customarily tipped staff), what each person pays in, how it is split and rounded, and that pooled tips are paid by the regular payday, and an employer taking a tip credit must tell staff the required contribution.

```text
TIP POOL POLICY: [Business name], [location]

1. Who is in the pool. The pool is shared by: [roles, e.g. servers, bartenders, bussers, hosts].
   No owner, manager or supervisor receives any tips from the pool.
   [If you take a tip credit] Only employees who customarily and regularly receive tips are in the pool.
   [If you pay everyone at least the full minimum wage in cash] The pool may also include [kitchen roles].
2. What goes in. [All tips / X% of each server's tips / X% of each server's sales].
   Card tips go in [in full / less the card company's fee on the tip, X%, where the law allows it].
3. How it is split. By [hours worked / points: list them / hours x points / role percentages: list them].
   Shares are rounded down to the cent; leftover cents go one each to the largest remainders.
4. When it is paid. On the regular payday for the workweek in which the tips were collected.
5. Records. We keep each pool's total, each person's hours, points and share, for [period].
6. Notice. Each employee in a required pool is told the contribution amount before it applies.
   [If you take a tip credit] Employees are told the cash wage, the tip credit claimed, that all tips
   are theirs except for this pool, and that the credit does not apply to anyone not told.

Signed: ____________   Date: ____________
```

## Why each line is there

- Line 1: “An employer may not receive tips from such a tip pool and may not allow managers and supervisors to receive tips from the tip pool.” (29 CFR 531.54(c)(3)); “The employer may require an employee for whom the employer takes a tip credit to contribute tips to a tip pool only if it is limited to employees who customarily and regularly receive tips” (531.54(c)(1)).
- Line 2: card fees: “when tips are charged on customers' credit cards and the employer can show that it pays the credit card company a percentage on such sales as a fee for payment using a credit card, the employer may pay the employee the tip, less that percentage.” (DOL Fact Sheet #15). Some states forbid it; see the card fee guide.
- Line 4: “An employer that facilitates tip pooling by collecting and redistributing employees' tips does not violate section 3(m)(2)(B)'s prohibition against keeping tips if it fully distributes any tips the employer collects no later than the regular payday for the workweek in which the tips were collected, or when the pay period covers more than a single workweek, the regular payday for the period in which the workweek ends.” (531.54(b)(2)).
- Line 5: “An employer that does not take a tip credit, but still operates a mandatory tip pool, must keep records of each employee who receives tips, and the weekly or monthly amount of tips received by each employee.” (Fact Sheet #15).
- Line 6: “The employer must notify its employees of any required tip pool contribution amount, may only take a tip credit for the amount of tips each employee ultimately receives, and may not retain any of the employees' tips for any other purpose.” (531.54(c)(2)).

States add their own rules; Minnesota, for one, does not let an employer require a pool at all. Check [your state](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/tip-pool-calculator/laws/).
