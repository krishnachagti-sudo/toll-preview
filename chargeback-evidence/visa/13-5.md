# Visa 13.5: Misrepresentation

Visa 13.5 (Misrepresentation): the cardholder says they were misled about the goods or services, the terms, or what would happen after purchase (for example a trial that turned into recurring charges). Stripe files it under "Product unacceptable". The strongest evidence for a physical product is how the product was described, messages with the customer and refund or return policy, and where it was shown.

- Stripe category: Product unacceptable
- Issuer time limit: 120 days

## What it means

The cardholder says they were misled about the goods or services, the terms, or what would happen after purchase (for example a trial that turned into recurring charges).

## What Visa says to show

- Screenshots of the offer, checkout page and terms exactly as shown before payment
- Proof the cardholder acknowledged or agreed to those terms at purchase
- Notices of future charges and the customer's acceptance of recurring billing
- Records of the service provided and of the customer's use after purchase

## Watch for

- Only what the customer saw or was told before paying counts; later explanations do not.
- Verbal explanations count only if documented.

## Evidence by product type

- [A physical product](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/chargeback-evidence/?network=visa&code=13.5&productType=physical): Lead with how the product was described, messages with the customer and refund or return policy, and where it was shown; 7 pieces in all, strongest first.

## Time limit

The issuer may raise it up to 120 calendar days from the Transaction Processing Date or from the date the cardholder received the goods or services (never more than 540 days after processing), or 60 days from the first cardholder notice where earlier negotiation with you is shown. (Visa Core Rules, Table 11-120 (24 October 2026)) Your own deadline to respond is shorter: Stripe and Shopify usually allow 7 to 21 days, PayPal 10 days for a card chargeback.
