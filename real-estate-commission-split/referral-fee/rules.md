# Referral fee rules

Agent-to-agent referral fees fall under RESPA's exemption for "cooperative brokerage and referral arrangements or agreements between real estate agents and real estate brokers" (12 CFR 1024.14(g)(1)(v)); whether you may pay someone who is not licensed is a question for your state's licensing law, which this page does not cover.

## What the regulation says

Section 8 of RESPA (12 CFR 1024.14) bars giving or taking a fee for referring settlement business on a federally related mortgage loan. Paragraph (g)(1)(v) lists, among what it does not prohibit: "A payment pursuant to cooperative brokerage and referral arrangements or agreements between real estate agents and real estate brokers." The regulation adds that this exemption "The statutory exemption restated in this paragraph refers only to fee divisions within real estate brokerage arrangements".

## What it does not settle

- Paying a referral fee to someone without a real estate license, or to a licensee in another state: that is state licensing law. Ask your broker or your state's real estate commission before agreeing to it.
- How much the fee is: it is whatever the two brokerages agree in writing. This site does not state a typical percentage.
- Whether the fee is taken before your brokerage split, and whether it counts toward your cap: your plan.

## What a referral fee does to your net

On a $500,000 sale at 3%, your side is $15,000.00; after the $3,750.00 referral fee and the $3,375.00 brokerage split, you net $7,875.00.
