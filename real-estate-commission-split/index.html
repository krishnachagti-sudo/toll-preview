How it works

The order it works in, step by step.

Each step takes its share of what the step before left, rounded half up to the cent.

What it does not cover

Typical rates (every figure is yours), what your brokerage's statement says, and tax or legal advice.

Read the notes
  • Each amount is rounded half up to the cent. Caps reset on your anniversary (a February 29 anniversary falls on February 28 in common years); a deal on the anniversary starts the new cap year.
  • The 1099 totals add what the brokerage paid you by calendar year, and your share before the fees it charged you, because brokerages differ in which they report. Form 1099-NEC is for nonemployee compensation of $2,000 or more paid in 2026 ($600 before 2026), per the IRS Instructions for Forms 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC, read November 8, 2026.
  • Arithmetic on the plan you enter: your brokerage's commission statement and your agreements decide what you are paid. Not tax or legal advice.

Seven steps, in your plan's order

Commission, referral, royalty, team, brokerage split up to the cap, team after, then per-deal fees.

Read the full order
  1. Your side of the commission: price times your rate, or the amount you type.
  2. Less a referral fee you pay out.
  3. Less a royalty or franchise fee, off the top or out of your share, up to its own cap.
  4. Less the team's share, if your team takes it before the brokerage split.
  5. Less the brokerage split, until the brokerage has received your cap for the cap year. The deal that crosses the cap pays the pre-cap rate on the part that fills it and the post-cap rate on the rest.
  6. Less the team's share, if your team takes it after the brokerage split.
  7. Less per-deal fees: on every deal, only until you cap, or only after.
