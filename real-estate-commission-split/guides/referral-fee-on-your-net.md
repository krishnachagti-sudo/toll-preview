# A referral fee and your net

A 25% referral fee on a $500,000 sale at 3% is $3,750.00, taken off your $15,000.00 side before the split; on a 70/30 plan with a $300 fee you net $7,575.00 instead of $10,200.00, and $1,125.00 less counts toward your cap.

On a $500,000 sale at 3%, your side is $15,000.00; after the $3,750.00 referral fee, the $3,375.00 brokerage split and $300.00 in fees, you net $7,575.00. $16,625.00 is left to your $20,000 cap.

| Step | How | Amount |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Your side of the commission | $500,000.00 x 3% | $15,000.00 |
| Referral fee paid out | 25% of your side | -$3,750.00 |
| Brokerage split | 30% of $11,250.00 | -$3,375.00 |
| Transaction fee | every deal | -$300.00 |
| You net |  | $7,575.00 |

## Who may be paid

RESPA's anti-kickback rule does not prohibit "A payment pursuant to cooperative brokerage and referral arrangements or agreements between real estate agents and real estate brokers." (12 CFR 1024.14(g)(1)(v)). Paying someone who is not licensed, or a licensee in another state, is a question for state licensing law; see [referral fee rules](https://krishnachagti-sudo.github.io/toll-preview/real-estate-commission-split/referral-fee/rules/).

## Paperwork

- Put the fee, the percentage and the side it is taken from in a written agreement between the two brokerages.
- The fee is paid broker to broker; your commission statement shows it as a deduction before your split, or after it, as your plan says.
- The referring agent's brokerage pays them; it is part of what that brokerage reports on their 1099.

## Questions

### Do referral fees count toward my brokerage cap?

It depends on your brokerage agreement. When the referral fee is taken off the gross first and the split is worked out after it, the brokerage's share is smaller, so less counts toward your cap; check your plan.
