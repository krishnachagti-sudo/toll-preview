# How a commission cap works

A cap is the most you pay the brokerage in a cap year; the deal that crosses it is split at both rates: with $18,000 paid toward a $20,000 cap, a $10,000 commission at 70/30 pays 30% on the first $6,666.67 (filling the cap) and nothing on the rest, so you net $7,700.00 after a $300 fee.

## The crossing deal

Your side is $10,000.00; after the $2,000.00 brokerage split and $300.00 in fees, you net $7,700.00. This deal crosses your $20,000 cap.

| Step | How | Amount |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Your side of the commission | as entered | $10,000.00 |
| Brokerage split | 30% of $6,666.67 fills the cap, then 0% of $3,333.33 | -$2,000.00 |
| Transaction fee | every deal | -$300.00 |
| You net |  | $7,700.00 |

## Reading your own plan

- The cap: the most the brokerage keeps from you in a cap year.
- The reset: your anniversary date or January 1.
- After the cap: your new split and the fees that still apply, and whether any fee or royalty counts toward the cap.

## Questions

### Is a commission cap per year or per deal?

Per cap year, not per deal: the brokerage's share adds up across your deals until it reaches the cap, then stops (or drops to the post-cap rate) until the reset. Plans reset on an anniversary or on January 1; yours says which.

### Do I still pay fees after I hit my cap?

Often yes: many plans keep a per-deal transaction fee after the cap, and some only charge one after it. It varies by plan; the calculator takes fees that apply always, only before the cap or only after it.
