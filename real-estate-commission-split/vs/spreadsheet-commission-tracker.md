# A spreadsheet tracker compared

A spreadsheet is free and takes any column you add, but the crossing deal, the anniversary reset and the calendar-year 1099 total are formulas you write and check yourself; the tracker here does them and gives a CSV for your sheet: 6 deals: $77,062.50 on your side, you net $52,887.50. You capped on February 19, 2027 (Birch Ln). Paid to you in 2027: $10,865.00 (the 1099 basis; $11,165.00 before fees charged to you).

## What a spreadsheet tracker offers (read November 8, 2026)

- Google Sheets is a free spreadsheet; its help documents lookup functions such as VLOOKUP, the kind of formula a hand-built tracker uses to find a plan's split or cap.
- Excel works the same way; any formula is yours to write and keep correct.

## What this calculator adds

- Your own plan, in the order it takes its cuts: referral fee, royalty (with its cap), team share before or after the brokerage split (with a team cap), the split up to the cap, per-deal fees before, after or always.
- A year of deals in date order across anniversary caps, with the crossing deal split at both rates, deals left to cap, and the 1099 totals by calendar year, with a CSV.
- Plans compared at your deal count, and the deals needed for a net goal.
- One deal, a comparison or a goal is free on the calculator's page and $0.002 a call over the API or MCP; the year tracker (every deal in date order, cap progress, the 1099 total and a CSV) is $3.99 per agent cap year, free to re-run for 12 months.

## When a spreadsheet tracker is the better choice

When you want your own columns (leads, marketing spend, mileage) next to your deals and already trust a sheet you built. You can paste this tracker's CSV into it.
