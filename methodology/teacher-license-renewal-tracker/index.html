How teacher renewal hours are counted: state rule method

Each activity is converted to the state's unit (in Texas, 1 semester credit is 15 CPE hours), counted only inside the cycle, and capped where the state caps a category; the hours still needed are divided over the months left. 20 states are covered from their own agency pages, read November 8, 2026.

What does the teacher license renewal hours tracker compute?

Given a state, a license type, the cycle's start or end date and the activities logged, the tool gives the hours (or credits) earned under that state's rule, the hours still needed, any required topic still short, the due date and about how much a month is left to do. It also lists each state's rule with its quotes, sorts a staff list by how soon each license expires, and exports an audit log for one cycle. Covered: Alaska, California, Colorado, Georgia, Iowa, Maine, Massachusetts, Montana, Nebraska, New York, North Carolina, Ohio, Oregon, Pennsylvania, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Utah, Vermont and Wyoming. Rules quoted from each state's own education agency page, with the date we read it. Not legal advice: your state agency, LPDC or district decides what counts.

Read the rest: What does the teacher license renewal hours tracker compute?

Operations (the op field):

  • rules: Returns the renewal rule for one state (or every state), with each license type, the requirement, the cycle, the conversions, the caps and required topics, the quotes and sources, and the reason a state is not covered.
  • status: Works out one educator's cycle and totals: each activity counted or not with the reason, the caps and required topics, the hours earned and still needed, the days and months left, and the hours a month to finish.
  • export: Does what status does and also returns a CSV log of every activity and a printable summary for an audit, naming the rule's source and the date it was read.
  • staff: Sorts up to 2,000 staff licenses by how soon they expire (expired, within 6, 12 or 24 months, later), with the hours each state requires and, given hours logged, the hours left, and a CSV.

Inputs:

  • state, license: A two-letter state code and, where the state has more than one, the license type id (op rules lists them).
  • cycleStart, cycleEnd, birthMonth: When the license or cycle began, or the expiration date printed on it (which wins); New York needs the birth month when no end date is given. Dates are written like 2022-07-01.
  • entries: Up to 500 activities, each with a date, an amount, a unit (clock hour, semester hour, quarter hour, CEU, renewal credit, PDP, PDU, PDE-approved CPE credit, PLU, micro-credential), and optionally a category, title, provider and evidence.
  • carriedOver, options: Hours carried over from the last cycle where the state allows it, and the optional English learner requirement where a state makes it optional.
  • asOf: The day to count from. Today by default.
  • rows: For staff: each educator's name, state, license, expiration date and, optionally, hours logged.

Outputs:

  • answer: hours earned of those required, hours left by the due date and about how many a month, with any topic short and any hours over a cap
  • cycle: the start, the end, the window activities count in and whether the end was entered or worked out
  • logged, earned, carriedOver, remaining and stillNeeded, in the state's unit
  • caps and minimums: each capped category and each required topic with what counted
  • rows: each activity, its value in the state's unit, whether it counted and why not, and what an audit would ask for that is missing
  • quotes and sourcePages; csv and summary for export; rows, bands and csv for staff

How is it calculated, step by step?

  1. The state and license pick the rule. A state not covered answers with the reason it is not, and no totals.
  2. The cycle end is the date entered, or is worked out from the start by the state's rule: a number of years from issue, June 30 or September 30 of the expiration year, or the last day of the month before the birth month (New York). A state with no single license length needs the date printed on the license.
  3. Activities count only inside the cycle; where the state counts a shorter window (the last years of a longer license) or a grace period after June 30, the window follows it. An activity dated after today is flagged.
  4. Each activity is converted to the state's unit at the state's own rate (for example semester hours to clock hours). A unit the state does not accept counts for nothing and says why. Amounts are kept to two decimals.
  5. A capped category counts only up to its cap, a share of the requirement or a fixed number of hours; the rest is reported as not counted.
  6. Required topics are totalled on their own, and a topic still short is named. Hours carried over are added up to the state's limit.
  7. Where a state accepts alternatives that do not combine (Maine, Georgia), each is totalled on its own unit and the closest is named.
  8. The hours still needed are the larger of the hours left and the hours short in required topics, divided over the months left (days divided by 30.4375) and rounded up to a tenth.

The state rows.

Every rule was read on November 8, 2026 on the state's own education agency or licensing board page and is quoted word for word with its source. 31 jurisdictions whose pages could not be read are listed as not covered with the reason, rather than guessed.

Which edge cases does it handle?

  • A February 29 issue date gives a February 28 anniversary in a common year.
  • An Oregon license that expires before the date its hours rule starts needs no hours reported.
  • A license that renews with no hours says so, with its cycle end.
  • Hours carried over in a state that does not carry hours over are refused, naming the field.
  • A cycle that ends before it starts, or a date that is not real, is refused naming the field.

What does it not do?

  • Totals come from the activities you enter; this tool does not read PERMS, TEACH, ECOS or any state portal.
  • States whose official pages we could not read are listed as not covered rather than guessed.
  • It does not judge whether an activity meets a state's content rules; the category you give is used as given.

How is it tested?

The tests pin the data (51 jurisdictions, 20 covered from their own agency pages, 31 not covered with a reason) and each state's figures as it quotes them. They check the cycle end for each rule, unit conversions, caps, required topics, the window, alternatives that do not combine, no-hour licenses, refusals, the staff table, the export and every worked example.

What its test suite asserts:

  • The data is pinned: 51 jurisdictions, 20 covered from their own agency pages read on 2026-11-08 and 31 not covered with a reason, with each state's figures as it quotes them.
  • The cycle end is checked for each rule (issue date plus the years, fixed days, the birth month, the date entered winning), with conversions, caps, required topics and the window.
  • Alternatives that do not combine, no-hour licenses, Oregon's later rule, uncovered states, the refusals, the staff table, the export and the worked examples are checked.

Questions

Is there any AI in the teacher license renewal hours tracker answers?

No. The answer is worked out by fixed rules from the data on this page, so the same input gives the same answer every time.

What will the teacher license renewal hours tracker refuse to answer?

Totals come from the activities you enter; this tool does not read PERMS, TEACH, ECOS or any state portal. A request outside what the data covers is refused with the reason and costs nothing.
