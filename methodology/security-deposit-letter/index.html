How the security deposit letter works: state deadlines

The due date is counted from the trigger the state statute names (move-out, end of tenancy, the later or earlier of the two, or Arizona's tenant demand): California 21 calendar days, Texas 30 calendar days, Florida 30 calendar days, New York 14 calendar days, Pennsylvania 30 calendar days, Illinois 30 calendar days, Ohio 30 calendar days, Georgia 30 calendar days, North Carolina 30 calendar days, Michigan 30 calendar days, Virginia 45 calendar days, Washington 30 calendar days and Arizona 14 business days. Arizona counts business days and skips its legal holidays; the others count calendar days, and a weekend due date is not moved.

How does it work?

  1. The move-out must fall from January 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027, the window the statute text read on November 2, 2026 is good for; other dates are refused.
  2. The trigger is taken as the statute names it.
  3. The days are counted from the trigger. Arizona counts business days, skipping Saturdays, Sundays and the holidays in A.R.S. 1-301, and lists each day skipped.
  4. Florida gives two answers: 15 days to return the whole deposit with no claim, or 30 days to send the notice of claim by certified mail and 30 days after the notice to pay the rest.
  5. Each deduction is checked against the state's rules: documents for repairs and cleaning, the photographs and move-in checklist where the statute asks for them, the allowed kinds of deduction in New York, and the delivery method.

Steps 6 to 8, each rule and the edge cases are in the full method below.

What does it not cover?

  • States other than California, Texas, Florida, New York, Pennsylvania, Illinois, Ohio, Georgia, North Carolina, Michigan, Virginia, Washington and Arizona are refused until their statute has been read.
  • You decide what is damage beyond normal wear and tear; the tool checks only that the documents and words the statute requires are there. Not legal advice.
  • Penalty figures are the statute's maximums on the amounts entered; bad faith, willfulness and awards are for a court.

What does the security deposit return deadline and itemized letter compute?

Given the state and the move-out dates, the tool works out the day the itemized statement and refund are due and the days left.

In detail: operations, inputs and outputs

With the deposit and each deduction, it checks the documents and delivery method the statute requires, works out what is kept and refunded and shows the statute's maximum penalty for missing it. The letter is a dated PDF from a fixed clause library, made only when nothing the statute requires is missing, with a proof record and a calendar file for the due date. States covered: California, Texas, Florida, New York, Pennsylvania, Illinois, Ohio, Georgia, North Carolina, Michigan, Virginia, Washington and Arizona.

Operations (the op field):

  • rules: Returns the covered states with each statute row: the trigger, the day count, the delivery rules, the penalty and every quote with its source. Free.
  • deadline: Returns the due date, the trigger used, the days left, any holidays skipped and the steps with their cites. Free on the tool page; $0.10 per call over the API or MCP.
  • check: Returns the due date plus the money (held, kept, refunded, owed by the tenant), each statutory requirement missing or to confirm with its cite, and the penalty formula with its maximum. Free on the tool page; $0.10 per call over the API or MCP.
  • letter: Returns the letter PDF, its proof record PDF and a calendar file for the due date. A watermarked preview is free; the letter is $4.90 per move-out, and edits to the same move-out within 30 days are free.

Inputs:

  • state: Two-letter code. Covered: CA, TX, FL, NY, PA, IL, OH, GA, NC, MI, VA, WA, AZ.
  • moveOut, tenancyEnd: The day the tenant moved out and gave back possession, and the day the lease or tenancy ended (defaults to moveOut).
  • tenantDemand, abandonmentLearned, noticeSent, forwardingAddress: Arizona: the day the tenant demanded the deposit. Washington: the day you learned of an abandonment. Florida: the day the notice of claim was sent. The day a forwarding address was given.
  • units: Illinois: units in the building (section 1 covers 5 or more).
  • deposit, interest, deductions: The deposit held, any interest owed, and each deduction with its amount, category and how it is shown (invoice, receipt, estimate, own labor with hours and rate, or none).
  • photos, moveInChecklist, waiver, receiptsUnavailable: The documents the statute asks for: California photographs, a Washington move-in checklist, a California documentation waiver, Illinois receipts that cannot be had.
  • delivery, emailAgreement: How the letter goes (first-class, certified, personal or e-mail) and whether a written agreement allows e-mail.
  • tenants, landlord, tenantAddress, property: For the letter: the names and addresses printed on it.

Outputs:

  • answer: one paragraph with the due date, the money and how many requirements are missing
  • dueDate, trigger, days, count, daysRemaining and status (open, due today or late)
  • money: held, deductions, kept, refund and owed by the tenant, each in dollars
  • issues: each requirement missing or to confirm, with its statute cite; exposure: the penalty formula and its maximum
  • letter, record and calendar files
Every step in full (8) and the rules
  1. The move-out must fall from January 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027, the window the statute text read on November 2, 2026 is good for; other dates are refused.
  2. The trigger is taken as the statute names it. When the tenancy end is not given, it is taken as the move-out day and the answer says so.
  3. The days are counted from the trigger. Arizona counts business days, skipping Saturdays, Sundays and the holidays in A.R.S. 1-301, and lists each day skipped. A calendar-day due date on a weekend stays where it falls.
  4. Florida gives two answers: 15 days to return the whole deposit with no claim, or 30 days to send the notice of claim by certified mail and 30 days after the notice to pay the rest.
  5. Each deduction is checked against the state's rules: documents for repairs and cleaning, the photographs and move-in checklist where the statute asks for them, the allowed kinds of deduction in New York, and the delivery method.
  6. What is kept is the deductions up to the deposit plus interest; the rest is refunded, and anything over is shown as owed by the tenant.
  7. The penalty is the statute's formula on the deposit or the amount kept, shown as a maximum; whether a court would award it is not predicted.
  8. The letter is built from a fixed clause library filled from the check. It is not made while any requirement is missing; a preview shows what is missing.

The statute rows.

One row per covered state, each with the quoted sentences word for word, the URL and the date read (November 2, 2026). Where the official site could not be reached, the quote was read from the copy named with it.

Illinois section 1 applies to buildings with 5 or more units; with fewer, the tool says it does not bind and that no other Illinois rule is loaded.

What is not checked.

City ordinances (Chicago's among them), local adoption and exemptions are not checked, and North Carolina's penalty section was not read, so no figure is given for it.

Which edge cases does it handle?

The edge cases (5)
  • Arizona's clock starts only once the tenant demands the deposit; without the demand date the tool says the clock has not started.
  • Washington counts from the day you learned of an abandonment, when given.
  • In Texas the landlord need not refund or itemize until the tenant gives a written forwarding address, and a postmark on the due date is on time.
  • A California deduction for repairs and cleaning above $125 in total needs the bill or receipt, or the time and hourly rate for your own labor.
  • Interest owed is added to the amount held before the refund is worked out.

How is it tested?

The tests check each state's trigger and count, the Arizona holiday skip, Florida's two paths, each state's deduction and delivery rules, the penalty formulas, that the letter is refused while a requirement is missing, and that the browser engine gives the server's answers.

Questions

Is there any AI in the security deposit return deadline and itemized letter answers?

No. The answer is worked out by fixed rules from the data on this page, so the same input gives the same answer every time.

What will the security deposit return deadline and itemized letter refuse to answer?

States other than California, Texas, Florida, New York, Pennsylvania, Illinois, Ohio, Georgia, North Carolina, Michigan, Virginia, Washington and Arizona are refused until their statute has been read. A request outside what the data covers is refused with the reason and costs nothing.
