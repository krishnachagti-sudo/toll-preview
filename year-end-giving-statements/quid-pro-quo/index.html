Quid pro quo gifts and the $75 disclosure

A charity must give a written disclosure to a donor whose payment is more than $75 and partly for goods or services, with a good faith estimate of their value and a note that only the excess is deductible: a $100.00 payment for a $40.00 ticket is a $60.00 contribution and needs the disclosure; the penalty for missing one is $10 a contribution, up to $5,000 an event or mailing (IRS Publication 1771).

Seven payments, worked

A donor gives a charitable organization $100 in exchange for a concert ticket with a fair market value of $40. In this example, the donor's tax deduction may not exceed $60. Because the payment is over $75, the disclosure is required even though the deductible part is not.

What the disclosure says

  • It must inform the donor that the amount of the contribution that is deductible for federal income tax purposes is limited to the excess of money (and the fair market value of property other than money) contributed by the donor over the value of goods or services provided by the organization.
  • It must provide the donor with a good faith estimate of the fair market value of the goods or services.

An organization must furnish a disclosure statement in connection with either the solicitation or the receipt of the quid pro quo contribution. A year-end statement can repeat the disclosure, as the statements this tool makes do, but it comes after the event: give it on the ticket, the invitation or the receipt.

When no disclosure is needed

  • The goods or services are of insubstantial value (the token and membership exceptions in Pub 1771, with amounts adjusted for inflation each year).
  • There is no donative element, such as a gift shop sale.
  • Only intangible religious benefits were provided.

Questions

When is a quid pro quo disclosure required?

An organization must provide a written disclosure statement to a donor who makes a payment exceeding $75 partly as a contribution and partly for goods and services provided by the organization.

What must the disclosure say?

It must inform the donor that the amount of the contribution that is deductible for federal income tax purposes is limited to the excess of money (and the fair market value of property other than money) contributed by the donor over the value of goods or services provided by the organization, and provide the donor with a good faith estimate of the fair market value of the goods or services.

Is a $75 payment over the threshold?

No: the rule is a payment exceeding $75. A $75.00 payment needs no disclosure; $75.01 does.

What is the penalty?

The penalty is $10 per contribution, not to exceed $5,000 per fundraising event or mailing.
