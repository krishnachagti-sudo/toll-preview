The 2026 deduction for donors who do not itemize

From the 2026 tax year, a donor who does not itemize may be able to deduct up to $1,000 of qualifying cash gifts to eligible charities, or $2,000 for a married couple filing jointly (IRS Publication 6196). Donors claim it on 2026 returns filed in 2027, so the statements you send in January 2027 are the records many of them will keep.

Who can claim it?

IRS Publication 6196 (new for 2026 tax returns filed in 2027) lists:

  • Taxpayers qualify if they do not itemize.
  • Made cash contributions during 2026.
  • Donated to an eligible charitable organization.
  • Kept records of donations, such as receipts or bank records.

Donations made to donor-advised funds generally do not qualify for this deduction. Save your receipts, canceled checks or bank records to support your deduction when you file your 2026 tax return during the 2027 filing season.

What it means for a church or nonprofit

  • More donors will want a statement. Before 2026, a donor who took the standard deduction had no deduction to claim; now their cash gifts may count.
  • Keep money and property apart. The deduction is for cash contributions; a statement that lists money gifts separately from gifts of property (described, never valued) makes the donor's job easy.
  • Single gifts of $250 or more still need the written acknowledgment: A donor cannot claim a federal income tax deduction for any single contribution valued at $250 or more unless the donor obtains a contemporaneous written acknowledgment of the contribution from the donee charitable organization.
  • Do not promise a deduction. Whether a donor qualifies depends on their whole return; the statement records the gifts.

What changes for donors who itemize?

IRS Publication 505 (2026), What's New: "Beginning in 2026, if you itemize, you can only deduct charitable contributions that are more than 0.5% of your adjusted gross income. Any amount that falls under the 0.5% floor can't be deducted in 2026."

Neither change alters what an acknowledgment must contain; Publication 1771 is the rule for that. See what a giving statement must include.

Can my church be checked in the IRS search?

Publication 6196 points donors to the IRS Tax Exempt Organization Search (Pub. 78 data) and adds: "Some eligible organizations, such as certain churches, government organizations and organizations covered by a group exemption, may not appear separately in the search results."

Questions

How much can a non-itemizer deduct for 2026 gifts?

Eligible taxpayers who do not itemize may be able to deduct up to $1,000 of qualifying cash contributions made to eligible charitable organizations ($2,000 for married taxpayers filing jointly). (IRS Publication 6196).

Do gifts of stock or goods count?

The deduction is for qualifying cash contributions. Publication 505 calls it "a deduction for cash contributions made to eligible tax-exempt organizations".

Do gifts to a donor-advised fund count?

Publication 6196: "Donations made to donor-advised funds generally do not qualify for this deduction."

Does the statement need new wording?

No new wording is required by the IRS pages read for this guide. The usual acknowledgment (amounts, dates, the goods-or-services sentence) is the record donors keep. The statement maker can add an optional note telling donors about the deduction.

What changes for donors who itemize?

Beginning in 2026, if you itemize, you can only deduct charitable contributions that are more than 0.5% of your adjusted gross income. Any amount that falls under the 0.5% floor can't be deducted in 2026.
