# How each kind of gift shows on a giving statement

Money gifts (cash, check, card, bank transfer, online payment) are listed with their date and amount; gifts of property such as stock or goods are described, never valued (IRS Publication 1771); a vehicle, boat or airplane claimed at over $500 follows Form 1098-C rules instead. In the example below, $750.00 of money gifts and 1 in-kind gift share one statement.

## Each kind of gift

From IRS Publication 1771, and how the statement maker shows each.

| Gift | What the statement shows | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cash in the plate or an envelope | Date, fund and amount | Money gift |
| Check | Date, amount, and the check number if your sheet has a Method column | Money gift |
| Card, bank transfer, online or payroll | Date and amount | Money gift (Pub 1771 lists each of these) |
| Stock or other securities | A description, such as "20 shares of Example Corp common stock" | No value printed |
| Goods (in-kind) | A description, such as "Six cases of canned vegetables" | No value printed |
| Vehicle, boat or airplane over $500 | Flagged; not covered by Pub 1771 | Use Form 1098-C |
| Ticket, dinner or auction item | Payment, the good faith estimate of what was received, and the deductible part | Disclosure needed over $75 |
| Refund or reversal | Subtracted from the total, with a note | Enter as a negative amount or type "Refund" |
| Pledge not yet paid | Nothing until paid | List the payments |

## What counts as money?

A monetary contribution includes cash, check, a transfer of a gift card redeemable for cash, and a payment made by credit card, electronic fund transfer, an online payment service, or payroll deduction. Every money gift needs a record: A donor cannot claim a federal income tax deduction for any monetary contribution unless the donor maintains a record of the contribution or a timely written communication from the charitable organization showing the name of the organization, the date of the contribution, and the amount of the contribution.

## Why not put a value on stock or goods?

Publication 1771 asks the organization for "a description (but not the fair market value) of any contribution of property". Valuing it is the donor's job, and larger property gifts have their own appraisal and form rules that are the donor's to meet.

In a sheet, mark a gift of property by putting "In-kind" or "Stock" in the Type column, or by leaving the amount empty and filling in Description.

## Vehicles, boats and airplanes

The rules in this publication do not apply to a donated motor vehicle, boat, or airplane if the claimed value exceeds $500. The statement maker flags a property gift whose description names a vehicle, so you can handle it with Form 1098-C.

## Questions

### Should a statement put a value on donated stock or goods?

No. Publication 1771 asks for "a description (but not the fair market value) of any contribution of property". The donor values it.

### What counts as a money gift?

A monetary contribution includes cash, check, a transfer of a gift card redeemable for cash, and a payment made by credit card, electronic fund transfer, an online payment service, or payroll deduction.

### Does a donated car go on the giving statement?

The rules in this publication do not apply to a donated motor vehicle, boat, or airplane if the claimed value exceeds $500. Those use Form 1098-C, which this statement maker does not produce.

### Is a pledge a gift?

Not until it is paid. IRS Publication 526: "If you issue and deliver a promissory note to a charity as a contribution, it isn't a contribution until you make the note payments." List the payments as they come in.
